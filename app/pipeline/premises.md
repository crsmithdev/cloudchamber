# Overused premises

An enumerated list the resemblance checker matches a brief against, as
retrieval, never as a judgement. Seeded from the clusters the editorial lists
name (research/literature.md §4.2: Strange Horizons' horror and general
lists, Clarkesworld's guidelines). Chris's file: add, split and remove lines
freely; the checker quotes the line it matches verbatim.

1. The protagonist has been dead the whole time, or is a ghost without knowing it.
2. The protagonist is the killer, or the monster, and the reveal is the point.
3. The madman, the crank or the conspiracy theorist turns out to have been right.
4. A place is haunted and the story is the haunting, with nothing that the characters do or want.
5. A supernatural force is inevitable and the story is watching it arrive.
6. Ironic comeuppance: a bad person meets a fitting supernatural end.
7. Childhood abuse or a single trauma is the whole explanation for the monster or the villain.
8. A creepy child, a creepy doll, or a creepy clown carries the horror unaided.
9. A group enters an isolated place and is picked off one by one.
10. A ritual, curse, or found object is used exactly as the rules say and the rules are the plot.
11. A person wakes to find themself in an afterlife, a simulation, or a dream, and the reveal is the ending.
12. Adam and Eve, or the last two people, restart humanity.
13. A scientist creates something that kills them, and the moral is the story.
14. An artificial intelligence becomes conscious and decides to kill, enslave or replace humanity.
15. Aliens arrive and humanity is judged, harvested or farmed.
16. Time travel is used to kill or save one person and the paradox is the twist.
17. A person visits a small town with a dark secret that the whole town is in on.
18. Someone is granted wishes and the wishes go wrong in the expected order.
19. Death, the Devil or God appears as a person and makes a deal or a bargain.
20. A murderer or abuser tells the story from their point of view so the reader will be surprised by the reveal.
21. A weary writer, or a character who is a writer, struggles to write, and the story is about writing.
22. A person's reflection, shadow or double begins acting independently.
23. A mysterious new neighbour, tenant or coworker is revealed as not human.
24. A haunted technology: a cursed videotape, app, website, game or phone number.
25. The whole story is a vignette of mood with a final line that reveals the monster.
