# FOG BELT — CATALOGUE

*What has been written, what it keeps doing, and the instruments for reading a premise you already have. `playbook.md` is the other half of the pair: that one generates, this one describes. Nothing here is a rule and nothing here scores.*

**Two things live in this file.** §1–§5 are the slate as it stood on 2026-08-29: two passes over all twenty-four concepts, both run with `playbook.md` withheld — two standing expert panels checking factual and procedural claims, and eight readers taking one axis each and looking only for sameness. The story bodies were read with the 2026-08-28 red-team appendices stripped, so nothing there is downstream of that audit. §6–§9 are the descriptive apparatus, moved out of the playbook on 2026-08-29 because holding description and generation in one document made the generative half read as a rubric: the configurations the corpus has used, the failure modes it has produced, thirteen lenses, and the source lookup.

**Story-specific findings are not in this file.** Every panel correction, collision ruling, protect-list entry and proposed change sits in a `## Slate review and fact check, 2026-08-29` section at the end of its own story file. What is here is what is true of the slate rather than of any one story. Nothing anywhere has been applied.

*The eight axes: narrator and voice · institution and apparatus · artifact and container · time, schedule and bell · dread machinery and withholding · structure, turn and ending · the harm and the proposition · setting, period and texture.*

---

## 1. The finding

The stories do not collide much on premise. They collide on **temperature**.

Twenty-three of twenty-four are narrated by a warm, competent, mid-to-senior professional woman who never loses composure, holding a document that has no field for the thing the story is about, and thirteen of them end on that document being correctly completed. A reader going through the collection in order learns the house sound by story four and can predict the last clause of story eleven from its penultimate paragraph.

That is a register, and it is a good one — it is the thing the collection has that nothing else has. It is also, at this concentration, a tic. The premises are more various than the delivery by a wide margin, and the fix is almost never at premise level.

Three numbers carry the whole report:

- **13 of 24** end on a small correct administrative act, **8 of those** on a document being filed, and in **5** the *correctness* of the filing is itself the sting. One joke, five times.
- **12 of 24** narrators are warm competent professionals whose composure never breaks; in **7** of those the composure is the load-bearing horror. Play the middle ten minutes of 01, 02, 03, 05 and 12 back to back and you hear one voice.
- **5 arguments, told 24 times.** Only *Bloom* and *"Without Regards"* sit outside all five.

## 2. What is actually the same

### 2.1 The narrator

The slate has one narrator with twenty-three costumes. Every named narrator carries a hyphenated cross-cultural double surname — Anand-Vogel, Etxeberria-Nunes, Nkemdirim-Baptiste, Oyelowo-Marchetti, Petrakis-Amari, Achterberg-Silva, Sandoval-Achebe, Osei-Thorvaldsen, plus Ruth Abara-Lindqvist — and two of them are called Priya. A reader four stories in has learned the naming rule and will read the fifth name as the same authorial hand rather than a new person. Every unnamed narrator is also a woman. Twenty-three of twenty-four stories are narrated by women; 18 is the one mixed cast.

Worth stating plainly, because it is invisible from inside any single file: **the slate has almost no sampled narrator prose.** The only words actually put in a character's mouth anywhere in twenty-four files are *Root of Title*'s "why is my queue filling up", *Dwell Time*'s "finished, correct, beautiful", and *Recognition*'s "Undo what we did." Everything else is voice specified from outside — "warm in every single one", "unhurried, unsentimental", "measured, competent". Those specifications are interchangeable, and nine of them could be swapped between files without a reader noticing.

Four stories independently reach for the same sentence about the narrator's excellence being the mechanism:

> 01 — "her goodness at it is not a mask over something else — it is the mechanism functioning as designed."
> 02 — "the horror is that she is good, and that the better she trains them, the more precisely the rite is performed."
> 09 — "her signature is on the best worlds precisely because she was best at it."
> 17 — "every professional improvement deepening it. Her career is the liturgical apparatus. She wrote the index."

### 2.2 The closing gesture

Read all twenty-two written last beats consecutively and the collection has about four endings.

The dominant one, fourteen instances, is a single sentence shape: *a competent person performs a routine act, and a trailing subordinate clause makes it unbearable.* "which has never happened before" / "which were fictional" / "which is that the client may increase exposure" / "Which is true, and correctly observed" / "and it notes the excursion in her chart". The sting is always deferred to a dependent clause and the clause is always flat.

The second, five instances, is *the narrator returns to work*: "she goes back to doing the work well" (09), "So she stays and watches" (13), "Back on shift" (23).

The third, four instances, is *the apparatus makes a note about her*: 05, 15, 18, 24.

Two lexical tells run through the set and should be counted before anything else is written. **Correctly** or a variant closes 01, 09, 15, 22 and governs the ending logic of 03, 05, 08, 16. The **nobody** construction closes 09 ("Nobody checks"), 11 ("nobody asked her to"), 14 ("nobody has ever complained"), 22 ("No alarm, no cavalry"). These are one reflex, not four writers converging.

The genuine outliers are six: 06 (ends on a person going quiet mid-sentence), 07 (a stranger's small talk), 11 ("nobody asked her to, and she does not know if it helps"), 17 (a state, not an act), 20 (deadpan understatement over an atrocity), 21 (the only last beat with duration in it — "The child came back the next day, and the day after, and asked her name again each time").

### 2.3 The reader's position

- **Colleague** — you stand at a competent peer's shoulder and would have filed the same note: **13 of 24** (01, 02, 03, 05, 08, 09, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 22).
- **Auditor** — you are handed the record and run ahead of the narrator: 4 (06, 07, 18, 24).
- **Beneficiary** — this was done for you and you have already consumed it: 3 (04, 14, 20).
- **Complicit** — 4 (17, 19, 21, 23).

Eight of the thirteen colleague-stories are also composure-stories, so the default reader of this collection is a professional watching a warm peer do a job correctly, and that is more dominant than any count of premises suggests.

Separately: **22 of 24 put the reader level with or ahead of the narrator.** The pleasure of arriving *after* someone who already knows exists twice on the whole slate (20 and 21). That is the least varied column in any table produced by any reader.

### 2.4 The container

- **Interleaving two or more document types: 10 stories (42%)** — 05, 08, 10, 13, 15, 18, 19, 22, 23, 24. In the later files it is stated as a format decision rather than discovered: "Two registers, per *Dwell Time*." Within the ten there are three interleave logics and each is used three times, the tightest rhyme being *a filed register plus something unfileable* (10, 19, 24) — in all three a lone person maintains a second document beside the official one, and the second document is the story's moral centre.
- **The mandated professional filing: 6** — 03, 09, 10, 16, 19, 22. Four end on the recommendation itself.
- **Procedure performed on a person: 7** — 01, 02, 03, 05, 08, 11, 15.
- **A form with fields: 5** — 05, 08, 16, 19, 24. In four of the five the narrator is herself a value in the form.
- **A roster of numbered persons: 6, plus one index** — 10 ("Fourteen of them, alphabetical"), 11 (subject nineteen), 12 (411 to 411), 19, 24 (arm ninety-one), 02, plus 17's index. Two of them — 11 and 24 — take their pivot from a person's number, and both are about a roster entry that turns out to be looking back.
- **The maintained conscious body as prop: 7** — 03, 11, 12, 19, 20, 21, 24 — and in six of the seven the maintenance standard is explicitly good.

Categorical absences: **nothing on the slate is epistolary.** Not one letter, no correspondence file, twenty-four narrators and not one addressee who writes back. **Nothing is delivered through a money document** — no invoice, bill, statement or denial letter, despite four finance stories. **Nothing uses images.**

### 2.5 The clock

- **No terminus: 10 stories** end by explicitly refusing to end, several in near-identical phrasing — "no break clause" (06), "no principal and no term… it does not conclude" (12), "no attrition, no mercy" (11), "It was never told the study ended" (24), "keep the servers on" (04). This is the slate's actual house clock, larger and less noticed than the bell.
- **Quarterly: 5** — 01, 02, 03, 05, 12. The corporate reporting quarter is the single most common named interval, and it arrives with the same furniture each time.
- **The bell: 8 explicit, 11 counting sound-as-residue.** A third of the slate. It would be noticed — but not because the count is high. It is noticed because the sound always does the same job (interval marker, never atmosphere), is nearly always on a decades-long slope, and is nearly always *unreceived*: a receiver decommissioned in the nineties (07), a signal with no correspondent (19), a tone never presented (16), a response arriving before the decision (14). The first bell that turns out to be talking to nobody is superb. The fourth is a house move.
- **The bell as production vocabulary.** Four story files carry it as an open checkbox — 12 "[TODO] Ending and bell not yet written", 14 "[TODO] Ending and bell", 16 and 17 "Ending and bell are provisional". The device has been promoted from an image to a structural slot with a name. That is what a tic looks like from the inside. 14 and 16 are already the closest tonal pair on the slate; if a bell is written into both endings they will finish the same way as well as running the same clock.
- **Mid-century founding: 4, and all four are Cold War federal defence science** — 07 (1946–63 Navy dump), 16 ("getting louder since 1957"), 24 (NRDL, Bikini), 14 (a 1972 spec). 07 and 24 are the same founding twice: a Navy programme on contaminated ground that outlived its reason and cannot be decommissioned. Note the unplanned symmetry — 07's signal has been getting *quieter* since the mid-fifties, 16's *louder* since 1957.
- **Deep-time backdrop: 5** — four billion years (08), eighty million (18), twelve thousand (10), three thousand (16), three hundred (21). Each is there to make the recent interval look like a rounding error. Used five times, it stops doing that.

### 2.6 The dread

- **Composure as engine: 7** (01, 02, 03, 05, 11, 12, 13), plus five more wearing it as texture.
- **"And they were right": 11 stories** contain a sentence whose only job is to say the institution was correct. "the facility passes, because it does pass" (03) · "which is true, and which Joan believes because it is good data" (05) · "it is good medicine, and no reasonable person declines it" (08) · "everyone in the room is doing their job properly" (13) · "Which is true, and correctly observed, by someone doing their job" (15) · "Nobody did anything wrong at any point" (16) · "It is the correct decision" (04).
- **Duration with no exit: 5** (06, 11, 12, 19, 21), and four of the five end on a permanently conscious, permanently immobilised person. That image is at capacity.
- **Care that is capture: 5** (02, 20, 21, 23, 24) — the second-largest cluster, and unlike composure its five members produce genuinely different feelings.
- **Withholding** splits three ways: redaction or an unwritten log (13 stories), a narrator who genuinely does not know (11), and *no vocabulary exists* (4 — 07, 12, 19, 21). The doubling in 11, 13, 21, 23 is where the withholding is strongest, and 23 is the only story where the two are the same act.

### 2.7 The propositions

Twenty-four one-sentence claims resolve to five arguments, and two of them carry more than half the slate.

**A. Interiority is overhead, and any process that measures it will remove it — 9.** 01, 05, 08, 14, 16, 18, 19, 20, 23. Three costumes: the self removed as surplus (01, 19, 20), the *interval* removed (14, 16), the *noticing* removed (05, 18, 23).

**B. A deferred obligation matures and settles on whoever is holding the paper — 5.** 06, 07, 10, 12, 22. All five locate the injury in the gap between an act and its consequence, and all five have a state or church employee administering the settlement.

**C. Competence is the mechanism — 4 as a primary claim** (02, 09, 13, 17), and texture in all twenty-four.

**D. Care is the delivery vehicle and the warmth is real — 3 as the argument** (20, 21, 24), **8 as machinery.** Two files flag "the warmth was real and it was also the test" as a duplicate beat. It is in eight.

**E. The metaphysics is true, so the institutions are correct, and correctness is unbearable — 5.** 06, 07, 17, 19, 21, with 22 as the Catholic variant.

A and B are both arguments about time — *the thing was removed and the removal is invisible*, and *the debt was deferred and the deferral is invisible*. The slate has one deep instinct, which is that harm is legible only from outside the interval in which it happened. Everything else is casting.

### 2.8 The ground

**"San Francisco" does not appear once in twenty-four stories.** Neither does *concrete*, *fluorescent*, *laminate*, *beige*, *chain-link*, *steel*, *glass*, *linoleum*, *brick* or *asphalt*. The only colour word in the corpus is "a faint green tracery at wrists and inner elbow" (11). *Fog* appears in the collection's name, in a retired story's title, and nowhere else.

So the texture tic is the absence of texture. The recurring physical objects are all documentary nouns — queue, log, register, chart, roster, rubric, schedule — and **twelve of twenty-four stories could be relocated to Denver without changing a sentence.**

Geography: San Francisco proper **4** (07, 11, 22, 24) · East Bay **1** (09, Emeryville and Richmond) · Peninsula **0** · South Bay **0** · North Bay **0** as a setting, one word as a colleague's family origin · California but not setting-a **2** (10, 12) · setting-a asserted with nothing behind it **2** (15 "very setting-a", 23 "near-future setting-a", the latter conceding "needs work — currently the weakest element") · explicitly elsewhere **4** (16, 17, 19, 21) · anywhere at all **11**.

Six stories have a setting a reader could find on a map, and one of those imports its central building from Lower Manhattan (24 is modelled on 33 Thomas Street).

Period: the decade the collection keeps returning to is **the 1850s** — four stories anchor there and three for the same reason, the moment Anglo-American legal instruments were laid over Spanish-Mexican California. The second cluster is the mid-1950s. The collection has two founding moments, the land instrument and the atomic-era federal parcel, and uses them repeatedly.

Nobody in twenty-four stories eats a meal except in 20, and nobody looks out of a window except in 24, where the point is that there isn't one.

## 5. What the slate does not use

Not a to-do list. A map of where the variety is, for when a new premise needs somewhere to stand.

**Institutions.** No utility and no rate base — no rate case, no municipal utility district, no water district; nobody in twenty-four stories ever pays a bill. No special district or JPA of any kind, though 12 routes a perpetual-care obligation through a bank trust department when California built the public cemetery district for exactly that. No integrated nonprofit health plan: four clinical stories and not one payer, so nothing here is ever denied, prior-authorised or out of network. No university as a body — no department, IRB, tenure case or tech transfer — despite how many narrators are researchers. No school district. No union, local, hiring hall or grievance, though 02 turns on employment law and 23 says one tier is "unionised". No federally recognised tribe as a governing body, though 07 and 22 are both about a duty that passed out of Native hands — 22 in particular has a NAGPRA-shaped hole in it. No nonprofit on government contract. **No local government at all**: no county, no city hall, no board of supervisors, no commission, no elected official, no public meeting, nobody at a podium — in twenty-four stories about California institutions, and in a story (22) that runs entirely on environmental review.

**Container forms.** No letter and no correspondence file. No money document — invoice, bill, statement, denial. No images. Specifically available and unused: a CPUC rate-case exhibit with an intervenor's data request · a coroner's inquest file with an amended death certificate · a Phase I site assessment with a recorded land-use covenant · a union grievance file through arbitration · an adverse-event series plus a recall notice · a chargemaster line beside an EOB denial · a probate inventory and a conservatorship accounting · a watermaster's annual report under a groundwater decree · a stamped calculation package with a peer-review comment matrix · a patent file wrapper · a photographic survey sheet with a later cataloguer's correction slip · an airworthiness directive with a maintenance logbook · a registrar's purge list with a cure period.

**Clocks.** An interval that is *getting shorter* as the spine — it appears three times as late decoration and is nobody's architecture. A sunset clause nobody expects to reach. A schedule kept by something other than a person — tides, a spawning run, a freight timetable that outlived its operator. A redefined unit: the instrument honoured perfectly while the calendar moved. A *protective* clock expiring — every countdown here matures a claim; none removes a shield. The handover, the ninety seconds between two competent shifts. The wait itself. Compounding: every accumulation on the slate is linear. Per-birth time, which 12 gestures at and buries.

**Harms.** No harm is ever stopped, refused or survived — twenty-four for twenty-four, which is a prohibition rather than a register. Money as itself: three markets, a trust department and a title chain, and no story about a person losing a house. Collective action of any kind: twelve thousand moderators and forty million workers, all alone. Race as a live present mechanism rather than a colonial past. Policing, incarceration, detention. Children as anyone whose experience is on the page — the slate injures children constantly and has never once been inside one. Food, land and outdoor work. A trade that was real and correctly priced. Old age as itself. Being the person in the room without the vocabulary — odd, on a slate whose recurring mechanism is a document only one person can read.

**Places.** The Port of Oakland and drayage · Silicon Valley's actual poisoned ground, the fab-site plumes and vapour intrusion under the office parks · Colma · refinery row · a hospital corridor, in four medical stories · water as architecture, Hetch Hetchy to the Pulgas temple · the Delta at levee level · wine-country fire, in the fire story · farm labour · Alameda, Mare Island, Treasure Island · SRO and residential-hotel stock, in a story whose premise is four hundred single-occupancy rooms · BART itself, in the transit story that dates its own founding document to 1972 · Oyster Point and Mission Bay · Vallejo, Antioch, Pittsburg, where everyone this slate harms would actually live · and the fog.

**One slate-wide change, at no structural cost:** retire the hyphenated-double-surname rule for at least half the named cast, and give at least two narrators to men. As it stands the naming convention tells a reader four stories in that they are in the same book, before a word of voice arrives.

## 6. Recurring shapes — the configurations the corpus has used

*Descriptive, not prescriptive. Stories routinely run two at once, and the best ones have found a shape that isn't here yet. An unused shape is not a vacancy — what is worth borrowing from an entry below is usually its questions, not its silhouette.*

### The maintained schedule

> **Something has been operating for a long time on a correct principle nobody remembers, maintained by competent people who are not deceived, at a cost that is scheduled, documented, and — on the evidence — worth paying.**

The oldest shape and still the most common: about half the slate cleanly, another quarter partially. It does not fit *Bloom*, *"Without Regards"*, *Designated Pathogen Free* or *Dwell Time*, and *Second Circulation* contradicts one of its clauses outright — everyone there is deceived, and the deception is the premise.

**To generate:** name the schedule before you name the anomaly. What is the interval — weekly, quarterly, every 378 days? Who signs the variance request when it slips? What does the requisition list?

**Where it stands.** Eleven stories fit this cleanly — an observation about the slate, not a quota. A twelfth is worth writing if it isn't doing what the other eleven already do.

### The correct intervention

*A real, well-evidenced, genuinely good intervention whose success is the mechanism.* Nobody is wrong, the data is clean, the outcomes improve, and that is the problem. The tell is that there is no reformable failure to point at — the thing worked.

**To generate:** name a real programme with a published metric. Ask what the metric stopped measuring, who is now structurally unnecessary, and what happens to them, on a schedule, with a form.

**Running:** Continuity of Care · Viability · Right Concentration · Threshold · Designated Pathogen Free · Super-Donor.

**Well-worn:** the intervention whose cost falls on the people it treats. Try one where the cost falls on people outside the programme entirely, or one where the intervention is *sincerely designed by someone who is not us* — SCP-9138's paradise is engineered in good faith by an obligate filial cannibal, and every beneficiary is grateful. `[S]`

### The arriving fact

*Something true and already in motion that no institution can stop, where the story is its administration rather than its discovery.* Nothing is hidden and there is nothing to uncover; the horror is the timetable, the ranked list, the roster change.

**To generate:** start from the memo that acknowledges it. Who is on the distribution list? What is the first thing that gets rescheduled?

**Running:** Suppression · Bloom · Offsets · Dwell Time.

### The inherited post `[S]`

*The job was already being done when the institution arrived.* It took over the rota without ever learning what the rota is for, and it cannot stop without finding out. SCP-1348 inherits a liturgy from a ten-limbed predecessor that starved rather than break the schedule, and staffs it by maternal descent while disqualifying anyone who can understand the words. SCP-2845 commissions its ritual from an outside occultist who tells them plainly that nothing can ever be substituted or revised. SCP-7219 is one occult agency's prisoner inherited by another, with no interval of freedom in between.

**To generate:** who was doing this before? What did the institution keep, and what did it not understand well enough to know it was discarding? What is the disqualifying criterion for the staff — and what does that criterion tell the reader about the job?

**Unused on the slate.** Nothing on the current 24 runs this cleanly. An unused shape is not a vacancy and not something the slate is missing. What is worth borrowing is the four questions above — who was doing this before, what got kept, what got discarded, who is disqualified — which are useful against any premise. The shape itself is only one answer to them, and a slate where several stories took it would be worse, not better.

### The sincere paradise `[S]`

*Someone designed the good outcome, meant it, and got it wrong in a way that cannot be appealed.* Not a trap and not a lie — a specification. SCP-7179's afterlife solves comfort completely and never considers duration. SCP-9138's heaven rewards closeness, selflessness and fecundity, sincerely, according to a species for whom closeness means being eaten by your children.

**To generate:** write the design document for the good outcome. What did the designer optimise? What did they not think to bound? Then write the testimony of someone enjoying it, in the register of gratitude, and let the reader do the translating.

### The one-way channel `[S]`

*Someone is reporting from inside something nobody can reach, and the institution's entire role is to receive and file.* SCP-7034 is fourteen days of a researcher narrating his own moral collapse into a communicator that cannot answer him. SCP-9377 is a porthole and a sixty-second window. SCP-2718 is a woman write-protecting a file in the minutes before she expects to be killed.

**To generate:** make the reader occupy the receiving seat, not the rescuing one. What is the institution's standing instruction about what to say into the channel? (SCP-9377's is to keep promising rescue to men the same file has already classified as killed in action.)

**Adjacent on the slate:** *Dwell Time*'s two registers, *Designated Pathogen Free*'s generational accounts. Neither is one-way.

## 7. Anti-patterns — the failure modes the corpus has produced

*Listed so they are recognisable early. A premise sitting near one of these is worth a second look rather than a rejection — the difference between a failure mode and a deliberate move is usually whether the story knows it is there.*

**Watts failure modes**

- *Cartesian bait-and-switch* — "it was a simulation / she was a machine all along." Threatens the contents of the self, not its necessity. Files as a twist.
- *Vibes-only nihilism* — announcing at length that free will is a lie, with no mechanism, no cost, no scene where the claim does work.
- *The flattering monster* — the hive exists so someone can defy it and prove individuality matters.
- *Edgelord grimness* — grimness without the citation apparatus is weather.

**Chiang failure modes**

- A second impossibility introduced to escape a corner.
- The speculative element used as a metaphor for a psychological one — which lets the audience defuse it by translating it.
- Escalation substituted for implication.

**Institutional failure modes**

- The cargo cult. Do not write a ritual that is a misunderstanding; write one that is a complete, functioning technical procedure whose meaning has been lost while its efficacy has not.
- The obviously-right reformer. Correctness should increase isolation, not leverage.
- Delusion instead of competence. If the audience can say *if only they knew*, the story is defused.
- Domestic centre. Every rejected pitch had one relationship or one person's reckoning at its heart.
- *The institutional gloss.* A premise that describes an apparatus and leaves the harm implied — a schedule, a criterion, a filing, and no statement of what is physically done to anyone or why they cannot leave. It reads as competent and lands as nothing. The cruelty has to be derived from the mechanism and named in the specific; where it is only gestured at, what exists is a setting.
- The artifact that exists to narrate. If it isn't doing a job, it's a testimony wrapper.
- *The villain as shortcut.* The institution is permitted to be the antagonist outright; it is not permitted to be stupid, gloating, or uninterested in justifying itself. What is optional is the exoneration, not the rigour.

**Evangelion failure modes**

- *The mercy as a lie.* If the terrible solution does not actually solve the suffering, refusing it costs nothing and the piece is a fable.
- *The interiority tour.* Psychological collapse rendered at length for its own sake, with no apparatus around it — the domestic-centre rejection, arrived at from the other direction.
- *The iconography without the engineering.* Borrowed sacred imagery doing mood work while the metaphysics stays unspecified. Steal the apparatus, not the vestments.
- *The child as pathos delivery.* If the vulnerable operator exists to be pitied rather than correctly processed by a competent institution, it is sentiment in a uniform.

**SCP failure modes** `[S]`

- *The gore inventory.* Escalating physical description that accumulates nothing — the failure is the accumulation, not the description; the register's best work is extremely physical. Annex F's worst material is anatomical and its best is arithmetical — the difference is whether the reader is doing a sum.
- *The interpretive narrator.* A file that tells the reader how to feel about its own contents. The strongest entries in the annex have no assessment section at all, and stop the instant the question becomes unbearable.
- *The blank that hides nothing.* Withholding only works where the withheld thing is load-bearing and the format supplies a legitimate reason for the gap. An expunged tag over material the reader can trivially reconstruct is decoration.
- *The twist that is only a reclassification.* Changing the object class is not the same as changing what the reader understands. The reveal has to alter the meaning of the pages already read.
- *Cosmology for its own sake.* Naming a wider mythology is only free when the document has no access to it either. If the file can explain the librarians and the deathless city, it should, and then it is a different and lesser story.

## 8. Thirteen lenses — reading a premise you already have

*Fifteen minutes, once a premise exists. `playbook.md` helps you find one; these help you find out what you have. Nothing here scores: a lens that shows nothing has told you the story isn't using that dimension, which is a fact about the story rather than a fault in it. Item 11 is the exception — see it.*

1. **Is the apparatus real?** A setting-a institution with a founding date, a cover story, a rate card, and a schedule. What does it *do* every week?
2. **Is the theme stated as a mechanism with units,** rather than as a mood?
3. **Would the artifact exist without the story?** If not, it is a testimony wrapper — worth knowing before the premise gets built on it.
4. **Follow the premise to its second-order effects** — the price list, the exception process, the revision history, the people who game it. If it needs a second impossibility, go back to the theme.
5. **Where does the self stop being necessary,** who noticed first, and what did the returns look like afterward?
6. **The normalisation lens.** Ratepayers, not fanatics. Who files the variance request? What is the bond measure called? *(This makes the institution legible, not innocent — it may still simply be the antagonist.)*
7. **The load-bearing lens.** Remove the awful thing. Does the system stop, or merely get nicer? Either answer tells you what the institution is actually for. A story built on an arriving fact will often answer neither, because nobody can remove it — that is the shape working, and this lens is structurally blind to it.
8. **The instrument lens.** Could a machine do it instead? If yes, it's engineering. If it needs a specific person, whose signature is on the authorisation?
9. **The inheritance lens.** `[S]` Who was doing this before the institution arrived, and what did it fail to understand when it took over?
10. **Which dread mechanism (`playbook.md` §3) is carrying this, and which withholding move (M11–M19, M58, M59, M62, M65, in `playbook.md` §6.4)?** If more than one of either is running they are likely diluting each other, and it is worth knowing which one you would keep. The count is a diagnostic, not a limit.
11. **Check the slate.** `stories/`, 24 developed plus the bench. Does this feel like one of the others — same voice, same rhythm, same institution under a different name? This is the one item anywhere that is a criterion rather than a lens, and it outranks everything else in either file.
12. **Look for the last line.** One beat past the horror, small, administrative, correctly filed. Not having one yet is normal — these are concepts, not finished stories — but the search usually shows you where the story actually ends.
13. **Name the bell.** The recurring sound that marks the schedule.

### 10.3 Errors that recur across stories — Panel A


**R1 — Giving a regulator's powers to a standards body, and vice versa.** This is the slate's single most repeated institutional mistake, and it appears in three files with three different disciplines:
- **16 Threshold:** a "national audiological standards officer" signing a federal reference curve — the curve belongs to ANSI/ASA S3.6, a private consensus standard OSHA merely cites.
- **09 Offsets:** a verification body that also writes baselines and methodologies — those belong to the developer and the registry respectively.
- **03 Runway:** an "independent auditor" who inspects collateral and then recommends an investment position — assurance practitioners issue findings, not recommendations.

In each case the story has collapsed *the body that sets the rule*, *the body that does the work*, and *the body that checks the work* into one person, because one person is easier to narrate. But that three-way separation is not decoration — in each field it is the entire integrity architecture, and practitioners feel its absence the way a musician feels a wrong note. The good news is that the fix is the same each time and it is always small: name the right body, and let the narrator be the person who *represents* their employer to it. A modeller who has to defend a baseline to an external VVB is more interesting than one who verifies her own.

**R2 — Assuming the document is public.** Stories **22** and **13** both build a plot beat on a record being disclosable, and **22** has it exactly backwards (archaeological and burial records are among the most strongly exempted records in California). **10** has a milder version — the narrator's "register of what the settled towns were called," a second document nobody asked for, would in fact be a public record subject to CPRA disclosure the moment anyone requested it, which is a live threat the story does not use. The panel's general note: this slate loves documents, and it consistently assumes documents default to open. In California public agencies, the interesting ones default to *contested*, and the exemption fight is often where the horror actually lives.

**R3 — The missions as a single undifferentiated instrument.** Stories **6**, **7** and **22** all reach into the mission period, and all three treat "the missions" as one institution with one continuous legal and ritual character from 1769 to the present. In practice the period breaks into sharply different regimes — Spanish (1769–1821, missions hold usufruct, no fee title), Mexican (1821–1846, secularisation in 1833–34 dissolves the mission estates and grants the land out as ranchos), and American (1846–, the 1851 Act, the Board of Land Commissioners, the patents, and the small Lincoln patents returning the church buildings). Story 6's error 6.2 is the acute form; 7 and 22 have the milder version. Any of the three would be improved by naming which regime it is in, and 6 cannot be fixed without doing so.

**R4 — Round dates that are one real event off.** 7's "1946–1963" (the site ran to 1970), 22's "1776" as the arrival of the rite in California (1769), 12's "1954" Delta flood (1950 or 1955), 9's "founded 1994" against a concept dated 1997–2001. In each case the surrounding research is good enough that the reader trusts the number, which is why the miss stings. These are the cheapest fixes on the slate — four numbers.

**R5 — Confusing "prohibited" with "unavailable."** 20's carbapenems in livestock (not prohibited by 21 CFR §530.41 — simply never approved for veterinary use at all, which is a different and more absolute barrier) and 15's NDA (not merely disfavoured — voided by statute). Both stories reason from "this is the kind of thing that gets done" to "so it is done here," without checking whether the specific mechanism is legally reachable. In both cases the correction supplies a *better* mechanism, which suggests the underlying instinct was sound and only the instrument was wrong.

### 10.4 Errors that recur across stories — Panel B


**A. A named signatory invented where the real world has a ballot.** Story 16's *"national audiological standards officer — the person who signs the reference calibration curve"* (it is **ANSI/ASA S3.6**, balloted by **ASC S3**, traceable to **NIST**). Story 14's *"written design brief"* specifying a behavioural compliance latency (no transit standard specifies one; they specify level, band, duration and distinguishability). Story 9's *"verification body… certifying project-level additionality"* (additionality is assessed at **validation**; VVBs are **accredited** and do not certify; **registries** issue). Three stories, one shape. In every case the real machinery is *colder* than the invention — a working group of nine people balloting a constant is more frightening than an officer with a pen, and story 16 already knows this, since its ending is a vote.

**B. Statutory power attributed to the individual professional rather than to the triggering event.** Story 22's monitor *"legally empowered to halt the job"* (**H&SC §7050.5** halts it; the coroner, the **NAHC** and the **MLD** act, not her). Story 6's examiner whose note makes title unmarketable *"in ninety seconds"* (one underwriter's exception binds one underwriter). Story 3's auditor recommending increased exposure (independence rules forbid it). The house move — a competent functionary who is merely the final signature — keeps being paid for with an authority the functionary does not have. It is fixable everywhere by the same edit: make the **event** mandatory and the functionary merely the person who has to be standing there when it fires. That is also the stories' own thesis.

**C. A decade-scale total that does not reconcile with the annual rate stated beside it.** Story 10's century against twenty million acres. Story 7's 1946–1963 against sixty years of decay. Story 21's marking onset against its duration against its lifespan. Story 17's forty years against seventy. Four instances of one failure: the story states a rate and a total and does not multiply.

**D. Molecular and microbiological names attached to the wrong function.** Story 18: **syncytin** attributed to HERV-K (it is HERV-W); **HERV-K** attributed to pigs (it is primate-only); **PERVs** described as unremovable (59 of them are removed in the current source animal). Story 20: **carbapenem resistance** attributed to livestock dosing (carbapenems are not used in food animals; the agricultural drug is **colistin**); **botulinum toxin** cited as the phage-encoded example (true only of the animal serotypes). Four errors of one kind, all in the two stories whose notes claim the biology is "all real, all checkable, played at full complexity" and "none simplified."

**E. A plausible-sounding interval used where a published one exists.** Story 2's eight-month contract ceiling (the norms are **18 months** and **two years**; the benefits thresholds are **1,000 hours / 12 months**). Story 19's fourteen-month blood draw (**24–48 hours** for newborn screening; **12 months** for lead). Story 8's annual audit for accreditation (**CLIA competency is annual**; **CAP accreditation is biennial**). Story 13's 220 wpm (**225** RPR / **200** CA CSR). In all four the published figure was available and is no less usable.

### 10.5 Cross-story contradictions — Panel A


**C1 — The missions, twice, incompatibly, in the same decade and the same square mile.**

- **06:** "By the 1770s the ground's resident deity is losing… What the friars offer is not a purchase… continuity of attendance. We will concentrate your people in one place and require daily observance at fixed hours in perpetuity."
- **22:** "The rite arrives in California at Mission Dolores in 1776… emergency baptisms on the unconscious, the uncomprehending, the days-catechised. The register records every one, along with the priest's doubts."

Both stories date their originating event to the founding of Mission Dolores, both make the congregated Ohlone the material, both make the sacramental record the instrument, and both produce a two-and-a-half-century obligation that comes due in the present-day setting-a. The files each carry a differentiation note against the *other* story, and each note is about mechanism (covenant vs. sacrament, specific performance vs. consumption) — but neither notices that they have claimed the same year, the same mission, the same congregation and the same register. A reader who meets both will not experience two stories; they will experience one setting with two contradictory metaphysics attached.

*Which is right:* on the facts, **22 is the more accurate** and **6 is the more fixable**. 22's mission material is verifiably correct (the registers, the burial count, the conditional formula, the Ramaytush/Chochenyo linguistic geography); its errors are peripheral. 6's mission material contains the slate's deepest legal error and needs to move anyway (see 6.1–6.3). The panel's recommendation is therefore to move **6** off Mission Dolores entirely: put its covenant in a *rancho* grant confirmed by the Board in the 1850s — which is where it has to live for the doctrine to work — and let 22 keep 1776 and the mission.

**C2 — Whether the missions destroyed the office by fraud or by attrition.**

- **06:** "It does not discover the fraud by reading. It discovers it by starving while the instrument says it is being paid."
- **07:** "The missions do not betray that office — and the distinction from Root of Title is load-bearing — because this is attrition rather than fraud. What the mission system destroys is the office's ability to reproduce its personnel, not its knowledge."

This one is **handled** — 7's note names it explicitly as a deliberate distinction, and the distinction is real and well-drawn. The panel flags it only to observe that with 22 added to the slate, the reader now meets "the missions and what they broke" three times, and 6's version is the one asserting the most and standing on the least.

**C3 — The canonical hours as engineering specification, asserted three times as though newly discovered.**

- **07:** "The canonical hours are the specification, not the tradition; somebody worked out the required cadence, and the Church later inherited the numbers without the reason."
- **06:** "require daily observance at fixed hours in perpetuity… Bodies, hours, bells."
- **16:** "organised worship was never superstition here but maintenance, a duty cycle of fixed hours and fixed words aloud in unison, arrived at independently by every tradition because it was load-bearing rather than devotional."

Not a factual contradiction — all three are internally consistent, and the underlying fact (seven daytime offices, from Psalm 119:164) is correct in all three. But it is the same *revelation* three times, and in 16 it is stated as universal ("arrived at independently by every tradition"), which retroactively makes 7's and 6's versions parochial instances of 16's rule. If all three ship, one of them should be demoted from a discovery to an assumption.

**C4 — "Dwell time" is a term of art in story 14 and the title of story 19.**

Story **14** is about transit door-closing tones, and *dwell time* is the transit industry's own term for exactly the interval that story is about — how long a train sits with its doors open. Story **19** is called *Dwell Time* and means something else entirely. A transit-literate reader will assume 19 is the train story. Cheap to fix: 19's own note already offers four alternative titles.

**C5 — Two narrators named Priya.**

- **01:** "Priya Anand-Vogel, forty-one, the fund's Head of Platform"
- **16:** "Dr. Priya Sandoval-Achebe, the programme's national audiological standards officer"

Both are the sole narrator of their story, both are institutional professionals, both are the competent person inside the machine. Not a factual error, but on a 24-story slate with a house style of double-barrelled surnames, this is the kind of collision that reads as an authoring artifact. (Relatedly: **22**'s note calls its narrator a "Renata-figure," borrowing **06**'s narrator's name as a type — which is fine as a working note but should not survive into a treatment.)

**C6 — A genuine, verified, and currently unexploited connection between 07 and 24.**

Not a contradiction — a link the slate has not noticed it made. Story **7**'s barrels and story **24**'s tower are the *same institution*. The **Naval Radiological Defense Laboratory** at Hunters Point is where much of the Farallones waste originated and where the Bikini ships were decontaminated before some of them were scuttled at the dump site — including the carrier *Independence*, which is on the seafloor inside 7's dive area. Story 24 already cites NRDL correctly. Story 7 never names it. The drums in 7 were packed on the pier that 24's windowless tower stands beside. If the slate wants one connective thread that costs nothing and is entirely true, this is it.

### 10.6 Cross-story contradictions — Panel B


**1. Mission-era chronology — story 6 against story 22.**
Story 22: *"The rite arrives in California at Mission Dolores in 1776."*
Story 6: *"By the 1770s the ground's resident deity is losing… What the friars offer… the covenant will guarantee the form"* — with the covenant seated in *"the root land grant."*
**Story 22 is right; story 6 is wrong about the instrument.** 1776 is the correct founding date for Mission San Francisco de Asís. But there were **no private land grants in Alta California in the 1770s** — the first Spanish concession is 1784, and the Mexican ranchos are 1821–1846. The bargain can stay in 1776; the covenant has to be carried by an instrument that existed, and the natural one is the mission's own founding paperwork as later confirmed by **U.S. patent** — which is also how story 6's "diocesan holdings" got to the top of the seniority list.

**2. The canonical hours — stories 6, 7 and 16 run the same mechanism on the same real fact.**
Story 7: *"Seven attendances a day, spaced, forever. The canonical hours are the specification, not the tradition."*
Story 6: *"require daily observance at fixed hours in perpetuity."*
Story 16: *"a duty cycle of fixed hours and fixed words aloud in unison."*
No factual contradiction — all three are consistent with *septies in die laudem dixi tibi* (Ps 118/119:164) — **but the count must be settled once across the slate.** The Divine Office comprises **eight** offices, **seven** of them daytime. Story 7's seven is correct on the scriptural reading; if any story downstream says eight, one of them will read as an error. **Story 7 is right.**

**3. Institutional decay — story 7 against story 24.**
Story 7: oversight is lost through **jurisdictional transfer**, *"each transfer legitimate and each losing one piece of context."*
Story 24: oversight is lost because the review board *"dissolved when the funding structure that convened it stopped existing."*
Same move, and **story 7's version is factually accurate** — Navy → EPA → NOAA is what actually happened at the Farallones. **Story 24's is not** — IRBs do not dissolve with funding. The fix is to bring 24 up to 7's rigour: the **assurance lapsed**, and unfunded research at an unassured institution is outside the Common Rule's scope. Same emotional beat, correct machinery.

**4. Hunters Point and the Farallon barrels — stories 7 and 24 are describing one supply chain and should be made to agree.**
Story 7 has the Navy needing to *"dispose of fifty thousand barrels nobody had a plan for."* Story 24 places the **Naval Radiological Defense Laboratory** at Hunters Point. **These are the same operation:** the NRDL was the principal generator of the waste dumped at the Farallones, and the barrels were loaded out of Hunters Point. Not a contradiction — a connection the slate is already half-making. But it imposes a consistency requirement: **NRDL operated 1946–1969; the dumping ran 1946–1970.** Story 7's "1946–1963" is wrong and will collide with story 24 the moment a reader notices both.

**5. Screening returning a correct negative — stories 8, 18 and 20.**
Story 18: *"every monitoring programme looks for replication-competent virus… and the answer is always negative."* Story 20: *"screening is the binding constraint on supply."* Story 8: the rubric measures exactly the wrong thing and is right about what it measures.
No contradiction of fact; all three are individually sound. Flagged only because it is the third and fourth use of one engine, which the writers' own differentiation notes already catch.

## 11. Method, and what to distrust

This describes how §1–§5 were produced; §6–§9 came out of `playbook.md` unchanged except for their numbering. Eight readers, one axis each, all twenty-four story bodies with the red-team appendices stripped so nothing was downstream of an earlier audit. None read `playbook.md`; none was asked about conformance; severity vocabulary was forbidden. Two expert panels ran the same corpus for factual error under the same conditions, self-selecting their seats after reading rather than before.

What to distrust:

- **Single-axis rulings on which of two stories is "better".** Each reader judged that within its own column. Where the same pair surfaced on three or more axes the rulings agreed, which is why §3 is ordered by axis count rather than by any one reader's confidence. The two-axis pairs at the foot of the table are weaker calls.
- **Counts of things that are described rather than written.** These are concepts. A "voice" here is usually a specification of a voice, so voice similarity is partly an artefact of specifications being terser than prose. The narrator-rhyme finding survives that caveat — nine specifications are interchangeable — but the last-line finding is stronger than the voice finding, because last lines are actually written.
- **Places where the two panels disagree.** They were run independently and were not reconciled. Both readings are preserved side by side in the story files, labelled, rather than resolved: 20's phage-encoded toxins and the sterile-filtrate result, 18's PERV removal, 11's plant count, 13's words-per-minute figure, 21's lifespan arithmetic, and several confidence markings that differ between the panels for the same claim.
- **The absence lists (§5).** They record what is not there, which is not the same as what is missing. Half of them are only interesting.
- **Anything about the bench.** 00 was read but held separate. Worth one note anyway: four of the seven greenlit one-liners are premise-statements of exactly the essayistic-opening kind the developed slate already over-uses, and they will inherit the problem unless built differently.
