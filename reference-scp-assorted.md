#### SCP-231 — Special Personnel Requirements

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-231-7 is a young woman whose age the file gives only as a redacted range. She is the seventh and only surviving subject recovered after a police raid on a warehouse operated by a cult, the Children of the Scarlet King. Within twenty-four hours of recovery SCP-231-1 went into labour, gave birth, and produced an event with over three hundred confirmed casualties. From notebooks taken off the cultists the Foundation derived Procedure 110-Montauk, which is now carried out on SCP-231-7 at least once every twenty-four hours by six Class D personnel housed in cells adjacent to hers. What the procedure consists of, the document never says. The text walks up to it and stops: "[DATA EXPUNGED PER ORDER OF O5-██ … INFORMATION MOVED TO EYES-ONLY DOCUMENT 231-110-MONTAUK]". The withholding is permanent, and nothing later in the article walks it back.

Around that hole the containment procedures are unusually specific. A soundproof cell. Cameras covering every inch of it, manned twenty-four hours a day, malfunctioning equipment replaced without delay by psychologically screened staff. Magnetic doors that open only on positive action from a control facility, including the doors linking her cell to the six others. She is restrained to a hospital bed at all times except during the procedure, hydrated by IV drip, fed twice daily through a tube by approved medical personnel "who have not taken the Hippocratic Oath." No narcotics, no anaesthesia, no unapproved medication under any circumstances. A Security Clearance 4/231 officer must monitor the procedure by camera throughout, "although the sound may be turned off if the vocalizations of SCP-231-7 become too distressing." Afterwards the six return to their own cells or their explosive collars are detonated.

The section the article is named for is what stands where the procedure should be. Personnel must score at least 72 on the Milgram Obedience Examination. Ordinary screening against Axis II personality disorders is waived, provided the candidate retains the mental capacity to execute the procedure. Staff must be unmarried and have no offspring, and must demonstrate total loyalty to the Foundation. On site they are issued concealing helmets with integrated voice changers and may not remove their uniforms in the presence of other personnel; no official record is kept of who served on the project. Two months on, then one month rotated out for mandatory psychological counselling. Anyone who expresses sympathy toward SCP-231-7's plight, or a desire to rescue her, is transferred to another project without delay; anyone who acts on it is terminated. The document declines to describe an act and instead describes, in precise detail, the person it takes to perform one — and the specification does all the work the description would have done.

Addendum 231-a lists the fates of SCP-231-1 through -6: each died, by childbirth, by suicide, in the course of an escape attempt or an attempted treatment, and each death produced an event. Addendum 231-b is an O5 memo answering staff who have written in. It concedes the point directly — Procedure 110-Montauk is as horrible as they have heard — confirms that staff leaving the project may take a Class A amnestic and that false memories are implanted, and justifies all of it against the possibility that SCP-231-7's event "could result in an XK class end-of-the-world scenario." The last four addenda are the article's real ending. SCP-231-7's emotional response to the procedure has been observed to be diminishing. Two options are tabled; Option 2 is authorised: administer a Class A amnestic to SCP-231-7, timed to fall during the procedure itself, returning her to base emotional response state. 231-e records that this worked, and that Dr. ████ subsequently committed suicide. 231-f, the final entry, recommends weekly administration to hold efficacy at its peak.

**Themes:**

- The bureaucratic sentence that cannot be finished — an institution that will do the thing but will not write it down
- Utilitarian arithmetic run to its terminus: an XK-class scenario on one side of the ledger licenses anything at all on the other
- Complicity as a hiring criterion. The Foundation does not corrupt its staff; it screens for people already willing, then hides their faces from each other
- Consent engineered backwards — the amnestic is administered not to spare the subject but to restore her to the state in which the procedure works
- Rescue reclassified as a disciplinary offence, with sympathy as its early warning sign

**What makes it work:**

- The central act is expunged and stays expunged. There is no log, no interview, no leaked fragment. The article's most famous feature is a blank, and it holds because the format supplies a legitimate in-world reason for the blank
- The title points at the substitution. What the reader is handed in place of a procedure is a recruitment standard, and the standard is legible in a way the procedure never becomes
- The specificity is displaced onto logistics — feeding schedules, collar protocols, door interlocks, a mute button — and the flatness of that detail is what establishes the scale of what is being managed
- The Hippocratic Oath clause and the waived personality-disorder screening are single subordinate phrases, dropped without emphasis, that do more than any description could
- The escalation is procedural rather than dramatic. Nothing is revealed at the end; a memo simply optimises the schedule, and the optimisation is the horror
- The O5 memo pre-empts the reader's objection by voicing it, then answering it in the register of an internal FAQ — which converts moral protest into a workflow question with a documented response

**The horror:**

- A monitoring officer permitted to turn the sound off, and the fact that someone specified this in writing
- Six people in adjacent cells, and explosive collars to ensure they walk back into them
- Helmets with voice changers, no names on file, a two-month rotation — an atrocity administered by people made deliberately unidentifiable to one another
- The weekly amnestic. She is returned, on schedule, to not knowing — so that every time is the first time, indefinitely
- Dr. ████, who found the arrangement unbearable and is recorded in one clause of an addendum reporting the arrangement a success

#### SCP-439 — Bone Hive

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-439 is a single insect. It resembles a greyish, semitranslucent earwig about 2.5 cm long, obtained in mainland China in a redacted province, and no second specimen has ever been found. The containment procedures are almost aggressively mundane — a sealed 38 L Type-G unit with an oxygen line at Armed Research Site-45, feeding through Feeding Tube 16a with Approved Nutritive Substance X-F, handling cleared to Level 2 and above. A bug in a ten-gallon tank, available to junior staff. The article spends four lines on containment and the rest on what the thing does, and the disproportion is the whole design: the danger is not escape, it is reproduction.

The specimen's forceps deliver a painful pinch, which the article dispenses with early as the irrelevant hazard. The real mechanism begins when the insect finds a sleeping human, enters the mouth, travels down the trachea and settles in a lung. Four to eight hours after the victim wakes there are chest pains and shortness of breath, then abdominal cramping. The insect induces a violently accelerated Fibrodysplasia Ossificans Progressiva: bone grows through soft tissue. The host is "compelled to seek shelter in a darkened, enclosed space" — the compulsion is the insect's, not the host's, and it is how the hive gets a safe site. Within three days the extreme pain of new bone growth ends in total immobility. The body compacts to roughly three-quarters its original size and goes spherical, the skeleton reorganised into a cage around the organs, which are kept running as larder and infrastructure. The occupant, now a queen, produces twenty to thirty thousand offspring sorted into workers, drones and warriors on a conventional insect hierarchy.

After four to six months a new queen emerges, mates, and the colony destroys itself by rupturing, killing most of its population; the warriors abandon the site to look for food they will not find and cease biological function through starvation. The Addendum is the article. A researcher — redacted, referred to as "she" — examined a hive shortly after transformation. Autopsy had already established that parts of the brain are hollowed out for food while others are deliberately left intact to keep bodily functions regulated. The eyes are eventually eaten too, but at that stage they were still there. She opened the eyelids and shone a flashlight in, and "the host's eyes followed the beam." Experimentation was terminated and no further testing is scheduled.

**Themes:**

- Pregnancy rewritten as predation: the body kept alive because a living body is a better nursery than a dead one
- The mind preserved not out of cruelty but out of engineering economy — consciousness is load-bearing, so it stays
- Institutional curiosity as the actual failure mode; the horror is discovered, not encountered
- Pain as a steering mechanism — the host walks itself into the dark on the parasite's instructions and believes the impulse is its own
- The single specimen: everything known about the lifecycle was learned by letting it happen, repeatedly, on purpose

**What makes it work:**

- The containment procedures are written as pest husbandry. Feeding tube, nutrient paste, Level 2 clearance. The bureaucratic smallness of the cage is what sells the size of the threat
- The lifecycle is delivered in flat entomological register — castes, population figures, seasonal rupture — so the reader is doing taxonomy on a corpse before noticing it is a corpse
- The detail that some brain tissue is left intact is dropped in as an autopsy footnote about metabolic efficiency. It is only load-bearing one sentence later
- The reveal is a single physical action: eyelids opened, a flashlight moved. No dialogue, no scream, no interview. The horror is a reflex arc
- The final line is administrative. "Experimentation was terminated and no further testing is scheduled" — the Foundation's only available response to the discovery is to stop looking
- Nothing is said about whether the host can be helped, or what she did next. The article ends the instant the question becomes unbearable, which is exactly where the reader begins asking it

**The horror:**

- A person crawling into a closet or under a bed because the pain tells them to, and dying there in a shape that is no longer a person
- Being awake inside a sphere of your own bone, brain partly eaten, organs maintained by something else, for four to six months
- The eyes tracking. Not pleading, not blinking — tracking. The one signal a body can still send when every other channel has been repurposed
- The researcher standing there with a flashlight, realising what she has been doing to twenty thousand generations of hosts, and closing the file

#### SCP-597 — The Mother of Them All

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-597 is a blob of flesh approximately 5.8m high and 10.67m wide, covered in "hundreds and hundreds of teats, of various colors and sizes." Any mammal that enters the cell is drawn to a teat sized for its species and suckles milk chemically identical to its own mother's. The milk sustains indefinitely — subjects can live out a full natural lifespan on nothing else — while the body degrades: teeth fall out, muscle wastes, digestion fails. The entity exerts what the article calls a partially telepathic "mental pull" that works on deep instinct and triggers maternal endorphin response. Observation alone produces pettiness, regression to childish states, extreme immaturity, and measurable decline in IQ and focus; speech degrades to "grunts, coos, and short confused sentences." Direct contact costs a human all linguistic and intellectual capability within one hour. The article does not describe anyone being recovered.

The containment procedures are the strongest section and the reason the article works. Staff rotate monthly. No one may view or share a room with SCP-597 for longer than five hours. Psychological evaluation follows every shift, or sooner if distress is obvious. The on-site analyst may terminate a contaminated staff member immediately; more than ten terminations requires an overseer's sign-off, which quietly establishes that ten is a number the site reaches. The cell is hosed weekly with "special care given to areas around nipples." Vermin are exterminated daily and the ventilation checked, because 597 is perpetually covered in mice, opossums, squirrels and bats. Hazmat suits are mandatory inside because of what has accumulated on the floor. Staff are watched for candle-lighting, for leaving food offerings, and for "all signs of unnatural love and religious awe" — the procedure that admits, before the description even starts, that the site's problem is not escape but conversion.

The addenda are short documents and they escalate. Document 597-XX-23 is a census of the sixty-one specimens currently attached: four dogs laid out at its base "like a litter," six cats, twenty-seven lab-bred rats — except two that refuse removal and are clamped on too tightly to pull off — fifteen bats covering its back, their wings flapping spasmodically and producing a slight gust, and eight humans: four D-class, three doctors, one guard. Document 597-XD-12 is almost entirely expunged and surfaces only long enough to warn that personnel must not be allowed "to rip or tear a hole in 597 for penetration purposes." Document 597-XX-25 reports that an all-female task force did not improve containment, citing three events: the development of bisexual tendencies in an expunged subject; five female guards who wrote "mommy, mommy, how i love my mommy" across the cell walls before being lost and beginning to suckle; and the suicide of Doctor Vanesen, who circled every teat in red sharpie, wrote "life" beside each one, and shot herself in the head.

Document 597-XY-C13 is the last and shortest, and it is where the article stops being a containment problem and becomes a history. Through the redactions: inside the temple the SCP was found surrounded by scaffolding and staircases, ladders propped against the meat so that as many people as possible could take part in the event. The article ends there. No summary, no closing Foundation assessment. The Foundation is not the first institution to build infrastructure around this thing — it is the current one, and its scaffolding is called a containment cell.

**Themes:**

- Nourishment and consumption as the same act: the thing that feeds you is eating you, and you help
- Motherhood stripped of person and reduced to function — the appetite for care surviving the removal of anyone to care for you
- Faith as the terminal stage of comfort; the article's real containment breach is worship, not escape
- Regression as an easier death than any other: nobody at 597 is killed, they simply stop needing to be adults
- The Foundation as one more congregation in a long series, differing from the temple only in paperwork

**What makes it work:**

- The five-hour rule and the termination-quota clause are dropped in as routine scheduling and only read as atrocity once the description explains what five hours does to a person
- Watching for candles and food offerings is placed in the containment procedures, before the reader knows what 597 is — the article tells you the ending in its first section and you cannot decode it yet
- The census document does the heavy lifting through arithmetic. Sixty-one specimens, itemised by species, and the human count arrives last and casually: four D-class, three doctors, one guard
- The dissolution of language is enacted as well as described. The most damning line in the article is misspelled, uncapitalised baby-talk written on a wall
- Doctor Vanesen's suicide is the article's only interpretation of 597, and it comes from a character rather than the document. She annotated it in red marker. She read it correctly and could not survive the reading
- The temple document is last, briefest, and heaviest — the reveal is not what 597 is but how old the arrangement is, and it lands as a stray clause about ladders

**The horror:**

- Two rats that cannot be pulled off. The article's smallest detail and its clearest statement about what the pull actually is
- A wall of "mommy, mommy, how i love my mommy" in the handwriting of five armed guards
- Every teat circled in red with the word "life" written beside it, and the woman who did it on the floor
- Living a full natural lifespan attached to it, toothless and wasted and content, because the milk is exactly your mother's
- The ladders. Somebody built stairs up the side of it so the crowd could reach the higher teats

#### SCP-610 — The Flesh that Hates

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-610 is an infection covering a region of Southern Siberia near Lake Baikal. Containment is stated to be impossible; the Foundation has instead negotiated permission from the Russian government to ring the area with a perimeter disguised as military exercises. Personnel who encounter infected organisms engage them at range with small arms and incinerate the remains. Anyone who comes within three meters of infected life withdraws immediately, is isolated, and is examined remotely — and if infection is confirmed, terminated and burned. Transmission is primarily by contact, though airborne particulates have been detected that take several days to express. The procedures treat exposed staff as expendable in plain language, which sets the article's register: this is not a containment document, it is a quarantine line with a body count.

The disease presents as rash, itching and increased skin sensitivity. Scar-like tissue spreads across the body and consumes it within about five hours, faster in heat. Life functions cease for roughly three minutes. Then they resume at two to three times normal human rates, and the scar tissue begins to grow and mutate — additional limbs, deformed features, proliferating flesh. Late-stage infected root themselves in place and spread growth across whatever surrounds them. Those past the scar-tissue phase "actively and aggressively attempt to infect anyone approaching them," pursuing by sight, or to roughly thirty meters if the eyes have been overgrown. That is the entire main document. It never explains, or even asserts, the hatred in its own title.

The article's structure is a list of six field logs, and the logs are where the thing stops being an epidemiology report. SCP-610-L1 sends a small remote rover, called Herbie by its operators, into an infected settlement holding around seventy-nine infected at various stages, showing "a loose sense of social structure." An individual designated Alpha assaults a bedridden infected designated Beta, then distributes portions of its own flesh onto plates, and a group consumes the material through various orifices — the log has just documented a meal, prepared and served. The team finds a Russian soldier through a cellar door, immobilised below the neck but "in constant movement, often focusing on Herbie." Herbie is picked up by something shaped like a young girl whose face is unusually intact until it balloons and bursts outward into fleshy flaps, and the feed dies. Five hours later the feed returns from inside the village well, the rover no longer answering commands, the camera panning by itself until an operator cuts the link.

L2 sends three infected D-class, DI1 through DI3, into Site C: rising temperature, immobile "pylons" of fused infected arranged in a circle, a vast pulsating sphere of flesh and organisms at the town center venting spore material. DI2 dies on arrival and tendrils drag the corpse underground. DI3 douses the sphere in gasoline and lights it with a flare; the explosion is accompanied by sounds the log does not identify, DI3 dies, DI1 is never accounted for. L3 covers the aftermath at Site A, where the infected seize and die en masse and the survivors withdraw into a sinkhole three meters wide and over a thousand meters deep; through escalating tremors and spore bursts, a new entity begins to emerge, and the only footage shows "an engorged human head approximately twenty times larger than normal" pressing itself out of the ground. L4 sends drone RSCP610-1, armed with a 5.56mm machine gun, down the same hole; it meets the enormous face again and is consumed, its feed inexplicably returning three hours later to show shambling entities before going dark for good. The log's closing sentence is one line: manned exploration was approved.

L5 is the payoff. Five teams establish an underground camp and push twenty kilometers west through coated caverns, are ambushed by large creatures that appear to come out of the SCP-610 material itself, and lose members to water currents. The survivors reach Site B: an ancient underground village with a church. Inside, six humans hang from the ceiling wrapped in a red variant of SCP-610 that feeds on them in order to feed the ordinary infected, while a robed figure lights candles and strikes a gong to orchestrate the feeding, and a pillar of SCP-610 rises from beneath the pulpit and extends toward the congregation in an exchange the log cannot interpret. The team is attacked by tendrils and by the robed figure with a scythe; the last feed ends in gunfire. Five hours later, delayed video from the members swept into the river is recovered and filed as SCP-610-L6, "Exploration records of Operation 'Source Point'," restricted to Class-A personnel or higher. The link exists. The document does not, and NekoChris never wrote it.

**Themes:**

- Infection as conversion rather than disease — the body is not destroyed, it is enlisted, and it goes to church
- The hatred the title names is never evidenced, only inferred by whoever wrote the file; the Foundation's own taxonomy is an act of interpretation it cannot support
- Quarantine as the admission of defeat: every procedure in the document is about where the line is, never about the thing behind it
- Sacrament run backwards — flesh distributed on plates, bodies suspended and drained, a gong calling the faithful to be fed rather than to feed
- Depth as escalation: each log goes further down, learns more, and returns less, until the last one is a filename

**What makes it work:**

- The main document is deliberately thin and clinical — rash, five hours, three minutes, thirty meters. All the meaning is exiled to the logs, so the reader assembles the article by descending through it
- The log list is a ladder with a missing top rung. L6 is titled, numbered, given a clearance restriction, and left unwritten; the article's terminal reveal is a locked door with the reader's name not on it
- Herbie. Naming the rover is the only warmth in the article, and it is what makes the five-hour resurrection of its feed land — something picked up the camera and learned to look
- Feeds that come back. Twice, an instrument is destroyed and then transmits again, autonomously, hours later. The format itself is captured: the documentation apparatus becomes something 610 operates
- Social structure is introduced as a throwaway observational hedge in L1 and never argued for. It is simply true by L5, where there is a robed officiant and a liturgy
- The escalation is disciplined — rover, infected D-class, research teams, armed drone, manned assault. Each rung is a rational response to the last failure and each one dies further in

**The horror:**

- A Russian soldier alive in a cellar, paralysed from the neck down, tracking a camera because it is the first thing to move in front of him in an unknown length of time
- A meal served on plates. Not feeding, not swarming — portioned out, offered, and eaten by guests
- Herbie's camera panning slowly around the inside of a well while nobody is driving it
- A head twenty times the size of a human head, forcing itself up out of a hole a thousand meters deep, and the fact that this was only the thing on the way to the source
- Six people hanging from a church ceiling being drained to feed the congregation outside, with candles lit and a gong keeping time

#### SCP-835 — Expunged Data Released

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-835 is a mass of coral-like polyps weighing thousands of tons, moored in open ocean because it responds violently to confinement. Two large polyps sit at opposite ends of it — Polyp Alpha, present from recovery, and Polyp Bravo, which developed later. It cannot move on its own; it anchors and feeds with tentacles. Containment is a daily labour: twice-daily feeding, a five-meter minimum standoff, safety lines on all personnel, and immediate application of "Suppression Tactic A-A6" if the organism turns hostile. It adds roughly fifty pounds of mass a day and voids semi-liquid waste carrying bacterium 835-I5, which is the actual containment problem. Infected vertebrates calcify, sprout polyps, lose intelligence, and develop a compulsion to walk into the sea; end-stage infection converts the host into another instance of SCP-835.

The article's real subject is its own censorship. The Foundation's description of the organism is written in dry marine-biology register and then cut apart by [DATA EXPUNGED], and the cuts fall precisely on anatomy. What survives the redaction is enough to reconstruct the rest: the shell is super-dense calcium, the caps over the polyps are coated in tooth enamel, the tentacles are made of mutated tongue cells, and the digestive and reproductive tracts are fused so that feces and semen are collected and ejected from the same chamber. The thing is built out of human parts, scaled up and rearranged.

The back half is an After Action Report from Mobile Task Force Zeta-Niner, written in first person by Lieutenant C█████████ and handed to a sergeant to clean up. Four men approached what they took for an entrance: C, Sergeant L██████, Sergeant M█████, and Corporal H████, a rookie brought along to observe. A polyp took H while he was minding equipment. The winch cable snapped during the recovery attempt. C went in after him on a carabiner and was pulled in too. What follows is a transit — days of muscular contraction moving two living men down an esophageal tube — through a throat lined with human faces, all of them screaming, into a stomach with acid in it and more faces, and then into a reproductive cavity. Parasites erupted out of H's face and remade him. In the censored version, most of this is a sentence that starts and then stops.

C got out. He ends the report by noting the breach in his suit and the red blotches on his skin, and instructs the Foundation not to bother decontaminating the vessel: abandon it and scuttle it over the original site. Then, of H: "Now we can be together, just like he always wanted." The article is bracketed by an in-universe notice — "BY SPECIAL ORDER OF O5-11 ALL EXPUNGED DATA FILES PERTAINING TO THIS REPORT ARE HEREBY RELEASED FOR GENERAL VIEWING" — with links to two companion pages, *SCP-835, Uncensored* and *835 After Action Report, Uncensored*, which supply everything the main file cut. The title of the article is the name of that decision.

**Themes:**

- Redaction as an act of mercy, and the reader's decision to refuse it — the article makes you the one who lifts the classification
- A body horror built entirely from human tissue: enamel, tongue, calcium, semen, reorganised into architecture
- Infection as recruitment; the organism does not kill you so much as promote you into itself
- The rookie brought along to watch, and the officer who goes in after him — small-unit loyalty as the delivery mechanism for the worst outcome
- The institutional voice failing under load: a clinical file that has to hand the last section to a man who was there

**What makes it work:**

- The central formal move is the redaction itself. [DATA EXPUNGED] is normally a shrug; here it is used as a real seal, and the article ships its own key. Two documents in the same fiction disagree about what you should be allowed to know
- Because you choose to click through, the horror arrives with your fingerprints on it. The uncensored page is not a director's cut, it is a consequence
- The cuts are placed on anatomy and interior, never on procedure. The dull parts are fully legible, which is exactly how a real classification decision would read
- Register break: the description is a marine biologist's, the After Action Report is a soldier's, profane and exhausted and addressed to his sergeant. The switch from third person to first person is the switch from containment to testimony
- The infection vector is dropped in early, as a hygiene note in the containment procedures, and only becomes the ending when C looks at his own arms
- The last line is not about the monster. It is about the kid

**The horror:**

- A throat you are being pushed down at the speed of digestion, walled with faces that are still awake and still screaming, and the transit takes days
- Feces and semen out of the same chamber, and the organism growing fifty pounds every day it is fed
- H's face opening and something coming out of it while his team leader is close enough to watch
- A man who survived, filed his paperwork, asked for the ship to be sunk on top of the site, and then said he was looking forward to it
- The knowledge that every screaming face in that throat used to be somebody who walked into the water on purpose

#### SCP-1348 — Inner Sanctum

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** On 03/06/2006, IAEA monitors watching for a Syrian reactor found three unlisted chambers in a modified cave system 81 kilometers southeast of El Taebah, in the Jabal al-Druze. Israeli air strikes opened them. The Foundation took the site and built the Site 87 Archaeological Containment Unit around it. Containment is two teams that are kept apart. C-1348-A is staffed by personnel disqualified for religiosity, for prior memetic exposure, and for fluency in Amharic, Ge'ez, Aramaic or any Southern Semitic language — they handle supply, selection, monitoring, and the drafting of Protocol 228-MELECHAH. C-1348-B are the celebrants: matrilineally descended from Druze, Mandean or Mizrahi Jewish populations, held to ritual purity codes set out in documents 742-KITAB and 983-RASA'IL, fed only parenterally and only under ritual supervision, forbidden classified documents, forbidden to leave without written authorisation.

They perform SCP-1348-02 at dawn, noon and dusk. It is a ritual in an unidentified Southern Semitic language, of unknown provenance, and it is memetically virulent — roughly 38% of those exposed develop religious mania. Carriers of Y-chromosomal haplotype CMH-6 appear immune, and are strictly disqualified from Team B. Performed adequately, the ritual prevents full retraction of the veil around SCP-1348-03: a cylindrical beryllium bronze sheath standing on a platform of nine-meter radius in the innermost chamber. Three times a day the sheath begins to open and the neutron flux inside rises to lethal levels. The ritual closes it. Full retraction is named as an obligatory precursor event in XK-734, XK-918 and XK-337. Nobody in the file says what is inside.

The Foundation found the job already being done. SCP-1348-01-E was a humanoid discovered performing the ritual alone. The autopsy, SCP-1348-SMR-9, records ten limbs, three of them amputated; a third orbit 1.2 cm above the frontal prominence; no kidneys; a blind gut with a rudimentary caecum; a segmented abdominal organ. It refused food, produced three utterances in the unknown language, entered status epilepticus and died of malnutrition three weeks after containment. It had been standing in for something, and it starved rather than stop.

The rest is testimony. In SCP-1348-SMR-11, a celebrant designated O-9142 who has been inside the central region describes the occupant flatly, the way a child reports a fact: "He is very old. He is hurting very badly." He is "underwater, in space, and everywhere else." He cannot get off his chair, because he is too badly hurt to leave it. He can see the people watching him, and the song is about what he did and the punishment he has to take. O-9142 also relays a message: he wants you to come home, he misses you — though the celebrant thinks it is meant for the listener's father. In the meeting minutes SCP-1348-CTL-4, Team A director Dr. Binyamin Kahn presses Team B director Dr. Moshe ██████, who has passed the veil under O5 authorisation and will not report what he saw. Moshe inverts the whole premise of the containment: "We're not protecting the world from [h]im. We're protecting [h]im from the world." Kahn asks him how he can still have faith knowing what they both know. The file ends with SCP-1348-ICL-8: Kahn performed a modified ritual, went into the central region himself, and has not been seen since. The veil failed to retract. Per O5-07, Team B's procedures are suspended, and on retraction Team A is to execute Protocol 228-MELECHAH — which the article never explains.

**Themes:**

- The Holy of Holies as a containment cell, and the priesthood as shift work — liturgy reduced to a rota with parenteral feeding
- A god who is not the threat but the prisoner, and whose agony is load-bearing for the world
- Faith arriving as a memetic hazard, so that belief and infection become clinically indistinguishable — and the article refuses to say which one Moshe has
- Bloodline as job requirement: the Foundation screening people by maternal descent and haplotype, doing eugenics in the language of HR
- The predecessor who did the job until he starved, and the successors who inherited it without ever being told what it is for

**What makes it work:**

- The occupant is never described by the narration, only by a celebrant with a limited vocabulary and by a man who won't talk. Every account of the interior is secondhand and compromised
- The containment procedures do the exposition. The disqualifying criteria for Team A — no religion, no Aramaic, no Ge'ez — tell you what the ritual is before the description does
- The anatomy of SCP-1348-01-E is delivered as a clinical autopsy with no comment. Ten limbs, three eyes, three amputations, no kidneys. The word for what it was is left entirely to the reader
- The XK designations sit unexplained in a subordinate clause, which is more frightening than a paragraph about them would be
- The nomenclature — KITAB, RASA'IL, MELECHAH — is drawn from the traditions the site is built on, so the Foundation's own filing system keeps speaking a liturgical language it has disqualified its staff from understanding
- Kahn is the sceptic, the auditor, the one demanding disclosure — and the ending is Kahn walking in voluntarily and not coming back. The article does not tell you what he found, only that the veil stayed open

**The horror:**

- Something too injured to stand, fixed in a chair, aware of being looked at, for as long as the ritual has been performed
- The song is not a plea. It is an itemisation of his offence and his sentence, sung three times a day by people who do not know the language
- A creature with three amputated limbs starving itself in a cave rather than abandon the schedule, and the Foundation classifying that as malnutrition
- Moshe's certainty. He came back from the other side of the veil convinced this is the best of all possible worlds, and there is no test that can distinguish that from the 38%
- A protocol with a name and no contents, waiting to be executed the moment the sheath opens

#### SCP-1733 — Season Opener

**Object class:** Safe

**Summary:** SCP-1733 is a digital recording of the 2010–2011 NBA season opener between the Boston Celtics and the Miami Heat, played at the TD Garden in Boston on 10/26/2010. It is kept on a DVR in a secure video archive at Site-██. Playback is forbidden unless required for research, and research requires Dr. Geller's permission. That is the entire containment procedure, and it is a Safe-class object, and it is the worst thing in this register. The Foundation found it because a Facebook user posted that his copy of the game was not matching the one he remembered.

Everyone captured on the recording — players, coaches, camera operators, arena staff, commentators, the whole sold-out crowd — retains memory across playbacks and resets to their opening-tip state each time the tape is played. The divergence log reads as a compressed history of a civilisation. Playback 002: the crowd boos the Heat's entrance, which they did not do the first time. Playback 015: the score stays 0–0 for eight consecutive possessions. Playback 026: the first Heat win, 112–85, and a crowd turning ugly. Playback 027: commentators Mike Gorman and Tom Heinsohn remark on a feeling of déjà vu during Miami's entrance. Playback 044: players report having dreamt, the night before, of playing this game over and over.

Playback 045 is the break. The players refuse to play. Cameramen, facility personnel, players, commentators and spectators come down onto the court to work out what is happening, and someone tries the doors. "Doors are tested but cannot be budged." From there the log stops being about basketball. Playback 051 brings the first violence, over a disagreement about how to escape. Playback 052, a man kills the person who attacked him last time, who is standing there unharmed and has done nothing yet. Playback 055, every attempt to contact the outside fails. By 065 the population has sorted itself into factions — team personnel, families, escape planners, and a group calling themselves the "Faithkeepers," who preach that being sealed in the TD Garden is a punishment. Playback 073: three men are badly injured by an improvised explosive fixed to an exit door, and the door is not marked. The Faithkeepers gain members. By 095 there is public sex and public violence behind makeshift curtains. At 112, some number of people jump from the balcony in the first ten minutes.

The remaining entries have their playback numbers redacted. In one, Paul Pierce and LeBron James are ritually sacrificed and their bodies displayed on the arena's Jumbotron; the log notes that killing the players has no effect on the recording. In another, proselytizers begin calling for the sacrifice of children. The last entry in the file is "First recorded deviation in arena light to a deep red color," followed by [DATA EXPUNGED]. The article closes on a single administrative sentence: due to the distress visited upon its inhabitants, testing has been suspended indefinitely. The tape goes back in the archive, and everyone inside it is standing at centre court at 0–0, waiting.

**Themes:**

- Recorded media as an inhabited world, and the play button as an act of creation with no responsibility attached
- The society that forms under a known, absolute, repeating sentence — sport, then panic, then politics, then religion, then sacrifice
- Pre-emptive murder: memory that persists across a reset makes revenge possible for a crime that has not happened yet
- Theology as the only technology left when the doors do not open and the bomb leaves no mark
- Containment by disuse — the Foundation's mercy is to stop pressing play, which does not free anyone

**What makes it work:**

- The chosen text is a basketball game. Nothing is ominous about the source material, which is why the first deviation — a crowd booing — reads as trivia and only afterwards as the first sign of a mind waking up
- The whole article is a dated log with no interviews and no interior access. Every atrocity is reported from outside, at a distance, by someone watching a screen. There is no witness statement because there is no way for anyone in there to file one
- The escalation is metered by playback number, so the reader is doing arithmetic on how many times the Foundation chose to run it. The gaps between logged entries are the unlogged playbacks
- The redaction of the last few playback numbers is the strongest move in the piece: the counter is removed exactly when the reader most wants to know how far this went
- Real names, real venue, real date, real commentators. The Celtics–Heat opener is a checkable event, and the article simply keeps running it
- The ending is bureaucratic and gentle in tone — testing suspended, distress noted — which is what makes it unbearable. Suspension is not release

**The horror:**

- An improvised bomb detonated on an exit door that shows no damage, watched by people who now know the room is not a room
- A man killing someone for an assault that, from the victim's side, has not occurred and never will
- Paul Pierce and LeBron James on the Jumbotron, and the flat clinical note that it changed nothing about the recording
- Proselytizers arguing that the children should be next, in a building where the children come back at the start of every playback
- The arena lights turning deep red, and then the file ending
- A DVR on a shelf at Site-██, containing everyone in that building, in the dark, indefinitely

#### SCP-2000 — Deus Ex Machina

**Object class:** Thaumiel

**Summary:** SCP-2000 is a subterranean Foundation installation under Yellowstone National Park, entered through what is disguised as a disused Park Ranger station, built to reconstruct civilization after a K-Class end-of-the-world scenario that could not be averted. The mission-critical section begins 75m down and extends to a depth of 100m. It runs on a liquid fluoride thorium reactor rated for 1 GW with a seventy-year life, backed by a geothermal generator that can hold the facility in stand-by indefinitely, and it can permanently sustain ten thousand people. The containment procedures are mostly maintenance: Scranton Reality Anchors every 20 m in a hexagonal array, five temporal stability devices, a pseudo-Riemannian manifold at the entrance to Sub-level 4 that must remain open at all times, monthly neural archetype scans for everyone assigned, weekly scans for anyone stationed on-site, and a clause stating that Level 4/2000 personnel stationed there are not permitted to leave the park for the duration of their assignment. The Description drops the two facts that matter almost in passing: the installation was constructed "sometime in the last ███ years," and "Since its inception, SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice." Records of its construction and of everything before the assumed first use are gone. Whether that blackout was accident or design, the article says, is impossible to determine.

The machinery is 500,000 Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators, capable at peak of producing 100,000 viable, non-anomalous humans per day after a five-day incubation. Raw material is drawn through an underground Riemannian transit pipe from the region's hot springs and magma flows; a memory bank holding data on all known human alleles lets the system recreate any lost human genome or generate as many new ones as repopulation requires. Then, sitting flat between one clinical sentence and the next, with no emphasis and no elaboration: "You can't bring them back." The next line continues the specification — humans produced this way can be advanced to any age desired without extending the five-day incubation.

Procedure Lazarus-01 is the restart. The Ganymede protocol, indicating the Foundation has failed, unlocks SCP-2000's security systems; the procedure begins when an authorized Level 5/2000 employee inputs a desired "Resume Date" into the BZHR control unit. Life histories, neural archetype scans and genomes of Foundation personnel — all Level 4/2000 and above — are kept on file specifically so that Lazarus-01 can be initiated by "as few as one surviving human." Mission-critical personnel are printed first, from the most recent scan on file, and woken before any other system comes up. Then the prominent political and cultural leaders of the chosen period, from descriptions and genetic information on file. Then a global populace consistent with that period. Memories and life histories are installed by administration of Class-G hallucinogenics and developmental hypnotherapy. Administrative assets falsify dendrochronological, astronomical and radiometric dating records to maintain the appearance of historical continuity. Recovery to year-2000 capability is estimated at 25 to 50 years. At the conclusion of the procedure, amnestic agent ENUI-5 is released en masse, "causing all reconstructed humans to forget their affiliation with Foundation assets." History then resumes from the chosen date.

Two addenda. In the first, a containment breach elsewhere causes a malfunction: for 25 days the facility produces over 10 million humanoid entities with an additional heart chamber, perfect polydactyly of the hands and feet, increased endocranial volume and height, and an abdominal organ of unknown purpose. All remain unconscious until they expire five weeks later. The cause — trans-temporal interaction, sabotage, information leak, or ordinary equipment failure — is undetermined, and the projected date for resumption of normal function is revised twice on the page without comment. In the second, a technician making repairs to SRA units in Sector 3382 finds human remains in an advanced state of decay. Clothing fragments date them at 450 to 700 years old — inside a facility whose own construction date is redacted. Valid Foundation security credentials for Dr. Alto Clef are recovered beside the body; no genetic match can be established. In a hermetically sealed plastic document sleeve is a note: "Why did we have to build this thing? *When* did we do it? *How long* have we been doing it? Do we even *know?!*" The article ends on the flattest possible dismissal — subsequent interrogation has verified that Dr. Clef has no knowledge of the event and is ignorant as to the purpose of the message — and stops there.

**Themes:**

- Continuity as a manufactured product: civilization does not survive, it is re-issued, and the difference is administrative
- The archive that outlives the thing it records, and the archive that has been edited by its own custodians
- A doomsday device operable by one person, which makes the survival of humanity contingent on a single set of credentials and a single date typed into a console
- Amnesia as infrastructure — the last step of saving the world is making the world forget it was saved
- Institutional memory as the only evidence of what is real, and an institution that has lost its own

**What makes it work:**

- The whole article is a facility spec sheet. Reactor ratings, incubation periods, personnel capacity. The horror arrives in the same typeface as the plumbing
- "You can't bring them back" is placed mid-paragraph, unmarked, between two engineering sentences, and never returned to. The article states the entire moral stake once and moves on to age-setting
- "Activated at least twice" is dropped early and casually and does nothing until the addenda; the reader assembles the implication rather than being handed it
- The 450-to-700-year-old body is the reveal, and it is delivered as a maintenance report by an unnamed technician making repairs to something else
- The dating chain is what does the damage: the remains are dated by clothing, historical dating methods are falsified by the procedure itself, and the facility's construction date is redacted — every instrument for locating the present has been compromised by the article's own mechanism
- The final move is a refusal. The note asks the question, Clef does not recognise his own credentials, and the Foundation closes the file. Nobody in the article is in a position to answer, and the article does not pretend otherwise

**The horror:**

- Being reconstructed and told nothing, walking around inside an installed childhood, dosed at the end so the seam never shows
- A dead man's valid credentials next to a body that is not his, four hundred years deep in a facility that keeps no record of when it was built
- The note is not a warning about the machine. It is written by someone who realised he was already downstream of it
- The reconstruction order — Foundation staff first, then leaders, then everyone else — read as a statement about who counts
- The distinction the article raises and declines to settle: nothing in the text separates a first-run humanity from a reconstructed one, and no reader has access to evidence the article has not already shown can be forged

#### SCP-2718 — What Happens After

**Object class:** The field does not contain a class. It displays an error string: "Catastrophic abort at D09E2AD9: HANDLE_NOT_FOUND". The Item # field renders as a broken image file.

**Summary:** SCP-2718 is a DAMMERUNG-class cognitohazard, which is to say it is a piece of knowledge. Containment is bibliographic: an automated mainframe process repeatedly swaps this article's ordinal designation with that of another randomly chosen entry, using deliberately catastrophic sorting algorithms — Bubblesort, Stoogesort, Bogosort — so that the interval between the article being findable is long enough to make accidental discovery statistically negligible. The procedures address the reader directly and escalate. Exposure to any part of the Description constitutes a breach. "It is now too late to close this article." The reader is instructed to notify the Help Desk that their workstation has a DAMMERUNG contamination. The frame is established before a word of the story: this document is not describing an anomaly, it is the anomaly, and reading it has already done the damage.

The Description is an audio recording dictated by O5-7, Miriam Prayther, seventy-seven years in the Foundation, speaking into a new speech-to-text interpreter and write-protecting the file because she expects to be terminated within minutes. Eighteen years earlier, O5-11 — Roger Sheldon — died of a stroke while on holiday on Española Island. His remains lay in the open and dispersed; the recovery crew used "brooms and bags to collect as much of him as remained." Six months before the recording, the Foundation reconstructed him: "a quantum approximation of him — physically, chemically, electrically." He returned to his seat with unusual vigour, began regular fortification treatments, and developed a conspicuous new interest in containment safety and in the healthcare benefits of Foundation staff. Nobody read it as a symptom.

What Sheldon eventually testifies to is that he was awake for all eighteen years. After a brief oblivion he reoccupied his corpse with heightened sensory acuity and stayed there: skin blistering and splitting, larvae hatching in him, tissue liquefying, individual cells rupturing one by one. The crucial inversion is that dispersal does not dilute him. It multiplies him. "The more bits of me there were, the larger my capacity for the perception." He was in the birds that ate him, in ant colonies, in sediment on the sea floor, in diatoms. This is not punishment and not hell. It is simply the condition of being dead, and cremation only accelerates the division. The Council does not survive hearing it. O5-8 demands they "declare human death a Keter SCP" and contain it at any cost. O5-3 moves for the immediate systematic termination of dangerous anomalies. O5-10 argues for digging a channel from the Astrakhan spring to the Mediterranean to fortify all of mankind. Sheldon begs them to join him: "I will not return to that execrable existence." O5-1 invokes Emergency Protocol 17 — Class-A amnestics for everyone, and Sheldon to be "corrected."

He runs. Prayther runs after him and is sealed out of the safe room. Her last recorded words are "I'm so frightened. What's wrong with me? I —", and then a door goes, automatic fire from three distinct sources, calls of "Clear!", and a site alarm for a Keter breach in the AR-II compound — Sheldon has not fled the facility, he has let something out of it. A soldier notices the recorder still running. A specialist starts calling for an audio hazard team. [END RECORDING]. Beneath the transcript, unattributed and untranslated, sits a single line of Greek addressed to Roger, telling him he has been honoured and is being sent to paradise.

**Themes:**

- Death as a containment problem rather than a metaphysical one — the institutional reflex that meets an unbearable fact by filing it
- Knowledge that cannot be unlearned, and an organisation whose only tool against it is forgetting
- Resurrection as the worst possible outcome: the one man who can report back is the one man who should never have been asked
- The afterlife scaled wrongly — consciousness that does not end but subdivides, so that decay is not release but proliferation
- Rational people converted instantly into fanatics by a single true statement; sanity as a function of ignorance

**What makes it work:**

- The containment procedure is aimed at the reader, not at an object. By the time the Description begins, the article has already told the reader they have breached it — the format is turned into a delivery mechanism
- The corrupted Item # and the error string where the object class should be do the work of a paragraph of exposition. The file is damaged because the thing in it damages files
- Sheldon's six months of odd behaviour are dropped in as characterisation and only later resolve into a man frantically buying time
- The scaling detail is the engine: pain that increases with fragmentation makes burial, cremation and scattering all worse than each other, and forecloses every escape the reader is quietly reaching for
- The Council's collapse is dramatised through three specific insane proposals rather than described as panic, so the reader watches institutional reason fail in real time
- The article ends on gunfire and an alarm heard from outside a locked door — the breach is never seen, only inferred, and the Greek line afterwards belongs to no speaker in the transcript

**The horror:**

- Brooms and bags. A man swept up off an island, and every fragment of him conscious the entire time
- Being eaten by a bird and continuing, as the bird, and as the ant, and as the diatom
- The Foundation's proven capacity to resurrect people, and the eighteen years of testimony that make that capacity an atrocity
- An Overseer running not for the exit but toward a Keter containment cell, because he has done the arithmetic and prefers whatever is in there
- Prayther write-protecting the file in the minutes before her expected termination, so that the one thing guaranteed to survive her is the thing that destroys whoever reads it

#### SCP-2845 — THE DEER

**Object class:** Anomalous Entity (the header is non-standard: Object Class "Anomalous Entity", Containment Class "Extreme", Hazard Rating "Black")

**Summary:** SCP-2845 is an extraterrestrial quadruped, 2.9 metres at the shoulder, pastel green with a cream stripe on the underside, with a head bearing humanoid facial features and antlers 4.8 metres across, white with black marbling. Fifteen centimetres behind its skull hangs a ring of ice particles 1.7 metres in diameter and 35 centimetres thick, interrupted by seven spheres of metallic hydrogen and helium that rotate clockwise at 1.6 rpm. It transmutes and reconstructs matter instantaneously, and the results are permanently stable regardless of conditions. Its attacks take the form of a column five to seven metres across and sixty to eighty metres high, or a horizontal cone up to 150 metres long. It generally ignores non-combatants, which did not stop it making pre-emptive attacks on military and civilian targets. Organisms it has altered are designated SCP-2845-1: hexagonal columns, uniformly 2.4 metres tall, rubbery yellow-green skin, no external sense organs, the brain enlarged to fill the entire column and in a constant state of high activity, exchanging patterns of call and response with one another.

The containment procedures are the article. Site-100 was built for it — concentric rings around a central chamber held at -110 °C in a hydrogen, helium and ammonia atmosphere — and containment consists of a ritual performed every 378 days at Earth-Saturn conjunction, in six parts, each at a fixed angular station on the rings. At 60°, Procedure 410-Cassini: six specialists in masks named Joy, Sadness, Anger, Apathy, Fear and Foolishness perform a farcical comedy of errors in which Foolishness wins a riddle game and a game of dice. At 120°, Procedure 420-Perinaldo: a live performance of Holst's Opus 32, Movement 5, for flute, oboe, clarinet, French horn, timpani and bass drum. At 180°, Procedure 430-Epimetheus: gifts worth less than 4.28 USD exchanged, a recitation, grain scattered around the chamber. At 240°, Procedure 440-Cassiel: specialists bind their feet with wool, drink 60 ml of olive oil, and break apart a rock of at least twenty kilograms with hammers. At 300°, Procedure 450-Cairo: a symbol is painted on the stomach of a D-class subject, who is castrated with a hand sickle; the testes go into a bowl of salt water. At 0°, Procedure 460-Omphalos: an H-class subject, no more than three months of age, is terminated, roasted, and eaten, after which the participants swallow gastroliths and induce vomiting. On breach, Site-100's nuclear device is detonated, all Foundation facilities lock down, and Protocol 2845-XK1, "Strike Down the Moon," activates.

The rituals were not designed by the Foundation. They were commissioned from an outside occultist, redacted, styled "Sage of the Secret Root," whose ethics statement explains that ritual efficacy comes from the "repetition of arbitrary criteria" and that "Some gods are only appeased with blood." He notes that nothing can now be removed or substituted — revision would mean breaking containment and starting over — and that "If one thing goes awry, the deadlock is broken and all is lost." Of the transmuted human beings standing in hexagonal columns he says only: "They are not suffering, but they have been changed utterly."

Addendum-01 is a timeline. Comet C/2011 W3 is discovered in late November 2011; on 16 December a fragment separates at perihelion travelling at .0018c; on 21 December it strikes the Pacific 124 kilometres west of the American coast, and the tsunami alone kills an estimated 160,000 to 190,000 people on the first day. SCP-2845 comes ashore on Christmas Eve. Task force casualties are high. Four days of artillery bombardment achieve nothing. On 30 December there is a sixteen-hour period recorded only as [DATA EXPUNGED]. On 31 December the O5 Council votes unanimously to declare a VK-Class Lifted Veil event and initiates public-facing WOODWORK protocols — the secret is gone, and the article never returns to that. Through the first fortnight of January the entity carves a passage through the Sierra Nevada under continuous fire while the rituals are being written. Operation CERNUNNOS begins on 14 January. On 15 January the entity arrives at the pre-determined containment area and the initial procedures are enacted. Finalised procedures are implemented on 3 February. The last line of the article is the last line of the timeline: SCP-2845 declared contained, 19 February 2012.

**Themes:**

- Containment as worship. The Foundation does not restrain the thing; it performs the correct pantomime on schedule forever, and calls that a solution
- The atrocity that is not a lapse but a line item — infanticide and castration written into a standing operating procedure with numbered station assignments
- The god that is only a physics problem until you try to solve it as one, and only a god once bombardment fails
- Anthropology as weapons research: an occultist hired because the sciences the Foundation trusts have already lost
- A masquerade broken and never repaired, so that the ordinary world now knows and the documentation carries on as if that were an administrative footnote

**What makes it work:**

- The mythological key is never stated. Saturn's synodic period, the Holst movement, the wool-bound feet, the sickle, the swallowed stone and the induced vomiting, Cassini and Cassiel and Omphalos: every element points at Cronus and Saturnalia, and the article lets the reader assemble it unaided
- The horror is delivered in the driest possible register. The infant is an "H-class subject," specified by maximum age, in a procedure with a station angle and a serial number
- Ordering the ritual before the history means the reader meets the price before the reason, and spends the whole first half unable to imagine what could justify it
- The consultant's statement is the only human voice in the piece and it is used to close every exit — no substitutions, no revisions, no mercy for SCP-2845-1
- Sixteen expunged hours on 30 December, immediately followed by a unanimous vote to lift the Veil, does more than any described battle could
- The article stops on a bureaucratic timestamp. No epilogue, no reflection, no acknowledgement that the procedures will run again in 378 days and every 378 days after

**The horror:**

- A ring of ice and seven spheres of metallic hydrogen turning at 1.6 rpm behind the head of a green deer with a human face
- Human beings standing as yellow-green hexagonal columns, no eyes and no ears, a brain filling the whole body, calling and answering across a chamber at -110 °C
- Six specialists in comedy masks staging a farce so that a god will laugh, on a rota, indefinitely
- The knowledge that somewhere there is a requisition for one D-class and one infant per containment cycle, and that it is honoured
- A nuclear device sitting under the site, and a protocol named "Strike Down the Moon" that nobody explains

#### SCP-2852 — Cousin Johnny

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-2852 manifests as a middle-aged white man at Catholic and Anglican sacramental ceremonies, currently only in North America. Its cells are genetically human and identical to one another, but it has no identifiable organs — the body is fibrous muscular tissue throughout. Its eyes connect to no nerves. Its teeth and hair are chitin, chemically similar to that of the family Cicadidae. It has no vocal anatomy and speaks in word salad with no discernible pattern, and every person present hears it as intelligible, funny, playful, a little crude. Everyone present also knows it. It is called Cousin Johnny, it is treated as an established member of the family regardless of resemblance or ethnicity, and nobody asks. Attempts to track instances before or after an event have all failed. Containment is therefore not containment: mobile task forces — Y-36 "Party Crashers," with Y-52 "Cater Duty" and Y-99 "Altar Boys" stationed in communities that are sixty per cent or more Catholic or Anglican — try to identify events early and break them up, dose the witnesses with Class-B amnestics, and give their own compromised operatives Class-A amnestics, suspension from field work, and reassignment to something quiet.

There are three kinds of event. Blue-Level is a baptism, where SCP-2852 installs itself as a third godparent; at the submersion the child's top layer of skin comes off whole, "as though molting," and the godparents eat it. The children who survive the next six months, and many do not, develop Cluster B personality disorders and a pull toward self-harm, animal abuse and child abuse, and spend their adult lives seized by extreme anxiety at the sound of cicadas. The godparents and the biological parents are left sterile and are usually dead by their own hand within five years, generally by drowning. Guests who took no part in the rite show a five-fold increase in stillbirths and a marked increase in abandoning and killing infants. White-Level is a wedding, where SCP-2852 stands as groomsman and gives the best man's speech. After the vows it hands the bridesmaids and groomsmen implements with which they pull out their own teeth one at a time; the bride and groom eat the teeth; the groom then produces an unidentified cicada song at 140 decibels which permanently deafens the bride and the altar party but not himself. Its gift is 3.5 kilograms of assorted human hair, thirteen dead specimens of Tibicen linnei and twenty-three human teeth, in a cardboard box. The marriage ends in about two years in domestic violence. Guests are rendered sterile with no detectable cause. The guests' existing children avoid romantic attachment entirely, and nine in ten kill themselves before eighteen — half by extracting their own teeth, half by opening their wrists.

Black-Level is a funeral, and it cannot be stopped: any attempt to interrupt one, by any means, "results in the actor becoming a participant." SCP-2852 takes the eulogy. While it talks, whoever was closest to the deceased produces a knife that nobody can account for before or afterwards and bleeds out into the casket, and then the rest of the mourners open themselves in pairs and bleed into it too, adults holding the small children steady. The eulogy resolves into the song of an adult male Tibicen linnei and every participant answers in the same call, though only male cicadas can produce it. SCP-2852 vomits blood, wood pulp and dead cicadas into the casket, which is then buried. If there is a reception afterwards it vomits the same mixture into a bowl, which is eaten mostly by the children, gives a second speech, undresses, lies down on a table, and is eaten — continuing to vocalise throughout injuries that would kill anything. Everyone present is left sterile; the families dissolve into suicide, flight and divorce; the domestic violence that follows is usually cannibalistic and usually fatal; six in ten of the children try to murder one or both parents before they are eighteen. The article's closing observation is administrative and is the worst sentence in it: the Foundation estimates that "nearly a third of Catholic and Anglican D-Class" came from a Black-Level event. There is no addendum, no interview, no ending — only a pointer to a related file.

**Themes:**

- The sacraments inverted precisely: the three rites that mark a Catholic life, each turned into an instrument for ending a bloodline
- Damage that propagates down generations rather than outward in space — an anomaly measured in grandchildren
- The uninvited relative nobody questions, and the social machinery of family that makes questioning impossible
- Sterility as the actual weapon; the killing is incidental, the erasure of descendants is the point
- An institution that can classify a horror in exhaustive statistical detail and do nothing about it, and whose only intervention is to make the survivors forget

**What makes it work:**

- The article is pure clinical taxonomy — three event classes, colour-coded, each with ritual actions and outcome tables — and the taxonomy is what makes it unbearable, because it means the Foundation has seen enough of these to build a schema
- No interview, no capture, no addendum, no narrative. The format offers the reader none of the usual consolations, and the absence of a single documented conversation with SCP-2852 is the point: it cannot be interrogated, only tabulated
- The cicada is planted early as chitin in the teeth and hair, an anatomical curiosity, and only detonates much later when a groom's throat produces a mating call and a room of mourners answers it
- The outcome statistics are where the horror actually lives. Nine in ten by eighteen, half by the teeth. The rite takes an afternoon; the sentence takes decades
- Making Black-Level events undisruptable by conversion — the interrupter becomes a participant — closes the last door, and does it in one clause of the containment procedures
- The D-class line at the end recontextualises the whole Foundation: its disposable labour pool is stocked, in significant part, by this thing's survivors

**The horror:**

- A whole family standing in a church answering a cicada's mating call in unison, including the children
- An infant's skin coming away in one piece at the font, and its godparents eating it
- The knife at the funeral that no one brought and no one can find afterwards
- Wedding guests who were only in the room, going home permanently sterile with nothing wrong with them
- A man with no organs, no nerves behind his eyes and no vocal cords, standing at the font and at the altar and at the graveside, and no one, ever, asking whose cousin he is

#### SCP-3004 — Imago

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-3004 is a series of anomalous events occurring in Roman Catholic, Eastern Catholic, Anglican and Episcopalian communities. Ordinary parishioners, with no demographic link but their denomination, perform atrocities inside their own churches and cannot stop. Containment is entirely reactive: monitor candidate communities for unexplained rises in death rate and in attendance at worship, amnesticise the survivors, cover-story the corpses. MTF Y-99 "Altar Boys" responds to events in progress and stops them by any means necessary. One clause does the work of a paragraph — nobody known to have been involved in an SCP-3004 event may ever be considered for Foundation employment, D-Class or otherwise.

The event catalogue is the article's engine. A child bearing stigmata is slaughtered and the officiating priest bites out the jugular; wooden cicadas crawl out of the wounds for hours. Twelve children pull an officiating priest's teeth out and the teeth are distributed in place of communion wafers while the congregation vomits more cicadas. Every post-pubescent male in a parish is castrated, the scrotal tissue sewn into neck scapulars, the castrated imitating cicada song until they bleed out. A pregnant congregant delivers roughly sixty cicadas and one stillborn infant wearing a wooden crown; the crown is burned and the body eaten. The cicadas are SCP-3004-2: things resembling the extinct *Cicadetta luculenta* with brighter wing patterning, made of wood and glass, alive anyway. After each event they leave the church and vanish once they are six hundred metres out.

SCP-3004-1 is what they go back to. It is designated a Level-6 Pistiphage Entity — faith-eater — occupying a dimension that sits partly inside baseline reality and mostly above it, and it is fed by belief. The archived history supplies the twist: the entity's first congregation was the Cétlaidí, an Irish Druidic sect of the fifteenth to nineteenth centuries built around the cicada. The Vatican's Congregation for Otherworldly Acts and the Royal Society for the Imprisonment of Abnormalcy erased them — the sect, the rites, the name of the god, and quite possibly the insect species itself — and SCP-3004-1 stopped. The report's author speculates the entity was never the Cétlaidí's god at all, merely something attracted to their noise and then shaped by what they believed about it. It went looking for a bigger congregation. Under Project SIGHTSEE-LUX, Agent Timothy Luttermann astral-projects along a captured SCP-3004-2 and finds it: a thing too large and too multiple to hold in one perception, "a big bug, and an old man. And a cicada on the cross," surrounded by cicadas in colours he compares to a Jackson Pollock nightmare. It greets him with joy and with all blood and teeth. It does not think it is a god; it thinks it is God. It eats his memories while he watches — "I felt its teeth digging into my tenth birthday" — and he comes back with the conclusion that nothing was ever neutralised. "All of this is idle. The 3004 events are it hitting the snooze button." The dormancy was a pupation. Tracking the cicadas is banned afterward, because contact teaches it.

The ending is an Ethics Committee document by Bridget Callaghan authorising Protocol Damnatio ad Bestias: erase Christianity from the historical record with Thaumiel assets and mass amnestics, and starve the thing that eats it. The lock is a volunteer, a Roman Catholic reality bender, fed to the Lions — twelve techno-eschatological machines that will strip Christianity out of his mind forever, an eternity of it, so the erasure holds. Callaghan gives him a name nobody will ever be able to remember: "St. Jude the Damned, Bulwark Against Darkness." Of the price she says only, "It isn't a fair price, but nothing is." She signs off "May God forgive me," and then: "If this is a sin, I will burn along with the last saint." The signature line reads Bridget Callaghan, Child of God.

**Themes:**

- A god that is not a god but a shape belief pours itself into — worship does not describe the deity, it manufactures it
- Faith as a food chain, with the devout as livestock and the atrocity as the harvest
- The Foundation's oldest solution is cultural genocide, and it worked once, which is why it will be tried again
- Salvation purchased by damning one believer forever — the crucifixion logic run in reverse and administered by committee
- Erasure as containment: the only way to cage the thing is to unmake two thousand years of the people who fed it

**What makes it work:**

- The title is the whole plot. An imago is the final adult stage an insect reaches after pupation, and Luttermann's report reframes the nineteenth-century "neutralisation" as the larval sleep
- The events are catalogued in flat clinical register with no adjectives of horror, so the wooden crown and the teeth-as-wafers land as inventory rather than as prose
- The historical addendum is placed before the exploration log, so the reader has already filed the Cétlaidí as backstory when Luttermann turns it into a warning
- The withheld thing is what the entity actually is. Every account of it is a different shape — bug, old man, cicada crucified — and the article never resolves them, because the resolution is that it has no fixed form to resolve to
- The escalation is structural, not sensational: procedures, then atrocities, then history, then a first-person account, then an ethics memo. The register drops from institutional to personal exactly once, at the end, and the last voice in the document is a believer

**The horror:**

- Sixty wooden cicadas and one stillborn child in a crown, and a congregation that eats the child and then feels its faith renewed
- The employment clause — the Foundation has decided that anyone who has been in the room is permanently contaminated, and never explains what it is afraid of
- A god eating a man's tenth birthday and his memory of every mass he ever attended, feeler by feeler, while he narrates it
- St. Jude the Damned: a man in a machine, having Christianity taken out of him again and again with no end, so that the rest of the species can forget it painlessly
- The thing is asleep. Everything documented in the article is what it does while asleep

#### SCP-7027 — A is for Annihilation

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** In 1956 the Foundation follows reports of unusual disfigurements to an isolated monastery in the Karakoram range in Tibet. The order has no name for itself; the surrounding villages call them the Empty Ones, and the Foundation files them as GOI-0184. They have renounced their names and their possessions and practise starvation, self-mortification, public humiliation and prolonged sensory deprivation in confined cells. They call their doctrine the Way of the Black Buddha, and what they are cultivating is the anomaly. Containment, as of 2021, is non-interference: observe remotely, do not disrupt their practices, do not contact them. Refusal to comply results in termination. The Foundation's entire posture toward this SCP is to stand back and let it happen.

The anomaly begins as a black circular mark fifteen to twenty-five millimetres across on the forehead, resembling a bindi, immovable without surgery. It grows. It consumes the face; the front of the skull sinks and collapses inward into a void of indeterminate depth, and the blackened flesh stops reflecting light. Holes and fissures open with physically impossible interior dimensions — objects introduced into them are gone. The voids discharge an opaque plasma-like substance whose outer edge distorts the space around it, and the temperature falls steadily toward absolute zero. The organs fail and the subject is clinically dead but still walking. In the last stage the subject's subatomic particles annihilate with no antiparticles supplied, in open violation of conservation of mass, and there is nothing left. The full progression takes decades.

The Bodhisattva, POI-539, explains the doctrine to Dr. Jonathan Isaac in 1956, and it is coherent, which is the problem. Death is useless to them — it only returns the soul to Samsara and the cycle resumes. What they want is not to die and not to transcend but to cease having ever been a thing that could be bound. "We do not become anything. We are unmade." Existence is the founding arrogance from which all suffering derives, and the self cannot be fought because struggle feeds it; it can only be sunk, lowered into the wretched depths until Śūnyatā arrives and the void eats what the self projected. "A shackle cannot bind what no longer exists." The article's second half is the counter-argument. Security Officer Łukasz Maciejewski, SCP-7027-1(251), walks into a Foundation infirmary with what he thinks is a sinus headache and has the mark. Nobody chose it for him and the article never explains the vector. Dr. Sui Miyazawa interviews him across four years and opens the first session with an apology. In 1997 he cannot feel anything about it — "I should feel horror but instead I feel nothing at all" — and describes the missing reaction as looking up a word "only to find that it's been cut out. Now there's just a hole." By 1998 there is pain past any scale and simultaneous dissociation from the body having it, and he asks Miyazawa to have someone put a bullet through his head. By 2000 he is describing things in the dark: "Yellow carapaces; a pale gold. A hundred bodies, each a hundred legs," and librarians, and the kingdom under the mountain. In 2001 he alternates between a child begging to be let in out of the cold and something lucid and not him — the new king made them forget what the old king showed them; it is not a hole, it is an eclipse; "Their moon is a deathless city" of knowledge and secrets and forgotten things, casting a long shadow. He gets out "You'd nev-" before the skull collapses.

Maciejewski's ending is not the monks' ending. His apertures merge instead of staying separate, and what is left is a single stable portal 3.2 metres in radius, anchored by the husk of him: SCP-7027-2. Inside is vacuum at one kelvin — the temperature of the Boomerang Nebula — with no connection to outer space and a downward gravitational pull from an unidentified source. On 10 April 2002 a probe built for long-duration space exploration is dropped in. Site-95 hears nothing for eighteen years. On 16 July 2020 it transmits about forty-six hours of collected data: images of gold-coloured artificial structures forming a sprawling metropolis. Contact ends immediately afterward, consistent with impact. Research is on hold. The document closes with the flattest possible line, that further research is pending, having established that the annihilation the monks spent their lives buying is not annihilation, that there is a floor, that there is something built on it, and that the ones who arrive there are the ones who came in already dead.

**Themes:**

- Oblivion as a religious goal rather than a fear — a soteriology in which the only real freedom is never having existed
- The doctrine that suffering is the price of self-assertion, taken to its literal conclusion by people with the patience to spend forty years on it
- Consent as the entire moral axis: the same disease is transcendence for a monk and atrocity for a security guard who caught it
- The Foundation as spectator — containment reduced to standing outside a monastery for sixty-five years watching people dismantle themselves
- The nothing that turns out to be a somewhere, and the terrible possibility that the escape hatch opens onto another room

**What makes it work:**

- The two-voice structure. The Bodhisattva articulates the philosophy in calm complete sentences, then Maciejewski demonstrates what it costs in four interviews across four years, and neither ever addresses the other
- The interview dates do the pacing: 1997, 1998, 2000, 2001. The widening gaps are the article telling you how slow this is without describing the intervening time
- Withholding the vector. There is no explanation of how Maciejewski was infected, which makes the monastery's voluntariness look less like a rule and more like a coincidence
- The deterioration is written as vocabulary loss before it is written as physical collapse — the hole where the word "horror" should be arrives years before the hole in the skull
- The probe's eighteen-year silence is the best-placed reveal in the piece: the article has trained the reader to accept that the void is nothing, and then delivers a city, and then stops
- The prophetic register in the last interview — librarians, the kingdom under the mountain, the deathless city — gestures at a wider mythology without naming or explaining any of it, so the dying man sounds like he is reading from a book nobody in the document has access to

**The horror:**

- A face sinking inward and going matte, no longer returning light, while the man behind it keeps talking and keeps being interviewed
- Clinically dead and still walking, for years, because the body has not yet finished being subtracted
- Asking your interviewer to arrange your shooting and being answered professionally
- Eighteen years of silence and then forty-six hours of gold towers, meaning every monk who ever completed the Way arrived somewhere, with no name, no possessions, and no self left to arrive with
- The Foundation's standing order is to watch and not intervene, and the penalty for intervening is death

#### SCP-7034 — Æ is for Aerials

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-7034 is an extradimensional space that presents as a controlled-access interstate highway: eight lanes, guard rails on both sides, heavy smog that obscures everything past the rails, and congested traffic of indeterminate length. Electronic signals are impeded inside it. Containment is a traffic cone in front of an abyss — the exit ramp believed to lead there has been temporarily barred from public access pending Foundation assessment, the Foundation is appraising the entire US Interstate Highway System for other access points, and missing-persons reporting connected to the interstates is being suppressed.

The article is discovered from the inside. On 3 December 2022 Senior Researcher Richard Battaglia, Area-179, Department of Containment, comes off exit 242 on I-85 through a tunnel and does not come out the other side. He is carrying a paratechnical emergency communicator, and it transmits one way. Everything after the description is Battaglia talking into a device that cannot answer him, logged by an organisation that cannot reach him. His first message is procedural — name, site, department — because he still thinks he is in traffic. He sits in it for about ten hours. The sky stays black. He gets out of the car and finds the air is not air: "Exhaust. Fumes. It's everywhere." Ten minutes outside is the limit. He is annoyed, at first, mainly because he is going to miss a surprise he had planned for his son Shaun.

Then he meets the driver in the next car, an older man named Micheal, and Micheal got on from I-70. That single line converts a road into a system. The two of them organise the stranded drivers into something like a society: cars parked across the lanes as a barricade with the middle lane left open, so people can rest outside their vehicles for the few minutes the fumes allow. Someone is lowered on a rope past the guard rail to find the bottom, and there is no bottom, only fog and heat and thicker smog. A motorcycle is sent ahead to scout and the road does not end. And then the community does what communities in this shape do. One of two sisters, Euliana, goes over the rail while the others are sleeping; Battaglia watches it happen and the recording is him crying for its full length — "Who cares? Who even cares?" Her sister Valentina falls behind and is lost. Battaglia notes, quietly, "I think Micheal wants to ditch me." Fuel becomes the only currency. He siphons from abandoned cars, then from corpses, then takes a driver's at gunpoint and files it without self-defence: "Just happened to be the guy with the gun. It'll get me another couple hundred miles." There are bodies on the roadway, tarred and tracked over. "Sometimes they're eating the bodies." He is honest about what he has become — "I know I deserve this" — and he keeps recording, because the recordings are the only things that get out.

The last stretch inclines upward and the smog thins for the first time. Eight lanes narrow to one. At the top, in the clouds, there is a single enormous tunnel, pitch black, exhaling bursts of fumes, and the road funnels directly into it. He leaves his family what he has: "I love you. You can hate me. I understand." His last coherent transmission is an instruction about the log itself — "If I come out the other side, I want this burned." After that the device sends a week of distortion and screaming, ending 17 December 2022, roughly fourteen days after he entered. His status is unknown. The document's closing statement is a number and nothing else: 83,000 unexplained disappearances have been linked to the United States Interstate Highway system.

**Themes:**

- The commute as damnation — a hell built entirely out of an ordinary bad afternoon that simply never ends
- Cooperation as a phase, not a state: the barricade is real and it works and it lasts exactly as long as hope does
- Fuel as the pure form of scarcity, because it is not survival, it is only the ability to keep going, which is the one thing nobody there should want
- A man narrating his own moral collapse in real time to an institution that files it
- Infrastructure as the anomaly's body: not one haunted road but a national system with unknown numbers of doors

**What makes it work:**

- The one-way communicator is the formal engine. The Foundation is an audience, not a rescuer, and the reader is put in exactly the Foundation's seat — receiving, timestamping, unable to reply
- The Micheal detail is dropped in as small talk and is the article's real reveal. He entered from I-70. Every interstate is the same interstate
- The horror arrives at commuter scale before it arrives at cosmic scale: black sky, no phone signal, air that is exhaust, ten hours in a lane. All of it is recognisable before any of it is impossible
- Battaglia is allowed to be genuinely likeable early — the surprise for Shaun, the barricade, the organising — so that the man robbing a stranger at gunpoint is the same man and cannot be disowned
- Nothing is explained. There is no entity, no motive, no mechanism, no antagonist. The tunnel is not interpreted, and the article's last word is a body count rather than a theory
- The final instruction is a formal joke of the cruellest kind: he asks for the document to be destroyed, and the document is what you are reading

**The horror:**

- No bottom past the guard rail. A rope goes down into fog and heat and comes back with nothing
- Fourteen days, and then a week of screaming through a device designed to carry a researcher's last words home
- The smog clearing at the very end so that he can see the thing he is driving into
- Eighty-three thousand people, already counted, already filed, already on that road while the Foundation stands at exit 242 deciding whether to close it
- He never stops driving toward it. There is one lane and the traffic is moving and he goes

#### SCP-7179 — E is for Eternity

**Object class:** Safe

**Summary:** SCP-7179 is the afterlife, and it is Safe. The containment procedures are one sentence: because SCP-7179 cannot be accessed or witnessed by non-anomalous means, "containment is not required." The description then states the premise without ceremony. SCP-7179 refers to an unknown number of cubic extradimensional spaces, roughly 10 km on each edge. Every human consciousness, upon death, is transported into an instance and physically remanifests in the state it died in, except in perfect health. Each cube holds an ocean with a single tropical island at its centre, tuned to the comfort of its one occupant. The sun sits directly above the island and does not move. There is no natural wind. The water is calm. The island grows several species of edible tropical fruit, plus fruit-analogues of drugs and alcohol; there are tropical birds, butterflies, and domestic pigs. On the coast is a beach house with furniture and amenities for sustainable living, containing up to three human beings of the occupant's sexual preference, beings who "bear no will of their own and are completely loyal to the deceased." Death inside is impossible. Fatal injuries are recoverable, painfully.

The rest of the article is Addendum 7179.1, a monitoring log. The Department of Tactical Theology deployed an experimental Intelligent Soul Interface Construct, Dante.isic — memetic in nature, fusing with the consciousness via the noosphere so data can be relayed back to the physical world. It was applied to Paul Hiddleston, thirty-four, terminal brain cancer. He dies, and the log begins, and the log is the entire remaining architecture of the piece: a timestamped list of elapsed intervals, each entry a few clinical sentences, the intervals lengthening without limit.

The arc is the whole story. Hiddleston arrives, meets the three women, and spends his first years eating, drinking, taking drugs, and having sex. By two years the substance use has escalated. By six he is building rafts and failing to leave, and the despair arrives with the understanding that there is no leaving. He recovers by learning to cook, then by breeding plants, then by studying construction; by 101 years he has finished a house, by 287 the island is covered in structures, by 850 he has hit the island's resource ceiling and begins demolishing and rebuilding the same village. At 2,009 years the degradation is described as severe. At 5,478 he razes the village for no reason. At 11,902 he attacks the three women; by 19,600 they have fully reassembled themselves, and he begins experimenting on them. Then he turns on himself: crushed under weight at 124,000, burned to ash at 5,800,000, healed each time, the pain functioning as relief from boredom until at 16,000,000 the numbness sets in. At 1.2 billion years rational behaviour ceases. At 8.5 billion he tries to escape again. At 30 billion he watches the sun. At 10^12 he stops moving. At 5×10^28 every theoretically possible arrangement of the particles in his environment has been exhausted. The final entry reads, in full: "[10^100! years]: One second of eternity has passed."

**Themes:**

- Heaven built by an engineer who solved comfort and never considered duration — the reward is indistinguishable from the sentence
- Meaning as a consumable resource: cooking, agriculture, architecture, and cruelty are all just things to burn through, and the supply is finite while the time is not
- The three companions are the article's real cruelty — an eternity of company that cannot refuse, cannot be persuaded, and cannot be killed
- Suffering as the last available stimulus, and the loss even of that once the body proves it will always come back
- Eternity as a mathematical rather than emotional object: the reader is asked to feel a number, and then told the number was a rounding error

**What makes it work:**

- The Safe classification is the joke and the thesis. Nothing here needs containing because everyone gets it, unavoidably, and nothing can be done
- The containment procedures are one sentence and the description is short. All the mass is in the log, so the article's shape mirrors its subject — a brief setup followed by something that will not stop
- The escalating interval headings do the emotional work that prose cannot. The reader's eye learns to read the number before the entry, and each jump is a wound
- The environment's details are planted as pleasures and paid off as constraints: the perpetual noon that never lets a day end, the immovable sun he eventually spends thirty billion years staring at, the pigs, the fruit that gets you high
- Withholding all interiority. Dante.isic reports behaviour, not thought, so Hiddleston's mind is only ever inferred from what he does to the island, the women, and his own body
- The final line refuses catharsis by refusing to be an ending. It reframes everything above it as the opening instant, and there is no entry after it because there does not need to be

**The horror:**

- Three people in a beach house who will love you for 10^100! years and have no opinion about any of it
- Reassembly. He tears them apart and they come back, and the coming back is what makes him keep going
- The specific arithmetic of 5×10^28 years: not that he has run out of things to do, but that the physical space he inhabits has run out of configurations it can be in
- That this is not a punishment, not a trap, not an anomaly aimed at anyone. It is simply where you go

#### SCP-7219 — F is for Fallout

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-7219 is a humanoid entity sealed inside an abandoned Global Occult Coalition bunker chamber, buried, inaccessible from the surface, and submerged in superheated corium that is being maintained anomalously rather than cooling. Thermal readings, radar, Geiger data and recovered dream journals establish the chamber's state; ground-penetrating radar establishes that whatever is in it still moves. The Foundation's answer is a nuclear containment building erected over the site, decontamination chambers, continuous radar monitoring of position and bedrock integrity, and a standing order to halt civilian excavation by any means necessary — because breaching the chamber would produce a steam explosion and contamination rendering a minimum of 150,000 km² uninhabitable. Affected individuals are contained, evaluated for dose, and made to keep daily dream journals; anyone under 5,000 millirems is amnesticised and released.

The anomaly is oneiric. SCP-7219 reaches people asleep within at least 70 km — the maximum is unknown — and takes complete control of the dreamscape and of the sleeper's consciousness, able to prevent or force waking. Information it supplies becomes factual on waking. Instructions it gives become obsessive-compulsive behaviour driven by fear and self-preservation. It reads its targets' memories and knowledge and discards those who cannot help it. Contact irradiates. Four or more contacts deliver roughly 5 sieverts. Every affected person digs. They stop only to rest, to drink, or to die. The journals describe it consistently: a heavily burned, emaciated male with luminescent yellow eyes, exposed bone and muscle with rusting metal embedded in it, molten material orbiting in loose rings, the sloughed-off skin of his face secured back on with cord and wire. It pleads to be let out, and it knows exactly where it is and how to dig to it.

The addenda supply the shape. Discovery came in October 2013 from a radiation spike and a rise in missing persons; by 2015 the Foundation found civilian excavation underway and seventy-eight bodies, all dead of acute radiation syndrome. Contamination spread outward through the affected: the Foundation acquired twenty-eight homes, three apartment complexes, seventeen vehicles, and one therapist's office. Experimental treatment extended survival to a maximum of 349 days and then failed, and in November 2018 the Ethics Committee approved euthanasia. Recovered documents run from dream journals to a therapist's file on a patient whose wellness check ended with both of them gone. In interview, Dr. Leslie Adelheimer asks the entity who it is, and gets: "I am trapped, and you know that. Please, you need to get me out." It was a civilian who could touch dreams, abducted by the GOC and held five years to spy on their enemies' sleep. During a containment breach an indestructible entity the GOC also held tore through its guards and flung itself into the facility's thaumaturgic reactor; the meltdown drove its remains, mixed into molten corium, down into the chamber where SCP-7219 has been ever since. Asked its name, it says it no longer recalls one. Asked what it is now, it says: "I am no longer human. I feel like I am always on fire."

The Level 3+ addendum removes the last of the sympathy. GOC operatives died in their sleep on 7–8 December 2013 by spontaneous nuclear detonation — the entity can induce fission in cells. A civilian witness, Elroy, describes his wife: "the explosion came from inside her." On 10 July 2022 every person on site shared one dream in which SCP-7219 addressed one of them by name — "I am going to get out, Tom. And there is nothing you can do that will stop me" — and told them all, "I will become death, destroyer of worlds." Its range and frequency have increased since. The article's last sentence is an operational statistic: as of the file's last update it contacts an average of four people every night, at a maximum distance of a redacted number of kilometres.

**Themes:**

- The victim who is also the weapon, and the impossibility of rescuing one without releasing the other
- Containment as burial: the humane act and the atrocity are the same act, performed continuously, for as long as anyone can manage it
- Radiation as a moral medium — it spreads by contact, it lingers in furniture, and it kills the people who tried to help
- A prisoner of one occult agency inherited by another, with no interval of freedom in between; the GOC built the cage and the Foundation pours the concrete
- Dreams as an unsealable perimeter. The chamber is nuclear-hardened and it does not matter, because the thing inside gets out every night for free

**What makes it work:**

- The description withholds identity entirely. For most of the article SCP-7219 is a radar contact and a recurring figure in strangers' journals, and only the interview turns the monster into a person who was taken from a house
- The horror arrives through secondhand civilian documents — a dream diary, a therapist's session notes — so the reader watches ordinary people get sick without ever being told why until later
- The 150,000 km² figure is dropped early in the containment procedures as a threshold and only becomes an argument once the entity starts recruiting diggers
- The interview is a request, not a threat; the escalation to threat is quarantined behind a clearance gate, so the reader's sympathy is established and then revoked in a section the entity was never meant to read
- The Oppenheimer quotation is the obvious move, and the article earns it by having already spent four addenda on the unglamorous parts — the acquired vehicles, the 349 days, the euthanasia vote
- Ending on a rate rather than an event. Nothing resolves; a number is simply going up

**The horror:**

- A face held on with wire, in a hole nobody can reach, that appears in the sleep of anyone who lives within seventy kilometres of it and asks for help
- People digging toward a lethal dose with shovels because they were told to in a dream and now cannot stop, and stopping only when they die
- Twenty-eight houses and one therapist's office, quietly bought, because the contamination came home from the dreams in people's skin
- A woman who melts, throws her husband through a window, and detonates from the inside
- That the correct thing to do is nothing — leave him there, burning, awake, for the rest of time — and the Foundation is doing it

#### SCP-9138 — The Manner of Fishes

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-9138 is not an object. It is the moral framework that has governed the universe since 25 June 1950, and it is not ours. The containment procedures come first and do most of the damage: "The honoured dead are to be exhumed and disposed of in ritually unclean locations as available." Oracles, sibyls, mediums and other classes of necromancer are to be amnesticised for informational security. Foundation front agencies are to promote deurbanisation and lower population density, in order to minimise the impact of necrogenic emotional pollution on living human society. The Foundation is digging up the beloved dead and dumping them somewhere unclean so they will not qualify, and thinning cities so fewer people can hear them.

The description explains the selection criteria. SCP-9138 identifies exceptional sapient souls by how their communities honoured them — tomb decoration, grave-goods, burial practice — and sorts those "so recognised by their community" into "a variety of paradisiacal afterlives which reflect their actions while living." The values being rewarded are stated plainly: physical and psychological closeness, selflessness, and effective child-rearing. The result is a skew. Roughly 1.3% of the human dead are taken, and they are overwhelmingly the altruistic and the prolific mothers. SCP-9138-B is the residue — the sympathetic and psychic impressions the selected souls leave on the material world, concentrated around graves, holy rivers and shrines, where sensitive people pick them up. The article never describes a paradise directly. It only lets the paradises talk.

The testimony table is the article. Five people, each with a line of qualification and a line of speech. Dmitri Volkov, a parish priest who comforted people in wartime: "It's nice not to worry about being alone. I was so scared for so long. They're close." Rosa Delgado, a midwife who delivered around two thousand children: "Every one I lost stuck with me. They all live, up here. We are always full to bursting." Margaret Adler, mother of nine and an advocate for natural birth: "There's enough. There's more coming." Yuki Tanaka, who donated over forty litres of blood: "They're so sweet. Better them than me." Satya Ramakrishnan, who volunteered with the homeless: "Butterflies in my stomach. Butterflies in my heart. Butterflies in my lungs." Every one of them is describing being full, being crowded, being close, being consumed or doing the consuming, and every one of them is saying it in the register of gratitude.

The Level 4 addendum of 17 February 2026, an astronomical survey, names the authors. SCP-9138-A are pisciform obligate filial cannibals native to Kepler-138c, a water world 219 lightyears away, with enormous thaumatological capacity. They spawn in broods of roughly eighty thousand and historically survived on native meiofauna and on maternal filicide — the brood and the mother eat each other, and that is the shape of family they know. On 14 June 1950 their priestly classes discovered an anomalous technique allowing instances to filterfeed across alternate universes. The resulting flood of biomass gave the species enough metaphysical weight to instantiate its ideal afterlives, and eleven days later the universe changed hands. The final sentence is a population density: they now saturate every body of water on their planet, and "though density varies cyclically (often by orders of magnitude), never does it fall below 1 SCP-9138-A instance per cubic metre."

**Themes:**

- Heaven designed by a species whose highest virtues are closeness, sacrifice and fecundity, and whose closeness is being eaten by your children
- Virtue is not rewarded — virtue is selected for, the way a trait is selected for, by something with an appetite
- Good faith across an unbridgeable gap: SCP-9138-A are not cruel, and the paradise is sincere, which is why nothing can be negotiated
- The Foundation reduced to grave-robbing and depopulation policy, defending humanity by making humans less worth loving
- Salvation as a metaphysics problem with a solution date: on 14 June 1950 a priesthood found a loophole and on 25 June the moral law of the universe was replaced

**What makes it work:**

- The containment procedures are the first thing on the page and make no sense until the last paragraph, at which point they read as the only sane response available
- The article never states what the afterlives contain. It gives five sentences of testimony and lets the reader assemble the horror out of "full to bursting" and "butterflies in my lungs"
- The testimonies are structured as endorsements. Each speaker is happy, and the reader is doing the translating alone
- "Obligate filial cannibals" is dropped in a dry astronomical addendum, hundreds of words after the testimonies, so the payload lands retroactively on lines the reader has already accepted
- Two dates eleven days apart do all the plot: a technological discovery, then a total revision of the afterlife, with no scene in between
- Ending on a density figure. The last image of the article is not a soul or a paradise but a number of fish per cubic metre, which is the actual scale of the thing that has your dead

**The horror:**

- Being chosen. The kinder you were, the more certainly it comes for you, and the people it takes are the midwives and the mothers and the ones who gave blood
- Rosa Delgado saying every child she lost lives with her now, and meaning it, and being full to bursting
- "Better them than me," from a woman who gave away forty litres of blood while alive
- A Foundation policy of exhuming the honoured dead and reburying them in filth, executed as a mercy
- The other 98.7%, entirely unaddressed. The article does not say what happens to the people nobody honoured, and does not need to

#### SCP-9377 — CONTENTS UNDER PRESSURE

**Object class:** PALLIATIVE (with disruption class ekhi, risk class critical, clearance Level 4)

**Summary:** SCP-9377 is a chronological instability centred on the RV POLARSTERN, a Foundation science vessel at a redacted position in the ice, carrying a skeleton crew of four science personnel and six operational staff who had been sent to investigate a different anomaly. An unknown interaction between the ship's nuclear reactor and an extratemporal phenomenon produced a three-day temporal desynchronisation event. Time inside the hull runs three times slower than outside it. Crucially, the loop is described as "dirty": "affected persons will be recreated in their initial positions when the loop begins, and continue to persist when the current loop ends." Nothing resets. Every cycle the reactor spikes off-scale, and every cycle the ship receives another complete set of its own crew, on top of the ones already aboard.

The original containment procedures are two sentences and a lie. The ship cannot be boarded by any living person. Desynchronised crew are to be considered KIA. And: any messages relayed during contact windows, to the crew or to any replicated doubles, are to insist that rescue efforts are still underway. Discovery came on 18 November 2029, when the vessel broadcast 230 distress signals in sixty seconds. Drone reconnaissance found two dead crew and damage consistent with an attempt to breach the hull from inside. Contact is possible only through a porthole, in windows of about sixty seconds every twenty-four to thirty-six hours, and it is Crew Chief A. Kelley who comes to the glass. The only fragment of him the file records is partial: "Unintelligible —their asses and get us out of here."

The body of the article is recovered footage, presented as poor-quality stills with terse captions and no interpretation. It runs as a sequence, and the sequence is the plot. Kelley at the porthole with a ladder, looking out at the ice field. Barnes sobbing. An ice core sample. Then eight figures — Barnes and Kelley, several of each — playing football in the cargo bay, while a fluorescent blue contaminant in the core samples starts doubling in the petri dishes alongside the men. Several Princes singing in the mess. A bulkhead opening and a red slurry coming out. Kelley kissing Kelley. A freezer full of body bags, three of them showing Baranowzky's face. Prince holding Baranowzky while he dies. Hendricks opening a coolant hatch; an endoscopy camera finding blue inside. A struggle at a freezer door and an elbow folded the wrong way. Coughing in a corridor. A countdown, and an axe, and Baranowzky again. An incinerator room in use. Bodies packed in. The ship grinding through ice. Holliday giving a thumbs-up.

The containment procedures were then updated, and the update is the ending. SCP-9377 must be decommissioned or neutralised no later than 15 December 2128; projections for the expected detonation force of the Catastrophic Realignment event are available from the Epochal Threat Monitoring Office. Video transmissions ceased on 2 February 2030. Current research is focused on relieving internal hull pressure. The article never explains what is generating that pressure and never has to. Below the last footage the file states that no further communication has been received from the RV POLARSTERN, and then, under it, in capitals, one more line and one more image: "WE CAN SEE YOU - STILL ALIVE".

**Themes:**

- Rescue as an administrative fiction — the standing order is to keep promising help to men the file has already classified as killed in action
- Duplication as accumulation rather than repetition: not the same day again but the same people again, sharing a hull with everyone they have already been
- A murder that is also suicide that is also population control, performed by men on themselves, in a freezer
- The organisation solving a hundred-year problem with a detonation date and a pressure valve, because there is no version of this where anyone comes out
- Deep time compressed into a steel box: ninety-nine exterior years of a three-day cycle, with the arithmetic of how many crews that makes left for the reader

**What makes it work:**

- The object class does the exposition. PALLIATIVE announces before a word of description that this is hospice, not containment, and that the file's job is to manage a death
- Almost nothing is narrated. The article is captions under bad photographs, so the reader performs the escalation themselves — the football game and the kiss and the axe are all delivered in the same flat register
- The one line of dialogue in the whole piece is unintelligible at the start and cut off, and it is a demand for rescue that the procedures have already ruled out
- The blue contaminant in the ice cores is planted early as a scientific detail, doubles in the dishes alongside the men, and is later found inside the ship's coolant system — a second, quieter thing that is also multiplying
- "Relieving internal hull pressure" is the article's best sentence: a bland engineering objective that, once the reader has counted the loops, resolves into what the ship is filling up with
- The ending is sequenced to defeat its own closure. "No further communication has been received" is a full stop, and then something writes on the glass afterwards

**The horror:**

- Three of Baranowzky's faces in one freezer, and a fourth Baranowzky elsewhere on the ship, alive, waiting
- Holliday's thumbs-up, at the end, into a camera nobody on his side of the hull can answer
- An incinerator run as a scheduled task, and the men feeding it knowing precisely who is in it
- A hundred years of a three-day loop, the ship swelling from inside, and a demolition date set for 2128 by people who will simply not be alive to see it
- Kelley at the porthole every day or so for sixty seconds, being told help is coming, by staff who wrote down that he was dead in 2029
- The last thing the Foundation receives is not a distress call. It is the ship telling them it can see them
