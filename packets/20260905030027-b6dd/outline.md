## debt audit

The one impossibility: a reliquary assembled from martyred children's remains will act, once per confession, when its assembler confesses a sin with contrition and with the intent to repeat it, both sincerely and at the same instant.

Everything else follows.

She answers only Lauro because only the man who did the thefts can hold both truths about them at once; anyone else confessing "I stole the femur" is reciting, and a Jesuit confessing his own sins carries firm purpose of amendment by training, which is the gap sealed. "They'd mean it" is the mechanism stated plainly: a valid sacramental confession is, by definition, fuel-free.

What she did to the regiment follows from what he confessed: he took something small, easy, and load-bearing from a body that didn't notice until too late. She did the same. Her one act is the shape of his one sentence.

He can't fire her again because faith is the load-bearing half of the gap. Without belief that there is anyone to be sorry to, contrition has no object. Intent survives (he would do it again; he sold the rosary), remorse doesn't. He is not refusing; he is empty on one side, and he knows the difference, which is why he's not cruel about it.

The Office can't destroy her because destroying her is dismantling forty-one children's relics they hold without provenance and cannot return without confessing the thefts, and can't fire her because their entire apparatus is designed to close gaps, not hold them open.

What does not derive: the corrosive tears. A second miracle, wearing grief as a metaphor. Cut it. Replace with something already paid for: she is unbuilding. Starved of the gap, the wire and wax let go and the relics resume being relics, one element at a time, the Lyon femur first. "Deteriorating" is then literally true, Lauro's objection ("she can't deteriorate, she's a reliquary") is literally the answer, and the Office's problem acquires a clock at no cost. They've moved her twice because pieces fell off in transit and had to be catalogued separately, which is worse than tears, because a catalogued bone is evidence.

## arithmetic

Unit of her power: one act per confession. She has been fired once. Refills are not a quantity; they are a condition.

Relics: 41 children, 63 items (some contributed two bones). Deaths span 1412 to 1994. "Six centuries" is the Office's count, touching the 15th through 20th. "Four hundred years of bone" is Lauro rounding down; he is counting from the first theft he personally cared about, the Lyon femur (nine-year-old, d. 1562, taken from a parish sacristy in 2019). Assembly took six years of thefts, 2017 to 2023, across 38 churches in 11 countries. No church has reported a loss; two have noticed.

The miracle: 22 March 2025, a valley east of Hakkari. Regiment strength 1,106 on the morning report, 1,106 dead by 11:40. Cause of death listed as environmental. Lauro's "eleven hundred" is accurate, not loose.

Loss of faith: not a date. He places it "sometime in April." The Office waited three months, then began sending one priest per month from July 2025. Okonkwo is the eleventh, so it is May 2026. Napoli–Torino is the 35th matchday. Napoli need the point.

The Padre Pio rosary was envoy number four, October. He got 1,800 euros for it in the Quartieri. Pistachios are Bronte, 40 euros a kilo. He goes through a kilo a week.

Deterioration: began roughly when the envoys did. One element separates per week, on average; eleven have come loose, matching eleven envoys, which the Office has noticed and Lauro has not. 52 remain. At current rate she is a catalogued heap by May 2027. The tank column near Van is 34 vehicles, photographed 16 April, and is not going to wait a year.

Envoys: the Office has 23 Jesuits cleared for the file. Twelve remain unsent. At one per month that is exactly the same year.

## custody

Lauro holds the mechanism. He knows what he said, that he was sorry, that he'd do it again, and that she read both. He also holds the provenance: 38 churches, 63 items, which sacristy, which drawer. This half is inert. It is the testimony of a man who sold a saint's rosary for pistachio money, unverifiable except by repetition, and he cannot repeat it because the proof would require him to feel it.

The Office holds the result. Photographs, the morning report, the 1,106, the tank column, the crate she's in, and the eleven pieces catalogued after transit. This half is inert too. It proves she works and proves nothing about why she answered him and not a bishop. They also hold the commissioning order, signed by the Office's director, which is the one document establishing that the thefts were ordered rather than freelance.

Neither half fires. Lauro's half without the Office's is a story. The Office's half without Lauro's is a weapon with a missing trigger they keep describing as a personnel problem.

Why nobody connects them: the connection would be a confessor, and the Office has only confessors. Every man cleared for the file goes to confession, is contrite, resolves to amend, is absolved, and signs the next order. The director in particular is sorry monthly and orders monthly, in that sequence, in separate rooms, with absolution between. He is the only other man alive who stole those children in the sense that matters, and would do it again, and the sacrament keeps the two truths from ever occupying the same sentence. Lauro held them in one sentence once. The Church's entire machinery exists to prevent that sentence from being said.

So the Office sends priests to ask for a gap while employing the one man who has one and paying his confessor to close it every month. Lauro can't tell them, because he doesn't know who signed the order; he was never shown it. The Office can't see it, because from inside, the director's monthly absolution is proof of his fitness, not his eligibility.

The relics themselves are the third custody. The Office can't return them without confessing 38 thefts. Lauro can't be prosecuted without the Office producing the order. Each side holds the other's crime, which is why the visits are polite.
