He did not walk to the coast. The one before him had walked to the coast and the wall had heard him there, so distance bought nothing, and a man with eighty-five days should not spend twelve of them buying nothing.

He went as far as the village, which was one day, and learned what the village knew. It took him until Easter, because he had no tongue they could use and they had no hand for his signs, and because the wall never stopped. It finished with him on the first evening, all thirty thousand words of him, his mother once and eleven brothers in turn and the woman at the gate who had called him the thin one, and then it began again from his mother. It added his own steps as he made them. By Easter he could hear himself walking to the sexton's house before he arrived.

The sexton dug one a year. He did not know it was one a year; he knew it was when the priest sent for him. The graves were not in a row, only close together, at the north side where the ground was cheapest, and the parish paid. Anselm counted them with his hand on the turf. He had the list in his head, four hundred and eleven names with no dates, and here were the dates, in no order, with no names. He held both, and he understood he was the only man who ever had, and that he had fifty days.

He wrote it for the priest in Latin. He watched the priest read it and fold it and put it with the letters for the bishop, and knew where it went from there, because he had once carried the visitation to Abbot Hugh's table and stood while he read it and been told, afterward, to see that the lime was ordered before Advent.

So on the fortieth day after Easter, in the dark before Ascension, he went back.

The ground on the east side was furrowed and leaned in, and the ridges were worn where four hundred and ten men had come to them again and again, and now he knew what for. He put his palm flat against the stone and gave it the inside. The charter, the figure of the ear, that the world may speak and we be not troubled. The lot, and who was exempt. The rubric. The names. Then the outside: the graves, the count, the priest's folded paper. He pressed it in the way you press a seal, slowly, and the wall took it as it took everything, without preference, and sorted it, as an ear sorts, toward whoever it was for.

It was not for him. He had known that since the gate.

Inside, forty men were rising for Vigils. Two of them sat below him, and one of those would draw the lot next Lent and come out by the west gate and turn, as he was told not to, and lay his hand here, and hear it all in the first two hundred minutes, in his own voice, in order. He would have eighty-five days. He would know exactly what they were for.

Anselm sat down against the stone with his hand still on it. The wall had said Ascension. It was Ascension by the bell that had never rung. The wall would need to move its figure by less than a day, and did, tenderly, as he stopped.
