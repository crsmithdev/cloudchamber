The gate closed behind him with no sound at all, which was correct. Anselm stood in the snow with his bundle and turned, as the sent-out are told not to turn, and put his palm flat against the wall.

From inside, the wall was plaster over stone, whitewashed each Advent. From outside it was not white. It was the color of the inside of an ear, and it curled. The whole east face of the monastery rose in a ridged spiral, thickest at the base, thinning toward the bell tower that had never held a bell, and the ridges were worn smooth in places the way a step is worn, by something coming to it again and again.

He had thought, when they signed the sending at Chapter, that he would carry silence out like water in cupped hands. He had thought the difficulty would be spilling it.

The wall said his name.

Not aloud. Aloud was a thing he had never possessed and did not miss. It came the way a word comes when you read it: fully formed, in the voice you think in. His own voice, which he had never heard, which he now understood he had always had.

*Anselm*, the wall said, *born in the sixth year of the abbot Hugh, took the habit at nineteen, sits third from the end at Vigils and picks at the skin beside his thumbnail when the psalm is long.*

He took his hand away. The voice did not stop.

*Four hundred and eleven years,* it said, and he understood it was not a single voice but a sediment of them, every peddler and pilgrim and drunk and mother who had stood where he stood and spoken at the stones because there was no one inside to answer. They had said his name because the wall had asked them to. The wall had asked in the only way it could, by being shaped to receive.

*You have until Ascension,* it said, and he felt the ridges under his feet now, through his sandals, the ground itself furrowed and leaning in. *The one before you had until Pentecost. He walked to the coast. The wall could still hear him at the coast.*

He looked back at the gate. On the other side, forty men were filing to Terce, hands folded, hearing nothing, and he loved them with a violence that surprised him.

*They built me,* said his voice, *to keep you out. They only didn't know yet which one you'd be.*

He began to walk. The snow took his prints and the wall took his walking and turned it, tenderly, into words.
