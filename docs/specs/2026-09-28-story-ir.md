## 0. Summary

1. The schedule is already a primitive IR and `parseSchedule` is already a
   static analyser over it **[code]**. What the schedule lacks is symbols
   (facts are prose sentences, not references), per-beat entry and exit
   state, who is present and where, what a beat may invent, and any check of
   the schedule against the ledger or the setting.
2. On `08aa`, one planning decision that contradicts the ledger (a
   "delegation" of the Council sitting apart from it, joined by telephone)
   produced four prose flags on beats 2 and 12, three of them open at gate 2
   **[measured]**. A check of the 1,100-word plan against the 60-line ledger
   would have found it before any of the 10,000 words of prose existed.
   Nothing checks the plan today **[code]**.
3. The ledger's own typed facts are inconsistent twice: its calendar (day 11
   is 13 March if descent day is day 1, but it welds the Door on 14 March)
   and its vote (twelve seats plus a Guild delegate who votes, and a tally of
   twelve). The screen patched a scene from 14 to 13 March, which now
   contradicts the ledger, and left the vote flag open **[measured]**. Typed
   `when` and typed counts make both a deterministic check at $0, at gate 1.
4. Screening prose is 63% of a draft's cost on `08aa` ($3.51 of $5.55 at
   list) **[measured]**. A plan check is one schedule-sized call ($0.23).
5. The analogy breaks where it matters most: prose is not a function of the
   IR (draft-to-draft variance is as large as any change measured
   **[measured]**), invention is the product, and every instrument that would
   say "the prose is better" is noisy. So the IR can only be sold on what it
   makes cheap and deterministic: fewer open ledger flags, fewer rewrites,
   fewer cascades. That is measurable.
6. Smallest first step: extend the schedule with per-beat `uses`, `present`,
   `exit` and `may_invent`; put the beat's slice in the scene ask; draft the
   `08aa` brief twice; count ledger flags per beat against `e33a` and `08aa`.
   About $20 and a few hours of my time. Kill if flags per beat do not fall
   by more than the bind's own noise (0.62 a beat **[measured]**).
7. Gate 1 (§12): the ledger and claims checks become lowering plus
   deterministic unification and one verification per symbol per chain;
   derivation's sums and the reader's knowledge and setup questions become
   $0 checks; structure, resemblance and "one departure" stay text checks.
   The ledger should be declared by the outline call, not re-extracted from
   its prose. Gate 1 and a plan gate collapse into one IR gate with two
   panes. A $0 companion step classifies 40 of the 210 kept findings to
   see what share a type checker would take.

## 2. The analogy, mapped

| compiler | pipeline today | with an IR |
|---|---|---|
| source | the brief: outline in four sections, three vignettes, ending (CONTEXT.md, "Brief") | same |
| symbol table | the ledger (prose lines by category) plus the setting distillate, consulted per claim | a typed table of entities and facts, each with a provenance and a resolved value |
| front end | `ledger-extract`, then `schedule` | lowering: symbolise the ledger, resolve against the setting, plan beats over symbols |
| IR | the schedule | the schedule plus per-beat `uses`, `present`, `where`, `entry`, `exit`, `reveals`, `may_invent`, `must_not` |
| static analysis | `parseSchedule` shape checks | shape checks plus consistency of withheld across beats, a calendar, knowledge state, custody, reveal order, dangling setups, plan-vs-ledger, plan-vs-setting |
| optimisation passes | `rewrite k --instruction`, `branch --instruction` (model rewrites) | text edits to the IR, re-check at $0, re-lower affected beats |
| code generation | `scene` per beat | `scene` per beat with the beat's slice in the ask |
| ABI / linking | bind against scene k−1 | `exit(k) == entry(k+1)` on the IR (deterministic), and prose-vs-slice per beat (model) |
| object code verification | ledger screen over the whole ledger | conformance screen over the beat's slice |
| incremental build | rewrite k, rebind k+1, re-screen both | dependency graph: symbol → beats; regenerate only what `uses` the changed fact |

Where it holds: everything on the left of "code generation". Where it
breaks: the backend (see §9).

## 3. The IR itself

### 3.1 Units

The **beat** stays the compilation unit; every existing artifact, screen and
gate action is keyed by beat **[code]**. Inside a beat the IR holds:

| element | what it is | source |
|---|---|---|
| `when`, `where` | typed time (day number and date, hour) and place symbol | schedule, typed |
| `present` | the people and objects in the place | schedule |
| `uses` | the symbols the beat relies on, by id, with their fixed values | lowering |
| `events` | ordered lines: actor, act, object, what it changes | schedule |
| `entry`, `exit` | state lines: reader knows, each person knows, custody, location, standing conditions ("Mercer has not spoken in the Hall") | derived: `entry(k) = exit(k−1)`; `exit` is the schedule's |
| `reveals`, `withheld` | items the beat states as fact; items still withheld, with their reveal beat | schedule (as today) |
| `may_invent`, `must_not` | what is local to the writer; what the prose must not assert | lowering |
| `directives` | job, stakes, set piece, register, hook, pays, operator instructions | schedule, config, gate |

A **symbol** is one entity or one fact with a kind, typed attributes, a
provenance (ledger line, setting entry, schedule beat, operator) and a
resolution against the setting where the setting speaks.

### 3.2 A concrete shape

The pipeline speaks in tags, and the schedule is stored raw with parsed meta
(`write.ts:160`), so the IR can be the schedule's tags with more children.
Shown for `08aa`, beat 2 of the 13-beat schedule. The ledger lines are the
ones stored on the draw; the setting lines are from the setting file, lines 12 and 17.

```
<symbols>
  <sym id="varro" kind="person" from="ledger:detail">Knight Anselm Varro, sixth of the nine, wears the Third Tier suit</sym>
  <sym id="suit.third" kind="object" from="ledger:world; setting:bodies/Armoury"
       custody="varro" reused="yes">nine suits, one sealed per Tier; the Armoury recovers fallen suits and returns them to battle repaired</sym>
  <sym id="knight.rule" kind="fact" from="ledger:world">a Knight is sent once, never relieved, given rites before departure</sym>
  <sym id="cadence" kind="fact" from="ledger:knowledge" known_by="chamberlain,wardens,varro">three knocks, pause, three</sym>
  <sym id="wardens" kind="body" from="ledger:detail; setting:bodies/The Wardens"
       shift="40" lent_by="guild" resolved="CONTRADICTED">the setting: the Wardens hold the doors themselves</sym>
  <sym id="council" kind="body" from="ledger:detail" seats="12" presides="chamberlain" sits_at="hall"/>
  <sym id="calendar" kind="time" origin="1911-03-03T06" day1="1911-03-03" weld="1911-03-14T11" day11="1911-03-13"
       resolved="INCONSISTENT">eleven days from 3 March, sixth hour, end 14 March, sixth hour; day 11 is 13 March</sym>
</symbols>

<beat n="2" words="650">
  <when day="1" date="1911-03-03" hour="6"/>
  <where>hall</where>
  <present>mercer, varro, chamberlain, wardens, council</present>
  <uses>varro, suit.third, knight.rule, cadence, wardens, council, black_door, crank</uses>
  <entry>reader.knows: a sound under the Hall since day 2, a Knight below. mercer.spoken_in_hall: no. cadence.known_by: chamberlain, wardens, varro.</entry>
  <events>
    varro is given the rites, standing, in the suit
    the chamberlain puts the cadence to varro, out of the gallery's hearing
    the crank shuts the leaf behind varro; the vents start
  </events>
  <exit>reader.knows += the pit is walkable in the right armour; nine suits, nine Tiers; varro is sixth of nine; the leaf is one hundred tons on a crank; the Council sits in the Hall with the Chamberlain presiding. cadence.known_by: unchanged. crank.men: (local, fixed here) </exit>
  <withheld>the smear on plate seventeen — beat 4; the passive clause — beat 7; the Guild's cost — beat 5; the interval never varies — beat 12; what comes up the stair — beat 11</withheld>
  <may_invent>the ride, the weather, the clerks, the order of the rites, what Mercer feels in his knees, the words anyone says</may_invent>
  <must_not>state whether a suit is worn again (setting: it is); name a Council smaller or larger than twelve; let anyone but the three say the cadence; date anything past day 9 from the narrator's vantage</must_not>
  <directives>job: back up eight days to the departure. set piece: the crank turning the leaf shut while the vents replace the air. register: signal. pays: no.</directives>
</beat>
```

Everything in `<symbols>` and `<entry>` is derived once per chain and
pinned like the ledger (ADR-0009). Everything in `<events>`, `<exit>`,
`<may_invent>` and `<directives>` is the schedule's, edited at the IR gate.

Yes, a primitive one **[code]**. It has the beat as unit, `when`, reader

## 6. Optimisation passes: iterating on the IR

Today an operator's change at gate 2 is a model rewrite of whole beats
(`drafting.ts:545-573`), and a replan re-asks the whole schedule from K
(`write.ts:155-162`). At gate 1 an instruction rewrites brief parts
(`repair.ts:178-185`), with the sharpest evidence in the repo that every
regenerated word is a new surface for a finding (`repair.ts:9-11`;
`docs/specs/2026-09-05-drafting-pipeline.md:19-23`: eight rounds, findings 10,
8, 9, 10, 10, 9, 80–90% on spans the previous round had rewritten).

On the IR an operator edit is a text edit:

| edit | example on `08aa` | what reruns |
|---|---|---|
| a symbol's value | `council.sits_at = hall` | L3–L5 at $0; beats whose `uses` include it are marked stale |
| a beat's `exit` | Mercer's silence ends at beat 11 | L5; beats k+1.. entry recomputed |
| a reveal moved | "the interval never varies" from beat 12 to beat 11 | withheld consistency at $0 |
| a directive | an instruction on beat 7 | nothing static; the beat is stale |
| a replan from K | as today, but under the checks | L4 for beats ≥ K, then L5 |

### Where a new gate sits

```
brief → check → GATE 1 → repair …
      → lower (L1–L5) → GATE IR: edit | replan | draft
      → scene ×M with slices → conformance ×M → GATE 2: keep | rewrite k
```

The IR gate is the operator's, or an auto rule: zero static errors and no
`resolved = CONTRADICTED` symbol (ADR-0006, no model decides at a gate). Gate
1 stays on the brief: a calendar inconsistency in the ledger is a gate 1
finding, since the ledger is the brief's. Gate 2 shrinks to prose-only
flags: conformance, register, restated, listen, slop.

`Drafting.write` (`drafting.ts:302-308`) is where the stop goes; `act`
already returns a draw to the status it came from on failure
(`lifecycle.ts`), and a new status `awaiting_plan_gate` follows the pattern
of `awaiting_draft_gate`.

## 7. Backend: code generation

### 7.1 The ask

ADR-0010 fixes the shape: the fixed block rides in the system prompt, the
beat's part in the user ask. The IR fits: the system prompt carries examples,
the symbol table (smaller than the ledger plus outline it replaces, or beside
them at first) and the whole IR; the user ask carries the beat's slice.

| today's user ask (`write.ts:194-204`) | with the IR |
|---|---|
| story so far (first beat of a session) | same |
| material (absorbed vignette) | same |
| register | same |
| constraints (rewrites) | same |
| `job`, `when`, `known`, `withheld`, form, cap | plus `entry`, `events`, `uses` with their values written out, `exit`, `may_invent`, `must_not`, set piece |

The claim under test is that a beat-scoped list of six facts in the ask
binds where 60 lines in the system prompt did not. Evidence for: the bind's
patches show the writer knows the register well enough to fix a line when

### 7.2 Local and external symbols **[conjecture]**

| kind | the writer may | the writer may not |
|---|---|---|
| external (ledger, setting, an earlier beat's exit) | state it, imply it, leave it out | change its value; add an attribute the table lacks (the suit worn once) |
| local (invented in this beat) | invent it freely: names of clerks, weather, gestures, exact speech | contradict an external symbol with it |
| promoted local (a local a later beat needs: `crank.men = 2`) | fixed at this beat's `exit` by the conformance screen, once | later beats treat it as external |

Promotion is how the bind's "previous scene" rule becomes explicit: today
scene k is screened against scene k−1's whole text (`prompts.ts:497`); with
promotion, only the locals k−1 fixed travel.

### 7.3 Parallel beats

Once `entry`/`exit` are fixed, the beats are independent units and could be
written at once. `scenes.order = parallel` exists (`scenesession.ts:141-144`)
and has never been measured against sequential (proposal revisit table,
`2026-09-05-drafting-proposal.md:489-497`). Against it:

- the restated flags show the writer reusing lines as motifs across beats
  (twenty a draft on the signal template, kept on purpose, spec amendment
  2026-09-19 night); parallel beats cannot echo each other;
- sequential scenes cost less as turns of one session (ADR-0001 amendment;
  `evals/20260922-probes.md`: $0.011 to read what $0.225 wrote);
- the one-call structure screen agreed 53% with per-scene answers
  (`evals/20260922-probes.md`), a warning that bigger independent units are
  not free.

Keep sequential. Parallel is a later, separately judged experiment; the IR
makes it possible, not advisable.

## 8. Linking

| link-time question | today | with the IR |
|---|---|---|
| does beat k+1 start where beat k ended? | bind reads scene k+1 against scene k's prose | `entry(k+1) == exit(k)` by construction; nothing to check |
| does the prose of beat k realise its slice? | ledger screen: whole ledger plus previous scene against the scene | conformance: `uses`, `exit`, `must_not` against the scene; same `<finding>` shape, same patches (`applyPatches` unchanged) |
| which beat states a fact first? | nobody knows until the restated screen | `reveals` |
| after a rewrite of k, what is stale? | k+1 is always rebound (`scenesession.ts:261-267`) | k+1 is stale only if the rewrite changed a promoted local or an `exit` line; else nothing |

The bind base session (`prompts.ts:499-509`) exists because the ledger plus
every scene is large. A conformance prompt is the scene plus a 15-line slice;
it needs no base. **[conjecture]**: cheaper and fewer false positives (the
"grey temples" and "at its interval" flags). The "suit worn once" flag stays
a model judgement: it is an attribute added to an external symbol, and only
a reader of the sentence can see that.

## 9. Incremental compilation

Dependency graph, all derivable from the IR at $0:

```
symbol s  →  beats b where s ∈ uses(b)
beat k    →  beat k+1 through exit(k)
withheld item i  →  its reveal beat and every beat before it
promoted local  →  the beat that fixed it and every beat after
```

| change | today | with the IR |
|---|---|---|
| a fact's value (gate 1 amendment) | the ledger is amended (`chain.ts:38-39`); every scene already written stands until a rewrite; a branch rewrites all beats from K | beats in `uses(s)` are stale; a fact quoted verbatim can be patched with no call (`applyPatches`); others are rewritten |
| a beat's text (gate 2 rewrite) | 1 scene, 2 binds, 2 structure screens, claims | 1 scene, 1 conformance, k+1 only if `exit(k)` moved |
| a beat's plan | replan from K rewrites every beat ≥ K | edit the beat's IR lines, static checks, rewrite that beat, link |

The honest limit: under sequential order beat k+1 read beat k's old prose as
its story so far. A rewrite of k leaves k+1's echoes of the old text in
place. The IR does not fix that; only writing k+1 from the IR alone
(parallel) or accepting the echo does. Today's rule, rebind k+1 and let a
person read it, is the right one, and the cascade rewrite was rejected for
the same reason (`2026-09-05-drafting-pipeline.md:669-670`).

## 10. Where the analogy breaks

1. **Prose is not a function of the IR.** Two drafts of one arm differ by as
   much as the change under test; `cut1` never won when read second, 0 of
   72 (`evals/20260924-draft-variance-dominates.md`). Compiling the same IR
   twice gives different object code, and "does the object code match the
   source" is a judgement with 0.62 a beat of noise
   (`evals/20260926-reference-bind-noise.md`), not a diff.
2. **Invention is the product.** The crank's quarter inch, the shoe on the
   warm flagstone, "It was not a reason": none is in the schedule. A
   `may_invent` line is permission, not a specification. The boundary
   between welcome invention and a contradiction is exactly the judgement the
   ledger screen already makes; the IR narrows what it is made against, it
   does not remove it.
3. **Voice does not live in facts.** The register is a block of prose
   instructions and six example passages (`write.ts:169-172`,
   `prompts.ts:466-468`); the cast's voice is one line each. Nothing in the
   IR touches hook, presence or momentum, which is where the drafts lose
   (`evals/20260926-where-drafts-lose.md`: hook −0.59, presence −0.32,
   momentum −0.24). An IR can only lose on those axes, by over-instructing.
4. **Over-specification flattens.** The scene ask already says "write within
   it and add nothing it does not hold" (`prompts.ts:476`). The polish pass
   was rejected because it homogenises (`2026-09-05-drafting-proposal.md:307-311`).
   The caps ablation found a prompt clause load-bearing in a direction nobody
   predicted (`evals/20260923-caps-ablation.md`). A slice of fifteen lines in
   the ask is a prompt change of the kind the accretion rule governs, and it
   has to be judged, not assumed neutral (ADR-0010's last line).
5. **The IR is model output too.** The 13-beat schedule invented the
   delegation. Lowering will invent things the ledger does not say; the
   static checks catch the typed ones and a model reads the rest. The ledger
   itself was wrong about its calendar. Checks on the plan are cheaper than
   checks on prose; they are not sound.
6. **The instruments are weak.** The panel resolves about 0.5 of score gap
   at three drafts a side, and the ear costs nothing but time
   (`docs/reviews/2026-09-26-the-plan-from-here.md`). Only counts survive:
   flags, open flags, rewrites, calls, dollars, minutes. The IR has to win on
   counts.

### 12.4 Do gate 1 and the schedule collapse?

Gate 1 today acts on findings about the brief; the schedule is derived after
it with no stop (`drafting.ts:302-308`). With a declared table and a plan
over it, the checkable content of the brief is the table, and the plan
depends on the table. Two shapes:

| shape | gates | argument |
|---|---|---|
| A: one IR gate after lowering and planning | seed → premise → gate (candidate) → outline as table + prose → plan → **IR gate** → scenes → gate 2 | one place to read; a plan costs $0.23–0.5 to redo when a symbol changes; the plan checks and the symbol checks are one list |
| B: keep gate 1 on the table, add the IR gate on the plan | … → **gate 1** (table) → plan → **IR gate** → scenes → gate 2 | the table is settled before a plan is paid for; matches today's chain and auto machinery |

Recommend A with two panes (symbols, plan) and one findings list, since a
replan is cheap and the plan is where the `08aa` errors were. Auto rule at
the IR gate: no static error, no `CONTRADICTED` symbol, no model-assisted
finding at or above the floor with evidence; the same shape as today's
`autoRounds` rule (`drafting.ts:464-466`).

The brief's prose parts:

| part | today | under the IR |
|---|---|---|
| chosen vignette | the operator's pick at the gate; material for one beat (`write.ts:195-198`); checked against the ledger | **source** for symbols the outline does not state (names, speech, objects), lowered once; **material** for the beat that absorbs it, as now; not checked as prose at gate 1, since its facts are in the table |
| context vignettes | each tests one job of the outline; 31% of an eight-round chain's findings sat in them, so they are carried unless a finding lands | **backend material only**: they exist to test the outline, and the plan checks now test it. Lowered for symbols they add, never checked as prose |

So the vignettes stop being what gate 1 reads and become what the backend
reads. The check that a reader would see a conflict (`prompts.ts:321`) moves
to the scenes, where the reader actually is.

### 12.5 Failure modes of a type checker

| cannot be caught on the IR | why | where it stays |
|---|---|---|
| resemblance | a cliché premise is well typed | text check on the premise, as today |
| derivation from the seed | the IR has no edge back to the seed; the premise's stated probability and the departure are the only record of the reading | the premise gate; a `derives` edge from departure to symbols covers the outline, not the seed |
| one departure, not two | "a symmetry, not a consequence" (`08aa`'s outline cut a second power) is a judgement over prose | derivation's model half |
| obscurity, thickening, spectacle, threat | qualities, not facts | structure profile |
| the better option left open | a judgement about what a person would do | reader check, over the events list |
| a symbol declared wrong | the table is model output; a wrong value type-checks | the operator at the IR gate; L2 where the setting speaks |
| a symbol the writer needed and nobody declared | the suit's reuse was in the setting, not the ledger, until L2 pulled it | link time |

Errors that move from compile time to link time:

| class | today | under the IR |
|---|---|---|
| the prose states a fact the brief settles otherwise | caught at gate 1 on the vignettes, again on the scenes | caught once, at the scene, by conformance; the vignettes are not checked |
| the prose invents an attribute of an external symbol | the claims screen on scenes: 12 claims from one draft's scenes, 2 contradicted, none in the brief (`2026-09-05-drafting-pipeline.md:1011-1017`, $1.27) | the same screen, scoped to `uses`; the share of link-time errors rises because the compile-time ones are gone |
| a plan line contradicts the table | never caught until the scene is bound | compile time, before any prose |
| a promoted local contradicted later | bind against scene k−1 | link: `exit(k)` carries it |

The net: fewer findings on prose, more of the remaining findings are the
writer's inventions, and those are the ones a patch fixes when the span is
quoted (13 of 13 on the first bound draft, `evals/20260919174915-389a.md`).

### 14.9 S3 as built

The plan gate is opt in. `draft --plan` (the API's `plan: true`, the
draft form's "stop at the plan") writes the schedule, runs the plan check
on it and stops at `awaiting_plan_gate` before any scene. Without it, a
draft runs as in §14.8. The auto rule of §13.3 (stop when the plan check
names a conflict) is not built: every manual draft stopping at the gate
changed each flow that drafts, and five draws of S3′ have not yet said
whether the findings are real often enough to stop for.

Three actions are allowed at the gate (`lifecycle.ts`), each in
`drafting.ts`:

| action | does |
|---|---|
| `apply` | puts each ticked plan finding's fix into the schedule in place of its span (the operator's note, else the finding's `patch`; a finding with neither is refused), and each edited beat field in place of the old; records a schedule step with model `operator` and a `keep` verdict on each ticked finding; checks the new plan again |
| `replan` | plans the schedule again from beat K under an instruction (`runSchedule`'s `Replan`); beats before K stay; checks the new plan again |
| `write` | writes every scene from the latest schedule, then stops at gate 2 as a draft does; the plan check is not run a second time |

A span that does not occur exactly once in the schedule refuses `apply`,
so a fix never lands in the wrong beat. The UI's plan-gate pane is the
gate 2 plan view with the ticks, a click-to-edit line on each beat field,
and the three actions. The unused `app/pipeline/ir/gate.ts` stub is
removed.
