# SEEDING v7 — GENERATION, UPSTREAM OF THE PLAYBOOK

*A generator, not a filter. `playbook-v2.md` is composed entirely of kill tests; its stated default is discard. Correct for triage, useless for producing a candidate. This document produces candidates that already carry the horror rather than acquiring it at Stage 4.*

*Supersedes `seeding-v6.md`. Three changes: **Move 3 gains a second clause** (name the noun, not the class), **the exemption test** joins the affect check in Move 8, and **§10 is rewritten** — backward validation has now been run, and its findings replace the placeholder.*

## 1. THE ENGINES

### 1.0 The inversion rule — how engines are made

Every engine below was produced the same way, and the procedure is the point: **the kill tests in `playbook-v2.md` are not only tests. Each names a property that finished work has. Inverted — treated as a thing to build rather than a thing to check for — it names a family of story.**

To mint a new engine:

1. **Take a test that discards on a specific ground.** It must name a property, not a quality bar. §4.4 (can the situation be fled?) inverts. "Is this good enough?" does not.
2. **State the property positively.** Not "reject seeds where the thing can be escaped" but "the thing cannot be escaped."
3. **Ask what must be true at the outset for the property to hold automatically** — that is Move 1, the seed.
4. **Ask what makes it hold under pressure** — that is Move 2, the hardening.
5. **Name the affect it produces, and the tests it will still fail.** An engine that guarantees everything is an engine you have described wrongly.

**A valid inversion produces a starting position; an invalid one produces a restatement of the test.** If your candidate's Move 1 is "make sure X is true," you have not inverted it, you have moved the check earlier. The test is whether someone holding no premise at all could execute Move 1 and end up holding material.

**Not every test inverts.** Tests that check for a *deficiency* (§2.4 novelty, §3.1 removal) have no positive form — nothing generates by "be original." Tests that name a *structural property* (§4.1, §4.3, §4.4, §9.5, §3.2) invert well. §4.5 and §6.4 look invertible and are unattempted.

**Every engine costs something.** By foreclosing a question at the outset, an engine removes the material that question would have supplied — and that removal has to be paid for downstream. 1.C is the worked case: foreclosing the arrival removes the reveal, which is why the common-knowledge clause exists (§5). When minting an engine, name what its Move 1 forecloses and where the replacement comes from.

**Engines may be collided.** One engine drives — supplies Move 1, the standing, the camera, the affect — while a second supplies the mechanism at Move 2. Legitimate, and distinct from running two engines in sequence. The constraint is that the driving engine owns Move 8: material imported from the second engine is an *instrument*, deployed once and not revisited, not a change of register. A disgust mechanism inside a fear engine is stated and left alone.

### 1.A Revocation — *inverts §4.1, the demotion*

**Move 1:** Name the specific standing a person believes they hold — a relational position theirs by right. The one who is owed, the one who consented, the patient, the one who woke up. State it first person, present tense, as something too obvious to say aloud. If it does not sound obvious, it is a trait and not a standing.

**Move 2:** Name what revokes it, **administered rather than perpetrated** — at scale, correctly, no one doing anything wrong. Administrative (formula, ratio, threshold, queue), economic (a price, a market that noticed something true), biological (a load, a rate, a metabolic cost), or statistical (a distribution stable for a reason nobody looked into).

*If the mechanism required someone to choose harm, it is a villain. If it would keep running with every human replaced, it is sound.*

**Produces:** institutional-epistemic horror. **Affect:** fear.

### 1.B Proliferation — *inverts §9.5, the disgust instrument*

**Move 1:** Start from **excess vitality in the wrong place**. Not damage, not absence — growth, quantity, repair, fertility, functioning correctly and in the wrong location. The generator is the surplus, never the threat.

**Move 2:** Establish **why it was ever absent**. Weak when the thing is introduced; strong when it was latent and merely suppressed, because then nothing invaded and the suppression was the anomaly. Name the suppressor and remove it.

**Produces:** body horror, contamination. **Affect:** disgust.
*Contamination is permanent and self-propagating: one contact detail marks a site for good, re-description is waste, enumeration adds items rather than force.*

### 1.C Coexistence — *inverts §4.4, escapability*

**Move 1:** Start from what can be neither fled nor fought, and skip the arrival. It is already here and has been for some time.

**Move 2:** Cost the **daily accommodation** — what was rearranged, what is no longer said, which rooms are unused, what it is called in front of guests. Then, because this engine has spent its arrival, **name what the accommodation conceals**: see the common-knowledge clause in §5.

**Produces:** domestic dread, the haunting. **Affect:** fear, low and sustained.
*Fits the non-arc shapes (§6.6) better than the ratchet (§6.2); pattern legibility becomes binding.*

### 1.D The promotion — *inverts §4.1 in the other direction; the SF engine*

**Move 1:** Start from a **genuine capability gain** — real understanding, real power, arriving as a real promotion. No monkey's paw, no cost planted at the outset.

**Move 2:** Follow it **rigorously and without cheating** into second and third-order consequences. The dread must be a byproduct of the working-out, discovered rather than installed. If you know the unpleasant destination before you begin reasoning, you are running Revocation in disguise.

**Produces:** science fiction with emergent dread. **Affect:** fear at distance, often deferred past the ending.
*Most needs §3.4 (double lens); most easily fails §3.7 by putting the working-out on the page.*

### 1.E Camera reassignment — *inverts §4.3*

**Move 1:** Take an existing discovery, expedition, rescue or research narrative and **move the camera to the discovered-upon** — the thing investigated, catalogued, treated, or helped.

**Move 2:** Give the observed party a **correct and complete model of the observers**, including of their good intentions. Not incomprehension; comprehension, which is worse.

**Produces:** the gothic inversion. **Affect:** fear, with the reader's allegiance under strain.
*Cheapest to run; most dependent on §5.4 — the reflector must feel more than it knows. Most exposed to the exemption test (§7): remoteness is built into the setup, and remoteness is how the reader gets out.*

### 1.F The sentence — *inverts §3.2, Delany*

**Move 1:** Write a **sentence meaningless as naturalistic fiction** — not via an impossible noun, but via a syntax, deixis or assumption that only makes sense elsewhere.

**Move 2:** **Reverse-engineer backward** until the sentence is unremarkable to its speaker. What must be ordinary for that to be a boring thing to say?

**Produces:** anything; least directed, most likely to surprise.
*The seed is a texture rather than a premise and usually needs a second engine run against it to acquire an event.*

## 2. MOVE 3 — THE CHANGE, CONCRETE, WITH UNITS

### Clause one: units

State what is added or removed in terms someone could measure, observe or picture. An abstraction — "a quantity already spent", "the capacity to be present" — is a phrase with a spooky adjective on it: nothing can be reasoned from it and the marvel is not cognisable (§3.5).

**The test: can you name the unit?** No unit, no seed.

**And it must foreclose, not merely diminish** (§3.3). Name the option removed. Capability added is decoration; an option removed is structure.

### Clause two: the noun, not the class

Units are not sufficient. A premise can carry rates, loads and thresholds and still be built on a **generic noun** — a hardship, a condition, an exposure, a resource, an adaptation, a tolerance. A category is not an object. You can measure a category and still have nothing to point at.

**The test: can you name what is being tolerated, resisted, transmitted or suppressed — as a specific thing, not as a kind of thing?** "Tolerance to scarcity" is a class. "Tolerance to the specific thing in that ground, carried from birth" is an object.

This is the same failure clause one catches, one level up. Clause one asks whether the change can be measured; clause two asks whether the thing being changed can be named.

*The units and the noun named here are the only material Move 7 is allowed to work with. **Amplification only operates on specific nouns** — a value cannot be cranked on an abstraction, which is why a seed that fails this clause reads as tame no matter how hard Move 7 is run on it.*

## 3. MOVE 4 — THE COST, IN FUTURE TENSE

A reveal that re-describes what was always true costs the character nothing. They find out and go on living, because their situation did not change. *A fact about your architecture that costs you nothing is trivia.*

The recognition must arrive attached to something **still being taken or still arriving**, on a trend the listener can model. Not "this was always so" but "this is proceeding, and here is the next increment."

Supplies the tense check (§4.5) and buys §7.2, recognition coincident with reversal. **The test: if the character can learn the truth and have their situation stay put, this move has not been done.**

## 4. MOVE 5 — THE GENERALISATION PASS

One operation, two scopes: **run the mechanism past its first instance and see what it generates.** Margins first, because they are easier and they warrant the premise; centre second, because that is where the further layers come from.

### 5.A The margins — populations and exceptions

Name the populations, conditions and exceptions the mechanism must already account for, and answer them *inside* the premise. Two payoffs:

1. **The premise becomes more cognisable, not less** — the mechanism starts generating consequences instead of receiving them, which is the tell for a marvel that survives its own explanation (§4.6).
2. **A documented real condition the premise explains without straining is the cheapest warrant available** (§3.5).

**The test: are the exceptions answered by the mechanism, or by exception-carving?** "It doesn't apply to them" means the mechanism is not general.

### 5.B The centre — the unaffected

Now apply the mechanism to the people the story is *not* about: the unaffected, the control group, the healthy, the ones who were doing fine. A mechanism general enough to survive 5.A says something about them too, and it is usually the thing nobody ran the numbers on.

The output is a proposition of the form **"this was always the case, and nobody had a way to see it."** That proposition is the input to Move 6, and on its own it violates Move 4 — see below.

*Engines vary in reach. Revocation, Proliferation and The promotion generalise across populations easily. Coexistence often has no population to generalise across; there, 5.B runs across **instances** — every household, every site, every case — and asks what is true of the ordinary ones.*

### The Move 4 reconciliation

Move 4 says a fact that was always true costs nothing and is trivia. Move 5.B asks for exactly such a fact. Both hold; the resolution is sequence.

**An always-true fact is trivia until something is built on it.** 5.B produces the fact; Move 6 converts it into a cost by asking what gets organised around it once it is known — and what gets built is in future tense, arriving, on a trend. **5.B is never the last thing you do.** A seed that stops there has been made larger and no more dangerous.

## 5. MOVE 6 — THE ENTAILMENT PASS

A seed that passes Moves 1–5 is sound and usually one layer deep. It states a situation, the situation is bad, and there is nothing further to find out. The diagnostic is that a listener can nod and be finished.

**The constraint that makes this tractable: a further turn cannot be a second marvel.** The budget is one (§3.6). Every additional layer must be something the *first* mechanism already entailed and nobody reasoned through. Not escalation — extraction.

The ladder, and which move supplies each rung:

| Rung | Content | Supplied by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The mechanism | Moves 1–3 |
| 2 | What it costs someone | Move 4 |
| 3 | What was always true of everyone | Move 5.B |
| 4 | **What gets built in response** | **This move** |

**Rung 4 is the work.** A revealed universal condition does not sit there. Something is organised around it — an industry, a standard, a schedule, a norm, a market, a new category of person. Ask what the response is, and cost it. This is generally where the affect lives, because an accommodation is more legible than a revelation, and because it returns the story to units and rooms after rung 3 pulled it toward abstraction.

### The common-knowledge clause

Some engines produce a mechanism nobody is hiding — 1.C by construction, 1.A whenever the mechanism is published. Universal knowledge is then the setting, and there is no reveal available in the existence of the thing.

**In that case the withheld material is the terms, not the existence.** Everyone knows the thing is there; nobody has examined what living around it has been doing, because examining it is not part of the routine. Ask: *what has the accommodation made unaskable?* The answer is rung 2 for these engines.

Two consequences worth holding. The reveal is an inference, not a discovery — no hidden party, no secret, nothing withheld by anyone. And the strongest narrator position (§5.3) is someone whose work requires them to be the first person to ask the question, which is usually someone employed to measure the thing for a purpose other than this one.

### The §8.3 check, applied at every rung

A rung can land on a proposition that is ancient and undisputed — everyone suffers, everyone dies, convenience weakens people. As a *claim* it does no work; nobody is on the other side. But it works as a **condition**: the story is not arguing the proposition, it is showing what happens once the proposition becomes measurable, actionable, or administrable. At each rung:

- **Something contestable has been discovered** → the rung stands alone and can be the last.
- **Something old has merely been instrumented** → the rung is a premise, not a payload. Take the next one; what gets built is the story.

**Stopping rule.** Take one more rung only while it stays inside the marvel budget and while units survive. A rung requiring a new impossible thing is a different story. A rung that generalises past the point where you can name a unit, a population or a procedure has become a thesis. Four rungs is usually the ceiling.

**Downstream:** this supplies §6.2, the ratchet — same mechanism, hardening implication, rising cost — and gives §7.2 something to reverse into on the last turn rather than the first.

## 6. MOVE 7 — THE AMPLIFICATION PASS

A seed can be sound, layered, and still merely unsettling. The default instinct at that point — more victims, more violence, a second entity — is escalation, and escalation costs a marvel and destroys the units. **Amplification changes a value on a variable you already have. It never introduces a noun.**

**Precondition: Move 3 clause two must have passed.** Amplification operates on values attached to a named object. If the object is a category, no axis will bite, and the pass will produce volume without intensity. Check this before running the axes, not after.

The five axes, all internal to Move 3's units:

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| **Rate** | Is it faster than stated? |
| **Term** | Has it been running far longer, with most of it already spent? |
| **Scope** | Is what counts as input broader than assumed? |
| **Consent** | Did the person participate more than they knew — and would they have chosen it anyway? |
| **Recoverability** | Does stopping give it back? |

Run more than one. They compound without adding anything.

**The legitimacy test: did you change a number or widen a definition, or did you add a noun?** A number or a definition is free — the mechanism already implied that value, and the reader will feel that it did. A noun is a second story; park it separately.

**The ceiling: the accommodation must stay rational.** Amplify far enough and the people in the premise stop behaving like people who have weighed something, and fear becomes spectacle. The tell is not intensity — it is that no sane person would still live that way. When that happens, one axis has been pushed past its stopping point; back that one off, not the whole pass.

*Run Move 7 after Move 6, not before. Amplifying a one-layer seed produces a loud one-layer seed.*

## 7. MOVE 8 — THE AFFECT CHECK, AND THE EXEMPTION TEST

**Run last, and re-run after any change to register, container or scale — which means after Moves 5.B, 6 and 7 without exception.**

### The affect check

The engine selects an affect. **The register must serve that affect, not overwrite it.** Fear and disgust are different instruments with different physiologies; vagueness and specificity are not two intensities of one effect (§9.5).

The failure mode is not choosing wrongly at the outset. It is **drift** — a seed revised for an unrelated reason (novelty, differentiation, scale, an added rung, an amplification) landing in a register that cannot deliver what the engine generated. It runs both ways: a proliferation seed moved into a distanced institutional voice has had its payload removed while every other test passes; a coexistence seed that acquired vivid anatomical texture during Move 5 has converted sustained dread into disgust and lost the domestic register that made it work.

**Ask: does the chosen register let this affect operate at sentence level?** If not, one of the two is wrong, and it is usually not the engine.

### The exemption test

Same class of failure, different symptom. The seed is sound, layered, amplified, correctly registered — and it lands as **sad rather than horrific**. The diagnosis is not intensity and not register.

**Sad-not-horrific means the mechanism does not include the reader.** A story about people the reader can be sure they are not is a tragedy; the reader's response is pity, which is not fear.

**The test: can the reader be exempt?** Ask it literally — is there any position from which someone could read this and be confident it does not apply to them?

**Exemption almost always enters through the setting**, not the mechanism: remoteness, historical distance, a special population, a bounded community, a condition someone either has or does not have. The mechanism may be perfectly general and the setting quietly grants an escape anyway.

**The repair is not to widen the scope by assertion.** Do not append a line saying it turns out this is everywhere. Find the version in which the *specific* mechanism already had the general case inside it — usually by asking what the exempt population is doing that the affected population stopped doing, and discovering the exemption was the anomaly rather than the norm. The general case should arrive as an inference the reader completes, in exactly the way rung 3 does.

*1.E and 1.C are the engines most exposed to this: one builds remoteness into the camera move, the other builds boundedness into the site.*

## 8. HANDOFF

Enter `playbook-v2.md` at §2. §4 should pass automatically. What actually bites:

- **§5, the person.** Nothing here finds a narrator. Note §5.3 — exposure is positional, not personal, and the strongest placements put the narrator within the mechanism's own scope rather than outside observing it.
- **§8.3 and §8.4.** Move 6 pre-empts some of this; it does not replace the tests. A structural mechanism told as one person's decline still collapses into allegory.
- **§3.6, the marvel budget.** These seeds spend their marvel on the mechanism, correctly, leaving nothing for anything else — which is why Moves 6 and 7 both work by extraction.
- **§6.7, the container.** Register is decided *here*, not at seed time — then re-checked against Move 8.
- **§6, shape and ending.** See §10: this is a known boundary, not an oversight. Nothing upstream produces the ending.

## 9. WHEN A MOVE FAILS — THE REPAIR TABLE

The playbook's default is discard, correct for a filter. **This document's default is repair.** Most seeds fail somewhere, and where they fail tells you what to do.

| Failure | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **3: no unit** | A mood, not a mechanism | Return to Move 2. If Move 2 yields no units either, keep the material as texture and re-run under 1.F |
| **3: diminishes, doesn't foreclose** | A capability was added instead of an option removed | Repairable in place: name the option removed. If none exists, return to Move 1 |
| **3: the object is a category** | Generic noun — a hardship, a condition, an exposure, an adaptation | Name the specific thing. Repairable in place and usually cheap; the specificity is normally already implied by Move 2 and was lost in summary |
| **4: no future tense** | The seed re-describes a static condition | Return to Move 3 and look for a rate. A mechanism with units but no rate is usually a metaphor |
| **4: costs the character nothing** | Wrong person, not wrong premise | Do not touch the mechanism. Defer to `playbook-v2.md` §5 and reposition (§5.3) |
| **5.A: exceptions need carving** | The mechanism is not general | Fatal to the mechanism. The material may survive under a different engine |
| **5.A: no real-world condition available** | Not a failure | Continue. Warrant is cheaper with one, not impossible without |
| **5.B: nothing to say about the unaffected** | Common on 1.C; sometimes real | Try instances rather than people. If still nothing, the seed stops at one layer — viable as a short, weak as anything longer |
| **6: no reveal available, everyone knows** | The engine foreclosed the arrival | Apply the common-knowledge clause: withhold the terms, not the existence |
| **6: the rung needs a new marvel** | This is a second story | Park it separately. Do not spend the budget twice |
| **6: every rung lands on the undisputed** | The mechanism instruments something old | Take rung 4 and make the response the story. If rung 4 is also undisputed, discard |
| **7: creepy but not awful** | Amplification not yet run, or run on one axis | Run the remaining axes. Compound rate, term and recoverability before touching anything else |
| **7: loud but still tame after all axes** | Not an amplification failure — the object is a category | Go back to Move 3 clause two. Do not push the axes further; they have nothing to grip |
| **7: the accommodation stopped being rational** | One axis pushed past the ceiling | Back off that axis only. Do not revert the pass |
| **8: register can't carry the affect** | Drift, usually from 5.B, 6 or 7 | Revert the specific revision that caused it. Identify which move changed scale or texture and undo that change only |
| **8: sad, not horrific** | The reader can be exempt, usually via the setting | Find the version where the specific mechanism contains the general case. Do not assert generality; make it inferable |

**Distinguish the two tameness failures before repairing.** *Values too polite* is a Move 7 problem and the repair is to crank the axes. *The object is a category* is a Move 3 problem and the repair is to name the noun. The repairs point in opposite directions, and running the wrong one makes the seed worse — a category amplified is just a louder abstraction.

**Switching engines is cheap; abandoning material is not.** A seed that fails under one engine has often only been asked the wrong opening question. Re-running the same material under a different engine is a legitimate repair, and produces a genuinely different story rather than a salvaged version of the old one.

## 10. WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO — THE VALIDATION FINDING

**It supplies no material.** Sourcing is `playbook-v2.md` §1; several of its generators are somatic and need a body and a week. The two runnable on demand are collision (§1.2) and the found armature (§1.4).

**The engine list is not closed** — §1.0 states how to extend it. §4.5 and §6.4 remain unattempted.

**Move 7 is derived from one instance** and its axes are asserted, not tested. Whether five is the right number, and whether the ceiling is really "the accommodation stops being rational" rather than something register-specific, is open.

### Backward validation: what happened

The framework was run in reverse against ten published SCP articles known to work — asking of each whether these moves, executed by someone holding no premise, would arrive at it. Results: two derive almost completely (3000, 2818), one derives (7198), three half-derive (3985, 2812, and the extended runs), one does not derive at all (2935).

**What the framework reliably produces: mechanism, layering, and cost.** Every article that worked had a mechanism the moves would have found, a ladder the entailment pass would have climbed, and an accommodation rung 4 would have named. In the cleanest cases the correspondence is close to exact — 3000's failed synthetic is Move 3 foreclosure doing its job in three sentences; 2818's procedural filler is 5.A establishing that the weapon requires a volunteer; its final line is amplification on recoverability and nothing else.

**Three things it does not produce.** Two have homes; one does not.

1. **The specific object.** The framework yields "a person whose suffering yields something medically valuable." It does not yield *a toe*. This is exactly the gap Move 3 clause two now names, and clause two is the response to it — but note that clause two only *tests* for specificity. It does not generate the specific thing. That remains a taste operation.

2. **The shape of the ending.** 3985's payload is not its mechanism but that the office reshapes whoever occupies it: the reformer wins, takes the chair, files the same denial verbatim. That is a shape, not a mechanism. It lives in `playbook-v2.md` §6 and correctly does not live here.

3. **The formal move — what the document itself does.** No home anywhere. In the strongest examples this is the whole payload: an article told in pictograms, one presented as a corrupted file, one as a bare inventory, one whose document catches the compulsion it describes. §6.7 covers the container, but a container is a *register* choice — "this is a clinical report" — whereas "the file has the thing too" is a different operation entirely, performing the mechanism rather than housing it.

**This is stated as a boundary, not a hole.** The framework generates the engine of a story and cannot generate its form. That is a real limit and probably an appropriate one: form is chosen against a specific piece of material, late, and a procedure that produced it upstream would produce it identically every time. What the boundary requires is that form be *asked about explicitly* at handoff rather than defaulted to prose — which is the only reason it is written down here.

**No longer outstanding:** whether the framework generates or merely rationalises. It generates mechanism and structure; it rationalises nothing, but it also stops short of the object, the ending, and the form.
