*Job: Test the custody claim that the sacrament keeps the director's two truths apart: the director's monthly confession, where his confessor absolves him and he signs envoy twelve's order in the next room.*

The confessional in the Office's chapel had no grille. It was a room with two chairs, because every man who used it had been cleared for the same things.

"I ordered the removal of relics from thirty-eight churches," the director said. He said it every month, the same number, because the number had not changed. "I have not returned them. I have sent eleven men to a flat in Naples to ask a thief to be sorry."

Father Bassi had heard this ten times. He had a way of listening that involved looking at his own hands.

"Are you sorry?"

"Yes." It was true. It was the truest thing the director said in any given month; he felt it under the sternum, a specific weight, like the crate. "The Lyon girl was nine. Her femur is in a drawer in the sub-basement with a tag on it, because it came off in the van."

"And do you resolve—"

"There are thirty-four vehicles near Van."

Bassi waited. This was the part of the training that worked without thinking: you did not argue with a penitent. You let the sentence end, and then you asked again.

"Do you resolve to amend your life?"

The director looked at the wall where a grille would have been. In his desk he had twelve names. In the sub-basement he had fifty-two elements still attached and eleven not. He could have done the arithmetic aloud; he could have said *I would sign it again* in the same breath as *I am sorry*, and the two would have touched. Instead he said what the sacrament required, which was also what he wanted to be true.

"I resolve."

"Then I absolve you from your sins, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit."

"Amen."

He felt it lift. That was the thing nobody outside believed: it lifted. He walked out lighter, along the corridor, past the door to the sub-basement stairs, into his office, where the order for envoy twelve lay where his secretary had left it. Father Ricci. June. The same paragraph as the other eleven, because the paragraph had not changed.

He read it once. He did not think of Lyon. He was not avoiding it; there was simply no room in the paragraph for a nine-year-old. He signed.

In the chapel Bassi was already hearing the next man, who had also been cleared for the file, and who was also sorry.

That evening the secretary, cataloguing, noted a twelfth element had separated in the crate: a metacarpal, Bruges, 1640s. She wrote it on a tag. The tag went in the drawer with the others, where the count matched, and nobody had asked it to.
