*Job: Test the unbuilding replacement for the tears: an Office archivist cataloguing the twelfth loosed element in transit and realizing the count matches the envoys, which Lauro has not noticed.*

The twelfth came off somewhere between Frascati and the new site, in a crate padded with the same foam they used for altarpieces. Sister Immacolata Renzi found it when she opened the lid: a left clavicle, small as a fountain pen, lying loose on the foam with a curl of wax still on one end and no wire at all. The wire had not broken. It had been unwound.

She photographed it in situ, then on the tray, then against the scale. Catalogue card: *Item 63/12. Clavicle, sinister, juvenile. Provenance per assembler's testimony: Sant'Eufemia, Verona, sacristy drawer C, 2021. Subject: female, aged seven, d. 1631. Separated from assembly in transit, 3 June 2026.*

The card went into the box with the other eleven. Eleven cards, eleven small bones, each now a thing with a number, a date, a church, and a country, which is to say each now a thing that could be asked about.

She had not intended to read the box. But the cards were in date order and the dates were in her handwriting, and she had also, because the Office was small and she was the one with legible script, typed the dispatch orders for the envoys.

July: Lyon femur, and Father Achterberg to Naples. August: a rib, and Father Cissé. September, a phalanx, Father Moreau. October, the Padre Pio month, a tooth. She did not need the ledger. She went and got it anyway.

Twelve separations. Twelve priests. Not one per week, not one per month by the calendar, but one per visit, the piece coming loose in the days after each man returned with nothing. As if she were being asked a question and answering it the only way left to her: *no*, and a bone.

Immacolata sat with the clavicle on its tray. In the assembly the children faced inward, and this one had been at the throat. Fifty-one remained. Twelve Jesuits remained cleared. She did the arithmetic the way the Office did everything, in silence, and it came out even.

Lauro would not know. Lauro had never asked for a count. The envoys were told to tell him *deteriorating* and he corrected them, and no one had thought to bring him a card and say *Verona, drawer C, is it yours*, because that would be showing the thief his inventory, and the inventory was theirs now.

She wrote one line on a memo slip, for the director, who would read it before his confession or after it, she did not know which. *Separations correspond to envoy dispatches, 1:1. Recommend suspending envoys pending review.*

Then she looked at it and understood that suspending the envoys was also a count. She filed the slip under the clavicle, where it would be catalogued.
