# ANNEX — THE GROUND

*Mechanisms, not stories. Nine domains of physical processes in ground, water, air and tissue that run continuously, at population scale, and are administered by real bodies holding real instruments: statutes, permits, registers, monitoring obligations, advisories, condition ratings. Each entry states what the process is, the figure that measures it, and the institution and instrument that hold it.*

*Items marked `[?]` are specifics a fact-check should confirm before they carry weight. Nothing here is ranked, and the presence of an entry is not a claim about its importance.*

*Where an institution is named, what is stated is what that body is and what its instrument does. Nothing here asserts wrongdoing by a named organisation.*

## 1. Subsidence and the aquifer

- Aquifer-system compaction has two regimes divided by the preconsolidation stress: above it, drawdown compresses the skeleton elastically and the ground rebounds when heads recover; below it, the fine-grained clay and silt interbeds compact inelastically as platelets rearrange, and the deformation is permanent.
- Inelastic compaction destroys pore space, so the aquifer permanently loses the capacity to hold water — the loss of *storage* is a separate and less-reported quantity from the loss of *elevation*, and every subsequent drought is drawn against a smaller reservoir.
- A 2026 PNAS analysis of the Sacramento Valley describes an abrupt transition to irreversible damage once the system crosses that threshold `[?]` — citation believed real, figures not verified to the primary source (publisher returned 403).
- Water squeezed out of compacting clays carries arsenic with it into the pumped aquifer, and subsidence rate is a statistical predictor of arsenic concentration in the Tulare basin (*Nature Communications* 9:2089, 2018) — the land sinking and the arsenic in the tap are the same physical event.
- Chronic inorganic arsenic in drinking water produces hyperkeratosis of the palms and soles, skin, bladder and lung cancer, peripheral neuropathy, and measurable deficits in children exposed in utero and in early childhood.
- The exposed population is domestic-well households and small water systems without treatment capacity, and the rational response to a falling water table — drilling deeper, into the compacting interval — is also the exposure.
- Downtown San Jose fell roughly 13 feet between 1910 and 1969 and the fall was stopped, by imported water, managed recharge, and a groundwater production charge levied on the pumpers; the Santa Clara Valley Water District still measures it.
- SGMA (2014, Water Code §10720 et seq.) codifies "significant and unreasonable land subsidence" as one of six statutory undesirable results, gives every basin a named Groundwater Sustainability Agency, a plan and a 2040 deadline, and gives DWR authority to place a basin on probation.
- The measuring instruments are the borehole extensometer, the InSAR time series and the annual subsidence report — a basin's compliance record is also the documentary proof of what has already been spent.
- Delta peat islands subside by oxidation rather than compaction: drained organic soil is consumed by aerobic bacteria, and the surface of some islands now sits roughly 25 feet below sea level behind approximately 1,100 miles of levee.
- Peat oxidation is a carbon flux as well as a subsidence mechanism — the elevation loss and the CO₂ release are the same reaction.
- The 2000–2021 period was the driest 22-year interval in southwestern North America in at least 1,200 years, with a large anthropogenic contribution (*Science* 2020; *Nature Climate Change* 2022).
- The tree-ring chronology that establishes that is itself the instrument that reveals the twentieth century — the period in which every water right in the state was allocated — to have been anomalously wet.
- April 1 snowpack functions as a storage account rather than a hazard measure; the April 2026 survey found no measurable snow at the Phillips Station course after a record hot, dry March, and the snow course itself is a manual measurement made by a person with a tube, on a schedule, at a fixed location.
- Saltwater intrusion in the Delta is driven by reduced outflow against tidal forcing; the emergency drought salinity barrier at West False River — a rock dam thrown across a navigable channel, installed 2015, again June–July 2021, removed October 2022 — is the Department of Water Resources' physical instrument for holding the ocean off the export pumps.

## 2. Water at the margin

- Sea-level rise raises the coastal water table before it overtops anything: denser seawater pushes inland beneath the ground and lifts the freshwater lens above it, so shallow groundwater emerges at grade, floods basements and crawlspaces, and infiltrates sewers.
- SFEI's Shallow Groundwater Response to Sea Level Rise project (2020–22; Alameda, Marin, San Francisco and San Mateo counties, with Contra Costa following) mapped the highest annual shallow water table across the Bay side of five counties and modelled it forward; the response reaches up to three miles from the shoreline.
- Groundwater can emerge above ground as an urban flood hazard before coastal floodwaters overtop the shoreline — the flooding arrives from underneath and inland of any levee.
- Over 5,200 state- and federally-managed contaminated sites in the setting-a are exposed to emergent groundwater, more than ten times prior estimates, against 326 Superfund sites and an estimated ~115,000 total sites nationally (UC Berkeley and Utah State University; see *Earth's Future* 10.1029/2023EF003825, 2023).
- The exposed communities named in that work are West Oakland, the Richmond waterfront, and Bayview–Hunters Point.
- A rising water table intersecting a residual chlorinated-solvent plume drives vapour intrusion upward through a slab; the short-exposure endpoint governing the Middlefield-Ellis-Whisman site is a developmental cardiac endpoint with a window measured in weeks, so the exposure interval that matters is a first trimester in a ground-floor unit.
- Water standing over a closed municipal dump carries lead, arsenic and PAHs to the surface, where it dries into dust.
- Sub-slab depressurisation cannot depressurise a saturated sub-slab: the engineering control fails while the fan continues to run and to log its runtime, so the compliance record and the protection diverge silently.
- Containment-in-place is the modal Superfund remedy — a clay or geomembrane cap, a vapour barrier, a sub-slab fan with an operations-and-maintenance agreement — and its design assumption is that the pathway comes from above.
- The recorded **Land Use Covenant** fixes the permitted land use against the deed, and the **five-year review** under CERCLA §121(c) is the periodic determination that a remedy remains protective.
- **EnviroStor** (DTSC) and **GeoTracker** (State Water Board) are the public parcel-level registers of contaminated sites; the Cortese List under Gov. Code §65962.5 is the statutory compilation.
- The San Francisco Bay Regional Water Quality Control Board's Land Disposal Program issues waste discharge requirements for closed landfills and has begun requiring operators to consider rising groundwater; DTSC has issued draft guidance directing site managers to evaluate rising groundwater at remediated sites.
- Landfill post-closure maintenance obligations run for a defined period after closure (30 years under the federal Subtitle D baseline, extendable by the regulator) — an obligation with a stated end date over a hazard with none `[?]`.
- Multi-century sea-level commitment from emissions already made is the standard case of a physical debt owed and not yet delivered: the forcing is committed, the delivery is on a centuries-long schedule, and nothing decided now alters the already-committed fraction.
- ARkStorm 2.0 (*Science Advances* 8, eabq0995, 2022) finds the likelihood of an 1862-scale month-long precipitation sequence already roughly doubled relative to a century ago, and more than tripled for events with a ~200-year historical recurrence interval.
- In that work's future scenario, statewide precipitation runs about 45% above the historical analogue while Sacramento and San Joaquin peak runoff runs 200–400% higher — the runoff amplification is larger than the precipitation increase because warming shifts the precipitation from snow to rain.
- Forecast-Informed Reservoir Operations at Lake Mendocino (Sonoma Water with Scripps CW3E, under USACE-approved major deviations from the Water Control Manual) enabled a 19% increase in stored water in Water Year 2020 — more than 11,000 acre-feet — with total estimated annual benefits of $9.4 million; the Final Viability Assessment issued 4 February 2021.
- FIRO is a case in which the instrument that changed operations was a *forecast* rather than a structure: the reservoir holds more water because a skill-verified prediction was admitted into a rule curve.

## 3. Ground motion and made ground

- ShakeAlert detects P-wave arrivals and issues an alert before the S-wave and surface waves arrive; warning times range from a few seconds to tens of seconds, and the longest warnings are available only to users far from the epicentre.
- The USGS states plainly that if the earthquake begins directly below you, you have little or no warning — the system's warning time is a function of distance from the rupture, so coverage is inversely proportional to exposure.
- For a Hayward fault rupture, the roughly two million people living directly over the trace sit inside the late-alert zone by construction.
- Made ground behaves as its constituent material: hydraulically placed fill, dune sand, and 1906 rubble liquefy under cyclic loading where competent ground does not, which is why the Marina District and the Cypress Structure on Bay Mud failed in 1989 while adjacent bedrock sites did not.
- Liquefaction is a pore-pressure mechanism: cyclic shear raises pore water pressure until effective stress reaches zero and saturated cohesionless soil loses shear strength entirely.
- The Alquist-Priolo Earthquake Fault Zoning Act and the Seismic Hazards Mapping Act put liquefaction and fault-rupture zones onto maps, and Civil Code §1103's one-page Natural Hazard Disclosure statement is the instrument that transmits them to a buyer at the point of sale.
- The Hayward fault creeps aseismically at roughly 5 mm/yr, and the separation joint built through California Memorial Stadium is a purpose-built instrument for accommodating and reading it.
- Injection of treated municipal wastewater from Santa Rosa, piped roughly 40 miles to The Geysers geothermal field, demonstrably raises the local seismicity rate; the events are numerous and small, and the field is instrumented by LBNL and the operator.
- Induced seismicity at The Geysers is a case where a city's treated sewage, a steam field's pressure maintenance, and a monitored earthquake catalogue are one continuous system across two counties.
- The California Volcano Observatory operates a colour-coded volcano alert level and aviation colour code for Long Valley and the state's other centres; the 1982 Long Valley hazard-notice episode is the standing case study in a warning that altered a town's insurance and property market without an eruption.
- Geomagnetically induced currents flow in long conductors during geomagnetic storms and drive half-cycle saturation in transformer cores, producing reactive power draw, harmonics and localised heating.
- NERC Reliability Standard **TPL-007-4** defines a benchmark GMD event with a reference peak geoelectric field of **8 V/km at 60° geomagnetic latitude**, scaled by local latitude and earth conductivity, and applies to transformers with a wye-grounded high-side winding above 200 kV.
- Under that standard a thermal impact assessment is required where the maximum effective GIC reaches **75 A per phase**, due within 24 months of receiving GIC data; a corrective action plan must implement non-hardware mitigation within 2 years and hardware mitigation within 4.
- GIC damages equipment rather than tissue; the route to a person is through loss of supply, which in California is administered through de-energisation programmes and the utility **Medical Baseline** allowance that identifies households dependent on electrically powered medical equipment.
- The Anthropocene Working Group's proposed GSSP at Crawford Lake used the 1952 plutonium fallout spike in varved lake sediment as the primary marker; the Subcommission on Quaternary Stratigraphy rejected the proposal in early 2024 and the vote was upheld — a boundary defined by a weapons test, formally proposed and formally declined on stratigraphic procedure.
- Slow-moving landslides deform continuously rather than failing catastrophically: Portuguese Bend on the Palos Verdes Peninsula reached several inches to about a foot of movement per week in 2024, mapped by NASA airborne InSAR, with a municipal buyout programme as the administrative instrument.

## 4. The atmosphere and heat

- The empirical critical wet-bulb limit for young, healthy subjects is around **30.6–31 °C** in humid conditions (PSU HEAT Project; *J Appl Physiol* 2022, *Environmental Research Letters* 2023) — substantially below the canonical 35 °C survivability threshold that appears in planning documents.
- Heat mortality's signal is nocturnal rather than diurnal: the parameter that predicts death is the failure of the indoor night-time minimum to fall, because the body's recovery window is overnight.
- Coastal California's housing stock has almost no mechanical cooling and no code requirement for it, so indoor overnight temperature is set by envelope, orientation and infiltration alone.
- The urban heat island is a surface-energy-balance mechanism — impervious mass storing daytime insolation and releasing it after sunset — and it acts specifically on the nocturnal minimum.
- The regional heat signal in the setting-a is weak relative to inland California because the marine layer supplies advective cooling; the same stock that is unprepared for heat is unprepared because it has historically not needed to be.
- The Montreal Protocol (1987) and its Vienna Convention (1985) have **198 parties** — universal ratification — and control nearly 100 ozone-depleting substances.
- Implementation has phased out **99% of ODSs**, roughly 1.8 million ODP-tonnes; total column ozone is projected to return to 1980 levels around **2066 over Antarctica, 2045 over the Arctic, and 2040 in the 60°N–60°S belt**.
- Because most controlled ODSs are also potent greenhouse gases, the phase-out avoided roughly **135 billion tonnes of CO₂-equivalent between 1990 and 2010**, and the 2016 Kigali Amendment on HFCs is projected to avoid a further **0.3–0.5 °C of warming by 2100**.
- US implementation is projected to prevent on the order of 443 million skin cancer cases and 63 million cataract cases for people born 1890–2100 — a benefit stated as a count of avoided disease in a population that will never be identifiable.
- Ozone recovery is itself a forcing: restoring stratospheric ozone shifts Southern Hemisphere atmospheric circulation, so the repair changes the weather in a direction distinct from the damage.

## 5. Contaminant burdens in the body

- Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances are effectively non-degrading in the environment and are eliminated from the human body over years rather than hours, because they bind serum albumin and are actively reabsorbed in the proximal tubule.
- NHANES 1999–March 2020 finds PFAS in essentially the whole US population; 2017–18 geometric means were **PFOS 4.25 ng/mL, PFOA 1.42, PFHxS 1.08, PFNA 0.411** (ATSDR clinical guidance).
- PFAS cross the placenta into cord blood and are excreted in breast milk, so adult serum concentration falls with parity and with cumulative months of lactation — the fastest route by which an adult body clears these compounds is transfer to a child.
- The Women Firefighter Biomonitoring Collaborative (UC Berkeley, UCSF, Silent Spring Institute) compared **86 women firefighters of the San Francisco Fire Department with 84 women office workers** in downtown San Francisco; of twelve PFAS tested, seven were detected in most participants, and **PFHxS, PFUnDA and PFNA were significantly elevated** in the firefighters (*Environmental Science & Technology*, 26 February 2020).
- The cohort exists because San Francisco's fire line is roughly **15% women** against about 5% nationally; the study was initiated after the participants themselves reported a perceived elevated rate of breast cancer.
- Population PFOS serum concentrations in the US have fallen steeply and continuously since the principal manufacturer's phase-out beginning in 2000 — one of the clearest demonstrations that removing a chemical from commerce registers in human blood within a decade.
- **Biomonitoring California** (SB 1379, Chapter 599, Statutes of 2006; CDPH, OEHHA and DTSC) is the statutory programme that measures chemicals in Californians, and its instrument is the **individual result-return letter**, which states a measured quantity inside the addressee's own body with no facility, perimeter or defendant attached.
- CDC/NHANES is the national reference series against which an individual result is positioned, so a serum panel is also a position on a declining population curve and therefore, implicitly, a date.
- In 2025 the San Francisco Fire Department converted its entire turnout gear fleet to non-PFAS materials, following the city's move to restrict the chemistry in gear — the procurement record is the instrument.
- Pyrolysis gas chromatography–mass spectrometry on autopsy tissue found **brain median 4,917 µg/g** (IQR 4,026–5,608) of microplastic in 2024 decedents against **3,345 µg/g** in 2016 decedents, a rise of roughly 50% in eight years (P = 0.01), with liver median 433 µg/g and kidney 404 µg/g (*Nature Medicine*, published online 3 February 2025).
- Polyethylene is about **75%** of the measured brain load, and the particles are overwhelmingly nanoscale shard-like fragments **under 200 nm**; brains from dementia decedents carried **26,076 µg/g**, with direction of causation unestablished.
- There is no identified clearance pathway for the sub-200 nm fraction, which is what makes the measurement an accumulation rather than an exposure.
- The blood–brain barrier is transparent to a material first manufactured in 1907, because the exclusion mechanisms it evolved were not selected against synthetic polymer.
- The San Francisco Bay Microplastics Project (SFEI and 5 Gyres, 2017–2021, roughly $1M from the Moore Foundation, Patagonia and the Ocean Protection Council) found Bay water and sediment among the most microplastic-contaminated measured anywhere.
- That project estimates **7 trillion particles a year** delivered to the Bay by urban stormwater — hundreds of times the wastewater contribution — of which **nearly half are tyre wear particles from roads**.
- The generating mechanism is comminution: tyre tread abrades against pavement on every vehicle-mile, stormwater transports the fragments, and the finest fraction is respirable.
- The Municipal Regional Stormwater Permit's trash load reduction schedule and full-capture device requirements are sized to a screen aperture, so the fraction that passes every compliant device is by definition the fraction small enough to reach tissue.
- Atmospheric nuclear testing roughly **doubled atmospheric ¹⁴C** by the 1963–65 peak; the Partial Test Ban Treaty stopped the input, and every carbon atom a body incorporates arrives from that atmosphere via food, so tissue carries the atmospheric curve at the moment it is formed.
- Dental enamel is laid down at a known age and never remodels, giving forensic **year of birth to roughly ±1.5 years**, with a published casework guide (ANZPAA, 2022) and a systematic review of forensic use (*Forensic Science International*, 2025).
- Atmospheric ¹⁴C has now fallen back through pre-bomb levels and is being driven below them by fossil-fuel dilution (the Suess effect), so the dating window is closing and material formed today radiocarbon-dates as though it were centuries old.

## 6. Legacy contamination and remediation

- New Almaden operated from 1845 to 1976 as the largest mercury mine in North America, and its output is in Bay sediment permanently; hydraulic mining in the Sierra added a second inventory delivered downstream.
- Inorganic mercury in sediment is comparatively inert; **methylmercury** — the form that biomagnifies and crosses the placenta — is produced by sulfate-reducing bacteria under anoxic conditions.
- Anoxia is exactly the condition created by re-wetting sediment, so tidal marsh restoration manufactures the bioavailable species out of the legacy stock, and the production scales with restored acreage.
- Methylmercury crosses the placenta and concentrates in the fetal brain, where the neurodevelopmental effect is not recoverable.
- The exposed population is subsistence and cultural anglers and their children — the people least able to substitute the protein — and the administrative instrument aimed at them is the **OEHHA fish consumption advisory**, posted at the pier in several languages and stated in **meals per month**.
- The **San Francisco Bay Mercury TMDL** was adopted by the Water Board in 2004 and 2006, took effect 7 November 2007 and was EPA-approved 12 February 2008; it sets a **fish tissue target of 0.2 ppm** and allocations of 11 kg/yr for municipal wastewater (final 2028), 1.0 kg/yr industrial and 80 kg/yr urban runoff, implemented through NPDES permits reissued on a five-year cycle.
- The TMDL's own implementation chapter identifies tidal marsh and salt pond projects as the most promising avenue for reducing methylmercury over the long run — the same class of project that produces it in the short and medium term.
- The **South Bay Salt Pond Restoration Project** (California State Coastal Conservancy, USFWS, CDFW) is the largest tidal wetland restoration on the US West Coast, and its environmental documents address mercury methylation directly.
- USGS measured waterbird egg mercury concentrations in South Bay in response to restoration (Open-File Report 2014-1189), and there is a peer-reviewed literature on slough evolution and legacy mercury remobilisation induced by wetland restoration in South San Francisco Bay.
- Hunters Point Naval Shipyard served as the Navy's radiological decontamination centre: ships returned from Operation Crossroads were sandblasted there, and radium-226 deck markers, caesium-137 and strontium-90 are in the fill.
- Strontium-90 substitutes for calcium in bone; the exposure pathways at a surface-contaminated site are soil ingestion and inhalation of resuspended dust, in a neighbourhood with residential and studio occupancy.
- Soil sampling during remediation at the shipyard was found to have been falsified by substitution of clean soil for samples from potentially contaminated locations; two site supervisors each served eight months in federal prison in 2018.
- The remediation contractor paid **$40 million** to the United States in 2025 for Superfund Act violations, and on **25 August 2026** a US District Judge ordered a further **$57 million** in the False Claims Act case, of which nearly **$12 million** goes to seven former workers who reported the practice under the Act's qui tam provision.
- The Navy has been retesting since the falsification surfaced in 2013, radioactive material has been found as recently as spring 2026, and completion is estimated at perhaps another decade; the first parcels were transferred to a master developer from 2004 and are occupied.
- The correction is itself an exposure route: retesting means excavation, and excavation means resuspension, over the same blocks, on a schedule published in advance.
- The instruments are the Navy's **Finding of Suitability to Transfer**, the parcel-by-parcel **re-survey work plan**, and the sequence downstream of the FOST — conveyance, escrow, title insurance, entitlement, building permit, occupancy — each layer of which is designed to rely on the layer beneath it.
- The administering bodies are the US Navy BRAC Program Management Office, US EPA Region 9 (NPL listing), the CDPH Radiologic Health Branch and DTSC.
- Radon is the second leading cause of lung cancer in the United States after smoking, with an EPA action level of **4 pCi/L**; the exposure is chronic, domestic, odourless and lifelong, and it has no source facility, no responsible party and no defendant, because it comes out of the ground.
- Most of the setting-a falls in EPA Radon Zone 3, the lowest predicted-potential band, and the California Geological Survey's detailed radon potential mapping is concentrated elsewhere in the state — the hazard's regional intensity is low and its administrative apparatus is correspondingly thin.
- The **sub-slab depressurisation fan** is the standard mitigation for both radon and solvent vapour intrusion, and it is an engineering control with no end date: it must run for as long as the building stands, and its failure mode is silent.

## 7. Carbon in the ground

- Geologic carbon sequestration injects CO₂ in a supercritical phase into deep saline formations, where it is retained by structural trapping, residual trapping, dissolution and eventually mineralisation — four mechanisms operating on four different timescales.
- The EPA **Class VI** Underground Injection Control permit governs it, and carries a default **50-year post-injection site care and monitoring period**, an area of review, a site closure plan and a financial assurance instrument; EPA has issued Class VI permits for carbon sequestration in California.
- The failure mode the fifty-year monitoring period exists to catch is not an explosion: injected CO₂ that migrates upward into an underground source of drinking water forms carbonic acid, lowers pH and **mobilises metals — arsenic and lead among them — out of the aquifer matrix**.
- That is a slow chemical change in water above a plume, in rock nobody can observe directly, monitored under an obligation whose stated duration exceeds the expected life of most corporate entities.
- CO₂ is denser than air and does not disperse from low ground: on **22 February 2020** a CO₂ pipeline ruptured near Satartia, Mississippi, releasing for roughly four hours from 7:06 p.m., with **over 200 people evacuated and at least 45 hospitalised** — respiratory distress, disorientation, loss of consciousness, convulsive shaking and cardiac complications.
- Internal combustion engines require atmospheric oxygen, so **cars stopped running** inside the plume: people could not drive out and responders could not drive in, which is a transport-mode failure specific to this gas.
- The operator's notification to authorities was delayed by over 40 minutes, and PHMSA assessed a **$2.8 million** penalty, at the time the second largest in that agency's history; survivors reported headaches, concentration difficulty, muscle tremors and worsened asthma three years later.
- CO₂ is not an inert asphyxiant: at 7–10% it drives hyperventilation, respiratory acidosis and convulsion, and causes unconsciousness within about a minute, with the highest concentrations at grade where children and the immobile are.
- **SB 905 (2022)** created CARB's Carbon Capture, Removal, Utilization and Storage Program and imposed a **moratorium on CO₂ pipeline transport in California until PHMSA updates its federal regulations**; the federal rule that would satisfy that condition has been withdrawn `[?]`, leaving a state prohibition whose expiry trigger may not occur.
- PHMSA regulates CO₂ pipelines under **49 CFR Part 195**, and AB 2623 (2024) proposed the **State Fire Marshal** as the state standard-setter for CO₂ pipeline safety.
- The setting-a's capture candidates are the Martinez and Rodeo refineries and the region's cement and biomass sources; storage capacity is elsewhere, so the transport leg crosses the Delta and the Central Valley — the region's decarbonisation is discharged as a physical hazard on other ground, in the same pattern as its water supply.
- Catastrophic methane release from destabilising seafloor gas hydrate is not supported by the current synthesis (*Reviews of Geophysics*, 2017): dissociating hydrate methane is largely oxidised within the sediment and water column before reaching the atmosphere, and the oxidation product is CO₂ and local acidification rather than a radiative pulse.
- Peat and organic-soil oxidation in drained Delta islands is the region's largest ongoing terrestrial carbon flux, and rewetting to reverse it re-establishes the anoxia that drives methylation and methanogenesis — the carbon remedy and the mercury mechanism are the same hydrological act.

## 8. Noise, light and the sensory environment

- Transportation noise at night is a measured cardiovascular hazard operating below the threshold of waking: an analysis of **24,886 cardiovascular deaths around Zürich Airport, 2000–2015** found significant associations between aircraft noise **in the two hours preceding death** and mortality from all cardiovascular causes, strongest for ischaemic heart disease, myocardial infarction, heart failure and arrhythmia.
- The population-attributable fraction in that work was about **3% of night-time cardiovascular deaths**, and susceptibility was higher in women, in areas with low background noise, and in buildings constructed before 1970.
- The mechanism is established experimentally rather than only epidemiologically: a single night of aircraft noise produces endothelial dysfunction in healthy humans, partially rescued by vitamin C, implicating reactive oxygen species; animal work shows blood pressure elevation and vascular oxidative stress from exposure during the sleep phase only (*European Heart Journal* 42(8):844, 2021).
- The proximate physiology is sympathetic and HPA-axis activation during sleep, repeated nightly for decades, producing endothelial dysfunction and hypertension — the exposure does not wake the person, so there is no symptom and nothing to report.
- **14 CFR Part 150** governs airport Noise Exposure Maps and Noise Compatibility Programs, and residential sound insulation under it is funded through the Airport Improvement Program and passenger facility charges.
- At SFO the programme is administered through the Aircraft Noise Abatement Office with the **SFO Airport/Community Roundtable** (established 1981), and has acoustically insulated tens of thousands of homes in South San Francisco, San Bruno, Millbrae, Daly City and Pacifica, with quantified interior noise-level reduction verified on completion.
- **AC 150/5000-9B**, *Guidelines for Sound Insulation of Residences Exposed to Aircraft Operations*, is the technical standard, and the **CNEL 65 dB contour** is the eligibility boundary — a modelled line that determines which addresses are in the programme and which are not.
- Participation in a Part 150 sound insulation programme routinely requires the homeowner to **grant an avigation easement over the property, recorded against the deed** — standard practice at such programmes `[?]`; the specific SFO instrument should be verified before use.
- Sealing a residential envelope for acoustic performance removes its infiltration ventilation, which raises indoor CO₂, particulate matter and radon, and — in a stock with almost no mechanical cooling — raises and holds the indoor night-time minimum during heat events.
- The two loads act on the same building element: the envelope tightness that delivers the acoustic benefit is the same parameter that governs the thermal and air-quality penalty, and only the acoustic one is measured on completion.
- IARC classifies shift work involving circadian disruption as **Group 2A, probably carcinogenic to humans**; melatonin suppression by short-wavelength light at night is a documented mechanism, and meta-analyses of artificial light at night and breast cancer find a small positive dose–response.
- Successive systematic reviews of wind-turbine infrasound find no supported health effect at real-world exposure levels; the reproducible finding is that expectation of harm produces symptoms, which makes the phenomenon real and its mechanism psychological rather than acoustic.

## 9. Infrastructure ageing

- Chloride-induced rebar corrosion begins when chloride at the steel surface depassivates the alkaline oxide film; the corrosion product occupies several times the volume of the parent steel, which cracks and spalls the cover, admitting more chloride — the process is autocatalytic and not reversible in place.
- Alkali-silica reaction is a slow internal expansion in which reactive silica in aggregate forms a hygroscopic gel with pore-solution alkalis; the concrete cracks from the inside on a timescale of decades and no repair addresses the reaction.
- The **National Bridge Inventory** records condition ratings for every public bridge on a **24-month inspection cycle**; California has **25,975 bridges**, of which **1,284 (4.9%)** are classified structurally deficient, carrying roughly **21.9 million daily crossings**.
- A condition rating is an ordinal judgement made by an inspector on a schedule, filed to a federal database, and it declines monotonically between rehabilitations — the record of decline is public and the decline itself is unattended until funded.
- ASCE's 2025 Infrastructure Report Card grades California's infrastructure categories and is the standing public synthesis of that condition data.
- Building settlement is measured and filed: 301 Mission Street in San Francisco has settled on the order of 18 inches, and the readings are taken on a schedule and published, so the record of the deficiency is continuously available to everyone including prospective buyers.
- A monitoring obligation on a structure produces a time series whose principal consumer is not the engineer but the market: the same readings that establish safety establish unsaleability.
- Utility de-energisation (Public Safety Power Shutoff) has affected on the order of 738,000 customers in a single event programme, and the **Medical Baseline** allowance is the tariff instrument that identifies and slightly protects households dependent on powered medical equipment — a register of medically vulnerable addresses maintained by a utility for billing purposes.
- Wildfire risk transfer runs through the **FAIR Plan** as insurer of last resort, the Department of Insurance's Sustainable Insurance Strategy, and a statewide assessment-recoupment mechanism — the mechanism by which a localised physical hazard becomes a statewide financial obligation.
- Eucalyptus (*Eucalyptus globulus*) planted across the East Bay hills is a fire-adapted species that sheds volatile-rich litter and regenerates after burning; the 1972 freeze produced a large standing dead fuel load, and the 1991 Tunnel Fire ran through the resulting stand — the hazard is an inherited planting decision, not an event.
