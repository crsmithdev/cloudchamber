# ANNEX — COSMIC AND DEEP TIME

*A catalogue of mechanisms, not of stories. Eight fields of real physical process, each entry carrying the instrument that measures or administers it — a code, a standard, a scale, a licence condition, a compensation formula, a register — because the administering body and its instrument are facts about the mechanism and not decoration on it. Figures are given in the units the source uses; where a phenomenon is contested, the contest is stated.*

*Items marked `[?]` are specifics a fact-check should confirm before they carry weight. Nothing here is ranked and nothing is recommended over anything else.*

## 1. Stellar and solar activity

- The Sun's main-sequence luminosity rises by roughly 1% per 110 Myr, so it stood at about 70% of present output at 4.5 Ga — the faint young Sun problem, which requires a much stronger greenhouse in the Archean to keep liquid water on the surface.
- Rising luminosity drives silicate weathering, which draws down atmospheric CO₂: C₃ photosynthesis fails on the order of 10⁸–10⁹ years out, C₄ persists somewhat longer, and a moist greenhouse in roughly 1–2 Gyr removes the ocean, all of it long before the Sun leaves the main sequence at about 5 Gyr.
- The sunspot cycle runs about 11 years (a 22-year magnetic Hale cycle); Solar Cycle 25 was declared to have reached maximum in October 2024 by the NOAA/NASA/ISES prediction panel `[?]`, and the panel's forecast is itself the operational product that observatories and satellite operators plan against.
- The Carrington event of 1–2 September 1859 remains the reference storm: aurorae seen into the tropics, telegraph operators working on induced current with the batteries disconnected, and a magnetic crochet recorded at Kew — the estimated minimum Dst is around −850 to −900 nT `[?]`.
- On 13 March 1989 a geomagnetic storm collapsed the Hydro-Québec transmission system in about 92 seconds, cutting roughly six million customers for around nine hours — the standing worked example of geomagnetically induced current in a high-latitude, long-line, resistive-bedrock grid.
- The Halloween storms of late October–November 2003 included an X28-class flare that saturated the GOES sensor, forced spacecraft into safe mode, rerouted polar flights and caused a transformer-related outage in Malmö.
- The storm of 10–11 May 2024 was the first NOAA G5 (extreme) since 2003, degraded GPS-guided agricultural equipment across the US Midwest during planting, and increased atmospheric drag enough to move satellites in low Earth orbit.
- Miyake events are single-year spikes in tree-ring ¹⁴C attributed to extreme solar particle events: 774–75 CE (a ~12% jump, about twenty times ordinary cosmic-ray variation, and at least an order of magnitude beyond Carrington in fluence), 993 CE, 664–663 BCE, 5259 BCE, 7176 BCE and about 12,350 BCE, with further candidates at 5628 BCE, 5410 BCE, 1052 CE and 1279 CE awaiting multi-continent confirmation `[?]`.
- NOAA's operational scales convert a storm into a public-facing severity and an expected frequency: G1–G5 for geomagnetic, S1–S5 for solar radiation, R1–R5 for radio blackout, with S1 at about 50 occurrences per solar cycle, S3 about 10, S4 about 3 and S5 fewer than one.
- The NOAA S-scale text states from S2 upward that passengers and crew in high-flying aircraft at high latitudes may be exposed to radiation risk, which is the sentence that connects a solar observation to an aviation operating decision.
- DSCOVR, at the Sun–Earth L1 point about 1.5 million km upstream, measures the solar wind in situ and buys the terrestrial grid roughly 15 to 60 minutes of warning between measurement and arrival; ACE serves as the backup source.
- NERC Reliability Standard TPL-007-4 requires transmission planners to assess their systems against a benchmark geomagnetic disturbance event (a reference peak geoelectric field of 8 V/km, scaled by latitude and earth conductivity) on a 60-month cycle, with corrective action plans where thermal or voltage criteria are not met; FERC approved the standard family beginning with Order No. 830 (2016) and directed the later revisions.
- The Promoting Research and Observations of Space Weather to Improve the Forecasting of Tomorrow (PROSWIFT) Act of 2020, P.L. 116-181, is the statutory basis for the US space-weather architecture, assigning observation, research and forecast roles across NOAA, NASA, NSF and the Department of Defense and requiring a benchmark and user-needs process.
- ICAO's global space weather advisory service has been operational since 7 November 2019: four designated centres — the United States (through NOAA SWPC), the European PECASUS consortium, ACFJ (Australia–Canada–France–Japan) and CRC (China–Russia) — issue MOD and SEV advisories to aviation in a fixed message format for RADIATION, HF COM and GNSS effects.
- The solar energetic particle event of early August 1972 fell between the Apollo 16 and Apollo 17 missions; the dose it would have delivered to a crew in transit or on the lunar surface is the standard worked case for solar-particle shielding requirements.
- Ground level enhancements — solar particle events energetic enough to register on surface neutron monitors — number about 73 since records began in 1942 `[?]`, and the neutron monitor network that detects them is also the calibration source for aircrew dose codes.

## 2. Radiation environment and dose

- The US average annual effective dose is about 6.2 mSv per person (NCRP Report 160), of which roughly half is medical; the natural background component is about 3.1 mSv, radon and thoron dominate it at roughly 2.3 mSv, and the cosmic component at sea level is about 0.33–0.4 mSv/yr against a global average natural background of 2.4 mSv/yr.
- Above about 8 km the atmosphere no longer shields effectively: dose rate aloft runs about 4–6 µSv per hour and scales with altitude and geomagnetic latitude, so a full-time long-haul crew member flying 800–900 block hours a year absorbs about 3–5 mSv/yr, and 6 mSv/yr or more on polar routes at high cruise altitude.
- The FAA Civil Aerospace Medical Institute in Oklahoma City maintains CARI-7 and CARI-7A, which compute galactic cosmic ray dose for any city pair, flight level and date, free on request; commercial dosimetry services sell the same calculation per flight to operators.
- Under EU Directive 2013/59/Euratom, aircrew likely to receive more than 1 mSv/yr are occupationally exposed persons: the operator must assess the dose, keep the record, and organise rosters to reduce exposure, and a crew member who declares a pregnancy must be scheduled so that the dose to the fetus for the remainder of the pregnancy stays under 1 mSv.
- The United States has no equivalent: the FAA publishes advisory guidance and the calculator, and requires no dose assessment, no dose record and no mandatory reassignment on declared pregnancy — so the same person on the same aircraft at the same flight level is a monitored radiation worker or is not, according to the operating certificate.
- The occupational epidemiology of aircrew reports elevated melanoma and non-melanoma skin cancer in pilots and cabin crew, elevated breast cancer in female cabin crew, and roughly a threefold rate of nuclear cataract in pilots relative to non-pilots.
- The Linear No-Threshold model holds that stochastic risk is proportional to dose with no threshold, which makes every dose — including the unavoidable cosmic component of natural background — carry a nonzero probability of a fatal cancer; it is the basis of every radiation protection regulation in force.
- ICRP's stated position is that collective dose in person-sieverts is a tool for optimising protection and is not to be used to calculate numbers of cancer deaths in a population, particularly from small individual doses over long periods — a rule against performing an arithmetic operation the same body's model supports.
- Under the Energy Employees Occupational Illness Compensation Program Act, NIOSH performs dose reconstruction for a former nuclear worker with cancer, frequently from incomplete or missing monitoring records, and the Department of Labor runs the result through NIOSH-IREP to produce a Probability of Causation; at 50% or above — "at least as likely as not" — the claim pays $150,000 plus medical benefits, and below it nothing.
- The programme's methodology resolves uncertainty in the claimant's favour by evaluating the probability of causation at the upper 99th percentile credibility limit, so the number that decides a claim is deliberately the generous end of a distribution.
- The Special Exposure Cohort mechanism designates classes of workers at covered facilities for whom dose cannot be reconstructed with sufficient accuracy, allowing an entire workforce with a listed cancer to be compensated without the arithmetic being run at all.
- The Radiation Exposure Compensation Act does the same work for downwinders, onsite participants and uranium industry workers by a wholly different route — a statutory list of compensable diseases, a statutory list of counties, and a statutory date range — with fixed award tiers; the Act lapsed for new claims in June 2024 and was reauthorised and expanded in 2025 `[?]`.
- Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory are covered facilities under EEOICPA, with claimant populations in Livermore, Tracy and the East Bay; Hunters Point Naval Shipyard in San Francisco is a former radiological facility whose remediation soil data have been subject to re-sampling and independent review by the Navy and EPA `[?]`.
- NASA's spaceflight radiation career limit has since 1995 been a 3% risk of exposure-induced death evaluated at the 95% confidence level, which yields roughly 120 mSv for a 35-year-old woman and roughly 400 mSv for a 55-year-old man; a 2021 revision reviewed by the National Academies proposes a single universal career limit of 600 mSv for all ages and sexes, referenced to a 35-year-old female.
- The Radiation Assessment Detector aboard Mars Science Laboratory measured about 1.8 mSv/day of galactic cosmic ray dose equivalent during cruise, implying roughly 0.66 Sv for a round trip at current propulsion speeds, with about 95% of the accumulated dose from GCR rather than solar particles during a quiet period.
- ISS crew receive on the order of 0.3–0.8 mSv/day depending on altitude and solar conditions, most of it accumulated in passes through the South Atlantic Anomaly, where the inner Van Allen belt dips closest to the surface `[?]`.
- About one muon per square centimetre per minute reaches sea level — tens to hundreds through a standing person every second, one or two through a hand — and roughly 10¹⁴ neutrinos pass through each person per second, of which perhaps one interacts in a body over a lifetime.
- Endogenous oxidative metabolism inflicts on the order of 10⁴–10⁵ DNA lesions per cell per day, against which the contribution of natural background radiation is arithmetically small; the regulatory and compensation apparatus above is built on the model rather than on any measurable individual signal.
- Secondary neutrons from cosmic ray air showers flip bits in semiconductors: JEDEC standard JESD89 governs measurement and reporting of alpha-particle and terrestrial cosmic-ray-induced soft errors, IEC 62396 covers atmospheric radiation effects in avionics electronics, and accelerated qualification is bought as beam time at spallation neutron sources.
- A field study of two large production clusters recorded 4.5 million corrected and 71 uncorrected errors across 2×10¹² MB-hours on ECC-protected server DIMMs, and over 25 million errors in 1.35×10⁸ MB-hours on unprotected mobile-class memory, with no detected correlation to neutron monitor counts.
- The mitigation stack — ECC, chipkill, memory scrubbing, triple modular redundancy, altitude-dependent derating in avionics single-event-effect certification — is designed to absorb the error before it becomes visible, and the audit trail therefore records a correction rather than an event.
- When NHTSA asked the NASA Engineering and Safety Center to investigate reports of unintended acceleration in Toyota vehicles, single-event upset was one of the candidate mechanisms examined; the report could neither demonstrate nor exclude it, which is the characteristic evidentiary position for this class of fault.

## 3. Latency and light-travel

- Neutrinos from a core-collapse supernova leave the collapsing core immediately while the shock takes hours to break out of the envelope: from SN 1987A, Kamiokande-II, IMB and Baksan recorded a burst of about 13 seconds roughly three hours before the star was seen to brighten.
- The SuperNova Early Warning System (SNEWS, now SNEWS 2.0) is a standing international coalition of neutrino detectors whose sole product is a prompt coincidence alert to the astronomical community, with lead time described as minutes, hours or days depending on the nature of the stellar envelope and the amount of obscuration.
- Betelgeuse lies roughly 500–550 light years away, so its present state is unobservable in principle and any core collapse there has either already happened or has not, with nothing that can be done in either case being upstream of the light.
- GW170817 (17 August 2017) delivered a gravitational-wave signal and a short gamma-ray burst about 1.7 seconds apart from a source at roughly 40 Mpc, followed by an optical kilonova located within about eleven hours — the working demonstration that a multi-messenger alert chain can be closed inside a day.
- GRB 221009A on 9 October 2022, from a source at redshift 0.151 (about 2.4 billion light years), was the brightest gamma-ray burst yet recorded, measurably disturbed the ionosphere and induced detectable currents at the ground — an event finished before animals existed on Earth, arriving as a line on a magnetometer trace.
- One-way light time between Earth and Mars ranges from about 3 to 22 minutes, which is the hard floor on any command-and-response loop the Deep Space Network can operate and the reason surface operations are planned as sols of pre-loaded sequences rather than as control.
- Accelerated expansion means the great majority of currently observable galaxies are already beyond the cosmological event horizon and can never be reached or signalled, and on ~10¹¹-year timescales the extragalactic sky empties for any observer inside a bound group.
- DESI, operated from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, released DR2 in March 2025 using nearly 15 million galaxies and quasars and reported evidence for evolving dark energy at roughly 2.8–4.2σ depending on the combination of supernova and CMB datasets — a result that, if it holds, changes the far-future expansion history.
- The acceleration itself was measured in 1998 by two teams, one of them the Supernova Cosmology Project based at Berkeley Lab, so the instrument that established the horizon and the instrument now testing whether it moves are the same regional apparatus.
- Light travel time is the only mechanism in this annex in which the cause is complete before the observation begins, which is why every institution downstream of it is organised around a computed arrival date rather than around prevention.

## 4. Impact and planetary defence

- The George E. Brown, Jr. Near-Earth Object Survey Act (2005) directs NASA to detect, track, catalogue and characterise 90% of near-Earth objects 140 m and larger; the mandate has not been met and NEO Surveyor, an infrared survey telescope, is the instrument intended to close it, with launch no earlier than 2027 `[?]`.
- The Minor Planet Center, operating under IAU auspices at the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory, is the single clearing house that receives and validates all asteroid and comet astrometry and issues designations; JPL's Center for Near-Earth Object Studies computes orbits and impact probabilities from that stream.
- JPL's Sentry system automatically scans the catalogue for possible future impacts and publishes the risk table without human intervention; Sentry-II, in operation since 2021, extends the method to objects strongly perturbed by the Yarkovsky effect and by close planetary encounters.
- The Torino scale (0–10) is a public communication instrument combining impact probability and kinetic energy into a single integer with a colour band; the Palermo technical scale is the specialist instrument, a base-10 logarithm of the ratio of an event's risk to the background risk from objects of the same size over the intervening time, so that a value of 0 means "as risky as the background".
- The International Asteroid Warning Network (IAWN) and the Space Mission Planning Advisory Group (SMPAG) were established under UN COPUOS in 2014; IAWN's notification criteria are keyed to object size and impact probability, with a commonly cited threshold of a 1% or greater probability for an object above about 10 m `[?]`, and SMPAG is the body that would recommend a deflection mission.
- Asteroid 2024 YR4 reached an Earth impact probability of about 3.1% for 22 December 2032 in February 2025 — the highest Torino rating ever assigned, level 3 — before further observation eliminated Earth entirely; the residual lunar impact probability then rose from 3.8% to 4.3% on JWST observations in May 2025, and JWST astrometry taken on 18 and 26 February 2026 ruled out the lunar impact, placing the roughly 60 m object more than 20,000 km from the Moon.
- The rise-then-collapse pattern is structural rather than anomalous: as the uncertainty region shrinks, the Earth occupies a larger fraction of it before being excluded, so a probability that is climbing is usually evidence that the observation campaign is working.
- Apophis passes within about 31,600 km of the Earth's surface on 13 April 2029 — inside geosynchronous orbit and visible to the naked eye from Europe and Africa — with impact excluded for at least the next century; OSIRIS-APEX is retasked to rendezvous with it after the encounter.
- DART struck Dimorphos on 26 September 2022 and shortened its roughly 12-hour orbit around Didymos by 33 minutes, far beyond the 73-second minimum success criterion; subsequent analysis showed the binary system's heliocentric orbital period changed by about 0.15 seconds, the first measurable human alteration of a natural body's orbit around the Sun.
- ESA's Hera mission, launched in October 2024, is the follow-up characterisation flight to Dimorphos, intended to measure the crater and the mass so that the momentum enhancement factor β can be pinned down and the kinetic-impactor technique made predictive rather than demonstrated.
- NASA Ames Research Center's Asteroid Threat Assessment Project maintains the Probabilistic Asteroid Impact Risk (PAIR) model, which propagates an object's size, density, entry angle and velocity distributions through atmospheric breakup, blast, thermal, cratering and tsunami effects and returns expected casualties as the output quantity.
- Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory maintains the simulation work on nuclear deflection and disruption of asteroids, which is the only technique with sufficient energy density for short-warning or large-object cases and the only one requiring a policy decision before it can be tested.
- FEMA and NASA co-host biennial interagency planetary defence tabletop exercises, running a hypothetical object through the warning, verification, deflection-decision and civil-protection chain and publishing the after-action report, including the evacuation questions.
- The Chelyabinsk airburst of 15 February 2013 came from an object of roughly 20 m releasing on the order of 500 kt, and injured about 1,500 people almost entirely through window glass — the standing demonstration that the casualty mechanism for small objects is architectural.
- The Tunguska event of 30 June 1908 flattened roughly 2,150 km² of forest with no crater; the date is now observed as International Asteroid Day.
- The Chicxulub impact at 66 Ma involved a body of roughly 10 km and is marked stratigraphically by a global iridium anomaly and shocked quartz, which is the layer that made impact a testable class of extinction mechanism rather than a speculation.
- Gamma-ray bursts have been proposed as a contributor to the Late Ordovician extinction on the basis of ozone-layer depletion modelling; the hypothesis remains unconfirmed and no burst has been tied to any mass extinction in the record.

## 5. Deep time and the far future

- Milankovitch forcing runs on eccentricity near 100 kyr, obliquity at 41 kyr and precession at roughly 21–23 kyr; the next glacial inception is plausibly delayed by tens of thousands of years by anthropogenic CO₂, which converts an orbital cycle into a variable that a licence condition can be written against.
- Finland's Posiva and Sweden's SKB must model the next glacial cycle in their repository safety cases — permafrost depth, ice-sheet loading, and glacial meltwater chemistry reaching the disposal horizon at about 430 m — over 100,000 years.
- Finland's Radiation and Nuclear Safety Authority (STUK) concluded on 4 August 2026 that there is no obstacle to granting an operating licence for the Onkalo spent-fuel repository at Olkiluoto, on a safety case engineered for a minimum of 100,000 years and assessed to a million, with the requested licence period running to the end of 2070.
- 40 CFR 197.20–197.21 defines the Reasonably Maximally Exposed Individual for the Yucca Mountain repository: a person who lives in the accessible environment directly above the highest concentration of radionuclides in the plume, drinks 2 litres a day of water from wells drilled at that location, and whose diet and living style are set to the mean values of a survey of the residents of the Town of Amargosa Valley, Nevada.
- Her permitted dose is 15 mrem (0.15 mSv) per year for the first 10,000 years and 100 mrem (1 mSv) per year thereafter, throughout the period of geologic stability, which the rule fixes at one million years after disposal; NRC's implementing regulation is 10 CFR Part 63.
- The compliance demonstration is a performance assessment: a probabilistic model that transports radionuclides through rock and groundwater into that defined intake and reports millirem per year at every timestep, re-instantiating the defined person for the whole period.
- The DOE Office of Legacy Management holds long-term custody of closed sites in perpetuity, with a mandate expressed in terms of surveillance, maintenance and records rather than of an end date.
- The Waste Isolation Pilot Plant must demonstrate compliance with 40 CFR 191 for 10,000 years, recertified by EPA every five years, and the dominant release scenario in the analysis is inadvertent future human drilling into the repository.
- The future drilling rate is derived from the previous hundred years of actual drilling in the Delaware Basin — New Mexico Oil Conservation Division and Texas records, about 99% oil and gas — and carried forward: 46.8 boreholes/km² per 10,000 years in the original application, 59.8 at CRA-2009 and 67.3 at CRA-2014.
- Intrusion times are drawn from a Poisson process in three regimes: 0–100 years of active institutional control with no intrusions, 100–700 years in which passive controls are credited with reducing the rate by two orders of magnitude, and 700–10,000 years at the full rate, with the repository discretised into 144 blocks.
- The published result is a distribution rather than a bound: the most likely number of intrusions into a waste panel is 5 at probability 0.1715, the probability of zero intrusions is 0.0041, and the modelled maximum is 14 at probability 0.0011.
- Super-Kamiokande's search for proton decay via p→e⁺π⁰ sets a partial lifetime limit above 2.4×10³⁴ years at 90% confidence with a 0.373 Mton-year exposure, which excludes the simplest grand-unified models; Hyper-Kamiokande is the successor instrument.
- The far-future timeline organised by decade of exponent — stelliferous, degenerate, black hole and dark eras, running past 10¹⁰⁰ years for the evaporation of the largest black holes — is a projection of known physics with proton stability as its principal unknown, and it produces no measurable quantity at any human scale.
- The measured Higgs mass near 125 GeV and the top quark mass place the Standard Model electroweak vacuum in a metastable region: a vacuum decay bubble would expand at essentially the speed of light, giving no interval between cause and arrival anywhere in its future light cone.
- The LHC Safety Assessment Group review (CERN, 2008; arXiv:0806.3414) is a formal published document in which an institution certifies with reasoning, references and sign-off that its instrument will not destroy the Earth or the universe, the central argument being that cosmic rays have delivered equivalent collisions to astronomical bodies for billions of years without effect.
- The Anthropocene was proposed as a formal epoch with Crawford Lake, Ontario as the candidate GSSP and the mid-twentieth-century plutonium fallout peak as its primary marker; the relevant ICS subcommission voted the proposal down in March 2024, leaving the term informal `[?]`.

## 6. Dating and clocks

- Atmospheric nuclear testing between 1955 and 1963 roughly doubled the concentration of ¹⁴C in the atmosphere, and every organism that has eaten since carries the year of tissue formation in its carbon: cells built in 1968 hold 1968's ratio, cells built in 1994 hold 1994's.
- Bomb-pulse dating reads a cell population's birth date directly and has been applied to human neurons, adipocytes, cardiomyocytes and T-cell populations (spleen populations proving older than those in blood, lung and intestine), and to collagen in aneurysm walls.
- Tooth enamel is laid down once in childhood and never remodelled, so bomb-pulse enamel dating returns a year of birth for an unidentified decedent to within a year or two, and the method consumes the tooth.
- Atmospheric ¹⁴C fell below its pre-1955 value for the first time in 2021, so new organic material now carries the signature of a 1955 sample and the direction of the calibration is no longer unambiguous.
- The Suess effect compounds it: fossil carbon is ¹⁴C-dead, so continued emissions dilute the atmosphere further, and on high-emission projections material produced around 2050 will carry the same radiocarbon signature as material from about AD 1050 — ending forensic dating of recent remains and much of art and ivory authentication, with δ¹³C and ¹³⁷Cs the weaker substitutes on offer.
- Lawrence Livermore's Center for Accelerator Mass Spectrometry performs the bomb-pulse assays for medical examiners and coroners, so a setting-a county's unidentified remains are read in Livermore, forty miles from where they are held, at a facility built for weapons work.
- Conventional radiocarbon dating reaches back roughly 50,000 years; the Cambridge half-life is 5,700 ± 30 years while the Libby half-life of 5,568 years is retained by convention in reported ages, and calibration runs through the IntCal consortium's curves, IntCal20 extending to 55,000 cal BP.
- Miyake events supply absolute single-year tie-points that anchor floating dendrochronologies to the calendar: the 993 CE spike dated the Norse timber at L'Anse aux Meadows to exactly AD 1021, and the 774–75 CE spike performs the same function for European and Asian sequences.
- The radiometric clocks that carry deep time each have their own window: ⁴⁰K–⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar for volcanics from thousands to billions of years, U–Pb on zircon for the oldest terrestrial minerals at about 4.4 Ga, U–Th for carbonates to about 500 kyr, cosmogenic ¹⁰Be and ²⁶Al for exposure ages, and optically stimulated luminescence for burial of quartz grains.
- Annually resolved archives — tree rings, ice-core layers, varved lake sediments, speleothem laminae — supply the counted chronologies against which the radiometric ones are calibrated, and the Greenland ice cores carry the volcanic sulphate and ¹⁰Be markers that let the two be aligned.
- Civil time is tied to the Earth's rotation by leap seconds, which keep UTC within 0.9 s of UT1; 27 have been inserted since 1972, all positive, and IERS Bulletin C is the instrument that announces each one about six months ahead.
- Resolution 4 of the 27th CGPM (2022) decides that the maximum permitted value of UT1−UTC will be increased in or before 2035, tasking the CIPM to propose a new value ensuring continuity of UTC for at least a century and to bring an implementation resolution to the 28th CGPM in 2026 — the effective end of the leap second without abolishing the tie outright.
- The Earth's rotation has been running fast enough since about 2020 that a first-ever negative leap second has become a live prospect for the late 2020s, which no operating system, time-distribution protocol or trading venue has ever executed `[?]`.
- The leap-smear technique, in which the extra second is spread as a slow frequency offset over a window of hours, was developed by a large search company in Mountain View and is now the de facto practice of major cloud providers, so civil time as delivered to most machines already differs from UTC by design.
- The second is defined by the caesium-133 ground-state hyperfine transition at exactly 9,192,631,770 Hz; optical lattice clocks now reach fractional uncertainties near 10⁻¹⁸, which is sensitive enough that relativistic redshift makes a clock's elevation a term in its reading, and the redefinition of the second on an optical transition is under active preparation.

## 7. Deep-time markers and messaging

- Sandia's marker studies for WIPP (SAND92-1382 and companion volumes) convened panels of anthropologists, linguists, astronomers, archaeologists and materials scientists, who concluded that no language can be relied upon over 10,000 years and that the message must therefore be carried by the shape of the land itself.
- The design concepts are on the public record and were assessed for durability and for legibility as hostility: Landscape of Thorns, Spike Field, Spikes Bursting Through Grid, Menacing Earthworks (lightning-shaped berms radiating from an open centre, sized to deny any sense of place), Black Hole (a slab of black basalt that absorbs desert heat and reads as uninhabitable), Rubble Landscape and Forbidding Blocks.
- The drafted message text opens: *This place is not a place of honor. No highly esteemed deed is commemorated here. Nothing valued is here.*
- The Human Interference Task Force, convened in 1981 by DOE and the Office of Nuclear Waste Isolation, is the lineage from which the marker work descends, and produced the surviving proposals of the field — including the transmission of warning through an engineered animal whose colour responds to radiation and through a self-perpetuating priesthood — recorded as serious design options for a communication problem with no tested method.
- The OECD Nuclear Energy Agency's Preservation of Records, Knowledge and Memory Across Generations (RK&M) initiative produced the operational instruments now in use: a Key Information File, a set of markers at different scales, deliberate archival redundancy across independent custodians, and the recommendation that memory be treated as a maintained system rather than an object.
- The Voyager Golden Record (1977) is a gold-plated copper phonograph disc carrying 116 images, natural sounds, music from many cultures and greetings in 55 languages, with a cover diagram giving playback speed in units of the hydrogen hyperfine transition, a pulsar map of the Sun's position, and an electroplated uranium-238 source intended to let a finder date it by decay.
- The Pioneer 10 and 11 plaques (1972–73) carry the same hydrogen-transition unit and a map locating the Sun relative to 14 pulsars by their periods, which doubles as a clock: the periods identify the epoch of launch to a finder who can measure their present spin-down.
- The Arecibo message of 1974 was a single 1,679-bit transmission toward globular cluster M13, arranged as a 23×73 raster; it remains the only high-power deliberate transmission sent from that instrument.
- The Long Now Foundation, based in San Francisco, maintains the 10,000 Year Clock under construction in West Texas, designed to tick once a year and chime a unique sequence once a century, and the Rosetta Project, a nickel micro-etched disc carrying parallel texts in over 1,500 languages as a durable linguistic key.
- The Arctic World Archive at Svalbard stores data on photosensitive piqlFilm in a decommissioned mine as an offline, format-independent medium; Memory of Mankind at Hallstatt inscribes submitted records on ceramic tablets stored in a salt mine, on the reasoning that fired ceramic is the longest-lived writing substrate with a demonstrated archaeological record.
- Nuclear semiotics as a field exists because the required communication interval exceeds the demonstrated lifetime of any writing system, and every proposal in it trades legibility against durability: the more resistant the medium, the less it can say.
- The formal geological instrument for marking a boundary in deep time is the Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point — a physical golden spike driven into an outcrop at a defined horizon — which is the only marker system in this section that has actually functioned across geological intervals, because it marks rather than warns.

## 8. Survey and alert infrastructure

- The Vera C. Rubin Observatory began the Legacy Survey of Space and Time on 30 June 2026, imaging with an 8.4 m primary and a 3.2-gigapixel camera built at SLAC, revisiting the sky on a cadence measured in days and issuing on the order of seven to ten million transient alerts a night `[?]`.
- The alert stream is not read by people: it is distributed to community brokers — ALeRCE, ANTARES, AMPEL, Fink, Lasair, Pitt-Google and others — which filter, classify and cross-match it against catalogues, so the first judgment about any transient is made by software.
- The IAU Transient Name Server and the Central Bureau for Astronomical Telegrams are the naming and reporting instruments for transients and their independent confirmation; the Gamma-ray Coordinates Network distributes machine-readable notices and human circulars for high-energy events within seconds.
- Fermi's Gamma-ray Burst Monitor issues automated burst alerts from NASA Goddard, while Fermi-LAT instrument operations run from SLAC in Menlo Park, so a burst detected in orbit is triaged through a setting-a facility before it reaches the community.
- The precursor and companion surveys remain operationally central: the Catalina Sky Survey and Pan-STARRS for near-Earth objects, ATLAS for last-minute impactor warning, and the Zwicky Transient Facility at Palomar for wide-field transients.
- Lick Observatory's robotic imaging telescope on Mount Hamilton (KAIT) ran one of the longest-lived automated supernova searches in the world, the Lick Observatory Supernova Search, on a nightly cadence over a fixed galaxy sample.
- Neutrino astronomy contributes IceCube at the South Pole, Super-Kamiokande in Japan and KM3NeT in the Mediterranean, coordinated for burst detection through the SNEWS coalition, whose alert is issued only on coincidence between independent detectors.
- LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA issue public alerts for gravitational-wave candidates with automated significance and sky-localisation products, followed by human vetting, and the low-latency pipeline exists specifically so that optical follow-up can begin before a kilonova fades.
- The IAA's Declaration of Principles Concerning Activities Following the Detection of Extraterrestrial Intelligence is the post-detection protocol in force; its core requirement is independent verification before announcement, and a revision process has been running through 2026 `[?]`.
- The Rio scale, revised as Rio 2.0, converts a candidate signal into a single-integer significance value combining the credibility of the detection with the consequence of the claim, so that a public statement carries a calibrated qualifier rather than a bare assertion.
- BLC1 is the only exercise of that protocol in earnest: a narrowband candidate from the direction of Proxima Centauri found in Breakthrough Listen data, which took about a year of analysis to establish as terrestrial radio interference, with the negative result published rather than withheld.
- The SETI Institute in Mountain View, the Allen Telescope Array at Hat Creek, and the Berkeley SETI Research Center — which holds the BLC1 dataset — are the standing search apparatus in this region, alongside the Berkeley Space Sciences Laboratory operating heliophysics spacecraft.
- NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center in Boulder is the US operational forecast office, issuing watches, warnings and alerts continuously and feeding the ICAO advisory chain; its products are the ones an airline dispatcher, a grid operator and a satellite operator act on.
- The Sentry risk table, the ICAO SEV radiation advisory, the SNEWS alert, the GCN notice and the broker alert packet are the actual documentary outputs of this infrastructure, each with a fixed format and a defined recipient list, and each is generated without a human decision in the loop.
