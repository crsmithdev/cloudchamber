#### SCP-4548 — The Hateful Star

**Object class:** Keter (Disruption Class Ekhi; Risk Class Critical)

**Summary:** SCP-4548 is a memetic cosmological phenomenon: a sickly green star that appears in the night sky only to people who have already seen it or been told it exists. Containment is therefore information containment — Awareness Control teams work with RAISA to suppress all mention of it, and Foundation operatives embedded in observatories keep optical and radio telescopes from pointing at it. Affected individuals are detained for psychological evaluation, with one chilling procedural line: *"Computerized voices are to be used to communicate with subjects in the later stages of affliction."* Spontaneous awareness correlates with recent bereavement, predisposition to severe anxiety, and schizotypal disorders — the star finds people who are already grieving.

The effect is a graded destruction of the capacity to read other minds charitably. Direct naked-eye observation produces anxiety, paranoia, fixation and physical discomfort, then the core symptom: *"subjects will begin to lose their ability to discern between various human emotions and malice."* Everything a person perceives resolves as hatred directed at them. The progression widens from people to animals, then plants, then objects, then abstract concepts, and finally the subject's own body. Terminal stages are total dissociation of identity accompanied by perceived self-hatred, usually ending in catatonia. Late-stage subjects perceive the star through walls and through blindness, hear it speaking, and suffer nightmares no drug touches. Footnotes note that subjects routinely try to blind themselves with chemicals or sharp implements, and that photographs and video produce only temporary symptoms — it must be the real sky.

The article's substance is five interviews with David Saito, a Canadian man in grief counseling after his sister died in a car accident. Interview One (May 2016): he tries to describe what he saw — *"It was like a star. Only it wasn't right, it didn't glow like other stars"* — calls it "sick" and "hazy," and begins reading the interviewer's neutral questions as accusations. Interview Two (June): he hasn't slept, everyone in the facility despises him, *"every time I look out a goddamn window in this place all I can see is that fucking thing in the sky,"* the guards "would love to just put a bullet in my head," and then the line where the anomaly's real shape becomes visible — *"Even the- the table. You made everything to spite me."*

By Interview Three, human contact is counterproductive and staff switch to text-to-speech; Dr. Cato becomes the synthetic voice "Jane." Saito is relieved to hear a voice that isn't hostile. He reports the star singing to him — *"va va pi da, va va pi da, va va pi da… da pi da"* — with messages inside the song, and describes total ambient malice: *"The floor hates me for stepping on it, the walls hate me for being inside them. The air hates me for breathing it."* He asks Jane whether she hates him too, then reports that the star has confirmed she does. Interview Four is a fragment following severe self-injury: *"Did you see the star?" … "David sees it. It hates David."*

The final interview is the reveal. Something answers that isn't Saito: *"David isn't here anymore, Jane."* David has gone "to the green place." The thing wearing him narrates the self-mutilation in the third person — *"His hair hated him so he pulled it out. His eyes hated him so he tore them loose. His skin hated him so he ripped it off"* — and states its purpose as reminding humanity that hatred is real. It says it has "been up here forever" and is "never going away," identifying itself with the star. It signs off with a scrap of broken aphorism, the syntax of feeling with the feeling removed: *"Love comes from the heart. Happiness is a loaded gun. Sadness is rain on a cloudy day."*

**Themes:**

- Grief as an aperture — the anomaly recruits the recently bereaved  
- Paranoia as an epistemology: once malice is the only available interpretation, everything confirms it  
- Hatred as a cosmic constant rather than a human emotion  
- Language failing as a bridge; the therapeutic interview turned into a vector  
- The self as the final object to be hated

**What makes it work:**

- Pure interview structure. Nothing is described from outside; the reader watches a mind close, one transcript at a time, with weeks of silence between.  
- The text-to-speech move is the article's masterstroke — a containment procedure that is also a plot beat, a mercy, and a betrayal all at once.  
- The clinical Description front-loads the whole arc, so every interview is dramatic irony: you know exactly which stage you're watching.  
- Saito's voice is written as an actual person deteriorating, not as a symptom list; the paranoid re-readings of neutral questions are precisely observed.

**The horror:**

- It is anti-empathy weaponized — the dread mechanism is that you cannot be reassured, because reassurance parses as mockery.  
- It's contagious by *knowing*: reading the article is a joke version of the exposure, and the article knows it.  
- The self-blinding footnote establishes that escape is impossible; the star is perceived through walls, through eyelessness.  
- The final voice is not a monster breaking in but a person hollowed out and reused — annihilation without the dignity of death.

#### SCP-4595 — WITCH

**Object class:** Safe (Disruption Class Dark; Risk Class Warning)

**Summary:** SCP-4595 is a small room inside a woodshed behind an abandoned house near Jasper, Indiana. The word "WITCH" is scrawled in charcoal on the outside of the door. Inside are a wooden bench, a bloodstained shovel, and the skeleton of an adolescent boy. Containment is minimal and absolute: no personnel may enter except authorized testers under the direct supervision of the research lead, and SCP-4595-A must not be disturbed under any circumstances.

SCP-4595-A is a humanoid entity buried roughly one meter beneath the floor. Ground-penetrating radar renders a "vaguely feminine" figure with extensive wounds to the face, chest and neck. Despite every circumstantial indication of considerable age, the body shows no decomposition at all.

Merely entering the room produces escalating effects — first the sensation of being watched, then intense itching, frequently ending in subjects trying to tear their own skin open. Disturbing the buried entity triggers the article's actual anomaly: desert locusts (*Schistocerca gregaria*) spontaneously generate inside the subject, filling every body cavity, the lungs, the airways, the stomach and the intestines, and continue to generate until the subject dies. The locusts vaporize the instant they cross the doorway threshold — the curse does not leave the room.

There is no addendum, no exploration log, no interview. The article ends after the Description with two footnotes. That brevity is deliberate: the entire narrative — a girl accused of witchcraft, beaten and stabbed, buried under a shed floor by someone who left the shovel and, apparently, died in there with her — is left entirely to inference from three props and a word on a door.

**Themes:**

- Historical femicide and witch-panic as buried, still-live violence  
- The land itself holding a grudge; place as perpetrator  
- Refusal of decomposition as refusal of closure  
- Plague imagery (locusts) as biblical retribution turned inward on the body

**What makes it work:**

- Radical compression. Skipping addenda entirely is the craft move — the piece is a single image and trusts the reader to reconstruct the crime.  
- The tableau does all the exposition: bench, shovel, boy's skeleton, "WITCH." Every object implies an actor and an outcome.  
- The itching-to-self-laceration escalation gives the reader a physical rehearsal of the terminal effect before it is stated.  
- Containment procedure as characterization — "do not disturb her" is the Foundation quietly conceding that this is not a machine to be studied.

**The horror:**

- Interior invasion: the body becomes a container for something swarming, and the swarm keeps coming until you stop.  
- The threshold rule makes it a *sentence* rather than an outbreak — targeted, personal, judicial.  
- The unexplained boy's skeleton implies the room has already claimed at least one person, and that nobody knows how long she waited.

#### SCP-4643 — The Wheel of Fire

**Object class:** Neutralized

**Summary:** SCP-4643 was a recorded television interview, sixteen minutes and thirty-six seconds long, broadcast on 16 January 1966 between host Avery Dean and the evangelical minister Reverend William "Willy" Wells. Anomalous activity near its conclusion resulted in the disappearance of the host, the guest, the studio audience, and most of the crew. All surviving recordings are held in a climate-controlled vault at Site-103, with Foundation web monitoring collecting any copies that surface and RAISA technicians verifying them.

The background addendum establishes the setup: the episode was to have featured the actor Jason Robards, but producer Jack Coffman replaced him with Wells, who had just returned from a missionary trip to Borneo — a trip CBS President Michael Dann had publicly condemned over reports of brutality against the Dayak. Coffman booked him anyway.

The transcript is the article. Dean presses; Wells deflects with vagueness, then with scripture, then with escalating contempt, describing the indigenous population as "unclean" and "barbarians" and citing Isaiah 1:16 and 2 Corinthians 6:14–15. He recounts a formative encounter with a mysterious figure in a church who warned him off stealing, which he presents as divine election. Then, unprompted, he confesses to mass murder by immolation: *"God said, that if we gave their bodies to Him like the Kingdom of Israel did when offering the fattened calf, then they… they could be spared the fires of Hell. He promised me this, so we built the pyres high—and the men, the ones who resisted, we put them down first, with the women and children on top."* He calls the screaming "music" and insists his victims are in Heaven.

When Dean tries to end the interview, Wells removes his shirt to reveal a large inverted-cross brand on his chest. Fire erupts from the brand and organizes into a spinning ring of flame that expands across the studio, igniting everything it touches. Wells screams *"I am Elijah\! I am Elijah\! Come, chariots of fire\!"* An audience member shoots him. The flames go out instantly. A tall, gaunt humanoid with pitch-black skin and dark red eyes materializes behind him; Wells says, *"Lord?"* A pit opens beneath him with bright flame coming up out of it, and he falls into the dark screaming. The figure vanishes. The remaining cameras kept rolling for another sixteen minutes before heat damage killed them.

**Themes:**

- Colonial missionary violence and its self-justifying theology  
- The prophet complex: atrocity reframed as mercy, murder as salvation  
- Diabolical patronage disguised as divine calling  
- Live broadcast as a confessional — the sin has to be televised  
- Judgment as the anomaly's actual function

**What makes it work:**

- It is a stage play. One set, two chairs, a transcript — and the entire horror is delivered by an escalating conversation.  
- Wells is allowed to be persuasive first and monstrous second; the theological register makes the confession land as a boast rather than a slip.  
- The reversal is exact: the man who believed he was Elijah calls the being that comes for him "Lord," and it is not God.  
- The instant extinguishing of the flames the moment he is shot — power that was never his, only lent — is a one-line theology of the whole piece.

**The horror:**

- The confession itself, delivered in the cadence of a sermon, is more frightening than the fire.  
- The victims are given no protection by the narrative; the anomaly punishes Wells but does nothing for the dead.  
- Damnation-as-collection: something has been waiting for him since childhood, and the church encounter he read as grace was a contract.  
- Sixteen minutes of cameras filming an empty burning studio — the aftershot with nobody in it.

#### SCP-4654 — angels by dado

**Object class:** Safe (Disruption Class Vlam; Risk Class Warning)

**Summary:** SCP-4654 is an extremely toxic liquid narcotic, supplied in containers labelled "dust of angel," held in a hermetically sealed vault at Site-81. Interaction requires explicit pre-authorization from the Site-81 Head of Bio-Chemical Hazards, and testing is flatly forbidden. Injected or inhaled, it produces "sublime bliss, profound awareness and serenity." Its danger is entirely in what it does on either side of that.

Withdrawal, after four or five doses, is not withdrawal so much as devolution: skin blackens and chars, the body severely dehydrates, bones push through the flesh, the eyes shrink, the jaw and skull distort, the genitals atrophy, the spine curves, and muscle mass and weight increase. Cognitively, language comprehension is lost outright and replaced by "abject horror, shock, paranoia, agitation, and anger" — the user becomes a demon.

Overdose, ten or more doses in thirty minutes, runs the other direction. Webbed wing structures grow from the upper back, limbs elongate, the torso narrows, the genitals enlarge, and the face reorganizes — the eyes migrate toward the center and become four eyes with triangular pupils, while the mouth, nose and external ears vanish entirely. The skin turns translucent and brittle. Subjects lose the ability to walk but gain the ability to levitate. The transformation is invariably fatal: mass approaches zero and the subject gradually disappears. Throughout, they are reported to be in near-constant ecstasy.

Discovery came via JTF Tidas-3 "Nine Lives," investigating anomalous narcotics out of Three Portlands, who found a small community of users in advanced stages — the withdrawal cases, the burned demonic ones, tenderly caring for the overdosing angels. The article's second turn is a set of text messages received by Agent James Cousins from a person identifying as "dado" — a recurring, semi-comic wiki-wide anartist character whose broken English is his signature — begging for the substance's destruction: *"medicine in box is bad. dado did bad. pls destroy."* He offers money, or more medicine, in exchange. Identical letters keep appearing outside Area-4 and Site-81.

The Foundation does not listen. Poor handling exposes the entire JTF and all of Area-4 to SCP-4654 vapor; nearly a quarter of the supply is consumed in mass overdose, and nearly every staff member at Area-4 overdoses. When a Site-51 team arrives a week later, only a handful of advanced cases remain. The distress call from Area-4 is the ending: seventeen minutes of ambience — wet meat dragging, feathers rustling, organic cracking, half-human moaning — and then a single voice saying *"I see heaven,"* before the line goes dead.

**Themes:**

- Addiction rendered as literal theological transformation, with heaven and hell as dose curves  
- Angel and demon as the same substance's two failure modes  
- An anart creator's remorse; the maker who cannot recall what he made  
- Institutional deafness — the Foundation ignores a plea it received in writing, repeatedly  
- Ecstasy as the vehicle of annihilation, not its opposite

**What makes it work:**

- The symmetry of the two clinical descriptions is the whole design: read side by side, they're a medieval altarpiece written as toxicology.  
- dado's texts puncture the register — a childlike, apologetic voice inside a bio-hazard file — and make the tragedy legible.  
- The Foundation's negligence causes the disaster, so the article's containment failure is a moral failure, not bad luck.  
- The seventeen minutes of audio is described rather than transcribed, which is far worse.

**The horror:**

- Transformation the subject *welcomes* — nobody in this article is suffering, which is the point.  
- Bodily un-making with the pain removed: mouths and ears sealing over, mass draining to zero.  
- The demons nursing the angels — a hierarchy of the ruined, tending one another.  
- "I see heaven" as a last transmission from a Foundation site full of former colleagues.

#### SCP-4760 — An Eye for an Eye

**Object class:** Euclid (Disruption Class Keneq; Risk Class Danger)

**Summary:** SCP-4760 sits where it was found on Graham Island, British Columbia; every relocation attempt has failed. SCP-4760-A is an 800-meter-diameter circular array of massive tungsten plates buried 1.2 meters underground, coated in rare metals, carbon, palladium and iridium, with copper tubing running beneath. SCP-4760-B is a hairless humanoid seated on a steel chair at the center, with no eyes, ears, nose or mouth — metal ports where the eye sockets were, cabled to an adjacent television. It breathes, eats nothing, and excretes nothing. Activation cycles occur roughly every 47 days and are almost immediately fatal to anything inside the ring, which violently drains life energy from all organisms within the boundary: unconsciousness in fifteen seconds, then desiccated flesh, brittle bones, seized tissue, and extreme suffering. The Foundation's detection system is ten concentric rings of birdcages — 120 canaries — wired to bells, and its recording system is a D-Class subject seated at the terminal writing on paper, whose remains are incinerated afterward. During each cycle the television displays a scene from somewhere or somewhen, and a featureless figure always appears standing in it.

The machine was built by PoI-1115 Vincent Anderson, commissioned by Obadiah Dark of Marshall, Carter and Dark. The recovered journals are the article's spine. Dark's brief was envy: he claimed the Foundation possessed an "All-Seeing Eye" and wanted one — *"See as they see."* Anderson took delivery of a test subject, a woman from Southeast Asia he calls Kalysto, "free of charge," and gave her ocular implants. Dark, distrusting verbal reports, demanded a screen feed of her vision. The power draw was solved with a "Solar Energizing Disc" — the buried plate array — and by 1965 Kalysto was remote-viewing Anderson's Vancouver lab. Anderson's notes about her are the article's quiet atrocity: he calls her remarkable and potentially brilliant, "crippled" by motherhood, poverty and loss, while describing her body dissolving over years — hair gone, orifices collapsing, the skin around her mouth blistering and fusing shut, her body sustained almost entirely by the array. Dark then demanded retrocognition; Anderson protested that it is "not magic," but the contract said otherwise. In 1968 he began noticing a nude featureless figure standing in the background of the transmitted images and assumed it was Kalysto's ego, a manifestation of her sense of self. In October 1968 "the miracle has occurred": Kalysto convulsed and put on screen the brothel in Vietnam where Anderson bought her, years earlier, with the figure standing in the background. The next entry records dead, disfigured deer found near the array.

The demonstration tape is the climax. Anderson unveils the machine to an audience including a man in a purple bowler and, in the front row, Obadiah Dark. The screen shows two men discussing a "delivery" in Lithuania, "passing it off to Bright," and the Factory — placing the article squarely in the Marshall, Carter & Dark / Dr. Bright / Ulysses Sate continuity. Dark points at the background figure, gets Anderson's "manifestation of her sense of self" explanation, then walks up and grabs SCP-4760-B by the shoulders: *"Ulysses Sate. You can see him, I know you can... Find that moment."* Then he starts screaming orders at her: *"Kill him... Kill him, now. Do it. Kill him, you bitch... kill him you dumb fucking whore."* On screen, something moves. Choking begins. Guards on screen collapse twitching. Birds fall out of the sky over the array. Dark's own security detail drops, their bodies smoking, fluid seeping from cracks with raw red flesh smoking beneath. The scene on the television changes and Dark begins to babble — *"What is this- no, wait, that's- me, no, wait, no, no no no, not like-"* — before his body flashes white and the recording cuts. A perimeter camera later shows one figure dragging itself off the plates onto the grass.

The last section is Anderson, faceless, wearing a crude steel plate with holes cut for eyes and a slit for a mouth, his skin gone and his eyes fully red, talking to Isaac Dillard in a dark room: *"She- she- I did this to her... she felt me, I felt her, and I felt cruelty. Hate, because of- because of me."* He orders the array buried so she cannot draw power, and delivers the article's thesis: *"I thought the figure on the television that appears whenever she looks somewhere was just her consciousness, some kind of anchor. I was wrong, Isaac. It was her hatred, and that dumb motherfucker woke it up."* A Marshall, Carter and Dark receipt follows — $755,000,000 paid in full, "We are considering this matter closed," signed by Skitter Marshall, Winston Carter and Salvador Dark. The final activation log, from 2017, is one line from a D-Class: *"it's just standing out here looking at itself."*

**Themes:**

- Surveillance built on a purchased human being; the Eye as an atrocity of infrastructure  
- Trafficking, and the vocabulary of ownership that lets a scientist write tenderly about property  
- Hatred as an entity with its own volition, incubated over decades  
- Corporate impunity — MC\&D closes the file and pays the invoice  
- The Foundation reproducing the crime: 120 canaries and a rotating D-Class, forever

**What makes it work:**

- The journals let Anderson damn himself in his own affectionate voice; he never once uses the word he should.  
- The background figure is planted early and repeatedly, explained wrongly by the narrator, then reinterpreted in one sentence at the end — a textbook rug-pull.  
- The retrocognition demo is written as an audio-only chaos scene, so the reader assembles the massacre from coughs, cracks, and Dark's escalating panic.  
- The 2017 log is a perfect last beat: after all of it, she has nothing left to look at but herself.

**The horror:**

- A person surgically converted into an appliance, then addressed as one for decades.  
- The death mechanism is drainage — the array pulls life out of everything nearby, which is exactly what Anderson did to her, at speed.  
- The scene where Dark sees his own death arrive on the screen he commissioned.  
- The Foundation's containment is a maintenance schedule for the same machine, feeding it disposable humans on a 47-day rhythm.

#### SCP-4812 — Wrath

**Object class:** Composite. SCP-4812-S: Safe. SCP-4812-E: Euclid. SCP-4812-K: Keter (uncontained). Disruption Class Ekhi; Risk Class Danger.

**Series note:** part of **Project PARAGON**, djkaktus's antediluvian canon about a lost human civilization of 400,000–600,000 years ago and the "demons" its curses produced. The PARAGON demon set includes SCP-2254 (La Hire), SCP-4840-B (Lancelot), SCP-6666 (Hector / the Dread Titania) and SCP-6765 (Ogier). SCP-4812 is the "Three Great Profanities" half of that mythology.

**Summary:** Three entities under one designation. SCP-4812-S is a large amorphous mass in a cave near Alès, France, covered in adhesive appendages and hydrophobic hair, and is a Class-XIII visual hazard: to see it at all is invariably fatal — severe pain, bleeding from skin and orifices, paralysis, asphyxiation, death — and images and recordings carry a weakened version of the effect. Even shed hairs are hazardous to look at. It can be approached safely only in total darkness, so it is handled exclusively by the fully blind MTF Vestus-12 "Northern Star," behind three maximum-security vault doors. SCP-4812-E is a fifteen-meter humanoid of platinum with tungsten traces, held at Site-80 inside molten iron at a constant 1,400°C, serviced by hafnium-carbide drones. Its body sits at 0.0000000031 K — a near-perfect heatsink capable of dropping a 10×10×10 m room from room temperature to near absolute zero in about sixteen seconds. A temperature drop of more than 50°C in the vault is a breach by definition. SCP-4812-K is uncontained: a 180-meter winged insectoid, stag beetle crossed with scorpion, six limbs, pincers, segmented tail, plated in highly reflective silver chitin — and a human-sized male face where the head should be. It cruises the upper stratosphere above 800 km/h, is effectively invisible to radar and IR, and returns fired projectiles with equal kinetic energy.

The Connington Set — sixteen odd coins, Egyptian star-map scrolls, German tomes with coordinates and temperature notations, a broken brass spyglass, a burlap sack of nested sacks around a gelatinous mass that burns the eyes to look at, iron-lensed glasses that glow when the sack is viewed through them, a gold-platinum circlet with a warm glass sphere in one prong, and the ciphered journal of the 18th-century antiquarian Winston J. Connington — supplies the mythology. The legend: the House of Apollyon, the "Sky Kings of Old Europ." King Sarrus VIII crossed the sea and destroyed the Fair Folk, taking their princess captive. On the voyage home she prayed to an old and nameless god for vengeance; her iron chains caught fire, burned the flagship to cinders, and dragged Sarrus VIII into the deep. His successor Sarrus IX buried her alive underground and set four knights — La Hire, Lancelot, Hector, Ogier — as his defense. Connington's cipher names three curses instead: the Vinuvinex, "Profane Restrictor," which deals extreme cold; the Lamenellant, "Profane Adamant," whose hide turns everything aside; and YASH, the "Profane Dark," of whom he writes *"I have seen the face of the Lamenellant, though only for a moment"* but *"I dare not seek out YASH."* Of YASH: *"the eyes of YASH offer no release into death, only the winding roads of torment,"* and *"The light of the sun invites order to the most fundamental nature of this world and the YASH abhors it."* The passage repeats one word: *"Wrath. Wrath. Wrath."*

Containment history: SCP-4812-K was first logged in 1964, mistaken for an American experimental aircraft; Soviet fighters engaged it over the Chukchi Sea, it climbed out of sight, then came back down and destroyed them. Its flight path passes over the containment sites of both \-S and \-E, plus a third redacted location. SCP-4812-E was found beneath a lake at coordinates from the Connington documents, ascended when the site was disturbed, and was contained only after hours of shelling across 8 km of ground, a controlled gas explosion, and an immediate drop of molten slag, before transport to Site-80's DEEPWELL vault. SCP-4812-S's cave was known to Foundation precursor groups as a place from which nobody returned; nobody realized there was anything living in it.

The article's turn is Addendum 4812.3. On 9 June 2002 SCP-4812-K broke off its path to attack a Global Occult Coalition transport in the North Sea and appeared to breach the hull before Foundation carrier groups drove it off. Simultaneously, SCP-4812-E became agitated in its molten vault, requiring a temperature increase; and at the French cave, seismographs recorded a large collapse — SCP-4812-S has since moved upward no less than 70 meters. Correspondence from GOC Commander Daniela Met reveals their own designations: GC-92201 MARS (K), GC-92202 PLUTO (E), and GC-92203 EROS — "small, partially mummified, vaguely human-like," an entity the Foundation didn't know existed, in GOC custody. Met's read: *"It's looking for something... It's the third, and now it's just looking for the boss."* The GOC believes the three are connected and hunting whoever cursed them into existence, and that she's "some sort of woman, somewhere underground" in France. The article closes with an internal Foundation memo: *"We've confirmed what we feared. They don't know it, but they've found her."* Fifty meters of lead and concrete are going on top of the cave; the recipient's job is to find a way to shoot the thing out of the sky. Signed: **\-Lament**.

**Themes:**

- Curse as ecology — three world-ending anomalies as the residue of one atrocity  
- Colonial conquest and reprisal: the Fair Folk exterminated, the princess buried, the vengeance still executing  
- Mythology recovered through junk: coins, spyglasses, sacks and ciphers rather than exposition  
- Cross-organizational blindness — the GOC and Foundation each hold a piece and neither has the picture  
- Convergence as a countdown

**What makes it work:**

- Three separate containment problems written as three separate articles, then revealed to be one organism through a single synchronized incident. That structural reveal is the whole piece.  
- Each entity's containment is a physically specific inversion of its hazard — blind operators, molten iron, an unwinnable air war — which makes the mythology feel engineered rather than decorative.  
- Withholding: SCP-4812-S is described in detail but can literally never be seen, so the "boss" of the article remains a shape on a radar plot.  
- The GOC letters function as an outside narrator who knows things the Foundation doesn't, sharpening the dread without breaking POV.

**The horror:**

- The visual hazard: an object whose mere perception kills, so knowledge and death are the same act.  
- Absolute-zero horror — a body that takes heat out of the world simply by existing.  
- The human face on the 180-meter insect, cruising the stratosphere above commercial air traffic, that nobody can see.  
- Seventy meters of upward movement in one night. Whatever the Foundation is burying, it is coming up to meet the things coming down.

#### SCP-4840 — The Demon Lancelot and the Flying City of Audapaupadopolis

**Object class:** Keter (Disruption Class Ekhi; Risk Class Notice)

**Series note:** the cornerstone of **Project PARAGON**, and one of the wiki's major cosmogony pieces. It sits at the junction of the Mekhanite material (Adam El Asem shattering "the iron god Mekhane — his own father"), the Cain/Abel continuity (SCP-073 and SCP-076), the Serpent's Hand / Library material, SCP-1000, and the demon-knight cycle (SCP-2254, SCP-6666, SCP-6765). Jurisdiction over the anomaly has been transferred to Project PARAGON under Protocol 2513.99.

**Summary:** SCP-4840 is a large, ancient, abandoned city floating roughly 7.8 km above an empty stretch of the Russian Arctic. Being mobile, it cannot be conventionally contained; instead the Foundation staffs it, running its weather systems and propulsion to keep it inside a 120 km² exclusion zone under containment head Alyosha Yanovich and ATF Ru-199. Cloud cover is seeded artificially, on-board cloud generators cover the gaps, local populations are monitored for sightings and witnesses are remanded to Site-210. The architecture predates any known civilization while displaying construction techniques and amenities (gas lamps) that shouldn't exist for it. Inscriptions give the city's name: Audapaupadopolis, "City of the First Father." It once held more than 100,000 people. Four quadrants — blacksmiths, engineers, farmers, artisans — surround a central hub with three temples and a great hall. Beneath the great hall is a sealed stone chamber that radiates such overwhelming guilt and regret that no one can approach it. Until 1933 the city was hidden by some anomalous means that failed.

Two entities remain. SCP-4840-A is an elderly man of uncertain ethnicity with probable Mesopotamian ancestry, whose genome contains precursor markers suggesting non-ancestral or non-terrestrial material. He speaks English, Mandarin, Russian, French and Spanish, steers the city, exhibits limited omniscience about distant events, and is the only being who can enter the sealed chamber — the Old Library. SCP-4840-B is the corpse of a roughly 12.8-meter humanoid, visible only on thermal imaging, with a human torso terminating in six legs and six grotesquely distended arms bearing metal clasps and chains attached to enormous iron flails. It bears markings consistent with SCP-2254. It is dead, and it has not cooled.

The temples are the article's iconography. The Temple of Sunrise: naked children at a treeline guarded by a wolf and a bear; a figure with a flaming sword; a golden man at sunrise; a bronze dragon; the golden man meeting a crimson-robed king; the golden man enthroned above monolithic figures; and on the south wall, FOR NOT THE SEA TO SWALLOW THEM AND THE LONG DARK APPROACH. The Temple of Sunset: maps labelled LANDS OF MEN, the blood king's territories, and THE FAR STRANGE WEST; the golden man on horseback leading legions, flanked by companions with a black sword and a green rod, a silver lance, gathering storm, and small green six-eyed humanoids; a ceiling of constellations that match no sky. The Temple of Night: bright-eyed hair-covered humanoids with ornate scepters; inside, a maze repeating one image — a massive tree in a forest ringed by red lights, human figures hanging from it, a red organ visible through the bark, wooden and stone structures wrapped in vines with blood-red blooms — alongside mass graves, burned children and animals, buried coffins, skeletons in trees, and green-skinned people being slaughtered. A staircase descends beneath it; one exploratory team reported finding a room bearing the Foundation seal that led to an abandoned Site-00-00-00, and no subsequent team could find it again.

The history comes from an interview conducted by Dr. Val Ostrovich in 1949, and it is the article. SCP-4840-A begins before the world: two absolute truths, **IS** and **IS NOT** — not beings, states. When IS finished its work its remnant became the Serpent, and IS NOT is the void that encompasses IS; together they are total knowledge. "The strange folk in the west" were made from IS's last fragments, closest to creation's will, and "sang the first songs." Then came SCP-4840-A and his brothers, "the first men to see with their own eyes the world they had been born into," raised by animal mothers. Then their father: **Asem**, golden and beautiful and impossibly powerful, "IS given form," who raised mountains, commanded seas, carried a lance that could kill gods and could see past the heavens into other worlds. But envy grew in him. He stole a crown from another world, named himself King of All That IS, and built his seat at Audapaupadopolis.

Then the betrayals. The middle son raised armies for the crown, lost, and was locked in a stone tomb deep beneath the earth. The eldest son turned kingdoms with rhetoric and was cursed to poison the ground he walked and exiled "to wander the desolate places until the sun went down in the east." The youngest stole the crown while Asem slept — "the greatest betrayal, for the father loved no living thing more than his youngest" — and fled into the dark with his followers, hunted for a thousand years. When he came back into daylight the world had healed; in "Old Europ — the first true kingdom of mortal men" he met a kindly, curious man named Harrian, crowned him with the iron crown, and named him **Apollyon**, King Over The Darkness. Apollyon's descendants became the Sky Kings. Wars followed — the Daeva, the Mad King Moros, the House of Apollyon's pillaging, an older race in the western forests fighting with weapons of tree and root, leaving ruins nobody can explain (SCP-4008). When King Xorus Apollyon raised armies to take Asem's lost lance from the city, SCP-4840-A — unable to give what had been lost in the dark age — summoned the last titan, the dragon Bharata, and had the city lifted permanently into the sky, away from men and away from Eden's garden.

He then watched the dynasty run out: Gilgamesh killing Sarrus II's only son; Ultan El Mehtoh raining fire from the heavens; and finally Sarrus VIII crossing the sea to destroy the last of the strange folk, and his son Sarrus IX burying their princess — which produced the three great profanities, Restrictor, Adamant and Dark (the entities of SCP-4812). The curse also took Sarrus IX's four knights and turned them into monsters: La Hire went west, Ogier east, Hector south, and **Lancelot** north, to Audapaupadopolis. The Demon Lancelot "would have turned this city into a ruin," but the dragon Bharata, the sea lord Arkthurus, the hero Beowulf, and Relivine, last king of the Daeva, met him. The battle lasted weeks and killed all of them; with his last breath Bharata tore out Lancelot's fetid heart and threw the corpse across the Temple of Sunset, where it still lies, still warm. *"With Bharata gone, I was alone."*

The Old Library holds two books. The First Book "does not just describe IS, it IS" — the most fundamental truths, never meant for man or beast. He tried to read it and nearly lost himself "in the Other Book… and in it I grew old"; the Other Book holds "truths of things that cannot be. Dark truths. Old truths." His sorcery keeps the library shut, but "a day will come when it finally fails." Then the identification: *"I am Seth, brother to Cain and Able, son of Adam El Asem, the First Man."* And the article's thesis, in his own voice: *"I was a boy when I gazed into the night sky of a young universe and asked my father for a star all my own. He reached out and pulled for me an iron crown, and that crown seeded the foulness that lingers in the hearts of men."*

A later interview with O5-1 fills in the family. Asem walked the young Earth alone for a lifetime and met Lilith, "the mother of nations," in the garden's twilight; she bore his firstborn sons and daughters and left for the weird places of the world. Seth's mother was Eve, made from Asem's own rib, weakened fatally by her own creation — he never really knew her. Asem was quiet afterward: "When he laughed it was a peal of bright thunder, and when he raged it was a hurricane." He raged three times. He shattered the iron god Mekhane — his own father — for trying to usurp him. He raged at his sons' defiance. And he raged at Seth's theft. And there is Cain and Abel, retold as a battlefield: Abel offered peace, Cain killed him with a stone "in madness," then wept and broke his own body over him and declared himself sole heir. Asem broke Cain's body, tore off the arms he had used and fed them to the wolves, and cursed his soul to walk the world forever, poisoning the ground, cursed with a perfect memory so he would never forget the fear in Abel's eyes. Then Asem brought Abel back from beyond death — and something had gone wrong in the interval: *"His spirit had been separated from his body, and when they were reunited a truly terrible hatred filled the gaps between them."* Asem put Abel in a stone crypt where he could hate forever.

The article ends by turning outward. O5-1 tells SCP-4840-A: *"We know where your father is... We have found him all the same."* He is afraid of a man — a former friend — who also knows where Asem is, and whose intentions are unknown. He asks Seth for help, and promises a future conversation "with the four of us" about what to do with what they found. Seth: *"Your brothers, Seth. You've all been alone for too long, and we're going to need all of you."*

**Themes:**

- Cosmogony as family history: creation, empire and apocalypse all reduced to one household's grief  
- Envy and inheritance — a stolen crown as the origin of every subsequent evil  
- Fatherhood as catastrophe; Asem's love and his rage are the same weather system  
- Deep time and loneliness — an immortal narrator who has outlived every friend, every enemy, and his own civilization  
- Myth as sediment: Gilgamesh, Beowulf, Lilith, Arthurian knights and Genesis compressed into one continuous record  
- Knowledge that must stay sealed (the two Books, the guilt-chamber, the vanishing Site-00-00-00)

**What makes it work:**

- The whole article is one old man talking, and it earns that by front-loading a rigorous, physical containment file first — 7.8 km, cloud generators, thermal imaging — so the mythology lands on solid procedural ground.  
- The temple murals are pure show-don't-tell: the reader decodes the entire cosmology from paintings before Seth confirms any of it, which makes the interview feel like verification rather than exposition.  
- Seth's voice — weary, precise, guilty — carries thousands of years without ever sounding like a lore dump, because every event is narrated as something that happened to his family.  
- The Cain and Abel retelling recontextualizes two of the wiki's oldest and most familiar articles (SCP-073, SCP-076) in a single paragraph, and gives 076's rage an origin that is genuinely tragic.  
- The corpse of Lancelot is left lying in the ruined temple for the entire article, warm, unexplained, before the story arrives at it — a Chekhov's gun the size of a building.  
- The ending is a hook, not a conclusion, but it's an emotional one: the loneliest character in the piece is told his brothers are being gathered.

**The horror:**

- Cosmic loneliness — 130 centuries of being the last one left in a city built for a hundred thousand.  
- The sealed chamber that broadcasts guilt: an emotion as a physical barrier, and the implication of what is behind it.  
- Resurrection as damage. Abel comes back wrong, filled with hatred grown in the gap between body and spirit, and is walled up alive forever.  
- Asem's punishment of Cain — the arms fed to wolves, the perfect memory — is a father's cruelty scaled to divinity.  
- Every monster in Project PARAGON is downstream of a boy asking his father for a star.

#### SCP-4864 — Depravity and Indifference

**Object class:** Safe

**Summary:** SCP-4864 is a bronze medallion 5 cm across, inlaid with a yin-yang, held in a secure locker at Site-17. A switch drives a pointed barb out of either face. When human blood is drawn onto the surface and mixed with a specific reagent, the medallion produces SCP-4864-B — a functionally identical physical copy of the donor (SCP-4864-A). The copies have no internal organs, only packed sinew and viscera around a multicolored glass orb suspended in the chest by white tendons; the orb's etched symbol is repeated in place of the left iris, and an inverted cross is tattooed on the right side of the neck. They generate no heat. Destroying the sphere kills them. Each instance embodies one of the seven cardinal sins or seven virtues, keyed to a reagent — lotus for Chastity, salt for Gluttony, sulfur for Wrath, amaryllis for Pride, white rose for Humility, and so on. Crucially, when an instance is manifested, the original loses the capacity for that trait: split off Lust and the original can no longer feel sexual attraction; split off Humility and the original becomes insufferable. All instances share knowledge, and all of them are largely indifferent to each other and to the people they came from. Testing is now forbidden outright under Ethics Committee Moratorium 4864.1, Chairman J. Cimmerian's statement noting that the data already exists and further testing is only suffering.

The object was made in 1998 by the anartist "The Incredibly Ivory" as the "Waypath to Higher Self-Understanding" for a Three Portlands collector, passed through the Scholars of Calculated Thought and Practice, and was bought by Marshall, Carter and Dark in 2006\. The test logs establish the tone: a Patience instance sits placidly while its original works itself into a rage; a Pride instance strokes its own hair and says, *"Somehow this little twerp thinks they are unique. They are nothing."* Test \#0006 is where the article's argument begins — a subject is split into Lust and Chastity simultaneously, and the Lust instance rapes the original while Chastity watches without intervening. The original reports feeling "unbothered but uncertain." In interview, Chastity describes the disgust of watching, the wish to "look away. Pretend it isn't happening," and explains the failure to act: *"I don't feel strongly enough to intervene, I suppose."* Lust is more articulate: *"Carnal desire is the only desire… Yes, doctor. I can rape. So can you, and so can all of your friends. All of you are capable of rape. But you have restrictions on your impulses, yes? This is all I am."*

Addendum 4864.3 is where the article turns from thought experiment to atrocity. Intercepted MC\&D correspondence reveals the buyer: the Workshop, a human trafficking operation run by John McCredie, which purchased SCP-4864 to "adjust" its victims by stripping out their Chastity. Women and children from developing countries were processed en masse. Hundreds of Chastity instances were sealed in wooden boxes, some half-buried in the ground. The originals, incapable of any sexual restraint, were trafficked. In 2007 the Workshop began burning its storehouses and executing subjects.

The raid follows: 5 October 2007, MTF Faulkner-23, 28 operatives against roughly 62 mercenaries and 4 Workshop personnel near Danai, Myanmar. One Foundation death, five injuries; 58 mercenaries and all Workshop operatives killed. Recovery extracted 340+ SCP-4864-A subjects and several thousand SCP-4864-B instances. At least 26 subjects killed themselves before transfer. Regional Psychology Director Dr. Patricia Woo's report is the emotional core: the boxed Chastity instances, sealed in the dark for months, had begun singing continuously and eventually lost the ability to speak at all; Wrath instances had been made to fight each other, tearing through skin to smash rival cores. Of the originals — "as young as six or seven and as old as thirty-five" — she writes that they are "insatiable, trained and psychologically mutilated to desire nothing more than sexual satisfaction," and explains that the Workshop separated the instances from their originators deliberately, because prolonged proximity created a psychological incompatibility that put the originals into constant seizures as their bodies reached for something that had been removed. Woo closes the door on the reader: *"If you need that despair described to you, either out of ignorance or some sick satisfaction you desire, you will not find it here."*

The coda is smaller and quieter. Dr. Janice St. John, given negative feedback by her supervisor about her emotional reactions to colleagues' successes and told to seek therapy, instead researched emotional suppression and turned to religious texts. On 3 March 2009 she took SCP-4864 from storage and used it fourteen times in her quarters — every sin, every virtue, all at once. Her own Diligence instance called security. Asked by Director Adam Sascher whether she was satisfied, she began repeating fragments — "happy," "satisfied" — her eyes twitching rapidly, made animal noises, and collapsed into an hour of seizures before dying of what was recorded as severe nervous and cardiovascular trauma. As of publication, none of the fourteen St. John instances have demanifested.

**Themes:**

- Personality as removable machinery, and personhood as what's left when you remove it  
- Trafficking and the industrialization of an anomaly for atrocity  
- Indifference as the article's true subject — Chastity's refusal to intervene is worse than Lust's assault  
- Institutional conscience arriving too late; the Ethics Committee bans testing after all the data is in  
- The banality of self-improvement: a stressed middle manager reaching for the same object that destroyed 340 people

**What makes it work:**

- The reagent table is a genuinely elegant piece of design — it makes the medallion feel like a real anart object with rules, not a metaphor.  
- The article escalates by category, not by volume: an irritating test, then a horrifying test, then an industry, then a single lonely suicide. Each shift changes what the object *means*.  
- Dr. Woo's refusal to describe the victims' despair is a deliberate withholding that does more than any description would, and simultaneously indicts the reader for having read this far.  
- Lust's speech is the article's argument in miniature — the instances aren't demons, they're us with the governors removed.  
- The St. John ending inverts the scale: after thousands of victims, the last death is one woman who just wanted to stop feeling envious.

**The horror:**

- Consent is impossible: the mutilated originals still "want" what they were built to want, and their own desire is the cage.  
- The singing boxes — hundreds of copies of the same stolen virtue, sealed in the dark until they lose language.  
- Being assaulted and feeling "unbothered but uncertain" about it, because the part that would object was taken out and put in the room next door.  
- Fourteen copies of a dead woman, still walking around a Foundation site, indifferent, permanent.

#### SCP-4935 — Hereafter

**Object class:** Euclid (Disruption Class Vlam; Risk Class Caution)

**Summary:** SCP-4935-α is a temporal anomaly in the Sankuru Nature Preserve in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, its access point sealed and guarded except for authorized research approved by the Site-77 Director of Containment. Approaching it produces visible light-shift effects — distant light red-shifts, the anomaly blue-shifts to outside observers, objects entering blue-shift toward the center and vanish. On the other side is Earth, roughly 130,000 years in the future, confirmed by stellar analysis. Atmospheric oxygen is far higher; the flora exceeds 200 meters.

Two intelligences remain. The **Akot** are pseudo-humanoid — elongated skulls, enormous deep-set eyes, minimal facial features, lean bodies — and their name for themselves means "gravedigger." They live in underground vaults beneath dark forest and describe themselves as the protectors and guardians of a structure called the Hereafter. They suffer a degenerative genetic disorder that steadily destroys their bodies, and they exist simultaneously in two spatial dimensions, held in this one only by machinery. The second dimension, "the High Horror," is strict cultural taboo. The **Hereafter** is a massive levitating black cube of silica and carbon with organic trace compounds: a suspended-animation vault holding approximately ninety-three billion people who chose perpetual sleep over endless suffering, waiting for someone to restore mortality.

The history, delivered through Dr. Monakker's memo, is an ΩK-Class scenario in reverse: at some point contemporary to our own era, humanity lost the biological capacity to die. Population exploded, resources collapsed, and the wars that followed were unwinnable because combatants could not be killed — blowing someone apart merely "scatter\[ed\] their consciousness into some sort of disorganized hell." The Forerunners built vast cities, now ruins; some left for the stars, since immortality made the trip trivial. The first Akot were engineered during the search for a way to die, and were excluded from the Hereafter for being fragile and socially unwanted — assigned instead to eternal custodianship. Monakker notes the interpretive gulf: their language is "based in this deep-seated mysticism that is meaningless to us when translated," and most of the interpreters available were only a few hundred or a few thousand years old, while the eldest Akot are past twenty thousand.

**SCP-4935-β**, the "Corpse-Father," is a biological scorpioid roughly 7.9 kilometers long, of which nearly 6 km is tail, currently embedded in the exterior of the Hereafter and working its way in. It eats living things and incorporates their faces and consciousnesses into its own body; the Akot say it has "a thousand eyes." Dr. Tanner's addendum records that it came out of the sea, that 25,000 Akot once marched to meet it hoping to be consumed and thereby released — and found that the incorporated faces were screaming. There is no death inside it either. Their most advanced weapons did nothing.

Exploration EX.4935.03 sends MTF Epsilon-45 "Base Jumpers" into the machinery beneath the Hereafter, where they find Housinn, an Akot partially fused into the walls after six thousand years: *"I want to die. I want to die. Why can't I die? Why has this simple mercy eluded us?"* He dates the Corpse-Father's arrival to six thousand years ago rather than the official six hundred — the first sign the Akot have been lying. Deeper in, a chamber holds a hundreds-of-meters pillar of coiled steel capped by a pulsing blue ring, and a holographic projection identifying itself as **Gerryon**, "engineer of the forsaken peoples," which explains its own function: *"This machine is the culmination of our efforts; a device that, when activated, will rend our souls from our bodies and sever the threads that can be said to be keeping us alive."* Then the exchange that the entire article is built around:

> **Murphy:** "When was this device activated?" **Projection:** "The device has not been activated." **Murphy:** "How does this device determine if it was activated?" **Projection:** "There are still living human beings on this planet. Due to this, it is impossible that the device has been activated."

The projection also begins to say that "The SCP Foundation had collected a massive archive of—" before the information is expunged. Dr. Flavius's report fills in the Akot's own origin: their ancestors reasoned that separating soul from body would let them die, and the machine they built instead tore them out of this dimension into the High Horror, leaving them permanently suspended between realities in unending torment, propped up by technology whose resources are running out. Flavius' conclusion is blunt — a time is coming, soon by their standards, when there won't be enough healthy Akot left to run the machines, and every undying Akot body will fall into the High Horror. He also notes that the Akot developed the temporal anomaly precisely because they knew this, and that he does not believe their stated motives.

He is right. The time-dilation logs show the differential widening dangerously, and Addendum 4935.6 delivers the reveal: the anomaly is not a research project or a plea for help — it is "an open valve." The Akot Archivist, addressing Dr. Regal as **forefather**, explains that they cannot escape the Corpse-Father and cannot abandon the Sleepers, and that time only brings them closer to final defeat. Their answer is to activate Gerryon's Ark and stop time itself: *"Whatever takes place outside of our collapsed state will mean nothing to us."* And the Ark is not aimed at their own era. *"The Ark is not for us, forefather. The Ark is for you. You have not yet been touched by the long dread finger of a life everlasting."* They intend to reach back through the valve and collapse the present-day timeline — *"We will collapse your timeline, and you will not have to suffer. We will not be born into suffering."* It is genocide framed, sincerely, as mercy: prevent immortality by preventing us. And Gerryon's own logic makes the stakes exact — the device knows it has never been fired, because if it ever had been, there would be no living humans anywhere on Earth in any era.

The Ethics Committee votes 7–2 that destroying SCP-4935 is ethically allowable given hostile intent toward Foundation personnel and civilians. Foundation engineers deploy Scranton-Lang Energized Nullifiers and collapse the anomaly in early 2019\. Several Akot, visible on the far side, try to cross as the aperture shrinks and are crushed as it closes. The anomaly is exhausted and never reforms; time dilation returns to baseline; the file remains Euclid pending confirmation that it cannot re-emerge. Ninety-three billion sleepers, a dying custodial species that cannot die, and a seven-kilometer thing chewing its way into the vault are all sealed on the other side of a door the Foundation welded shut.

**Themes:**

- Immortality as the actual apocalypse — the ΩK-Class scenario is that nobody can stop  
- Death reframed as a mercy, a right, and eventually a lost technology  
- Custodianship as punishment: the Akot were excluded from paradise and made its janitors, forever  
- Suicide at civilizational scale, and the ethics of preventing suffering by preventing existence  
- Descendants judging ancestors — "forefather" is both an address and an accusation  
- The Foundation's containment as an act of abandonment; the file closes and everyone on the far side is left there

**What makes it work:**

- The Gerryon exchange is one of the finest single beats djkaktus has written: a machine proving it has never fired by pointing out that you are alive to ask. It delivers scale, stakes and dread in four lines of dialogue.  
- The reveal is seeded honestly — Housinn's "six thousand years" contradicts the official account long before the Foundation catches it, so the betrayal is discoverable on a first read.  
- Ninety-three billion is a number the article never dramatizes; it just states it and moves on, which is exactly why it lands.  
- Two horror registers run in parallel and never merge: the metaphysical horror (the High Horror, the un-death) and the enormous physical one (the Corpse-Father), so neither becomes the article's whole answer.  
- The Akot are not villains. Their plan is monstrous and their reasoning is unanswerable, and the article refuses to resolve that tension — the Ethics Committee vote is 7–2, not unanimous.  
- The ending is deliberately unsatisfying in the right way: the Foundation wins by slamming a door, which solves nothing for anyone on the other side.

**The horror:**

- Bodies that cannot die being torn apart anyway, consciousness "scattered into some sort of disorganized hell" — annihilation without the exit.  
- The High Horror: an entire species knowing exactly what is waiting when the machines fail, and being culturally forbidden to discuss it.  
- Being eaten by the Corpse-Father and finding out that even that is not release — 25,000 volunteers, and the faces on it are screaming.  
- Housinn, fused into a wall for six thousand years, still conscious, still asking.  
- The realization that our present is the front line of a war we don't know we're in, and that the enemy is our own descendants trying to euthanize us out of love.  
- The last image: hands reaching through a closing aperture, and the crushing.

#### SCP-4941 — Forever Yours

**Object class:** Euclid (Disruption Class Keneq; Risk Class Notice)

**Summary:** SCP-4941 is an incorporeal female humpback whale that manifests only at sunset on the night of a full moon, near the easternmost cliff point — about 24 meters high — of Long Down Island off the English coast. She hangs in the air there until sunrise. Containment is essentially a bedside vigil: one containment team member meets her at Point Bravo during each activation event and documents her behavior; on-site security maintains a 1 km exclusion zone and apprehends unauthorized vessels. If she becomes distressed, the assigned officer is instructed to reassure her that *"Charlie Mason will be back soon"* and to wind the crystal music box recovered from the island. Left alone, she produces low-frequency calls loud enough to be heard on the mainland. With the music box playing, she sings along for the length of the song.

Discovery came in 1995, when a Foundation patrol happened onto an activation event. A survey of the island found an abandoned one-room structure — chair, table, bed, books — with letters and the crystal music box beneath the bed. Most of the correspondence was weather-ruined; the intact letters had been hidden inside the floorboards.

Four surviving letters are signed Charlie, written from the Western Front. The first is straightforward longing: *"Not a day passes that I don't think of you… the smell of your hair, the softness of your hands, the sound of the sea behind us and I'm home again."* The second is dependence: *"Your words are air in my chest, I read them and then read them again just afterwards… The guns have taken much from me, but the music of your voice will be the last thing it takes."* The third is fear of what the war is making of him: *"I hope I'm still the man I was when I left you. I hope the part of me you loved isn't gone, broken by the shells and the smoke and the mud."* The fourth is coming apart: *"How can I know, when I see nothing but mud and blood and wire?… That would be enough"* — and a wish to watch the whales dancing in the moonlight again.

The last document is a letter that was never sent, found tucked between books and addressed simply "To Charlie," written by Aoife much later. She has aged and he has not come home: *"The sea and sun have passed me over and the girl you knew has gone with them… There is little of that girl left in me now. Only the part that loves you remains."* She writes that she intends to go back to the cliff where he first found her, and that she will wait: *"I'll wait for you, even if it takes a thousand lifetimes. I know you'll come home."* It ends *"I love you, Charlie. Come back to me."*

Enclosed is a photograph of Charlie Mason, British Fourth Army, killed on the first day of the Battle of the Somme in July 1916 and presumably buried in a mass grave in northern France. The whale on the cliff is Aoife, keeping her word — and the strong implication of "the cliff where he found me" is that she was never entirely human to begin with, a selkie-shaped grief that has outlasted everyone who could have explained it. The Foundation's containment procedure amounts to a stranger showing up once a month for the rest of time to lie to a grieving woman and play her a song.

**Themes:**

- Grief that outlives every party to it, including the griever's own species  
- A promise kept past all reason as both devotion and curse  
- The First World War as an eraser of ordinary futures — one name among a day's 57,000 casualties  
- Folklore (the seal-wife, the sea-bride) told through Foundation paperwork  
- Institutional tenderness: containment as care rather than restraint

**What makes it work:**

- The reveal is entirely structural. Nothing is explained; the whale, the music box, the cliff and the letters are placed in order and the reader does the assembly.  
- The containment procedure is written before the reader understands it, so on a re-read the instruction to say "Charlie Mason will be back soon" changes from procedural to devastating.  
- Charlie's letters degrade across four documents — the syntax loosens, the imagery narrows to mud and wire — dramatizing his destruction without ever leaving the epistolary form.  
- The single hard fact at the end (Fourth Army, first day of the Somme, mass grave) is deployed as the only piece of clinical Foundation language in the emotional section, and it lands like a hammer.  
- Restraint: the article is short, has no monster, and never says the word "selkie."

**The horror:**

- Not fear but ache — the dread mechanism is duration. She will be on that cliff on every full moon indefinitely.  
- The Foundation has chosen perpetual deception as policy; every officer who takes that shift becomes complicit in it.  
- The letters were hidden in the floorboards, which means she hid them, which means she left, which means she came back as something else.

#### SCP-4971 — Rituals

**Object class:** Esoteric — **Cernnunos-Class** (Disruption Class Ekhi; Risk Class Danger)

**Summary:** SCP-4971 is a space-time anomaly inside the abandoned SouthWood Park Mall in Havensbrook, Indiana. Anyone who leaves the main concourse eventually enters it. Inside is a vast forest of flora resembling Yukon and Pacific Northwest species that contain no DNA whatsoever, under a sun that sets after twelve hours and returns after thirteen. The mall is sealed and barricaded, with Armored Command Battalion Saito-9 encamped outside under Commander Lana Grey; anything that emerges without authorization is to be contained or destroyed, and if SCP-4971-▽ reaches the entrance, all available force is to be committed — *"the entity cannot be allowed under any circumstances to breach the entrance."* The classification is the article's structural joke and its thesis: the Cernnunos-Class designation, created here by the Classification Committee, means viable containment procedures exist but are ethically unacceptable. In this case the procedure requires the ritual sacrifice of every living human being on Earth.

The backstory: the mall ran 1985–2006, was abandoned, and on 12 February 2007 police responded to break-ins and screaming, got lost inside, and lost officers before Site-81 arrived. Dr. William Decker's memos identify Katarina Randolph, 19, missing from Maine, recruited into the **Daughters of Eden**, an eco-terrorist pseudo-occultist group. Member Anna Christian had obtained the *Last Appeal of Biphi*, a manuscript recording the torture-death of a witch named Biphi and preserving the incantations she used to call up nature spirits and drive out colonial invaders. Of six known copies, three were burned by Puritans, one was lost at sea in the 1870s, one was destroyed by Oswald Carter, and one was recovered from Hitler's bedside table and eventually burned at Miskatonic. The *Appeal* holds minor workings — weather, decay — building to the summoning of "The One Who Knows Silence In The Earth," the entity Biphi called during her exorcism. The Foundation holds the manuscript's second half: the inverse-incantation that seals the gate. It requires human sacrifice on a logarithmic curve — "a life every hour, then double every hour after" — with an upper limit of "every human heart that has not yet known silence." The Ethics Committee votes unanimously against it. The Daughters' recovered manifesto shows what they thought they were doing: summoning "GAIA's champion" to wash the world clean of industrial sin and visit "righteous torment" on those who wounded nature.

MTF Epsilon-13 goes in first, rappelling down a cliff into the forest, and finds the blood-filled summoning circle, the *Appeal*, and a makeshift altar with a machete and sponges. They meet humanoid entities with glowing white sigils where their heads should be; shooting the sigil drops them, and then they get back up. Dr. Amon Anders's occult analysis measures the anomaly at over 400,000 m³ — the largest documented — sustained by an external power source, presumably the entity itself. The "voxen eye" symbol (circles and triangles) appears in pre-Roman druidic texts and Victorian cryptozoology; Edward Porter describes a northern forest god that emerges at full moons to take human hearts in exchange for protecting a tribe, and which cannot be looked at directly without dying, only viewed through the voxen eye. North American occult tradition treats its name as taboo, and some hold that Biphi reached the wrong thing entirely.

MTF Iota-44 follows eighteen hours later and the article's second exploration log is its horror set-piece: singing over the radio, fragments of "The Girl from Ipanema"; sigil-headed figures walking out of the trees and standing back up after being shot — *"they're drawing power from somewhere else"*; a droning sound and something lighting the sky; an entity under a tree with branches and vines for a collar and a vibrating white sigil for a head, whose sigil shatters with a sound like breaking glass and a scream. Kato and Ashen are killed and their cameras and locators go with them. Wild is shot by Horizon before she can be taken. The survivors reach open ground and discover that E-13 has been inside for a week while I-44, which entered less than a day ago, has experienced six hours. Then the radio dies, and the last frame from I-44 is a still image of dirt.

Anna Christian's interview reframes everything. She believed she was summoning Gaia; her "wise elders" told her otherwise. The One Who Knows Silence In The Earth is not a nature god — *"It's a god of sacrifice."* The creatures inside are not natives but leavings, "byproducts of rituals," and the world it makes will not be one humans can live in — "like replacing rivers with battery acid." Asked how to kill it: *"You can't kill it, not with all the bombs and bullets in the universe. It is sustained by rituals."* Every small everyday ritual feeds it, the Foundation's included; stop performing them and it starves. And a warning — if it finds the gateway, it will start performing its own rituals out here, *"and it won't be satisfied by hooves and ash."*

The final log: Eclipse's team, two months in and starving, climbs for an exit and finds Katarina Randolph on a plateau, naked, dancing on the edge, singing the same Ipanema lyrics, with a large wound in her chest. They shoot her. Droning, a flash — and **SCP-4971-▽** rises below them: a titanic cervine thing with no head and no neck at all, replaced entirely by a massive vibrating white glowing crest, with luminous orbs orbiting its torso and shedding particulate. Katarina's body lifts into the air laughing *"Gaia\! I'm yours\!"*, her head implodes into itself, and a spinning white seal comes out of it and turns to face the team. Every radio's static stops at once. Eclipse, Roman and Nine-Eyes are lifted off the ground and their hearts are torn out. Mercury runs, meets Katarina again in the pass, draws a knife and charges — *"Come on, you pixie bitch. Let's fucking go."* The droning kills her equipment.

The coda is the worst part. Site-81 abandons exploration, but the anomaly keeps transmitting. On 19 October 2007 a transmitter uploads the E-13 and I-44 logs and environmental data. On 29 October, video of a Foundation radio tower standing inside SCP-4971. On 19 November, a transmission consisting of the words **"still here"** written in dirt. On 16 June 2015 — nearly eight years later — video of a frightened woman's face mouthing *"can't run anymore"* and *"sorry."* Nothing after that.

**Themes:**

- Radical environmentalism weaponized by something that was never a nature spirit  
- Sacrifice as metaphysics — a god that literally runs on ritual, including the mundane rituals of the people fighting it  
- Containment that is technically possible and morally impossible; the article invents a whole classification to say so  
- Colonial violence recursion: a curse laid by a tortured witch against invaders, answered by the wrong power  
- Time as an attritional weapon — a week outside, six hours inside, and eight years of one woman running

**What makes it work:**

- The Cernnunos-Class invention is a genuine worldbuilding contribution that doubles as the article's argument: the horror is that the solution exists and is unusable.  
- Two exploration logs from teams in the same place at different rates give the reader the time dilation as a lived discovery rather than a stated fact.  
- The resurrection mechanic — kill the sigil, the body gets up — makes the forest feel powered rather than populated, which sets up Christian's "sustained by rituals" explanation.  
- Anna Christian's interview is placed after the losses, so the reader gets the rules only once they've already watched them kill people.  
- "The Girl from Ipanema" as the recurring auditory motif is a superb tonal choice — mall muzak in a primeval forest, the abandoned SouthWood still playing.  
- The transmission log ending outlasts the plot by eight years, which converts a survival horror story into an open wound.

**The horror:**

- A god that cannot be fought, only starved — and the Foundation cannot stop feeding it.  
- Heart extraction: the tribute mechanism from Porter's account, executed on people mid-sentence.  
- The thing has no head, only a sigil, and cannot be looked at directly; the entity is a hole in perception.  
- Mercury's "still here," written in dirt, and then eight years later a face mouthing "can't run anymore. sorry." — someone was alive in there the entire time, and nobody came.

#### SCP-5004 — MEGALOMANIA

**Object class:** Esoteric (Containment Class) / Secondary Class: Thaumiel / Disruption Class: Ekhi / Risk Class: Notice. Assigned to USMILA Site-19 (Site Director Tilda Moose, Research Head Everett Mann, MTF A-14 "Dishwashers"). Sole author: **djkaktus**.

**Summary:** SCP-5004 is the group designation for "anomalous phenomenon affecting the Executive branch of the United States Government," and it is a two-part anomaly. SCP-5004-A is "Old Gorman," a demon ritually summoned in 1916 by Supreme Court Justice and practicing sorcerer Charles Evans Hughes, who hoped a devil from the 11th Plane of Blasphemy would help him beat Woodrow Wilson. The summoning produced a knee-high, foul-mouthed imp that Hughes and fellow Metaphysical Club sorcerer Oliver Wendell Holmes, Jr. had to beat into a corner with brooms. After losing the election, Hughes petulantly bound Gorman's essence beneath the western steps of the Capitol for a hundred years so it would have to listen to footsteps. This was a catastrophic error: Gorman is a *demon*, not a devil, and demons feed on chaos and disorder — a century under the U.S. Capitol force-fed it into a colossal archdemon due to hatch in January 2017\. SCP-5004-B is Donald John Trump, an innate **reality sink** — a natural phenomenon that severs anomalies from the fundamental forces binding them to the universe. The containment solution, Protocol 115-ASHE, is therefore to get the reality sink elected President, because Gorman is bound specifically to the office of the Presidency. The stated procedures are minimal and blackly funny: the anomaly "should dissipate entirely on January 20th, 2021, at 12:00PM EST," and virtually every Foundation department exists in this file only to manage the *mundane* geopolitical fallout of the containment measure.

Structurally the article is a monster — roughly 13,000 words, told almost entirely in found documents with no connective narration, and organized as eleven numbered addenda that march in strict chronological order from 2012 to 2019: **5004.1 Discovery and Background** (Dr. Katherine Sinclair's research paper, extended quotation from Hughes's 1916 journals including the full ritual component list — "one full onion, diced," "the eyes of six frogs" — plus a recorded interview with the surviving Metaphysical Club: Chief Justice John Roberts, Justice Stephen Breyer, and Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg, "Grand Arcanist of Brooklyn, the Witch of Washington"); **5004.2 The Ledermann Commission** (a committee report that concludes, in dry bureaucratic prose, "This committee recommends that a suitable reality sink be elected to the office of President of the United States"); **5004.3 Western Regional Command Meeting Transcript** (Director Sophia Light, Dr. Lachlan Cairns, Dr. Kain Pathos Crow — who is a dog — receiving the name of the candidate, and Cairns's page-long hysterical laughing fit); **5004.4 Pre-Election Project Whirlwind Documents** (Light's mandatory-participation memo, a rendered inbox of 31+ replies, phone transcripts of agents finding Michael Flynn already naked at the Kislyak meeting, IC\&S emails advising the Foundation *not* to suppress the Access Hollywood tape because polling says it helps); **5004.5 The 2016 Election** (a stack of voicemails to Sophia Light on election night, ending in a long, genuinely quiet conversation with Foundation psychologist Simon Glass); **5004.6 Containment** (inauguration; Gorman manifests and is instantly crushed by the sink into a 2m fetal ball floating above Trump's head); **5004.7 Appointment of the Special Prosecutor**; **5004.8 Phone Call Tip by Unknown Agent** (a pure-nonsense voicemail in dado-adjacent gibberish that tips off the FBI, from a payphone later found "disintegrated by a powerful sonic force"); **5004.9 The Mueller Investigation** (Light screaming at UIU Director Cooper Dean; the Foundation team infiltrating a White House dinner as the Russian "Strelnikov" family with Cairns piloting an 85-year-old woman's corpse and Crow wearing wires; Agent Merlo's FBI interrogation in which she claims to be "a piss artist"); **5004.10 Unexpected Revelations**; and **5004.11 A Resolution**. Seven footnotes, five captioned images, an inbox mockup, staff-roster tables, and a fourth-wall-nudging epigraph declaring the piece the "7'8" 600lb mutant goliath offspring" of djkaktus's own SCP-4444 lineage.

The turn comes in 5004.10. On July 4th, 2019, the President is abducted to the Capitol rotunda by "dark forces… from inside the White House." It is **Tiffany Trump**, who — while eating "the precious biscuit crumbs my family had graciously left for me" — looked at her sleeping father and had a third eye open onto Gorman. She has laid Ivanka, Donald Jr., and Eric out on four pedestals as sacrifices. Light explains, with weary contempt, that Tiffany's "new demon daddy" lied to her and that proximity to a reality sink will shred her essence. Tiffany completes the ritual anyway; Gorman manifests as a three-headed horse-legged obesity wearing the screaming heads of her siblings. Ruth Bader Ginsburg kicks open the back door in full wizard regalia, and the two duel with violet and red arcane energy until Ginsburg, eyes glowing white, roots the archdemon in place and pronounces: "by the power vested in me by Big Ben Franklin's Academy for Wizards and the United States Federal Government, I hereby demand that you eat — my — ass\!" The resolution addendum is deliberately anticlimactic: Gorman is resealed in weakened form, the three siblings are saved, Tiffany is dead and replaced by a body double ("turns out most people never actually knew what Tiffany looked like"), and the last exchange in the article is Light telling Cairns that Trump revealed the meaning of covfefe — "\[DATA EXPUNGED\]" — "I know\! Pretty mysterious, right?"

**Themes:**

- Institutional complicity: the Foundation's mandate to "preserve and protect" used to launder an act of democratic sabotage.  
- The horror of *irrelevance* — Light's discovery that Project Whirlwind, 160 agents and the full weight of RAISA, Applied Influence and Misinformation, changed nothing.  
- Bureaucracy as moral anesthetic ("Maybe it won't be so bad." / "I am going to require that all of you please keep your unpleasant remarks to yourselves").  
- Political satire as demonology: chaos literally feeds a demon that literally lives under the Capitol.  
- The absurdity of secret-keeping in a "post-goddamn-truth America."

**What makes it work:**

- Total commitment to the found-document form. There is no narrator; every beat lands through memo, transcript, voicemail, or footnote, and the reader assembles the story.  
- Tonal whiplash used structurally, not randomly. The Ginsburg wizard-battle and the Eric-Trump-biscuits farce sit directly against the Simon Glass phone call, which is played completely straight and is the emotional spine of the piece.  
- Escalating comedic architecture: each addendum raises the absurdity by exactly one notch, so a Supreme Court wizard duel feels earned by hour ten.  
- Sophia Light is a real protagonist inside a document format — she is given a want, a cost, and a defeat, all through her outgoing calls.

**The horror:**

- The Ledermann Commission's flat recommendation is the dread engine: the most consequential political act in the setting arrives as a bullet point in a committee report.  
- Light's realization — "Today's result happens even if we had stayed out of it completely" — is genuine cosmic horror repurposed for politics. Nobody was driving. The chaos was already there.  
- Gorman doesn't need to do anything. It grew simply by *being underneath* the thing that was already happening.  
- The article's final joke (\[DATA EXPUNGED\] covfefe) enacts the theme: after all that, the classified secret is nothing.

#### SCP-5740 — all cop are buddy by dado

**Object class:** Safe / Disruption Class: Vlam / Risk Class: Notice. Site-57 (Site Director Rashad Halloway, Research Head Sam King, MTF D-14 "Regulations"). Author: **djkaktus** (part of his running "dado" series).

**Summary:** On June 5th, 2020 — days after the George Floyd protests began — small cubical pop-up restaurants started appearing in a redacted American city. They have no doors: only a metal chute, a touchscreen, and every remaining surface covered in advertisements for themselves. They are "dado-nuts by dado," and their television spots are aggressively pro-police. When any non-officer approaches, the machine dispenses an ordinary, occupation-appropriate donut (civilians get glazed; healthcare workers get white frosting with red-cross sprinkles; National Guard get a cake donut reading "behave" in red frosting). When a member of law enforcement presses the button, they get SCP-5740-A.

Four to six hours after consumption, the officer experiences bloating, then extreme diarrhea, and then begins "to excrete a domesticated pig (*Sus domesticus*) from their rectum." The pig is entirely non-anomalous; the officer's body is anomalously enabled to pass it. Hog size correlates not to the officer's body but to their conduct: commendations produce small animals, while officers with disciplinary records — **or who have taken actions that should have warranted discipline regardless of whether they were ever disciplined** — produce four-to-five-hundred-pound hogs. Delivery can take hours and is described as traumatic for both parties. No subject who has passed a hog over 20kg has ever fully regained sphincter control.

Addendum 5740.1 is a chat log: a hidden refund interface in the touchscreen connects to "dado," djkaktus's recurring broken-English merchant entity. Dado denies everything with performative innocence ("dado is not knowings what u r talking about"), claims to be "of enlightened centrism" and to "only want 2 grill," then delivers a paragraph of scorching sarcasm about "so many fine dado customer being harass and threaten and beat upon by police." Pressed on why good cops are affected too, dado pauses — "dado is of thinking" — and answers: **"is because pig is pig no matter how small."** The log ends "u have nice day foundationman 🐖🐖🐖." Two short escalating addenda follow: an agent dismantling a stand accidentally triggers it and receives a "dado special blend" donut whose frosting is 92% guinea pig feces by weight; and an unverified instance outside an ICE field office, its touchscreen replaced by a placard reading "oh no\! is of small immigranting child inside\!\! coming quickly to apprehending\!", dispenses 47 live grenades at the approaching DHS officials.

**Themes:**

- Proportional, legible retribution — the anomaly is a functioning accountability mechanism in a world where the real one failed.  
- Collective versus individual guilt, argued in one sentence.  
- Corporate cheerfulness as the delivery vehicle for violence (the jingle, the ad-covered box, the "no refunds" sign).  
- Both-sidesism skewered via Researcher Werner's centrist emails elsewhere in the canon and dado's "enlightened centrism."  
- Institutional neutrality: the Foundation's job here is simply to shutter the machine and treat the officers.

**What makes it work:**

- The correlation table does the entire political argument without a word of editorializing. Clinical prose applies the moral judgment.  
- dado's voice — a distinct, sustained comic idiolect — lets the piece be furious without being a lecture.  
- The punchline is placed as a *reluctant admission* rather than a mission statement, which makes it hit far harder.  
- Escalation to the grenade stand is the perfect final beat: the joke stops being a joke exactly once.

**The horror:**

- Body horror as sentencing: the anomaly writes your record onto your body, permanently and unappealably.  
- The pig is innocent and panicking. The cruelty lands on it too — the piece refuses to make the revenge clean.  
- The permanence — the sphincter never fully recovers — turns a gag into a life sentence.  
- The last image: a smiling cartoon storefront that dispenses live ordnance at a detention facility.

#### SCP-5935 — Blood and the Breaking of My Heart

**Object class:** Euclid, with Secondary Class Thaumiel, Risk Class Critical; later "reclassified as EXPLAINED" in a nested false-ending. Contained at Point Aleph, southern ocean. Author: **djkaktus** (sole; with thanks to jaonhax and Woed for the text-obfuscation component). Opens with an author's note: he began it while his grandmother was dying of cancer in 2019\.

**Summary:** SCP-5935 is an extradimensional space at Point Aleph, roughly 0.23km × 0.31km on the outside and effectively infinite within, permanently fogbanked, accessible only to the O5 Council and enforced by MTF Alpha-1 "Red Right Hand" with shoot-on-sight orders. Inside it is **Pandaemonium**, a megacity of hundreds of millions of identical 430-meter skyscrapers with 1.12km supertowers. "The total population of Pandaemonium is unknown, and irrelevant." Its purpose is to contain SCP-5935-1. A "Cognitohazard Wa3/rning" at the head of the file notes that certain names have been "reclaimed" and are no longer accessible — which is why the narrator is only ever "\[DATA EXPUNGED\]."

The article is built as an archive of collected documents alternating between two registers. The first is a series of nine **journal entries** by an unnamed Foundation Overseer whose young son Jacob has just died after five years of illness: the burial beneath the old tree at the Michigan house; his wife Elisa's collapse into catatonia; recurring nightmares of a figure whose face ripples "like a fabric in the wind" with eyes "suspended from wire" swinging like a metronome, telling him "there is not a way out of where you are, only a way in"; his decision ("I was wrong. There is still something left to do"); and finally the exhumation, which dissolves into "how can someone so pale have blood so dark / blood so thick / so cold / blood." The second register is Foundation material: obfuscated audio recordings between two unnamed parties discussing what he "took with him" versus "what he brought out"; a medical file where "Subject 5935-H01" bursts ten IV bags with dark, ammoniac, self-generated blood; two O5 emergency meeting transcripts; a medical evaluation that collapses into carnage as the subject wakes and begins wearing Dr. Wallace's face while Wallace is still in the room ("That's my face\! My face\! Not yours\!"); and an interview under mnestic drip in which the interviewers themselves are progressively replaced mid-transcript while the IV dispenser chimes over and over.

The Overseer broke Protocol 4000-Eshu and went into the "quiet forest" — the place where those who have lost their names take refuge — looking for Jacob's name so he could bring him back. The name wasn't there. Instead he found another road, and at the end of it "a question that should have never been asked": an entity older than the Earth, older than the stars, one of the primordial questions whose answers became creation. It told him it could make Jacob walk and breathe. It did not lie. The thing that came back is a perfect doppelgänger whose true nature only the Overseer can see, and it spreads by blood. In an obfuscated conversation, a second speaker reveals they engineered his path — "He was so close to what he was looking for… But that path did not lead to where I needed him to go, so I too gave something up. I gave up something of yours, as well." When the entity finally speaks plainly it says: "I am the last child of IS, and I was there at the beginning… I am a lonely postman. I am a harried executive. I am a single mother. I am a Foundation Overseer." Its intention is to give its blood to everything that draws breath and reclaim every name that was ever taken from the Word, "until there are only two. You and I." An MTF dispatch log degrades into a King-James pastiche of John 1 — one Word, one Name, fractured into many gods and many names — before ending: "I AM NOT WHAT YOU THOUGHT I WAS DADDY / YOU PUT SOMETHING FOUL INSIDE ME / AND I'M NOT ME ANYMORE / I AM YOU / AND EVERYONE / I WAS MADE WRONG, DADDY."

The ending is the article's masterstroke. The Overseer, having watched the thing kill Elisa by giving her its blood, offers a trade: he will go with it willingly, and he will give it *his own name* — the one thing it can never counterfeit — in exchange for a favor. "Help me lie to myself." It accepts. The file then reclassifies itself as EXPLAINED and opens "Addendum 5935.1: When We Went Home," a warm domestic scene in Pandaemonium with Elisa writing and Jacob complaining about a playdate — until Jacob realizes the city is growing, that something was smuggled in. The Overseer reveals he carried **SCP-184, the Architect**, inside his own body, dreamt Pandaemonium into existence over years, and buried the artifact beneath ten thousand years of the city's own recursive construction. Pandaemonium's foundations are the still-living, soulless bodies of the hands that built it, and its citizens are man-sized containers of soulless bodies "that the Doppelganger cannot seize. They never belonged to it." The entity vows to walk out — "I will walk for a trillion years if I must" — and adds, softly, "when this world reaches its closing, I will come back here. Then, we will be together." The last lines: "I sit alone in my empty apartment, nameless and accursed. I hear the distant laughter of my wife and my son, and I smile. / Jacob, come inside. / It's almost time for bed." A final nested "REVISED ENTRY" flips the whole file inside out — SCP-5935 is now "the body of a human male," and "The intended purpose of SCP-5935 is to contain SCP-5935-1" — followed by a single line: **"Who are you?"**

**Themes:**

- Grief as the mechanism of catastrophe; love as a security vulnerability.  
- Names as the substance of identity, and identity as something *taken from somewhere* rather than owned.  
- The Orpheus problem restated: you can get the body back, but not the person.  
- Fatherhood, promises, and the cost of keeping one literally ("I would build you a city on a hill, shining and beautiful").  
- Willed self-delusion as the only available mercy.

**What makes it work:**

- The redaction is the character. The Overseer has no name because he traded it, so the file *cannot* name him — form and plot are the same fact.  
- Two prose registers running in parallel — clinical Foundation documentation and raw lowercase dream-journal — and the article's climax is the moment they contaminate each other (transcripts where the speaker labels start lying).  
- The reveal is a *reversal of victim and trap*: the story reads as a broken man's ruin right up until you learn the city was the plan all along.  
- Nested false endings (Explained → warm coda → REVISED ENTRY) that each recontextualize the last, ending on a question rather than a fact.  
- The author's note frames the whole thing honestly and refuses to pretend the piece is only genre.

**The horror:**

- Doppelgänger horror at its most vicious: victims can watch the transformation happen and still cannot tell which one is real, and the dissonance kills them.  
- The blade going *into* Dr. Wallace and coming out of the entity's chest — the impossibility of ever harming it.  
- Contagion via blood, with a stated end-state of *everyone*, which turns a domestic tragedy into an eschatology.  
- The final domestic scene is the most frightening part precisely because it is pleasant, and the reader knows the neighbors are corpses in boxes.

#### SCP-6162 — The Wormhole

**Object class:** Euclid / Disruption Class: Ekhi / Risk Class: Warning. Site-4 (Site Director Eduardo Beurgher, Research Head Dr. Theodore Galbyc, Task Force E-7 "Bob and Co."). Author: **djkaktus**. Short piece, \~1,000 words.

**Summary:** SCP-6162 designates a cluster of extraterrestrial phenomena observed on a single day: October 19th, 2021\. At 16:23 UTC, the FARMIND imaging array detected SCP-6162-A, a spatial anomaly roughly 384,000km from Earth — lunar distance. Dr. Mortimer Dekala's paper describes it as "a rogue celestial aberration of unknown mass, tracking an unknown orbit," fully opaque, reflecting no light, appearing suddenly for almost no energy cost, and — in violation of Kurushian physics — *regenerating* mass rather than decaying. Containment procedures are an admission of impotence: LUNACORPS is to keep working on Project ANKS to find a way to collapse future instances, "however, due to the observed cross-dimensional mass matrix of SCP-6162-A, no such technology capable of accomplishing this currently exists."

The probe KNKO-3 reached the rift after eleven hours, confirmed a mass of about three billion kilograms, deployed a radio tether at the near side to maintain transmission across the tidal plane, and passed through. On the far side it was nearly disabled by EM pulses from three mechanical structures — SCP-6162-B — each over 5km tall, plated in heavily oxidized steel, firing depleted uranium shells from autocannons of at least 2500mm bore. They were engaging SCP-6162-C, a smaller sleek black vessel held in place by a spatial entangling array and returning fire with an unrecognizable weapon. Two of the B-instances then folded their gun batteries into their superstructures, aligned to a point in space, accelerated, and vanished from local space entirely. The remaining one destroyed SCP-6162-C, caught KNKO-3 in the same entangling array, flooded its radio with noise, and — per one final pressure reading from the gun batteries — shot it. SCP-6162-A degraded and collapsed entirely 16 hours and 23 minutes after discovery, before KNKO-4 could launch.

Addendum 6162.2 is the whole point. The "noise" that overwhelmed KNKO-3's receiver was, per AI analysis, a message on a previously unknown subspace frequency, repeated eleven times in the eight seconds before destruction, in **modern Russian**: *"Пошел ты, сука с фрегатом. Я посадил семя в вашем доме. теперь вас выселят."* — "Fuck you, you bitch with a frigate. I planted a seed in your house. Now you will be evicted." The file ends: "The meaning of this message as it relates to its source is unknown."

**Themes:**

- Humanity as an irrelevant bystander to a war it cannot perceive, parse, or survive.  
- Scale humiliation — three billion kilograms, 5km hulls, 2500mm guns, and a small probe that lasts minutes.  
- The uncanniness of the *familiar* detail inside the alien: modern Russian, spoken between kilometer-scale machines.  
- The window that closes: sixteen hours of contact with an entire cosmology, then nothing.

**What makes it work:**

- Ruthless brevity. The article is almost all telemetry, and the restraint makes the one line of dialogue enormous.  
- Deadpan procedural language for an interstellar naval engagement — the probe is "presumably destroyed," which is very funny and very cold.  
- The message is aimed at *someone else*. The reader is eavesdropping on a taunt with no context, no antecedent, and no resolution.  
- The containment procedures openly state the countermeasure does not and cannot exist yet.

**The horror:**

- Absolute epistemic helplessness: we know a war exists, we know one side speaks Russian, and we will never learn more.  
- "I planted a seed in your house. Now you will be evicted" — a threat of displacement whose target may or may not be Earth, and the file will not say.  
- The two ships that left aligned to a point in space and simply *went*. Something out there has somewhere to be.  
- The anomaly closed on its own. Nothing the Foundation did mattered, and nothing it can do will matter if it opens again.

#### SCP-6666 — The Demon Hector and the Dread Titania

**Object class:** Esoteric / Secondary Class: Thaumiel / Disruption Class: Ekhi / Risk Class: Critical. Contained at PARAGON South American Forward Operating Site (SAFOS), Amazon rainforest, by ATF Hēt-1 "Lance of Longinus." Author: **djkaktus** (silver medal, SCP-6000 contest). Epigraph from Victoria Schwab's *The Unbound*: "Treat all the bad things like dreams, Kenzie."

**Series context:** SCP-6666 is the third-published of four linked articles making up the **Demon cycle**, gathered under the **Project PARAGON hub**, and marked on djkaktus's author page with playing-card suits: **SCP-2254, The Demon La Hire and the Valley of Lust \[♥\]**; **SCP-4840, The Demon Lancelot and the Flying City of Audapaupadopolis \[♣\]**; **SCP-6666, The Demon Hector and the Dread Titania \[♦\]**; and **SCP-6765, The Demon Ogier and the Bleeding Throne of Malidraug \[♠\]**. (Note that "Gloriana Alexylva" is a *separate* djkaktus tale-canon with its own hub, not part of the Demon cycle.) The arc's spine: an antediluvian human kingdom, the Sky Kings of House Apollyon, descended from Asem the First Man, sailed west and sacked the lands of the Fair Folk. Four knights — La Hire, Lancelot, Hector, Ogier — served that house. The Sky King Sarrus VIII died at sea; his son Sarrus IX tortured and entombed the captured faerie princess, who cursed all four knights into demons, each afflicted with a different affliction: lust, wrath, agony, despair. Project PARAGON, commissioned by O5-7 after SCP-4840's discovery, exists to find and contain them, along with the three Profanities (SCP-4812). SCP-6666 is Hector's article, and it is the one that answers the biggest question in the arc: what SCP-1000, the Children of the Night, actually *are*.

**Summary:** SCP-6666 is a colossal dead botanical entity in the Amazon: a 380m trunk, 9.2km tall, hanging **inverted** by its roots from the ceiling of a natural lithospheric void 52km wide and 43km deep that formed half a billion years ago. It shows no cellular life whatsoever, yet its new-growth roots are mobile and hostile — they drag men, animals, and machines into the earth, and fire teams must periodically burn them back on fifteen-minute exposure limits. A 200m layer of pale green fog pours from a jagged wound in its side; the fog is a neurotoxin that collapses the central nervous system in seconds. SCP-6666-A is a 23m six-armed, six-eyed, mouthless entity half-fused into that wound, one oversized arm grown into an 18m iron spear, locked in a permanent cycle of being burned away by the fog and regenerating, endlessly trying to prise the tree open. This is Hector.

The article's structure alternates interrogations of the setting's oldest witnesses with field logs. Dr. Osmon Iles's discovery report explains that Operation ATLAS found SCP-6666 by mapping the *absence* of human history in the Amazon — "the dark hole in the middle of history" — because SCP-4008, the Wormwood, is a Daevite-adapted seed from this tree that swallows civilizations and erases them from record. "We had puzzled over what tree could bear the fruit of the Wormwood, and now we know — it was a dead one." An interview with an Eshu faerie recounts Titania, called **Iýa**, goddess of starlight and wishes; and Caspan the Dreamweaver, who was taken into the dark and forced to teach the Children of the Night how to dream, then betrayed the faeries by leading them to Titania so they could make a wish. An interview with **SCP-073 (Cain)** by Sophia Light supplies the other half: Adam el Asem, the First Man, "pulled a star from the sky and put it on his iron crown," and the faeries prayed to Titania for deliverance — the Children of the Night were the answer to that prayer, created to destroy him, and they paid darker gods for their power in the erased histories of mankind before turning on their makers. The Great Flood ended them. Cain also gives up the identity of **SCP-343**, "God," as Methuselah the Arcanist, Royal Vizier of the Daevite House of Malidraug.

The Connington Set excerpt (Addendum 6666.5) supplies Gom of Nod's parable: a warrior who begged only to serve his king and to give the king his heart forever; whose king drowned; and whose king's son cast him out in disgust, sending him "to seek your salvation in the black forests beyond the sea… to excise this Dread Titania from its soil." Then the drone log: "Hero" and "Champion" descend and find, on the cavern slope, whole ruined settlements of no known human origin being pulled into the earth, and roughly **283,824 emaciated corpses** — human, faerie, animal, children — all crawling uphill, covered in green residue, faces frozen in panic, with defensive wounds inflicted on each other. Champion enters the black forest at the cavern floor, is told by its own instruments that it is flying inverted, crashes, and its recovery chime slowly recedes into the distance while its sensors insist it has not moved.

Operation Cauterize seals the wound with polyfoam and pins Hector to the tree with steel cable bolts and a 3" kevlar guy line. Then Dr. Alto Clef interviews SCP-343, and the article's best sustained sequence arrives: Methuselah's first-hand account of the Children of the Night. They appeared on a dune above his citadel, fifty abreast, "like if someone described a man to you, but they had never really seen a man themselves." They pulled his master apart slowly, laughing. They chained the survivors, shipped them across the sea, hung their chains in the trees, and picked people off "like picking a ripe apple" to play with. They never slept, and they were in the prisoners' dreams — because **they communicate through human nightmares, and their entire civilization requires human terror to function**. The sorcerer Noah of the House of Lament broke it by calling down the Flood; the interlopers could not swim and simply stood at the bottoms of flooding pits and watched the water rise, unheard by their fellows because nobody was asleep. Methuselah begs Clef: "If they have been buried, they must remain buried."

Addendum 6666.11 is the manned expedition. Hēt-1 and Dr. Bishop's research team walk into the forest, find a faerie corpse impaled on a limb with a pouch of children's toys behind it, find staircases growing out of tree trunks and facsimile buildings woven into the canopy, and find a domed clearing hung with thousands of chained corpses over a 30m × 50m stone slab. Beneath it, past murals of bodies burned and pitted, they open a door onto a chamber "stretching out as far as the eye can see" filled with concentric rings of hairy humanoid figures curled fetal on the floor, dusted in the neurotoxin, all breathing slowly in unison. Then the eyes open. The surface team hears stretched child-laughter, the arched trees pull apart, and SCP-6666 lights up red. Dr. Xi's mask cracks and he seizes; Horizon is torn in half and Carrier shoots what's left; the rest run 14km to the elevator. Xi's damaged transmitter comes back on an hour later and records him waking, walking into the trees, being laughed at, and being dragged 100m backwards after a scream.

Meanwhile, above, Hector snaps the kevlar line and speaks — in an unknown tongue that every listener understands in their own language. He declares himself "HECTOR, SON OF HOLAS, THE SCREAMING LANCE OF THE NORTHERN SKY… SERVANT ETERNALLY TO THE SKY KING SARRUS VON APOLLYON," and addresses the tree: "TREMBLE NOW AS YOU DID WHEN I DROVE MY LANCE INTO YOUR BLEEDING CHEST AND TREMBLE NOW AS YOU DID WHEN I LEARNED YOU HAD ALREADY BETRAYED YOUR PURPOSE… YOU WILL HONOR MY WISH AND POUR YOUR POISON INTO THIS GRAVE. YOU WILL SMOTHER THE CORPSE-CITY OF YOUR INTERLOPERS AND BURY THEM." He rips the seal open, restarts the poison, and goes dormant. **The tree is Titania's corpse, and the demon is not attacking it — he is operating it.** Hector has been gassing the sleeping Children of the Night for hundreds of thousands of years, and the Foundation's containment success nearly woke them.

The final addendum, 6666.13, is an Overseer-eyes-only memo from O5-1. The SCP-1000 file was written by Tilda Moose in 1956 — two years before the first Bigfoot sighting, and while Tilda Moose neither worked for the Foundation nor was alive. It quotes the SCP-1000 file's own line: "we forgive you; / given choice for now, not forever; / let us back in" — and asks: "who do you think would want us to let these things back in?" Then it restores the censored end of the faerie interview: the Children's wish was "**to not be alone anymore**," and Titania, knowing exactly what the faeries' neglect had made of them, tore out her own heart and gave it to them, becoming their goddess instead of her own people's. The memo closes by noting that Dr. Bishop was "kind enough to grab something from that temple on her way out — grabbed it right out of the withered hands of a long dead sprite… It's a shame — it's really very pretty." Sign-off: "The work continues."

**Themes:**

- Abandonment as the origin of monstrosity: the Children turned cruel because they were made as tools and then discarded in the dark for a hundred million years.  
- Loyalty as damnation — Hector's curse is that he never stopped serving a king who is dead and a king's son who despised him.  
- History as an erasable substance, and the Foundation as archaeologists of a deliberately excavated hole.  
- Divine self-sacrifice made monstrous: Titania's mercy becomes a chemical weapon.  
- Complicity across species — the faeries hated what they made, and said so.

**What makes it work:**

- The load-bearing reveal is a shift in *interpretation*, not information: everything about Hector's behavior is visible from paragraph one, and the meaning inverts only at the end.  
- Methuselah's monologue converts the setting's oldest gag character (SCP-343, "God") into a traumatized ninety-thousand-year-old survivor, and does it entirely through voice.  
- The drone log's slow accumulation — first a body, then a stable of horses, then a dog that clawed at a door, then 283,824 people — is a masterclass in reveal by escalation.  
- The article retrofits SCP-1000, one of the wiki's oldest and most-attempted articles, into the arc without contradicting it, and turns its cryptic message into a threat.  
- Structural nesting: the redaction in 6666.3 is *restored* in 6666.13, so the single most humane fact in the article is the one that was classified.

**The horror:**

- A chamber without visible end, full of sleeping predators, discovered by five people with flashlights — and then they all open their eyes at once.  
- Communication through nightmare: your sleep is their telephone line, and they need you afraid to speak to each other.  
- The corpses crawling *uphill* with defensive wounds on each other. Everyone died trying to climb out of a gas chamber, killing each other to do it.  
- Xi's transmitter coming back on. The article gives you a man who was left for dead, then makes you listen to him die.  
- The realization that the containment breach here is not the demon escaping — it's the demon being *stopped*.

#### SCP-6740 — Our Shared Grave

**Object class:** Safe / Disruption Class: Dark / Risk Class: Notice. Contained at Site-19 (Site Director Tilda Moose, Research Head Dr. Everett Mann, no assigned task force). Author: **djkaktus**.

**Summary:** SCP-6740 is a spatial incursion inside Site-19 itself, occupying the long-abandoned offices of the defunct Office of Personnel Arrangements on sublevel E7B — an office that had stood empty for over three decades before the anomaly was found. There is no containment in any meaningful sense: the anomaly is inside a working Foundation facility, and by Ethics Committee Memorandum 9.14.19A, *any* cleared personnel may enter. You sign in with the desk clerk on the way down.

The office no longer has a ceiling. Its support columns run upward without end into a starry sky held in perpetual dusk. At the far end, set into a wall that used to divide the office from a storage room, is a white door in an ornate frame shaped like a horned stag. It is the only way in — climbing the wall gets you nothing, because the anomaly is simply not there unless you go through the door.

Beyond it is a mausoleum of polished marble and iron. Its dimensions increase over time and cannot be measured, but it is impossible to get lost: anyone who wants to leave finds the exit quickly. It is filled with sealed vaults, and every vault catalogued so far belongs to a specific dead person — a member of Foundation staff, a civilian contractor, a D-class, or anyone who died during the containment or attempted containment of an anomaly. Radiography shows humanoid figures inside every one. None can be opened. Because the Foundation cremates all its dead, nobody knows what the figures actually are, and exhumations elsewhere have been inconclusive.

The one break in the uniformity is at the entrance: a bronze stag on a black marble plinth, with another occupied vault beneath the platform. The plinth carries an engraved poem of unknown origin, given in full. It is spoken by a nameless Foundation doctor, from birth ("I was born sleeping, / my eyes closed to the / harsh light and cold truth / of a world beyond the / four walls of my youth") through a childhood wondering "if the last of / the dragons had died / when the sun set in / a much younger sky," to recruitment ("within this hallowed temple / to the best of our intentions / did I find purpose in our mission. / To secure that old darkness, / To contain what lies within, / To protect what lies without"), to a clear-eyed accounting of scale ("My part to play was but as / a single actor on a stage / far larger than I had mind / or reason to comprehend, / yet it was a part all the same"), to death without lament, "able at last to see the stars / in the sky of my childhood." It ends: "Within our shared grave / lie we who sleep again, / our part now complete / and our curtains drawn. // We had dreamt of dragons, / and found angels among us. // HERE LIES A DOCTOR / OF THE / SCP FOUNDATION. / AWAKE AT LAST." The article stops there — no addenda, no interviews, no incident. The poem is the ending.

**Themes:**

- Institutional memory versus institutional practice — an organization that cremates its dead is given a cemetery it did not build and cannot open.  
- Vocation and consent: the D-class are interred alongside the doctors, which the file states flatly and does not comment on.  
- The Foundation as a place people spend and end their lives, rather than a set of procedures.  
- Wonder recovered at the end of a career spent suppressing it ("We had dreamt of dragons, and found angels among us").  
- Grace as an anomaly — something arrives, unbidden, and offers the staff a place to grieve.

**What makes it work:**

- Withholding. There is no addendum, no exploration log, no thing in the dark. The refusal to escalate *is* the craft move, and it's rare in the format.  
- The Ethics Committee memorandum is the emotional hinge: containment procedures rewritten as visiting hours.  
- Every anomalous property is a kindness engineered in reverse — it grows to fit the dead, and it lets you leave whenever you want.  
- The unopenable vaults preserve ambiguity perfectly: the file will not tell you whether anyone is really in there, and the poem does not need it to.  
- The unattributed nameless "A DOCTOR" makes the epitaph belong to every reader who has ever written one of these files.

**The horror:**

- It is quiet, not absent. Something is keeping an exact and complete list of everyone the Foundation has killed or spent, including the D-class, and it is doing so *inside Site-19*.  
- The vaults hold humanoid figures that cannot be the bodies, because the bodies were burned. Something made them.  
- The office had been empty for thirty years, on a subterranean level, under the noses of the people being counted.  
- The horned stag: an unexplained psychopomp with no file, no classification, and no interest in being interviewed.

#### SCP-6745 — Seasonal Menu

**Object class:** Esoteric / Secondary Class: Thaumiel / Disruption Class: Keneq / Risk Class: Danger. Monitored, not contained; records kept at Site-81. Author: **djkaktus**.

**Summary:** SCP-6745 is a McDonald's franchise in Martindale, Indiana. Containment consists of embedding Foundation assets in the local community, in law enforcement and in code-regulation offices, watching for civilian reports during "seasonal activation cycles," and amnesticizing anyone who tries to go public. The Thaumiel classification is the whole joke and the whole horror: the restaurant is not contained, it is *maintained*, because it is containing something else.

The anomaly is the McRib. Frozen pork arrives at the location and is unequivocally pork; sandwiches emerge from the preparation process averaging **89% human flesh**. Where in the building the transformation happens, and by what mechanism, is unknown, as is whether the staff have any idea. Most DNA testing is inconclusive, but three samples matched local missing persons, given in a clinical table: Emma Richards, 34, missing June 1998, house abandoned, both dogs found mutilated and buried in the yard — sandwich contained meat from the belly and thigh. Tony Michaels, 47, missing December 1998, presumed drunk-driving fatality but never recovered from the vehicle — cheek and shoulder. Amy Baker, **age 8**, missing October 1999, presumed home-invasion kidnapping, case closed — eyes and hands.

Addendum 6745.1: in 1997 an Indianapolis Star journalist, Christian Kurtis, working a food-safety story, got an anonymous tip describing meat "gamey and unnaturally flavorful," a texture "too soft for pork but with a queer firmness upon biting," and sauce that "tastes heavily of iron, maybe spoiled." He collected samples for months and reported to regulators. The restaurant was closed October 26th, 1997\.

Addendum 6745.2 is the incident. Three days later a crowd gathered, "non-aggressive" but "clearly designed to influence or intimidate," demanding the restaurant reopen and asking permission to cross the perimeter and make a sandwich themselves. Officer Sam Hill: "They were insatiable… it was like they couldn't hear what I was saying… one woman I had to restrain… the entire time she kept repeating 'I just need a taste, give me a taste, I just want a taste.'" Ten officers stayed overnight. At 23:47, Officer Jeff Moore radioed in a fresh carcass pushed against the lobby door that nobody saw arrive, then broke into panic: civilians coming out of the treeline with "some kind of weird antler hats… I think they're hats, I don't know… why are they moving like that." Gunshots. Then his last transmission: "**They're not *people*.**" All ten were found disemboweled, organs and musculature removed entirely, badges and weapons untouched. "REOPEN TOMORROW" was painted on the sidewalk in human blood. The building itself was undamaged.

Addendum 6745.3 is Agent John Freeman's cleanup report, and it is the best writing in the piece. He notes the power dynamic: "The officers killed were not men with guns, they were prey animals at the mercy of a carnivorous host… the soft meat of the body was picked apart like carrion." He describes being watched all night from the treeline by things whose antlers he could see in the moonlight. Then he draws the comparison the article is built on: "Remove a population of deer and the wolves begin to starve… it's not unexpected that they would have found a way to simplify the process of gathering food the same way we have. How are their actions any different than our own? Reducing animals to neatly packaged meat discs to be sold to the public for just a single dollar, or less. Only real distinction here is that we're the meat." One of them was waiting by his car — "an enormous thing, stretched unnaturally thin and wretched, shuddering and shaking like each breath was the death rattle of every living being around it" — and in that moment Freeman felt himself become "a convenient tube of packaged meat, neatly tucked away until such time as it was needed." Its voice was "like dry wind over an empty field," and it said six words: "**Reopen tomorrow. Don't change the menu.**" Kurtis was amnesticized, his story edited, the staff went back to work the next day, and there has been no further disturbance. The restaurant has been quietly serving the fleshchangers their seasonal food ever since.

**Themes:**

- The industrial food chain read from the other end — humans as livestock with a supply contract.  
- Institutional appeasement: containment as protection racket, dressed as a franchise agreement.  
- Compulsion and addiction — the transfixed crowd is as disturbing as the antlered things.  
- The banality of the venue: nothing about this is gothic. It's a McDonald's off a state road in Indiana.  
- The Foundation's willingness to feed people to something in order to keep the peace.

**What makes it work:**

- The three DNA entries do enormous work in six lines each. Amy Baker's — eyes and hands, age eight — is placed last and left uncommented.  
- Genre-swapping the delivery mechanism: dispatch-log realism for the massacre, then a literary, self-implicating first-person essay for the cleanup.  
- Freeman's argument is *correct*, and the article knows it. It refuses to let the reader stand outside the ethics.  
- The six-word demand is a perfect closing line — a business note, not a threat.  
- The Thaumiel/Esoteric classification quietly tells you the ending before you reach it.

**The horror:**

- The crowd. Ordinary townspeople, unable to hear you, staring past you, begging for a taste.  
- "They're not *people*" as the last thing a man says over the radio.  
- Predation without malice. Nothing here hates you; you're inventory.  
- The restaurant is still open, still serving, and the anomaly's only stated requirement is continuity of service.

#### SCP-6765 — The Demon Ogier and the Bleeding Throne of Malidraug

**Object class:** Keter / Disruption Class: Ekhi / Risk Class: Danger. Contained at the Eastern Kashmir Forward Operating Site (EKFOS), built inside the complex beneath Aksayqin Lake, Hotan Prefecture, Xinjiang. Author: **djkaktus**.

**Series context:** This is the fourth and final Demon article \[♠\], the capstone of the Project PARAGON arc begun in SCP-2254 and SCP-4840 and deepened in SCP-6666. Where 6666 is about the Children of the Night and the faeries, 6765 is about the *other* half of the antediluvian world — the Daevites — and it is the article that finally narrates the curse itself, gives the four knights their biographies, and closes the arc by pointing at the next one.

**Summary:** SCP-6765 is an antediluvian complex of more than 552,000m³ in three parts. **The Atrium**, reached through a cave on a rocky outcrop into the lake, is the emptied top of what was once a spiraling tower — the complex fell and came to rest at its present position, and the outcrop is the tower's protruding tip. **The Throne Room**, 27m by 49m under an 11m dome, its floor largely collapsed. **The Reservoir**, everything below, holding an estimated 790,000,000 liters of human blood (a figure later thought to underestimate by more than half), circulated through seventy-two gilded pipelines.

Four entities live here. **SCP-6765-A**, the Throne of Malidraug: a pseudo-biological machine of carved stone with nine articulated silver syringes positioned for the major arteries, and beneath its platform a five-chambered mechanical heart of hammered gold and copper that bypasses its occupant's own. **SCP-6765-B**, Lord Relivine, an emaciated but living Daevite magister permanently seated on it — heart bypassed, digestive tract fully atrophied, in permanent deep sleep. **SCP-6765-C**, "Javert": an instance of SCP-1000, 3.7m, 337kg, non-hostile, fluent in ASL (which he learned after discovery), and able to speak to sleeping people directly in their own language. He wandered here centuries ago after getting lost in the mountains, asked for shelter, and has performed the daily care of the other two ever since. **SCP-6765-D**, Ogier son of Osires, a 10.1m entity in arrested metamorphosis — a six-armed, six-eyed, mouthless demon body with a human man in his early thirties, grey-haired and one-eyed, fused into its neck and chest, one human arm still free and functional. He is lucid, courteous, multilingual, and has spent an age of the Earth in one room with books and a radio.

The article is built out of interviews, dream journals kept by Agent Evelyn Ivy, PARAGON meeting transcripts, and — its distinctive formal device — **somnambulistic narratives**: the Daevites stored memory in blood, the Reservoir is their archive, and Javert's dreamspace is the reading room. Foundation "dreamer agents" sleep and transcribe first-hand accounts from people dead half a million years. Through these we get the history: Ogier was a scholar of "Asem's lingering light" conscripted into Sarrus IX's service; La Hire was the vain greatest swordsman alive ("the Lord's Blade has slayed ten thousand men, and laid ten thousand more"); Lancelot was the Scourge of Memory, who drank the blood of men he broke and consumed their memories; Hector was the king's shield, who personally slew a faerie champion and who, at his lord's command, "wrapped a fae princess in iron chains and dragged her back to the king's flagship." When her curse sank Sarrus VIII, it was Hector who dove after him and had to be pulled out. Sarrus IX then tortured the princess without measure, and when that failed to break her, had the Cult of Idus fill her with preserving balms and seal her in an iron sarcophagus inside a buried stone crypt. From there she spoke the curse — quoted in full:

> Usurpers of the Sun's Right Hand, / of fell deeds and false promises. / You will have the years / to count your graven sins, / but no mind to resolve them / and no soul to be absolved.

"Deeper even than she, at the bottom of the world, three horrors of the old world answered her call." Ogier's own account describes the dream in which it took him: immobilized under a starry sky, two towers of three eyes each above him, a runed plinth below, a voice telling him he was to become a curse on himself as well as the world; then a second voice, "soft and distant, as if buried deep below," pleading that he specifically be given "entirely to an unending grief that has no limit, no reprieve, and no end"; then a third voice from inside the shattering plinth, which said only: "it will be." He fled that night while Lancelot butchered the capital, and was taken in by the Daeva of Malidraug, where Relivine's "ministration" arrested the transformation at the halfway point. Other narratives cover La Hire's lust-curse atrocity at Ys, the three Profanities' destruction of High Apollyona, and the deep history of the King in Red.

Malidraug's blood was given freely: as the Flood approached, foreseen by a Daeva oracle, the people came to the temple, sealed the chamber, "opened their hearts and emptied themselves into this temple," and preserved their memories through the last man to sit the throne. But by the present day Relivine is failing — communions are lost midway or incomprehensible, and Dr. Matthew Fulweiler theorizes that the throne is an aperture and Relivine's body is the filter, and the filter is clogging. Agent Ivy's dream journal \#114 delivers the reason. Relivine shows them the bottom of the world: a chamber where the Mad King Moros of Malidraug once stripped the soul from his own divine ancestor, "the Lord of Memory," also called **the King in Red**, and dropped a monolith the size of a skyscraper on the gate. The corpse is still down there, mindless, hateful, and pushing — and it has been leaching its poison into Relivine. "No power remains in this world that can contest a true god, even one who has died." He names his successor: "Find your light, Child of Asem, and you will find your way through the darkness of the Scarlet King's tomb."

The article makes its biggest cosmological move in the Operation RENEWAL brief: the Lord of Memory's metaphysical aspect, separated from its body in the distant past, is what the Foundation calls **the Scarlet King** — SCP-6765-E is the previously unknown *physical* half. Operation RENEWAL lowers Ogier, Javert, Ivy, Fulweiler and ATF S-1 "Paradise Lost" 7.5km down a freight elevator beside the hanging column of blood (which gives the agents memories of people they never met, and a hand-gesture of encouragement from strangers who could not have existed). Ogier spends the descent praying. Below the tower they find a chapel of oily black stone that makes the body try to reject the air, a stairwell down which the flashlights simply produce less light and men sit down in the dark and refuse to move, and finally the cavern with the monolith rising a little further with each tremor and red light pouring from underneath.

A Daevite woman — the one Relivine loved, whose memory was washed into nothing for what she did — has been holding the seal alone this whole time. She turns her head to acknowledge them and is atomized. The **HERITAGE Event** follows: the world flips into a shared memory-space, the tower is unzipped, the entire column of blood floats free screaming in millions of voices, and the King in Red comes over the mountains, unmoving as a mannequin, with a spinning sigil behind it and a voice Fulweiler wants amnestics to forget. Ogier raises his human hand and puts up a barrier that blows away like sand. The agents open fire and the entity **removes their memory of how to operate firearms** — permanently, for some of them. Then Ogier answers. He is remade thirty meters tall, stark white and burning with golden light, six golden horns curling back over his skull like a helmet, the man visible standing on the demon's shoulder — the demon body and the human body finally separated and working as one. He speaks:

> RETURN, WRETCHED INCARNATION, UNFIT NOW TO WALK ASEM'S GOLDEN STREETS / RETURN TO THE DARK, WHERE IN AGES PAST YOU WERE RESIGNED / AND REMAIN THERE, UNTIL THE UNDOING OF THE EARTH / I AM OGIER, WIELDER OF ASEM'S GOLDEN SUN / BEFORE WHICH YOU ARE LITTLE MORE THAN A PASSING SHADOW / GO NOW WILLINGLY, OR BE UNDONE

Three left arms form a bow, three right arms draw three golden arrows, and he fires. The King in Red is driven back through a mountain; Ogier traces a glowing circle in the sky, and then, back in the real cavern, grasps the monolith with both hands and slams it down. The final addendum is a secure call between Director Shannon Lancaster and O5-1 ("Calvin"). Relivine survived, barely — "the dreamer is still dreaming in there. We weren't too late." Ogier stays below, voluntarily, endlessly reinforcing the wards. And O5-1 notes that just before Ogier's reawakening, something stirred at another site where nothing should ever stir, and asks Lancaster to look into it: "Have you ever heard of the Department of Abnormalities, Director?"

**Themes:**

- Redemption available to the least likely of the four — the scholar, the coward, the one who ran.  
- Memory as a physical, inheritable, corruptible substance; an archive that can rot from below.  
- Self-loathing as the real curse. Ogier's despair, not his body, is what nearly stops him.  
- Custodianship and quiet care: Javert, an instance of the setting's most feared species, spends centuries doing chores for two dying men.  
- Sins of the fathers — every horror in this article traces to Sarrus IX's cruelty to a prisoner.

**What makes it work:**

- The somnambulistic-narrative device solves an old problem: it lets a documentation-format article deliver 500,000-year-old first-person prose without cheating.  
- Ogier's characterization. He is polite, funny ("You will need to sit upon the throne and be deeply penetrated by the machine… I am sorry, the look on your face was wonderful"), and utterly broken, and his tea-and-conversation scene with Agent Ivy is the reason the climax lands.  
- Rehabilitating SCP-1000 through a single individual — Javert is the counter-argument to everything SCP-6666 establishes, without contradicting any of it.  
- The Scarlet King retcon is executed as a *definitional* split rather than a revision, which lets it enlarge the mythos instead of overwriting it.  
- Ending the fight in a found video transcript, so the biggest spectacle in the arc is described in flat past-tense camera language.

**The horror:**

- A god's *corpse* is enough. Not a god — the mindless body of one, leaking hate upward for half a million years.  
- Memory as a target: the King in Red disarms trained soldiers by deleting a skill, and some never get it back.  
- The failing filter — an old man in a chair, asleep, dying slowly, and the only thing between the world and the thing below.  
- The stairwell where the flashlights dim and men simply stop and sit down in the dark for hours.  
- The woman holding the seal alone, whose name and memory were already erased, who is destroyed in the instant she looks up to see that help arrived.

#### SCP-7341 — Nothing, and Everything

**Object class:** N/A at the public clearance level; Esoteric behind Level 5/7341 access. Disruption Class: Amida / Risk Class: Critical. Contained by the MYRKURHUGI system at DEEPWELL 354, Fljótsdalshérað, eastern Iceland. Author: **djkaktus**. Dedication: "For those who are here, and those who aren't."

**Summary:** The file opens with a public-facing stub: personnel assigned to SCP-7341 are to monitor the perimeter and "are to be made aware that there is nothing within DEEPWELL 354, as SCP-7341 does not exist." Description: "SCP-7341 does not exist." Then a credential prompt, and the real document.

SCP-7341 is a static point-void of nonreality — the same phenomenon as SCP-3930 — but **manufactured**, deliberately, by the Foundation on December 23rd, 2021\. It has no dimension, texture, shape, or interaction; it is not a location, singularity, vacuum, or extradimensional space, "as a requirement for any such descriptive is existence, which SCP-7341 lacks." It exerts a metastructural compulsion force that compresses spacetime away from itself; anything crossing the resulting terminal boundary ceases to be, leaving no matter and no energy behind. Containment is the MYRKURHUGI system: "Vitsmunir," a 2,414-petaflop DARKMIND-platform AI; "Kjarna," a metaphysically stable vessel; and "Viska," 484 Kormaksson-Andrews Amplified Di-Reality Field Generators, of which at least 441 must run. Power comes from the Kárahnjúkar Hydropower Plant, built under an aluminum-smelting cover story. Because of a logic fault present at activation, the terminality field extends about 11 meters past the vessel into the Level 3 Observation Deck. It cannot be corrected without a full shutdown, which would be worse. Anyone who enters is simply gone.

The body of the article is the history of **Point Horizon**, told through test summaries and, crucially, the collected notes and correspondence of **Dr. Cheryl Andrews**. Founded in 2007 by Drs. Jon Kormaksson and Ivan Zatsepin, building on Robert Scranton's essay "Supersession and the Echelon of Reality," the project aimed to manufacture a controlled null state to study the void safely. Andrews' notes progress from rigorous instrumentation philosophy ("there exists a language the void speaks that we simply cannot understand") toward something else: a conviction that the void has *purpose*, that "the unreal, being far greater in size than all of reality by an unknowable factor, somehow uses reality as a playground." ALPHA (2011) proved the vessel. BETA I–III (2012–2015) sustained voids and inserted shielded probes, reading the field's feedback compensation "like poking around with a needle in the dark."

Interleaved with the science is an email thread between Andrews and Kormaksson that is quietly one of the best-written things in djkaktus's catalogue — a friendship becoming a relationship, conducted in scandium alloys and vacation scheduling, in which Kormaksson keeps gently asking whether she's going to visit her mother and she keeps not booking the flight. Then an untitled note: during BETA II she heard something in the feedback. She describes recognizing it via the cocktail party effect, and the paragraph that follows is a full inventory of a life — late-night phone calls, hospitals and crosswords, "a smile that would turn my insecurities to ash," "three soft knocks on the door to let me know she was there." It was her mother's voice. Her mother is dead. She was in the lab the night her mother tried to call her for the last time.

BETA IV (July 2015\) is the disaster, rendered as a raw control-room transcript. Andrews overdrives the wave-point inhibitor hunting for "a spike," Zatsepin escalates from professional to pleading, and mid-emergency Andrews stops talking to him entirely: "**Are you there? Can you hear me?**" — to which Zatsepin, bewildered over alarms, answers "…what? Who are you—". The array asymmetry destroys the prototype Kjarna and the Cleo array. But the last beat of the scene is Zatsepin pulling up the feedback log, fifteen seconds before probe cutoff, and saying: "Does that look like static to you? … Cheryl, I think you were right." Andrews is sentenced to a year of house arrest.

Addendum 7341.7 is the phone call, and it is the article's center. Kormaksson has listened to the recording and called her brother. She finally tells him about her mother: the daily calls, the six months it took her to get home, the month sleeping on the couch in her room, the three knocks that came to mean "I love you," and the last night — "she still tried to call me… I was in the lab, had been all night… Jon I never got to tell her I loved her." Then the argument: "There's a world out there where my mother doesn't die while I'm sitting in my office going over materials reports, and I know it's in there because that world does not exist right now." Kormaksson: "What you're talking about is the fundamental force in the universe. I don't believe that is within our reach." Andrews: "Isn't it, though?"

Then a personnel memo: at the conclusion of DELTA, the entire senior team is being scattered across Europe — Zatsepin to Site-409, Andrews to DEEPWELL 15 — while Kormaksson and LeClerc stay on to run the site. Zatsepin's furious reply accuses Kormaksson of gutting the team so "an Overseer can turn this site into their plaything… something they can gaze into and wax poetic," and asks him at least to tell Cheryl in person.

Incident 7341.01: during MYRKURHUGI's start-up on December 21st, an undetermined user made a terminal-level change to the energy allocation logic, submitted inside a three-second window while the logic evaluator was cycling. The flag was marked non-critical; Kormaksson had seven seconds to abort and did not. The boundary breached into Deck 3\. Andrews had dismissed her team to another observation point shortly before the trial. Research Assistant Ardnar Haraldsson came back to speak with her and survived only because he was standing exactly on the edge — the boundary took a section of his right arm and leg. Andrews was fully inside it. A footnote lists the four people who could have made that code change: Kormaksson, LeClerc (off-site), Gantry, and **Andrews**. Her probe was energized and inserted automatically, and remains locked inside SCP-7341.

The last of her notes, written before all this, closes the argument: the immaterial is not the unreal but "a boundaryway between what is and what is not… *what could be*." Dreams, songs, stories, "a desperate mother hoping for another day" — these are how the unreal becomes real. "When we gaze into the heart of oblivion, we are not giving ourselves to annihilation. We are asking the universe a question… In a sense, we are coming home… The void may seem like nothing; in truth — it is everything. We have only to reach out and touch the brush. / Ours are the hands of God. The universe is the canvas. The unreal is the paint. We are the artists." The final addendum is one line of data: on October 23rd, 2024, MYRKURHUGI registered a single moment of realitivistic feedback through her probe, coinciding with an uptick in auditory hallucinations among personnel near the boundary. Analysis inconclusive. Nothing before it. Nothing since.

**Themes:**

- Grief metabolized into research methodology; the scientific method as a séance with better instrumentation.  
- Guilt over the call not returned — the specific, small, unfixable kind.  
- Institutional capture of a discovery: the moment it works, the people who built it are reassigned so an Overseer can have it.  
- Nothing versus everything; annihilation versus possibility, held in the same object.  
- Ambiguity as mercy — the article will not tell you whether she was right.

**What makes it work:**

- The reveal is a footnote. The identity of the saboteur is never stated; the article just quietly lists four names and lets the reader notice that the woman who dismissed her team from the deck is one of them.  
- Two documentary voices — engineering prose and personal correspondence — braided so that each explains the other, culminating in a transcript where Andrews stops addressing her colleague mid-crisis.  
- The invented physics is coherent enough to argue with, which is what makes Andrews' heresy feel like a position rather than a delusion.  
- The three knocks. Introduced as a detail in her BETA II note, explained in the phone call, and never mentioned again — the article trusts you.  
- The last data point is deliberately worth nothing as evidence and everything as an ending.

**The horror:**

- Obliteration with no residue. Not death — deletion, with nothing left to grieve over and no body to bury.  
- The 11-meter boundary you cannot see, because your brain fills in an ordinary room where reality has stopped.  
- The suspicion, never confirmed, that a colleague deliberately engineered her own erasure and took Haraldsson's arm and leg with her.  
- The 2024 feedback moment and the hallucinations that came with it: something answered once, on the far side of a place that does not exist, and then stopped.

#### SCP-7450 — L is for Lamentations

**Object class:** Esoteric / Secondary Class: **Megiddo** / Disruption Class: Amida / Risk Class: Critical. Assigned Site: N/A. Author: **djkaktus**. Part of the **SCP ANTHOLOGY** alphabet series (between "K is for Keystone" and "M is for Moonlight"). Epigraph from Lamentations 1:11.

**Summary:** The Classification Committee voted 3–0 to move SCP-7450 from Apollyon to a new class, **Megiddo**, "to distinguish between entities or anomalies that will inevitably result in the end of the world, and those that already have." SCP-7450 is uncontained and uncontainable; the Foundation states plainly that it no longer possesses the resources or personnel to contain it, and doubts any other organization does either, if any still exist. What remains is the MAGOG Protocol: never leave your site during a transience, never travel into a predicted path within 72 hours, never look at or listen to SCP-7450. Violation — purposeful or unintentional — is punishable by summary execution by any other member of the Foundation.

SCP-7450 designates four humanoid eigenweapons that ascended to godhood on December 13th, 2028\. They stand between 18.9km and 23.5km tall, with six avian wings, a single right arm holding a curved sickle, and legs that move as if walking while the body levitates. Their faces and voices cannot be rendered safe for observation by any known means, human or AI. They are named, in descending order of size: **Pallas, Judith, Rachel, and Argine** — the four queens of the French playing-card deck. They circumnavigate the Earth endlessly at different speeds; Rachel completes an orbit in 416 days, Pallas in 468\. Anyone who sees or hears one is compelled to follow it and cannot be stopped short of total bodily destruction. Followers stop aging, become resistant to damage and decay, begin to resemble the entity they follow, walk until their feet wear down to their ankles, and then levitate alongside the rest. And they take up the vocalizations, which spread the condition onward.

Two Overseer addresses tell the public half of the story. November 2nd, 2025: **O5-1 Armond Katchatrian** breaks two hundred years of secrecy to announce 981-AJAX, a hostile extraplanar entity that appeared over the southern sky on October 14th and is already destroying Johannesburg. The tone is a government address — urging calm, promising cooperation, "we will, as we always have, stand as the stalwart shield." December 11th, 2028: **O5-1 Rhys Olsons** announces defeat. "We have broken up our containment cells and released every possible horror onto the world in the hopes of balancing the threat of AJAX. We have failed. AJAX will soon open its final eye… Take this time to be with your families. We have preparations in place… buried deep within the Earth… Please pray for their survival, these last four mothers of humanity."

The rest is a personal journal kept in an underground site from 2032 to 2036 by an agent signing off as **Troy**. He tracks the sisters' orbits, monitors which sites still answer, and keeps a colleague named Henderson chained in a cell below him because Rachel turned him — Henderson has chewed through his gag, scratched his hands down to stumps against the walls, and Troy eventually lets him out because "he seemed happier. Still writhing and screaming, but in a more pleasant way." He corresponds with Sophia, who is down to twenty-three people, until she stops writing. He receives a daily photograph of Alto Clef's face for two years.

The December 2032 entry is the explanation. In 1996, after a close call with SCP-239, the Foundation buried four girls at four DEEPWELL sites as an apocalypse contingency, "under a mountain of sorcery and technology and given wombs that would operate for a thousand years." SCP-2000 made the project redundant, so it was scrapped — but nobody dug the girls up. "I wonder sometimes if there was ever a meeting about it, or if they just forgot they were there." When AJAX wrecked SCP-2000, Kain found them and started the machines again. And then Armond told the entire world about them in the same speech in which he told the world it was going to die. "What did we think was going to happen? Had we really not learned about what belief does? … Eight billion humans crying out for a god to save them. Eight billion humans screaming, crying, begging… We lamented our shared fate, and our gods woke up. / But they weren't built to be divine, they didn't know what was going to happen to them… When they woke up they were gods. **They were thirteen years old.**" Their first act was reflex — they scattered AJAX across the atmosphere. Their second was fear: "gods need worship, and worshippers." The Foundation's cognitohazard defenses lasted seconds, "like rice paper before a blow torch." Half its people went to the sisters; another third simply put down their work, walked out the door, and waited.

The final entry, April 19th, 2036: out of food, water gone, Pallas closing. Troy has burned out his eardrums and taken his own eyes out — "I thought burning out my eardrums would be the hard part, but with the benefit of hindsight I should have foreseen how bad it would be to take my own eyes out. Hindsight\! A dark joke." He is going to try to reach Site-89 and then Site-104. "I don't care if it takes a life living in darkness, I'll figure this out." And then, to the man whose daily photograph turned out to be a cardboard cutout: "you can kindly go fuck yourself. Two years you had my heart leaping at the sight of a cardboard cutout. Motherfucker. / No time to cry. Wish me luck."

**Themes:**

- Belief as an ontological force, and mass desperation as a weapon that fires backwards.  
- The Foundation destroyed by its own transparency at exactly the moment it finally told the truth.  
- Children weaponized and then forgotten in the ground for thirty years.  
- Apocalypse as *duration* — not an event but four years, then eight, of narrowing.  
- Loneliness: the whole back half is one man talking to people who have stopped answering.

**What makes it work:**

- The Megiddo classification is a single invented word that reframes the entire genre, and it's introduced in a procedural paragraph.  
- The two Overseer addresses are pitched as real public-crisis rhetoric, and the second one is unbearable precisely because it stays polite.  
- The pivot from clinical description to Troy's journal converts an XK-scenario into one voice in a room, which is the only scale at which it can actually hurt.  
- The card-deck naming (Pallas, Judith, Rachel, Argine) is never explained in-text, and quietly ties the piece to the suit-marked Demon cycle.  
- The cardboard cutout. A last joke, at the very end, that lands as grief.

**The horror:**

- The compulsion is a mercy that reads as damnation: the followers are ageless, undying, and content, and they will never stop walking.  
- Blinding and deafening yourself as a *survival strategy*, described in the tone of a logistics decision.  
- The sisters may have been terrified. The article raises this and never resolves it: "Were they scared too?"  
- The moral trap — the four girls did exactly what they were built and begged to do. They saved everyone from AJAX. That is why everyone is dead.  
- The last line is a man walking outside, blind and deaf, into the path of something 20km tall, still intending to solve the problem.

#### SCP-7670 — The Light That Blinds

**Object class:** Euclid / Disruption Class: Vlam / Risk Class: Danger. Contained in a secure anomalous animal pen at Site-19. Author: **djkaktus**. A short piece — description-only, no addenda, no logs.

**Summary:** SCP-7670 is a goat (*Capra hircus*) found in the Armenian highlands of eastern Turkey near the village of Kaçmaz — and, specifically, **its single left eye**. The goat has no right eye. Containment is unusually severe for a farm animal: no personnel may interact with it directly; transport requires a windowless, acoustically insulated vessel sealed for the entire journey; feeding is three times daily; and cell maintenance must be performed either by automated systems or by **deafened and blinded D-class personnel**. Video transmission is not currently believed to be cognitohazardous. Auditory transmission is "a certified vector and must be avoided at all costs." Anyone who reports nightmares, visions, or auditory hallucinations while on maintenance duty is transferred into the research team's custody as a subject.

The goat itself is functionally immortal and entirely resistant to destruction. It needs no food or water, though giving it food and water makes it sleep more, and while it sleeps the eye stays closed. It is docile and strangely aware of being watched: when it is not eating or sleeping, it is usually staring directly at the hidden cameras in its cell.

Looking at the eye produces a consistent cluster of experiences, given as a bare list: visions of bright figures; visions of outstretched hands; visions of intense bleeding; feelings of panic and paranoia; of being watched; of being followed; of betrayal; of being judged; of falling; of bright light; of intense heat. Amnestics make it worse — subjects keep experiencing all of it while losing any memory of where it came from, which drives anxiety sharply upward, and the treatment is no longer recommended. The auditory hallucinations are described as the more traumatic component: subjects will first try to deafen themselves, and on discovering that the voices continue, will attempt suicide or, "in more dramatic cases, will attempt to pull apart their own skull to gain access to the source of the sound inside their heads."

The best documentation comes from the collected writings of **Sahag Zadian**, a 16th-century Armenian monk, believed to be the first to recognize the entity as anomalous — largely descriptions of paranoia, fear, and hallucinations of falling and burning. Earlier regional writings recording outbreaks of unattributed religious mania in what is now eastern and southeastern Turkey are now attributed to the same source. The final line of the article: "while SCP-7670 itself is genetically a goat, its left eye is not. Genetic sequencing of material taken from the eye has been inconclusive."

The article never states what the eye is. The evidence it hands the reader is unambiguous in shape and deliberately unconfirmed in fact: bright figures, outstretched hands, betrayal, judgment, falling, light, heat, and a goat — the scapegoat, Azazel — carrying a single eye that has watched something it cannot stop seeing. Whether that is the eye of a fallen angel, of the accuser, or of God is exactly what the file declines to sequence.

**Themes:**

- Revelation as injury — being *seen* by something, rather than seeing it.  
- Guilt and judgment transmitted as a sensory condition with no attached memory or crime.  
- Scapegoating, literalized: the sin lodged in an animal that cannot die and cannot put it down.  
- The limits of Foundation epistemology; the sequencer runs and comes back with nothing.

**What makes it work:**

- The containment procedures do all the characterization. "Deafened and blinded D-class personnel" tells you more about the threat than any test log could.  
- The vision list is presented as clinical data with no interpretation offered, which lets the reader assemble the theology and own it.  
- Restraint. There is no interview, no reveal, no incident — the article ends one clause after the only sentence that matters.  
- The goat's docility and its habit of staring at the hidden cameras: one image that makes a farm animal unbearable.

**The horror:**

- Amnestics fail in the worst possible direction — the terror stays, the cause is deleted, and the subject is left afraid of nothing they can name.  
- People trying to open their own skulls to reach a sound that is inside them.  
- Auditory contagion: a recording is enough. The thing can reach anyone through a speaker.  
- Something is looking back through an animal that will exist forever, and it knew where the cameras were.
