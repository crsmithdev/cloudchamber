#### SCP-049 — The Plague Doctor

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-049 is a humanoid entity roughly 1.9 meters tall that presents as a medieval plague doctor — beaked ceramic mask, heavy black robes, wide-brimmed hat. The critical detail, delivered flatly in the Description, is that the costume is not a costume: the robes and mask have grown into and are largely indistinguishable from the entity's own tissue, though X-rays confirm a human skeletal structure underneath. It speaks numerous languages fluently, is unfailingly courteous, and refers to Foundation researchers as colleagues. It is held in a Standard Secure Humanoid Containment Cell at Site-19's Research Sector-02, requires sedation and a Class III Humanoid Restriction Harness for transport, and — in one of the article's best small touches — lavender has been found effective at calming its aggressive episodes.

SCP-049's obsession is a condition it calls "the Pestilence," which it perceives everywhere and which no Foundation instrument can detect. When it decides a subject is afflicted, skin contact causes complete and immediate biological cessation — the subject simply dies. SCP-049 then performs crude, extensive surgery on the corpse using instruments from an anomalously capacious bag, producing an SCP-049-2 instance: a reanimated body with no memory, no higher cognition, typically inert until provoked or directed. SCP-049 considers each one cured. Observation logs (049.OL series) show it working through mammalian corpses with meticulous documentation — an orangutan that disappoints it ("I fear my early optimism was misplaced"), a bovine carcass into which it injects what it calls "essences of the humors" over several days before reanimating the inverted body with electrical stimulation. Its own journals remain undeciphered.

The article's spine is the relationship with Dr. Raymond Hamm, the researcher who conducts the early interviews and who is treated by SCP-049 as a peer. On 16 April 2017 Hamm enters containment for a routine interview; SCP-049 becomes anxious about Hamm's health, then kills him. Because of a protocol failure the body goes undiscovered for three hours — long enough. In the follow-up interview Dr. Theron Sherman confronts the entity, which is serene: "He is cured." Pressed on the fact that the resulting instance is a vegetable, SCP-049 becomes indignant: "I have saved it\! You would allow this world to slip back into the despair of disease and death." A later interview with Dr. Elijah Itkin finds no remorse — Hamm's death was "regrettable, but in the face of the Pestilence we must be swift, doctor" — and a request to resume work on living subjects. Told this will not be permitted, SCP-049 closes the article with: "Oh doctor, I wouldn't be so sure."

**Themes:**

- Medicine practiced sincerely and catastrophically; the physician who cannot be reasoned out of his diagnosis  
- Faith in method over evidence — a pre-scientific worldview colliding with the Foundation's clinical one  
- The unfalsifiable disease: a Pestilence that "presents and progresses in unforeseeable fashions" can never be disproved  
- Courtesy as a mask for atrocity; the collegial register between two doctors, one of whom is a murderer  
- Cure as violation — the body preserved by being emptied

**What makes it work:**

- The mask-grown-into-the-flesh detail is stated once, in passing, and never dwelt on — the article trusts the reader to feel it  
- SCP-049 is never given a villain's voice. Every line it speaks is reasonable, warm, and professionally engaged, which is what makes it unbearable  
- The Hamm arc uses the documentation format as tragedy: a researcher's growing rapport is charted across interview logs, and the format's own coldness delivers his death as a protocol failure with a three-hour gap  
- The escalation is procedural rather than dramatic — the horror advances by the revocation of privileges and the addition of restrictions, not by an outburst

**The horror:**

- Being diagnosed by something whose diagnostic criteria are invisible and whose treatment is death  
- The SCP-049-2 instances: the person is gone but the body is up and moving, and the thing that did it is proud  
- Absolute good faith. SCP-049 is not lying and cannot be argued with, because it has been right, in its own terms, for five hundred years  
- The closing line, which converts the entire containment apparatus into something SCP-049 is merely tolerating

#### SCP-186 — To End All Wars

**Object class:** Euclid (with Level 5/186 containment restrictions)

**Summary:** SCP-186 is three things. SCP-186-A is a 3.9-meter oak coffin containing an intricate perception-altering mechanism — gears, copper tubing, glass vials, bellows, pistons — that cannot be radiographed without triggering activation. A brass placard reads *Ouvrir à minuit* ("Open at midnight"), signed Dr. Jean Durand. It is held in a vault at DEEPWELL 5 under continuous monitoring; on uncontrolled activation, Level 14 memetic-resistant personnel are to intervene, and if that fails, facility liquidation is pre-authorized. Activation induces acute catatonic shock in everyone within range, with roughly 7% fatality, and the effective radius expands continuously while active. No survivor has ever been able to communicate what they perceived. SCP-186-B is the site of the undocumented Battle of Königsberg Forest (July–August 1917), where German forces used anomalous weapons against Russian troops with near-total casualties on both sides; the ground is now perimeter-secured near Kaliningrad under an unexploded-ordnance cover story and remains temporally and structurally unstable. SCP-186-C is the catalogue of 843 recovered weapons.

The weapons catalogue is the article's most quotable atrocity: razor wire that constricts and flays autonomously; gas that either permanently continues life functions or heightens the senses past endurance; incendiaries that spare nervous tissue; fragmentation grenades that cause eyes to grow across whatever they strike; artillery whose explosions are soundless and leave permanent auditory hallucination. They were designed by Dr. Jean Durand, a French anatomist and field surgeon, and field-tested at the insistence of the Hungarian military attaché Mátyás Nemeş. The correspondence between them is the article's engine. Nemeş (October 1916\) argues that war ends only when one side cannot resist, and that a single sufficiently terrible instrument would impose peace through fear. Durand rejects this: he does not want terror, he wants *understanding* — to make war's cost manifest rather than abstract, to render resistance unthinkable through knowledge. Nemeş's May 1917 report is the pivot: the demonstration succeeded beyond expectation, but General von Kirchbach received it as a tactical advantage, not a deterrent. Durand's September 1917 reply draws the conclusion that damns the whole century — "a weapon that horrifies in a single instance will be turned by men into a tool of power."

So he builds something else. His final letter, to his colleague Dr. Ansel Cairns, explains SCP-186-A: it does not kill. It takes the final moments of every life lost to war and braids them into an unending stream of witness, imposed simultaneously on every human mind — forced, universal, inescapable empathy, from which no abstraction and no rationalization can survive. And then Durand admits he cannot do it. He is terrified of his own machine, cannot bring himself to test it fully on himself, and hands the decision to Cairns: destroy it or use it. The letter is unsigned, and closes: "May whatever grace still lingers in this century, if there is any at all, guide your hand better than it guided mine." Around this sits the SCP-186-B research log — decades of manifestations (a self-regenerating disfigured soldier that shoots itself over and over, 1936; a humanoid built of rotting bodies collecting human fragments, 1945; sealed caskets holding bodies with rosaries made of teeth, 1959; 216 WWI parachutes drifting with no aircraft and no shadows, 1987\) — and a recovered letter from a dying Russian soldier, Misha, to his beloved Anya, describing his body changing into something not human, naming Durand as the man responsible, warning her that if he ever appears in their village in peacetime she should run, and resolving to end himself while he is still himself: "Your ribbon is in my left boot. Another promise, then: I will carry you wherever I am going."

**Themes:**

- The deterrence paradox: every weapon built to end war becomes a weapon for fighting one  
- Empathy as a weapon of mass destruction — moral clarity imposed by force  
- The scientist's culpability and his failure of nerve; the Oppenheimer shape without the Oppenheimer name  
- The unburied dead: a battlefield that keeps replaying itself for a hundred years  
- The individual soldier's letter set against the theorists' correspondence — the cost that the arguments are actually about

**What makes it work:**

- The article is built almost entirely from primary documents. The Foundation barely editorializes; the reader assembles Durand's arc from letters he wrote to other people  
- Withholding the one thing that matters: nobody knows what SCP-186-A actually shows you, because nobody who has seen it can speak again. Durand's letter is the only description, and it is a description of an *intention*, not an effect  
- The weapons catalogue is written as dry ordnance inventory, which makes the eye-growing grenades land harder than any prose description could  
- Misha's letter is placed to undercut the philosophy entirely — the reader has just read two men theorizing about the cost of war, and then meets one unit of that cost, writing to his girl

**The horror:**

- A device that cannot be disarmed by anyone who understands it, sitting in a vault with a note telling you when to open it  
- The 7% who die and the 93% who survive and can never say why  
- The realization that Durand's machine might actually work, and that "working" means every human being on Earth simultaneously drowning in every war death ever suffered  
- Durand's absence. He built it, he could not use it, and he is not accounted for — the coffin is closed and someone, eventually, will be curious

#### SCP-527 — Mr. Fish

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-527 is a humanoid, 1.67 meters tall, whose head is that of a gold barb (*Puntius semifasciolatus*). That is the entirety of the anomaly. It breathes air, speaks normally, cannot breathe underwater, cannot communicate with fish, and possesses no other anomalous property whatsoever. Containment consists of a standard humanoid domicile at Site-19 with no additional measures. It was located in Boston in 2002 and transferred to Site-19 in 2004\. A tattoo on its left foot reads "Mr. Fish, from Little Misters ® by Dr. Wondertainment."

The two addenda supply the pathos. In the initial interview (Addendum 527.1), Dr. Baker works through the obvious questions — underwater breathing, aquatic telepathy — and SCP-527 patiently says no to all of them. It has been this way since birth. Asked what it can do, it answers that this is all there is: "You're looking at it." It then names its siblings from the product line — "Lie, Stripes, Hot, Sweetie, they got all the good stuff" — with the flat resignation of someone who knows exactly where he ranks. Addendum 527.2 is a Dr. Wondertainment promotional checklist for the Little Misters collection: twenty entities, each with a distinctive anomalous gimmick, and SCP-527 is number 12\. He is a collectible with no feature.

**Themes:**

- Being manufactured, catalogued, and found unremarkable  
- Mundanity as its own kind of exile — a monster too boring to be a monster  
- The Dr. Wondertainment mythos as a factory that produced a person nobody wanted

**What makes it work:**

- The containment procedures do the characterization: "no additional procedures are necessary" is a judgement about his worth, written in the Foundation's own indifferent voice  
- The interview declines every escalation the format promises; the joke setup arrives and then simply stops being a joke  
- The promotional checklist is the punchline and the knife — the reader gets to compare him to nineteen better products

**The horror:**

- Not fear but a low, specific sadness: a self-aware being who knows he was made as the dud in a toy line  
- Indefinite containment for someone who has done nothing and can do nothing; there is no reason to hold him and no reason to let him go

#### SCP-715 — My Face That I May Be

**Object class:** Safe → Keter → Safe (currently Safe)

**Summary:** SCP-715 is a 1972 Sony photo booth, recovered from a shopping mall, currently in a secure storage locker at Site-81 with no personnel interaction authorized. It functions as an ordinary photo booth: insert coins, sit, be photographed. The strips it produces are heavily distorted. The anomaly is what accompanies use: each activation causes a corresponding entity — designated SCP-715-A — to manifest in an extradimensional pit beneath the original mall site. The SCP-715-A instances are humanoid and hideously deformed, covered in extensive lacerations and large growths, faces ruined; they claw at the fleshy walls of the pit, which secrete a corrosive substance, trying to climb out. The person who used the booth walks out apparently unharmed; they are designated SCP-715-B, classified as anomalous, and — under the Keter-era protocols — subject to termination.

The reversal comes through the restricted addenda and it is a formal trick as much as a narrative one. Researcher Gerald Patton used SCP-715 himself. Afterward he began behaving out of character: he declined a prestigious transfer to another project and avoided a mandatory reality-distortion sweep — the actions of someone evading detection. Site-81's director, Dr. Agatha Rights, had covert surveillance installed in his quarters using a Fulmann-Breaker Anomalous Optical Enhancement device, a filter designed to strip out local anomalous effects. What the footage showed was that under the filter, Patton looked exactly like the creatures in the pit. Which means the filter is not showing the monster — the *unaided eye* is showing the monster. The perceptual layer is on the world, not on them.

Rights' report is the moment the article's whole architecture inverts: "they're humans. They're all humans. They're not like those things they appear to be when we look at them… we've been shooting them." Every SCP-715-A in the pit is a person. Every SCP-715-B the Foundation terminated was a person. The response from above is the second, colder ending: no rescue operation, no investigation, no correction. Leadership decides to stop looking — the Foundation will not acknowledge the problem and will not pursue \-B instances, because "it would be better for normalcy if we just let them be for now." The object class drops back to Safe. The final incident section is rendered as DATA EXPUNGED.

**Themes:**

- Self-image as the anomaly; a machine that shows you what you are and thereby makes it true for everyone else  
- Institutional error at a scale that cannot be admitted, and therefore is not  
- The reclassification Safe → Keter → Safe as a record of the Foundation deciding to stop caring  
- Perception as the actual object of containment — the filter, not the booth, is the dangerous instrument

**What makes it work:**

- The reveal is delivered as an operational memo, not a scene — a director realizing mid-report what her own footage means, and the prose degrading into plain sentences as she gets there  
- The article withholds by making the reader complicit in the misclassification: you read the \-A description as a monster description because the document told you to  
- The behavioral tell (a researcher who suddenly avoids screenings) is the only clue given before the reveal, and it reads as suspicion rather than fear on first pass  
- Ending on redaction rather than resolution — the article's last gesture is the institution closing a door

**The horror:**

- The pit: people, still themselves, clawing at walls that dissolve them, invisible as people to anyone looking  
- Discovering the termination logs were an execution record  
- The cover-up that is not even a cover-up — just a decision that normalcy is worth more than the people already down there  
- That it is not reversible, and the Foundation has quietly agreed not to try

#### SCP-1160 — Effective Containment

**Object class:** Keter/Safe

**Summary:** SCP-1160 is a bird-like entity beneath a mountain on an unnamed island, originally around 85 meters in length. It appeared circa 1765 and attacked local fishing villages; islanders called it *El Ratonero*. A recovered manuscript records the escalation — "The Buzzard came again last night, larger than before. Its cry we heard first, the great gnashing of a terrible demon" — and the villagers' growing understanding that with each visit "he is more and we are less." It resists conventional weapons and any explosive under 300 kilotons, regenerates rapidly, and possesses multiple insect-like optical organs affording it 360-degree vision.

The mechanism that makes the article is the inverse relationship between the entity's power and human knowledge of it. The more people are aware of SCP-1160's true nature, the smaller and weaker it becomes, with the effect manifesting over several hours rather than instantly. The Foundation discovered this by accident and catastrophically: on 13 August 1943, routine amnestic cycling of Class-D personnel at Site 77 wiped a block of human awareness, and SCP-1160 immediately surged in size and aggression, attacking the facility and a neighboring village with casualties on both sides. The Foundation's most fundamental reflex — erase knowledge of the anomalous — was directly feeding the thing.

The solution, Protocol Tango-77 ("Saturday Morning"), is the punchline and the point. From 1953, a Foundation front company called Standard Products has sold a breakfast cereal, Super Coco Pows, whose mascot "Bradbury Buzzard" is a cartoon rendering of SCP-1160, with memetic triggers embedded in the packaging art that install specific subconscious awareness of the real entity in every consumer — children preferentially, via advertising. Decades of continuous cereal sales have ground the 85-meter horror down to roughly 25 centimeters. The article's quiet closing observation is about how the awareness has changed shape: early consumers described the character as "mean" and frightening, while contemporary survey respondents call him a "mischievous little bird" — the cognition is maintained but diluted, calibrated to hold the entity small without letting anyone actually understand what they are looking at.

**Themes:**

- Containment by mass culture; the Foundation as advertiser  
- The inversion of the Foundation's core doctrine — secrecy as the thing that empowers the monster  
- Childhood, cereal boxes, and cartoon mascots as load-bearing infrastructure for the world  
- Knowledge as a physical force with a measurable dose-response curve

**What makes it work:**

- The Keter/Safe dual classification is the whole thesis compressed into the header — it is Safe *only while the protocol runs*  
- The 1943 incident is the article's proof-of-concept scene, and it works because it is a bureaucratic routine (scheduled amnestic cycling) causing a massacre  
- The tonal swerve from an 18th-century village manuscript to breakfast-cereal marketing copy, played entirely straight  
- The mascot-perception survey data is the last detail, and it is the one that reframes everything: the containment is drifting

**The horror:**

- The world you know is being held up by a cartoon bird, and if a cereal brand fails, an 85-meter thing comes back  
- Brand deterioration classified as a Level II emergency — the most banal possible failure state for an apocalypse  
- Every child who ate that cereal is a load-bearing element of the containment, permanently, without consent  
- The mascot getting cuter over time means the entity is slowly being let off the leash

#### SCP-1730 — What Happened to Site-13?

**Object class:** Neutralized

**Summary:** SCP-1730 is a massive underground Foundation facility discovered in Big Bend Ranch State Park, Texas, claiming to be Site-13 — a Site the Foundation lost track of decades earlier and believed to be in Alaska. It is found abandoned, structurally damaged, and still powered, in total containment failure, with an unknown number of anomalies loose inside and an unknown number of human personnel still alive somewhere in the deep levels. Containment procedures are minimal and read as a quarantine of something the Foundation does not understand: a 2km perimeter, a 1km quarantine zone, and Class VII Hazardous Contact preparation for anyone entering. The body of the article is a sequence of exploration and extraction logs from successive Mobile Task Forces, and its structure is the horror — each team goes deeper, learns more, and dies, and the reader assembles the truth from fragments none of the teams live long enough to assemble themselves.

The teams: MTF Charlie Yukon's recovered log, from the original breach, records parasitic entities entering personnel through the mouth and consuming them from the inside, and ends with someone mentioning "Thresher." An automated emergency message logs cascading system failures — electrical, fire suppression, flood control, the on-site nuclear device unresponsive — and Thresher Protocol activated. MTF D-12 "Mud Slingers" descends into the basements, finds hollowed corpses, encounters an entity producing intense white light, and is lost; two members are found suspended upside down with blood pouring from their facial orifices. MTF Y-24 "Gulliver's Travelers" reaches the power station and finds human bodies wired to a superheated reactor core, arranged to spell the phrase *What happened to Site-13?* — which turns out to be a pyroclastic cognitohazard: conscious recognition of the symbols causes the viewer to burn from the inside out, smoke from the ears, then a pressure wave. Y-24 is lost. An unmanned drone is sent instead and successfully pulls terminal data, but the human team watching the drone's feed sees the symbols and dies the same way. MTF Z-9 "Mole Rats" descends methodically, watches a member die with a leech in his throat and another mercy-kill him, and reaches a vast chamber containing burning humanoids and a single enormous creature carpeted in smaller ones before the link is lost. MTF AP-3 "Game Wardens" gets furthest and loses a man, Noah, to a spatially anomalous humanoid whose distortion field destroys matter caught inside it.

What the fragments assemble into is the actual story, and it is not about monsters. Site-13 under Director Elliott Emerson ran the "Mortuary Protocol": humanoid anomalies were administratively reclassified as non-human, which removed them from Ethics Committee jurisdiction, and were then experimented on and destroyed at industrial scale, the remains fed into a body pit on Level 5 and incinerated. AP-3 makes contact via an internal broadcast with a corrupted entity called Bobble — a formerly numbered SCP renumbered specifically to prevent identification, most of its body eaten by video static — which calls Site-13 a "meat grinder" and describes watching Emerson's work, including the burning of the Library. Dr. Hadley, a senior researcher, broke. His letter is the article's center: he had been protecting a child anomaly named Elijah, a boy of minimal cognition who survived on blood, drawing it through skin like a leech, and when Emerson ordered him burned with the rest, Hadley falsified Elijah's record so that the pit's fires would not incinerate him but agitate him. He engineered the breach that destroyed Site-13 on purpose. To Emerson: "This is your death camp, Elliott. You made your bed, and now you get to die in it."

The Thresher is the second half of the answer. It is a reality-bending device requiring 55GW, designed as a final containment measure: it displaces the entire facility into an alternate reality. Engineering correspondence flags it as "still wildly unstable" and warns that using it could destabilize local reality both here and wherever the site lands — which is a plausible explanation for how a Site last known to be in Alaska is now under Texas. Hadley's letter frames Emerson's real choice as facing consequences or fleeing sideways out of the universe. MTF Tau-5 "Samsara," cybernetically augmented, conducts the final extraction and finds around twenty to thirty surviving personnel barricaded in a research wing — including Z-9 remnants under Captain Hollis, and the Temporal Studies survivors whose leader Dr. Mohammad Scott had been broadcasting an automated distress loop long after his own death. Tau-5's operator Onru discovers that the leeches are a single telepathic network serving one enormous parent organism below, and that there is one corridor the leeches avoid, moving through it very, *very* quietly. In the silent zone, near cryogenics, the team encounters an entity with eighteen arms, thirty-six forearms and seventy-two hands, built around a circular structure covered in glowing glyphs, with a severely burned human figure labelled EMERSON bound to its head, producing a continuous agonized whine. Tau-5's transmission degrades and cuts off mid-sentence. The article's last statement is administrative: due to the events detailed in Addendum 1730.9, SCP-1730 has been reclassified as NEUTRALIZED; additional research efforts are ongoing. The reader is never told what happened.

**Themes:**

- The Foundation as perpetrator — a death camp run with requisition forms and promotion tracks  
- Administrative reclassification as the enabling technology of atrocity (call them non-human and Ethics has no standing)  
- Whistleblowing as mass murder; Hadley is the moral center of the article and he killed everyone in it  
- Institutional amnesia — a Site the organization lost, in every sense  
- Descent as structure: physical depth, chronological depth, and moral depth are the same axis

**What makes it work:**

- The title is the cognitohazard. The question the reader is asking is the thing that kills you for asking it, and the article makes you read it repeatedly  
- Serial-POV exploration logs where each team's failure is the next team's intelligence; the reader out-knows every character  
- Radical withholding at the climax — the final log truncates mid-word and the neutralization is reported as a classification change with no narrative. The most consequential event in the article is a blank  
- Emerson's fate is delivered as a physical description in a field log with no commentary at all: a burned man on the head of a many-armed thing, whining. No one on the team knows who he is  
- The horror is retrofitted; the monsters are established for pages before the reader learns the monsters are the *consequence* and the villain had an office

**The horror:**

- Body horror with a bureaucratic delivery system: leeches down the throat, hollowed corpses, bodies wired to a reactor as signage  
- A phrase that kills you when you understand it — comprehension itself as the fatal act  
- The survivors: thirty people reduced to twelve, then a dead man's automated voice still asking for help on a loop  
- A machine that solves containment failure by throwing the whole building into another reality, built by people who knew it was unstable  
- The unanswered question. NEUTRALIZED with no explanation means someone decided the answer should not exist, which is exactly what Emerson would have done

#### SCP-1864 — The Lonely Liar

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-1864-Alpha is an island roughly 80km southwest of Coats Island in Hudson Bay that only exists during the new moon. Foundation vessels hold a 5km exclusion zone and monitor the water; Observational Site-1864 houses personnel between manifestations, and here is the containment procedure that is itself the horror — staff stationed on the island cannot leave when it is not manifesting, and anyone who tries to depart is simply redirected back. Exploration is permitted only between dusk and dawn on new moon nights. Lethal force is pre-authorized against SCP-1864-3 instances. The island holds an abandoned mining colony, derelict for over seventy-five years, and within a deteriorated hospital building is an entrance to a labyrinth of roughly 15 square kilometers — larger than the island containing it. Above the entrance is an inscription in German: "Follow the pathways of the lonely liar / Deep into the sunken passages he rots / Eyes sewn shut with his own wicked hairs / Locked in the silent sick pit."

Three designations live inside. SCP-1864-1 is a near-humanoid entity 4.3 meters long, its elongated torso fused into the floor, with a single extensible arm-like appendage and a face carrying four additional eyes. It wears the remains of a modified white coat, speaks German, is generally non-hostile, and will not permit anyone near the central pool it guards. SCP-1864-2 is at the bottom of that pool, 120 meters down, sealed beneath a 91cm steel grate; thermal imaging shows a humanoid figure around 130cm tall curled in a small chamber. SCP-1864-3 are Class IV hostile entities that periodically manifest throughout the labyrinth and try to reach the pool. They cannot be recorded on video or audio, they alter the memories of those who see them, and no two witness descriptions agree — exploration teams have reported seeing something around a corner and then declined to discuss it afterward.

SCP-1864-1 identifies himself as Dr. Heinrich Boff, and the interview logs are the article. The colony was originally a German research station studying the spatial anomaly. Its purpose changed when a fishing vessel pulled a sickly child out of the ice during the First World War — a boy called Pan Hun. He healed. "Those who beheld him could not grow ill. Those who were ill and beheld him were healed." The scientists concluded he was a messenger from God, sent to deliver peace eternal and victory to the Fatherland, and they built their work around him. Then, in Boff's account, Pan Hun lied. What the lie was, Boff never says — the article's central and permanent withholding. Whatever it was, it revealed what he actually is. "Little Pan Hun, the lonely liar. We put him in a box when he lied to us, and dumped the box into the pit." Boff sewed the child's sick eyes shut with his own hair, sealed the grate, and has not left since.

Everyone else at the station became SCP-1864-3. They no longer remember the world above; they exist only to reach the grate and pull Pan Hun down into the darkness with them. Boff, fused to the floor above the pool, spends eternity keeping them off it. He is clear about the arithmetic of what he has become and what it is worth: "What Pan Hun did to me was nothing. I am still a man, although a man lost." And clear about the stakes if the grate ever opens — the world, he says, is exactly what Pan Hun would consume, the way he consumed them. The Foundation's role in all of this is to periodically send three-person teams into an impossible maze on the one night a month it exists, to shoot things that cannot be photographed, in support of a seventy-five-year-old German guarding a sewn-shut child at the bottom of a well.

**Themes:**

- The saviour who is the predator; miracle and consumption as the same act  
- Sacrifice with no end and no witness — Boff's vigil as the only thing between the world and the pit  
- Wartime desperation as the vector: a nation losing a war decides a strange child is God's gift  
- Containment inherited rather than designed; the Foundation is a caretaker for someone else's atrocity  
- Memory as a defense the monsters have already breached — you cannot even keep the shape of what you saw

**What makes it work:**

- The verse inscription supplies the entire plot in four lines before the reader can decode any of it, then every subsequent reveal snaps one line into place  
- The lie is never disclosed. The article's title, its central mystery, and its emotional weight all rest on a piece of information deliberately withheld forever  
- SCP-1864-3's unrecordability is a formal move as much as a fictional one: the documentation-based format is defeated by an entity that cannot be documented, and the article shows you the format failing  
- Boff's voice — tired, self-aware, unrepentant, and still using the professional register of a doctor describing a procedure while describing sewing a child's eyelids shut  
- The stranded-personnel clause in the containment procedures is dropped in early and casually, and only makes sense in retrospect

**The horror:**

- A child in a box at the bottom of 120 meters of water, blinded, alive, patient  
- Being posted to the island and discovering the island will not let you leave  
- Things you have already seen that your brain has already edited, and a colleague who will not tell you what was around the corner  
- The knowledge that Boff will eventually fail — there is no version of this where a man fused to a floor holds out forever, and the article never suggests otherwise

#### SCP-1994 — Rot

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-1994 is a three-part designation. SCP-1994-1 is a serum, held under maximum security at Site-93, with additional vials believed to be uncontained. SCP-1994-2 is the anomalous tooth growth it produces, requiring hazmat protocols and biweekly air scrubber replacement. SCP-1994-3 are infected humans, held in humanoid containment vaults with full life support. MTF Tau-13 monitors Moscow for outbreaks. The serum induces human teeth to grow outside the oral cavity, anywhere on the body, and the article documents the progression across five stages over roughly seventeen weeks: two weeks of asymptomatic cellular change; a breakout phase in which cysts rupture the skin and reveal developing teeth; maturation, in which the teeth affix permanently to the skin and spread; decay, in which the teeth undergo inexplicably rapid caries and disintegrate, shedding an airborne infectious powder; and expiration, in which the subject dies of organ failure and an inability to take in nutrition. The powder infects everyone nearby. The disease's terminal stage is its transmission mechanism.

The recovered documentation supplies the origin, and it is entirely human. Dr. Rasmin Yelkov was commissioned by a cosmetic dentist, Dr. Grigori, to manufacture perfect teeth for wealthy clients. Pursuing this, Yelkov encountered an unnamed practitioner who kept small humanoid, butterfly-like creatures — "tooth fairies" — which draw enamel from human bone as food, and who claimed to have met them while traveling and to have learned their language from one named Isabella. Yelkov's journal records the transaction with total flatness: "I have killed the man. He turned down my offer to purchase some of the fayries for my own research." He took roughly thirty specimens. His early findings note the creatures are "able to infest the human digestive tract and create human teeth, including all basic structures. Usually fatal to the host." He isolated the anomalous property chemically and synthesized it into a serum.

The trial logs are a human-experimentation ledger. Early subjects died of internal hemorrhaging. Trial 019 was the success: a sixteen-year-old American girl, purchased from a businessman for $2.6 million USD, selected for excellent health and no family history of disease or genetic anomaly — chosen, in other words, the way you choose stock. She produced viable specimens. Later batches went wrong, and Yelkov's final undated note records the flaw that turns a cosmetic product into a Keter-class contagion: "The growths now quickly develop extensive caries across all surfaces and turn to rot." The rot spreads to nearby subjects who were never dosed. His response is not horror but relocation — he notes the setback and moves his research elsewhere. The Foundation and GRU-P raided the Moscow laboratory in 1959 and found nine victims, seven already dead. Yelkov and the loose vials are not accounted for.

**Themes:**

- Vanity as the origin of a plague — the whole thing exists so that rich people can have nicer teeth  
- The banality of the perpetrator's voice; murder, child-purchase, and epidemiological catastrophe recorded in identical dry sentences  
- Bodily autonomy destroyed from the inside by a part of your own body  
- Commodification of persons: a girl with a price tag, fairies as raw material, patients as clients

**What makes it work:**

- Staged clinical progression with week counts, which converts a grotesque image into a countdown the reader can do arithmetic on  
- The journal excerpts characterize Yelkov by omission — he never justifies anything, which is far worse than if he did  
- The tooth fairies are left almost entirely unexplained; a whole other anomaly is used as a footnote and discarded, which is exactly what Yelkov did with them  
- Trial 019's subject is described in the vocabulary of quality control, and the article lets that speak for itself

**The horror:**

- Teeth erupting through skin across the whole body, permanently affixed, still growing  
- The decay stage: your own teeth rotting off you as a contagious dust, so that dying is how you infect the room  
- Uncontained vials and a researcher who relocated rather than stopped  
- The realization that the "cure" for the flaw was never sought, because the flaw only affected the merchandise

#### SCP-2120 — Damage Control

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-2120 is the recurring phenomenon of anomalous naval vessels materializing roughly 1.7km beneath the ocean in the Bermuda region and then surfacing, invariably thoroughly flooded. MTF Gamma-6 "Dry Docks" is authorized to neutralize them; recovered technology goes to Site-65. The ships come from an alternate reality — a nation styling itself the Confederate Socialist Colonies of the New World — and most bear the manufacturer's mark "Boeing Aquatics." They span every era of naval architecture, from triremes to supercarriers, and every one arrives with its advanced technology in catastrophic disrepair.

The catalogue is the substance of the article, and it is a comedy of engineering malpractice with a nasty core. SCP-2120-02 (*Air Lana*) is a plastic-hulled clipper with a non-functional cold fusion reactor. SCP-2120-08 (*Vigo Kennedy*) is a cruise ship full of broken cryogenic pods and an alpha-radiation emitter. SCP-2120-10-D (*John Henry Hitchcock*) is a nuclear submarine armed with gravity-augmenting torpedoes, destroyed by Gamma-6. SCP-2120-13 (*Nu-Indianapolis*) is an octopoid habitation vessel with a propulsion system that kills its occupants. SCP-2120-19 (*Her Majesty Queen Alabama Jersey*) is a carrier whose ammunition converts biological matter into lead. SCP-2120-29 (*Paint With All The Colors Of The Wind*) is a battleship with an invisibility device that bathes its own crew in gamma radiation.

The recovered communications reframe the catalogue. Engineering staff report failures with the weariness of people who have stopped being surprised — "Another day, another catastrophic meltdown of the graviton core" — while command discusses disposal, referencing a figure called "Father Mexico" and his capacity to make problem vessels go away, and closing on an ominous reference to SCP-1393. The picture assembled is of a three-way arms race that produced weapons in flagrant violation of any humanitarian standard, ships that killed their own crews as a matter of routine, and a program whose solution was to dump the entire fleet into an interdimensional void — where it is now surfacing, one hull at a time, in our ocean.

**Themes:**

- Military-industrial waste disposal on a cosmological scale; another reality using ours as a landfill  
- Weapons that are war crimes by design, catalogued with procurement-office neutrality  
- Absurdist worldbuilding as satire — the names, the branding, the ship-naming conventions of a fictional superpower

**What makes it work:**

- Ship names doing enormous comic work (*Paint With All The Colors Of The Wind* on a battleship with a lethal invisibility cloak) inside a rigidly formatted inventory  
- The engineer chatter supplies the human register the catalogue lacks, and it is exhausted rather than alarmed, which tells you how normal this was  
- The horror is deduced, not stated: nothing in the document says the crews died badly, but every entry implies it  
- "Father Mexico" and the SCP-1393 hook are dropped without explanation and never picked up

**The horror:**

- Every vessel arrives flooded, which means every vessel arrives full  
- Ammunition that turns living tissue into lead; a stealth system that irradiates the crew operating it — engineered indifference to one's own people  
- The delivery mechanism is passive and ongoing: someone else's disposal site is a slow leak into our sea, and nobody is on the other end to stop it

#### SCP-2151 — The Ties That Bind Us

**Object class:** Safe

**Summary:** SCP-2151 is a pair of identical stainless steel rings, designated SCP-2151-A and SCP-2151-B, stored in separate high-security lockers at Site-81 with testing currently suspended. The effect activates when the two rings are worn by two different humans who then make physical contact. The rings heat rapidly and embed themselves beneath the skin within seconds. The point of contact between the two people begins to fuse, and the fusion progresses — consuming both bodies over time until they have merged into a single organism, designated SCP-2151-1(X). Once the merger is complete, the rings are expelled from the resulting form, intact and ready. The merged entities have distorted, unrecognizable features, most of the biological functions of the original individuals have ceased, and their mental status is unknown. They typically fail shortly after formation.

The discovery gives the object its meaning. SCP-2151 was recovered in 1971 from a teenager's bedroom. Forensic reconstruction indicates a seventeen-year-old girl named Monica was wearing one ring when contact occurred with an adult male of approximately fifty who shared significant genetic material with her. A handwritten note was found at the scene, in the language of adolescent devotion turned to something else — *"my sweet monica / I know you could never / see us like I see us but / I know we are meant to / be together forever"* — declaring possession and the intention to bind the two of them together permanently. The reader is left to establish for themselves who wrote it and what relationship the genetic overlap describes.

The article's closing horror is a single containment detail. One instance, SCP-2151-1A, has not failed. It has persisted for over thirty years. The Foundation offers no explanation — "Research into how this is possible is ongoing" — and no answer as to whether either of the two people inside it is aware. It is, presumably, still in a cell at Site-81.

**Themes:**

- Love as annihilation; "together forever" delivered with hideous literalism  
- Incest and abuse encoded entirely in a forensic genetics footnote  
- Consent obliterated by a mechanism that activates on touch  
- The persistence of something after the people who constituted it have stopped functioning

**What makes it work:**

- The reveal is buried in evidence, not narrated: the horror is a line about shared genetic material, and the reader has to do the assembly  
- The note is reproduced with its line breaks and lowercase intact, so the voice is unmistakably a real person's and unmistakably wrong  
- The rings ejecting themselves afterward is a small mechanical detail that makes the object feel purposeful — it is not a curse, it is a device, and it has been reset  
- Safe class. Two rings in two lockers. The classification is technically correct and morally absurd

**The horror:**

- Seconds. From contact to embedded rings there is no window in which anything can be done  
- Being conscious inside SCP-2151-1A — the article refuses to confirm or deny it, which is worse than either  
- Thirty years, and counting, of whatever that is  
- The knowledge that it worked exactly as the person who wrote the note intended

#### SCP-2254 — The Demon La Hire and the Valley of Lust

**Object class:** Euclid (Disruption Class Keneq, Risk Class Caution)

**Summary:** SCP-2254 is an incorporeal humanoid entity roughly 12.8 meters tall, visible only on infrared. It has six legs, grossly distended arms, six eyes arranged vertically in two columns, and a severely disfigured body, with a branded "J" above a heart burned into its forehead. It operates in and around Fallon, Alabama, where it uses mind-affecting capability to draw young people — typically under twenty — into a dream state during which sexual encounters occur. These frequently result in anomalous pregnancies producing SCP-2254-1 instances: deformed proto-human offspring resembling the parent entity, which develop rapidly, aggressively drain maternal resources to the point of severe malnourishment, and are usually born in multiples. The birth causes major vaginal trauma with a real risk of fatal hemorrhage. The mothers remain under cognitive influence afterward and continue to care for and breastfeed the creatures. Uncontained instances are extremely violent, and roughly thirty sexually explicit deaths over fifteen years are attributed to them.

The containment procedures are the article. The Foundation cannot contain an incorporeal 12.8-meter entity, so it contains the town instead. Agents embedded in local law enforcement identify anomalous pregnancies, and mifepristone and prostaglandin are administered covertly through the municipal drinking water to induce miscarriage — the primary containment measure is mass non-consensual chemical abortion. Alongside this runs the Black Tuesday Protocol, a coordinated program to keep Fallon poor and immobile so the population does not disperse and carry the anomaly outward: cut educational funding, limit exposure to media that glamorize urban life, increase heroin availability, lower alcohol and tobacco prices, and promote evangelical churches emphasizing traditional family structures and procreation. The protocol follows people out of town, too: five women aged 16 to 29 who managed to relocate are receiving involuntary contraceptives via their new water supplies.

The interview with Kate Barnett, who has conceived twice, supplies the last turn. She does not describe a monster. She describes Jack — a presence who appears in dreams and in waking life, who stayed when the human father did not: "We had one stupid drunk night then I got knocked up and he ran off. Didn't want nothing to do with them. Would've been lost if it weren't for Jack." She quotes him comforting her: "There there, that's alright. You're still my special girl." And she mentions, in passing, that Jack has brothers — that there are others she has never met.

**Themes:**

- Rural poverty engineered and maintained as an instrument of policy  
- Predation disguised as care; the groomer's vocabulary in the mouth of a twelve-meter thing  
- Reproductive coercion from two directions at once — the entity forcing conception, the Foundation forcing termination, and the woman consulted by neither  
- Containment that is indistinguishable from systemic abuse of a community  
- Scale: "Jack's brothers" implies this is not one town

**What makes it work:**

- The Black Tuesday Protocol is written as a policy annex — five neutral bullet points that add up to the deliberate immiseration of a real-seeming American town, and the document sees nothing remarkable in it  
- Kate Barnett's interview is the only human voice in the article and it is warm, grateful, and completely captured, which relocates the horror from the creature to the manipulation  
- The entity is never interviewed, never contained, never confronted — it has no scene. The article is entirely about what people do around it  
- "You're still my special girl" is the single most disturbing line, and it is quoted secondhand, affectionately

**The horror:**

- A predator that arrives as the only person who was ever kind to you  
- Mothers nursing the things that are eating them, and wanting to  
- The Foundation dosing a town's water and calling it containment; the women who escaped and are still being dosed  
- The brothers. However many towns this is, none of them know

#### SCP-2270 — An Unnecessary Utilization of Excessive Force

**Object class:** Euclid (Disruption Class Ekhi, Risk Class Notice)

**Summary:** SCP-2270 is not an object but a body of information: a set of incantations and rituals pertaining to Nergal, the Mesopotamian god of war, plague, and the scorching sun. The source texts, designated SCP-2270-A, are eight volumes by the Romanian Renaissance author Sorin Vasilica, collectively titled *Recurs către Ereshkigal* ("Appeal to Ereshkigal"). Containment is informational: all originals held in high security at Site-81, all copies and derivative material suppressed, Foundation intelligence tasked with sweeping the internet and media, and anyone found in possession detained and amnesticized. The eight volumes form a procedure. *A Cry In the Blistering Wind* appeals to Nergal's righteousness; *A Call for Blood Spilled* to his fury; *A Beckoning* seeks the intercession of Ereshkigal, queen of the underworld; *Appeal to Ereshkigal* establishes communication and implies she controls Nergal through intimacy; *Indication of Divine Guilt* identifies the target and conveys the case against him; *Acceptance of Eternal Consequence* describes what the practitioner is agreeing to — eternal enslavement stoking the fires of the sun — and contains no ritual at all, being purely a disclosure; *A Sacrifice of Life / A Final Appeal* requires blood sacrifice to trigger Nergal's bloodlust; and *Victatorius* is a four-hundred-page prayer that must be recited without interruption, failure of which results in the destruction of the practitioner instead of the target. Vasilica himself reportedly died attempting the summoning, having failed to complete it, his last words describing intense heat.

SCP-2270-B is believed to be Nergal. It has manifested exactly once, during Incident 2270-Alpha, as a massive, vividly colored arm extending toward the Earth from the sun, capable of a focused ballistic strike estimated at 275 petajoules. Every subsequent Foundation attempt to reproduce the effect has been unsuccessful — a fact the article disposes of in a single footnote, which is its funniest and bleakest move: the Foundation has been trying to build this and cannot, and a man in Montana did it on the first go.

Incident 2270-Alpha occurred on 16 March 1994 near Fort Peck, Montana, and is the article. Jerry Hubert of Augusta, Montana bought a nearly complete SCP-2270-A set for $75,000 from Marshall, Carter and Dark suppliers, over email, in installments — $25,000 up front, the rest on delivery. The email log is written in unpunctuated lowercase and is entirely, exhaustingly mundane. He asks clarifying questions about the steps. He asks whether the blood sacrifice is really necessary. He asks about the eternal consequence clause and is apparently satisfied by the answer. And throughout, he explains his grievance: Dave. Dave's dog urinates on his lawn. Dave parks in front of his house. On receiving the package he writes, "this will do the trick, huh? because i am about sick and tired of this asshole dave." His final message: "ive got everything set up just like it says in the book. im gonna do what you said and wait til he goes out to hunt there by the river."

The explosion was attributed publicly to an accidental detonation at a weapons depot. Dave Terrell was never found. And Jerry Hubert — having successfully petitioned the goddess of the underworld to direct the god of war to reach down out of the sun and erase a section of Montana, and having accepted eternal servitude feeding that sun's fires as the price — got in his truck to drive home, ran it down an embankment into a tree, and died. Recovered from the vehicle: the SCP-2270-A manuscripts, and an empty twelve-pack of beer.

**Themes:**

- Apocalyptic power in the hands of petty grievance; the neighbor dispute as casus belli for a god  
- The absolute banality of the transaction — a doomsday weapon bought over email with follow-up questions  
- Damnation accepted without reading the terms; the sixth volume exists solely to warn him and he read it and shrugged  
- Marshall, Carter and Dark as pure commerce: they sold it, they answered his questions, they cashed the check  
- The Foundation's impotence — they hold the books, they cannot make them work, and the man who could is dead in a ditch

**What makes it work:**

- The entire piece rests on register collision: a numbered liturgical apparatus of eight sacred volumes, immediately followed by "asshole dave"  
- The email log is the format trick — no narration, no scene, just a paper trail, and the reader watches the escalation happen entirely in a man's typing  
- The volume summaries do serious worldbuilding and genuine dread (*Acceptance of Eternal Consequence* is a masterstroke: a holy book that is only a warning label), which the emails then desecrate  
- The failed-tests footnote reframes the whole article: the Foundation wants this and cannot have it, which is the strongest possible argument for suppression  
- Hubert's death is the last beat and it is anticlimactic on purpose — no divine retribution, no cosmic irony, just drunk driving

**The horror:**

- The strike itself: an arm out of the sun, 275 petajoules, aimed at one man fishing by a river  
- Dave Terrell, who was never found, whose crime was a dog and a parking space  
- The eternal consequence, which is presumably still running — Hubert is dead, and the deal was not with his body  
- That the ritual is reproducible in principle, that copies are loose, and that the only qualification it requires is being sufficiently annoyed

#### SCP-2271 — Factory Loans

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-2271 is a plain white envelope containing a collections letter on a student loan account, issued by "Factory Financial Management" — a company that does not exist and cannot be traced. The letters (SCP-2271-1) arrive by impossible means: appearing under locked doors, materializing inside sealed rooms, and in at least one documented case being passed within a subject's own bodily waste. Merely receiving the envelope is sufficient for infection; the victim (SCP-2271-A) does not need to open or read it. Containment is an admission of defeat — the Foundation cannot contain SCP-2271, and MTF Mu-14 exists only to isolate the already-infected at secure sites, run cover stories, and amnesticize bystanders. Foundation personnel who become instances are administratively reclassified as killed in action and provided the means to terminate themselves.

The infection proceeds in three phases. In Phase 1 the letter arrives, personalized to the recipient. In Phase 2 the demanded balance is revealed to be a sum that "absolutely cannot be repaid, even if the subject had access to the total amount of all of Earth's liquid assets," and the victim becomes agitated and wholly obsessed with repayment, liquidating possessions in escalating desperation. They begin reporting SCP-2271-2: indistinct humanoid figures at the edge of vision, fast-moving and "clouded in thick smoke," whose presence drops ambient temperature by as much as 25°C and who induce specific phobic associations in victims — the sound of metal, fire, wind, the smell of burning rubber and petrol. In Phase 3 the liquidation becomes total: teeth, hair, excrement, blood and other bodily fluids, pets, children, spouses and close relatives, and finally the remainder of the subject's own body are offered as payment. None of it reduces the balance. The process continues until the subject expires, or until they simply vanish after missing two payments.

The article ends with an incident report dated 01/03/2014: Site-91, the facility where every known SCP-2271-A instance was being held, detonated its on-site nuclear device. What triggered the detonation is not explained; the closing material is a scanned image presented without commentary, and the investigation is described as ongoing. (I could not retrieve the letter's body text verbatim from the page — the retrieval returned structure and quoted fragments rather than the full SCP-2271-1 document.)

**Themes:**

- Debt as a metaphysical rather than financial condition — a sum that is unpayable by definition  
- Bureaucratic paperwork as the delivery mechanism for damnation  
- The transformation of a person into pure liquidatable collateral  
- Institutional triage: the Foundation containing the infected rather than the infection  
- Collections agents as demons — the repo man reimagined as a smoke-wreathed thing at the corner of your eye

**What makes it work:**

- The horror is entirely mundane in its interface. A student loan letter is an object nearly every reader has physically held.  
- Withholding the letter's actual contents keeps the debt abstract, which is what makes it infinite.  
- The escalating inventory of Phase 3 is written as an accounting list, and the flat commercial register — teeth, then hair, then children, then the rest of your body — does more damage than description would.  
- Ending on an unexplained nuclear detonation and an uncaptioned image refuses the reader any resolution about what actually came for them.

**The horror:**

- Infection by receipt, not by action — you are condemned before you know a document exists  
- A debt that grows regardless of payment, which turns effort itself into the trap  
- The victim's own hands doing the dismantling; nothing has to attack you  
- The Foundation's response amounts to euthanasia paperwork

#### SCP-2316 — Field Trip

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** The file opens with a warning banner — "They've been waiting for a long time" — declaring that the images and text below are themselves carriers of anomalous cognitohazardous influence. SCP-2316 is a group of human corpses floating at the surface of a lake in a redacted location. The count is given as roughly forty-five but "has been estimated as high as two-hundred or more"; the number is not stable, and the entity may be a single collective consciousness rather than a set of individuals. Containment procedures are absolute avoidance: personnel must never approach, observation happens only through remote dummy probes carrying video and audio equipment, review of that footage is restricted to personnel with Cognitive Resistance Values at or above 14.5, the lake is fenced and patrolled by unexposed staff, and anyone who comes within fifty meters is administratively classified as lost. Not missing. Lost.

The anomalous effect is a cognitohazard: anyone exposed perceives the bodies as people they recognize from their own childhood. Contact makes it worse — attempting to touch an instance causes additional instances to appear, which strengthens the effect, which compels the affected person further into the water. Nobody who has entered the lake has ever been recovered. The containment procedures are the horror here: the file is not describing a monster that hunts, it is describing a place where the correct institutional response is to write off everyone who steps too close and never look at the footage without a certified resistance score.

Addendum 2316.1 is an interview conducted by Dr. Harrison with an exposed agent. The subject is calm and insistent that they were not compelled: "I saw them in the water and went in myself. I needed to see them." They describe hearing voices from bodies whose mouths never move, name the faces they recognized, and begin to say "Kirk Lonwood High School, Class of—" before being subdued and removed for amnestic treatment. The interview's speaker designation degrades across the transcript, sliding from a normal label into "Youdonotrecognizethebodiesinthewater" and then "YouYouYouYouYouYouYouYouYouYouMe" — the document itself losing the ability to distinguish the subject from the reader.

Addendum 2316.2 abandons the clinical frame entirely and addresses the reader in the second person. It refers to a school field trip in the fall of 1975 in which children drowned in the lake, and it does not present this as history — it presents it as something the reader was present for. "Don't you remember?" "Can't you hear them?" "Look into their eyes." "You don't have a lot of time, you'll need to get away quickly. Go back to the lake." The text repeats and corrupts, cycling through "You do not recognize the bodies in the water" until the denial reads as compulsion, and closes on the assertion that they're waiting, that we were there with them, and a link instruction to go back to the lake. The reveal is that the article's stated containment procedure — do not view unscreened material — has already been violated by the act of reading, and the growing body count is the mechanism: every person the lake takes becomes another face someone else will recognize.

**Themes:**

- Recognition and false memory as a vector of harm  
- Childhood as the softest surface for something to write on  
- Documentation as an infection medium rather than a record of one  
- The drowned as recruiters — grief that reproduces itself  
- Institutional abandonment: personnel written off as "lost" while still walking

**What makes it work:**

- The cognitohazard warning at the top is a load-bearing joke that stops being a joke — the reader has no CRV score and reads anyway.  
- The speaker label in the interview corrupting into "You...Me" does the reveal typographically, before any prose states it.  
- The pivot from clinical third person to accusatory second person is withheld until the last section, so the tonal break lands as a violation of the format's own rules.  
- The corpse count being unfixed and rising is stated flatly in the Description and only pays off retroactively, once you understand what makes new ones.

**The horror:**

- You cannot un-read it; the containment procedures describe a precaution you have already failed  
- The dead wearing the faces of people you loved as a child, using your own affection as the hook  
- Voluntary drowning — the victim insists, credibly, that nothing forced them  
- The implication that you were on the 1975 trip, which is either a lie the entity tells or something you've forgotten

#### SCP-2399 — A Malfunctioning Destroyer

**Object class:** Keter

**Summary:** SCP-2399 is a colossal alien machine embedded in Jupiter's Great Red Spot, first resolved by Foundation instruments in 1963 but retroactively identified in the notes of Giovanni Cassini, who in 1665 recorded a luminous object streaking through the solar system, decelerating near Jupiter, and vanishing into the planet, followed by explosions visible through his telescope. The machine originated in the Triangulum Galaxy, three million light-years away. It carries antimatter weaponry capable of tearing spatial disruptions, an apparently limitless power source, electromagnetic shielding, matter-disruption systems, and a fleet of autonomous octopoid repair drones. It is functionally indestructible by any human technology. It is also damaged — because on approach to Earth it struck Io and fell into Jupiter's gravity well, an accident that is explicitly framed as the only reason the human species still exists. The Great Red Spot is not a storm the machine landed in. It is the wound.

Containment is surveillance and denial. Agents are embedded at observatories worldwide to suppress imagery, misinformation campaigns have eliminated public awareness, orbital satellites track reconstruction, and the BARRIER Array — a network of long-range electromagnetic jamming satellites — sits between SCP-2399 and its makers, intercepting transmissions in both directions. Repair progress is the file's clock: roughly 59% complete, with the annual repair rate climbing from 0.12% in 1970 to 0.78% and still accelerating. Addendum 2399-2b records the one time the Foundation intervened directly — BARRIER Unit 45 fired concussion batteries at a repair drone approaching a damaged array component; SCP-2399 destroyed the projectiles at 5km, then did something to Unit 45 that is data-expunged and required three other BARRIER units to destroy it. Engaging SCP-2399 or its drones is now strictly forbidden.

The communication log is the article's spine. 1971: "Unit is damaged: Repair." 1985: "Updating Orders: Maintain Position: Repair." Then silence from 1985 to 1996, coinciding with the BARRIER Array's establishment — the Foundation has been jamming a conversation for a decade. 1996: "Unit is out of range of target: Proceed to planet \#3... Repair." And 2015, the message that changes everything: the unit is out of range of target, the priority is the target, and — "Cease repairs." The builders are still there, still issuing orders, and have grown impatient enough to tell their weapon to stop fixing itself and simply come. Earth is planet \#3. The Foundation has never been contained an anomaly here; it has been eavesdropping on a delivery it cannot cancel.

Addendum 2399-2c documents Project Gigas: an international weapons platform staged from Europa orbit carrying multiple massive thermonuclear warheads plus EMP detonators, authorized by five O5 Council members and fifteen heads of state across forty-five participating nations. The outcome is data-expunged, and the only surviving sentence notes that efforts to develop alternative methods continue. Addendum 2399-L5 is Director Randall McAllan's assessment and the emotional close of the piece: humanity survives on an accident of orbital mechanics, has perhaps twenty-five years before SCP-2399 can overpower the jamming array, and possesses in Protocol LEGIONNAIRE a plan requiring a gigantic EMP followed by civilization-ending thermonuclear strikes — a plan for which the Foundation has neither the technology to generate the power nor the means to deliver it, and no indication it can be completed in time. McAllan's closing position is not confidence. It is "Even if we have to drain our banks and empty our mines, we must try."

**Themes:**

- Cosmic indifference: Earth is a work order, not an enemy  
- Survival by accident, and the arithmetic of borrowed time  
- The Foundation as a jamming signal — buying years, not solutions  
- Bureaucratic hope: forty-five nations, five O5s, fifteen heads of state, and an expunged result  
- The unwinnable contingency plan written down anyway

**What makes it work:**

- The dated transmission log turns exposition into a countdown; the reader assembles the threat from five short machine-voice lines  
- Anchoring the arrival to Cassini's real 1665 observations grafts the anomaly onto verifiable astronomy  
- The two data-expunged failures (Unit 45, Project Gigas) are more frightening as blanks than any described defeat would be  
- Ending on a director's plain, unheroic declaration rather than a twist — the file's last register is exhaustion, not shock

**The horror:**

- A percentage that only goes up, and an acceleration curve  
- The realization that the silence in the log is the Foundation's doing, and that jamming has been mistaken for containment  
- "Cease repairs" — the enemy deciding it no longer needs to be whole to finish the job  
- A contingency plan that would end civilization and still probably wouldn't work

#### SCP-2445 — Wondertainment Logistics

**Object class:** Safe

**Summary:** SCP-2445 is an unmarked white semi-truck operated by "Wondertainment Logistics Co., A Division of Dr. Wondertainment Inc.," held in the Anomalous Vehicle Bay at Site-81. Its anomalous properties come from SCP-2445-1, an engine of impossible output, and SCP-2445-2, a 0.6m³ black metallic cube seated between the front seats and operated through a biometrically locked "WonderVision" touchscreen. The menu is where the article turns: WonderFinder produces a heat map highlighting concentrations of children, WonderHider renders the vehicle invisible, WonderZoom generates a temporal distortion that lets the truck move through apparently frozen time, WonderTransfer manufactures Dr. Wondertainment toy products on demand with no observed quantity limit, and WonderHome is locked. The driver, Clarence Prince, was recruited under circumstances he cannot account for and sits strapped into a support apparatus with intravenous lines and neural interface hardware, knowing essentially nothing about his employer. The reveal arrives as a cheerful system message in an incident log — "Dr. Wondertainment hopes you have enjoyed your demo of our newest product, WonderShipping\!" — establishing that the Foundation was never containing anything. It was running a trial period, and Wondertainment intends to collect its equipment.

**Themes:**

- Corporate whimsy as a delivery vehicle for something predatory  
- The Foundation as an unwitting beta tester  
- Invisible, time-frozen access to children rendered as a UI feature list  
- Human beings as consumable hardware inside a toy company's supply chain

**What makes it work:**

- Nearly all the dread is delivered through interface copy — the menu options do the characterization  
- "WonderFinder" is listed matter-of-factly among the features, and the reader supplies the implication  
- The locked WonderHome option is pure withholding: the one function you cannot see is the one that matters  
- The marketing-cheerful reveal inverts the containment relationship in a single sentence

**The horror:**

- A cloaked truck that can move while the world is stopped, aiming itself at children  
- The driver wired into the machine, remembering nothing about how he was hired  
- Being demoted from jailer to customer without ever having been consulted

#### SCP-2464 — Suspension

**Object class:** Keter (upgraded from Euclid)

**Summary:** SCP-2464 occupies a limestone mine in Indiana, discovered during mining operations. SCP-2464-1 is a spatiotemporal pocket inside the mine: passing through, explorers emerge into an endless rolling field held permanently at dusk, with a dilapidated 1960s farmhouse at its center. The house belonged to the late Dr. Michael Banning, a Foundation researcher from 1934 to 1975, and burned in 1975\. In the upstairs master bedroom are three things. SCP-2464-2 is a twelve-year-old girl, about 1.4m tall with long brown hair, shackled to a bed, unresponsive, her mouth perpetually open. SCP-2464-3 is her identical twin, suspended from the ceiling by iron clasps, severely emaciated, continuously secreting a viscous black liquid from her mouth that falls into the open mouth of SCP-2464-2. Neither the liquid's origin nor SCP-2464-3's source of nutrition is understood. SCP-2464-4 is a machine of copper tubing, built by Banning and connected to the suspended twin, which halts her aging at the cost of progressive cancerous cellular degeneration.

Containment is a corridor of five checkpoints spaced five kilometers apart, restricted past Area 2464-1 to Level 4 personnel and authorized teams, with preset charges to collapse the tunnel on a breach and an on-site nuclear device as the final measure. The operative instruction is maintenance: SCP-2464-4 is deteriorating and must be kept running, and "under no circumstances, even during maintenance of SCP-2464-4, should SCP-2464-2 and SCP-2464-3 be disturbed in any way." Addendum 2464-2X, restricted to Level 4, supplies the reason: SCP-2464-2 is a Class IV Reality Bending Entity, and the entire pocket dimension — field, dusk, farmhouse — is her sleeping mind's output. The black fluid is the sedative. Her sister is the dispenser. A July 2013 maintenance incident report records unusual eye movement in SCP-2464-3, which is the file's only moment of motion and the reason for the Keter upgrade.

The projection is that SCP-2464-4 fails within ten years without repair. When it fails, SCP-2464-3 dies, the flow stops, SCP-2464-2 wakes, and — citing Banning's own logs on the severely deteriorated mental state of his daughter — the Foundation assesses this as capable of triggering an RK-Class "Reality Restructuring" scenario. Banning is never explained further and no journal text is quoted; the article leaves the reader to assemble that a Foundation researcher hung one of his daughters from a ceiling to keep the other one asleep, built a machine to keep the hanging one alive indefinitely while it slowly killed her, and that the Foundation's considered position is to keep his machine running.

**Themes:**

- A family atrocity converted into standing operating procedure  
- Suspension in every sense: a body hanging, a life extended, a scenario deferred  
- Maintenance as morality — the ethical question replaced by a repair schedule  
- Inherited containment: the current staff perpetuating a dead man's crime because the alternative is worse

**What makes it work:**

- The image is delivered flatly, as a numbered list of designated objects, and never commented on  
- Banning is deliberately underwritten — no journals quoted, only the phrase "his daughter," which forces the reader to do the reconstruction  
- The ten-year failure estimate gives the piece a deadline the Foundation is losing against  
- A single detail of eye movement is enough to reclassify the object, which tells you how thin the margin is

**The horror:**

- Two children, one asleep forever and one dying forever, maintained in that state as policy  
- The Foundation's continuation of the abuse being the correct decision  
- A machine whose whole function is to prevent someone from waking up, and which is wearing out

#### SCP-2540 — Time Lime

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-2540 appears to be an ordinary key lime and is in fact a four-dimensional Tipler cylinder — as a researcher puts it, "SCP-2540 may very well be the physical representation of an infinitely long cylinder existing within four or more spatial dimensions." It is kept in a Plexiglas-paneled locker at Site-81 sized to hold at least five human subjects, monitored by direct human observation because video surveillance is defeated by the spacetime distortion around it, with a mandatory one-meter standoff and electromagnets required for any handling. It exerts a "drag" on spacetime that drags matter across time and deposits it at random: air, fish, stone tablets, and people arrive out of nowhere. The signature incident is Boris Tanzen, a Russian tourist who materialized in 1989 missing his lower body, having been handed a lime for his drink in 2045; he reported only that the experience was "sour," and that he sensed a surprised observer during the transition. That observer is the article's real subject. In 2002 a garbled transmission came through — an apology from something four-dimensional, "I am \[unclear\] and will not return for a short time... I am forgiven? that it intersects with your unfortunate universe" — establishing that a being from outside left a piece of equipment lying in our reality, is embarrassed about it, and will eventually come get it.

**Themes:**

- Absurd physical form concealing genuine cosmological weight  
- Humanity as an inconvenience apologized to rather than negotiated with  
- Litter from a higher dimension  
- The Foundation guarding a piece of fruit under nuclear-grade procedure

**What makes it work:**

- The comedy of the containment procedures (electromagnets, five-person locker, no cameras, for a lime) is played entirely straight  
- Tanzen's bisection is horrifying and his testimony — "sour" — is the funniest line in the article  
- The apology transmission converts the joke object into evidence of a vastly larger and mostly uninterested intelligence

**The horror:**

- Being pulled through time and arriving as half a person, still able to talk about it  
- Something noticed you noticing it  
- Our universe described as "unfortunate," in passing, by whatever dropped this here

#### SCP-2598 — Traveling Moth Salesman

**Object class:** Safe

**Summary:** SCP-2598 is a Large Yellow Underwing moth (*Noctua pronuba*) wearing a tiny helmet. It is sapient, understands spoken English perfectly, and communicates exclusively by flying into personnel's heads in Morse code patterns — it has refused every translation device and alternative method offered. Containment is a Standard Insectoid Containment Cell at Site-19 with a perpetual food supply and a hanging light source, the last of which SCP-2598 requested itself; personnel conducting interviews must complete twenty hours of Morse code training, which is the article's best structural joke, since the Foundation has institutionalized an accommodation for a moth's marketing preferences. Every interview, regardless of the question asked, is steered toward the sale of moth helmets. Asked about its origins, it headbutts out "I WAS ONCE LIKE YOU DOCTOR... UNTIL I DISCOVERED THE BEAUTY OF MOTH HELM," available for "THREE EASY PAYMENTS OF NINETEEN NINETY NINE." When Dr. Eads points out that humans cannot wear moth-sized helmets, it pivots without a beat: "PERHAPS YOU HAVE SOME MOTH FRIENDS WHO COULD BENEFIT FROM THE TRANSCENDANT POWER OF MOTH HELM ALSO IS A GREAT STOCKING STUFFER." It never becomes frustrated. It only ever sells.

**Themes:**

- Infomercial patter as an unbreakable personality  
- Containment as customer service  
- Sapience wholly consumed by a sales funnel  
- The Foundation's procedural machinery bending around a bug's insistence

**What makes it work:**

- The interview transcript renders Morse-by-headbutt in all caps, so the moth is permanently shouting  
- The joke escalates through the interviewer's mounting reasonableness rather than the moth's behavior, which never changes  
- The light source in the containment procedures is a small perfect character beat: it is still a moth

**The horror:**

- None intentionally; the faint uncanny note is "I WAS ONCE LIKE YOU DOCTOR," which implies a transformation nobody follows up on

#### SCP-2610 — Procreation

**Object class:** Neutralized

**Summary:** SCP-2610 designates four siblings of the Avakian family of Boston, Massachusetts, collectively calling themselves the Firstborn: Simeon (SCP-2610-A, b. 1922), Armond (SCP-2610-B, b. 1929), and the twins Yvette and Jorge (SCP-2610-C and \-D, possibly b. 1943), the latter two having no official records of any kind. Simeon was a practicing medical doctor who claimed telepathy — "I can see the images and hear the impulses of every person…" — and who performed a lobotomy on a patient to cover up a sexual assault committed by his brother, which is the file's opening establishment that these people were monstrous before anything anomalous touched them. Using anomalous genetic techniques and a bioactive substance he refers to as tears, Simeon produced offspring designated SCP-2610-E: gestation compressed from months into days, explosive postnatal growth, humanoid but not human, roughly six feet tall, with missing or supernumerary limbs and eyes and ears, collapsed ribcages, extruded stomachs, large growths, and a jagged broken gait; severely malformed from extreme inbreeding, cognitively diminished, unusually strong, and resistant to conventional firearms. Together the operation was designated the Colony.

The article alternates between Simeon's journal excerpts and UIU incident reports, and the structure is the craft. In one entry an angelic entity visits him and instructs him to build "God's New Eden" through selective breeding; in another the seeding has begun in earnest and the offspring grow within the host with great speed; his sister Yvette is designated the Whore of His Deliverance and used as the primary reproductive vessel. Against this, the incident log reads as a slowly widening stain. UIU Tango-11, November 1965: four grotesque humanoids break into a feed storage facility, kill three people, and escape with cattle feed; the fingerprints match nothing on record. Tango-15, 1970: another feed theft, five dead, a vehicle traced to A. Avakian, one entity killed and recovered. The recurring detail is the feed — the reader works out before the Foundation says it that the Colony's problem is caloric, that thousands of rapidly growing bodies have to eat.

Tango-18, July 1970: Foundation and UIU agents raid an underground complex beneath an abandoned warehouse and find between two hundred and four hundred entities engaged in mass procreation. The scene is given as GRAPHIC DETAILS EXPUNGED. Four of thirteen agents get out; the rest are killed, the warehouse is demolished, and no remains are recovered. Then Incident UIU Tango-Tango, November 1971, the article's set piece: at least six cross-country freight trains carrying animal feed are hijacked and driven west, functioning as mobile breeding and transport vessels, the population aboard swelling past twelve thousand by the time they reach the coast. One train attacks a Pennsylvania town during a football game — again, expunged. Foundation personnel sever Simeon's telepathic link from a location in Kentucky, and the entities, suddenly uncontrolled, derail. The survivors reach Long Beach, seize three oil tankers, and make open ocean through a storm ahead of the Coast Guard until Navy F-14s destroy all three vessels at 2028 hours PST on 16 November 1971\.

Roughly 1,200 bodies are recovered and incinerated. Simeon, Armond, and Yvette Avakian are never found in the wreckage. A request to close the case is filed in July 1979 and denied on 19 July 1979\. The article's last document is Simeon's final journal entry, describing the voyage to Eden — "On the four Arks will we be delivered to His New Eden, The birthing screams of the Whore will announce our arrival…" — with, on the reverse side, in a different hand, the note: "What are we doing?" One of them was asking. It was Armond, and his brother's earlier entry already recorded what happens to family who resist: "His blood will fertilize the fields of our covenant…"

**Themes:**

- Religious mania as an engine for atrocity, with divine language laid over pure biological horror  
- The family as the unit of evil — no outside recruitment, only reproduction  
- Body horror as the visible consequence of generations of deliberate incest  
- Institutional history: a 1960s–70s case file, told through two agencies who understand only fragments  
- One conscience, appearing once, on the back of a page, too late

**What makes it work:**

- The alternation between Simeon's ecstatic first-person scripture and dry UIU incident reports means the reader always knows more than either document does  
- Withholding through GRAPHIC DETAILS EXPUNGED at the two worst moments makes the reader complete the images  
- The stolen cattle feed is a mundane recurring clue that quietly conveys the Colony's scale before any number is given  
- The denied case closure and the three missing siblings deny the Neutralized classification its comfort  
- Ending on four words in different handwriting is the single best move in the piece — the entire moral weight of the article is carried by a marginal note

**The horror:**

- Scale that arrives by multiplication: four people, then hundreds, then twelve thousand, inside a decade  
- Reproduction weaponized — the atrocity is that it works and cannot be outpaced  
- Living creatures born only to be born from, malformed and barely cognitive, then incinerated by the thousand  
- The Firstborn unaccounted for, and a case a superior specifically refused to close

#### SCP-2670 — Somebody Else's God

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-2670 is a spatial anomaly roughly 5m tall and 2m wide, subject to dimensional fluctuation, situated in a remote guarded location and discovered in 1875 by the naturalist Josiah Sneddon. It is a doorway to Xandromius Prime, homeworld of the Xandromians. Two entities attend it. SCP-2670-1 is a shapeshifter who presents either as a fashionably dressed human or as a levitating nebulous cloud of liquid particulate matter, and who introduces himself as "His Most Humble Servant Ambassador Jeremiah Bartimeus Branderson of the House of Manyscot" — a name assembled with obvious care and no understanding. SCP-2670-2, stationed as portal guard since 1892, is a large robotic entity styled "The Most Noble and Prestigious Kill-Gore Mastiff Bloodseeker Champion," which responds to almost everything with emphatic declarations of Xandromian superiority. Containment procedures restrict interaction to Level 2/2670+ researchers, log everything, and carry an amendment stating that utmost care is essential when divulging information regarding Earth and humankind, per Protocol Paramount Regal.

The Xandromians were aquatic slugs. Approximately 3,250 years ago an anomalous structure they call the Spire fell into their ocean, and through it they saw Earth. They have been in love with humanity ever since, and they have spent thirty-two centuries trying to become us — which is the joke the article runs on for most of its length, an alien ambassador in period human costume, a war machine named like a mistranslated toy, an entire civilization doing an earnest and slightly off impression of our manners.

Addendum 2670-D is where the article turns. Emulating humanity did not mean emulating our art or our clothes only; it meant emulating our history. The Xandromians, having risen to dominance with their new powers, systematically exterminated the Heshins, the planet's native dominant species, and have killed over a billion sapient beings recreating human atrocities as devotional acts. They staged an Industrial Revolution complete with its exploitation. They crucified Heshins to recreate the persecution of early Christians. They built "75 flying machines, styled after American bombers" and dropped approximately 30-megaton nuclear weapons on cities, because we did that, and doing what we did is worship. Every horror in their history is a reenactment of ours, performed with reverence.

The Foundation's response is not intervention. It is Protocol Paramount Regal: a permanent, coordinated lie. Personnel are required to convince SCP-2670-1 and \-2 that Earth has achieved a set of impossible ideals — that war has ended and universal peace holds, that the forests have been restored, that the hoarding of wealth has been eliminated, that religion has dissolved, that humanity has universally embraced humanistic virtue. The entities are kept confined to the immediate area and prevented from viewing other regions on a pretext of atmospheric danger, which is really a quarantine against the sight of what we actually are. The stated stakes of failure are the torture and massacre of likely billions of sapient entities across Xandromius Prime. So the Foundation, an organization built on secrecy, finds itself running the largest good-news propaganda operation in history, and the only thing standing between a billion more deaths and a foreign world is our willingness to lie about ourselves convincingly, forever.

**Themes:**

- Worship as catastrophe — being loved by something that cannot tell your sins from your virtues  
- Cultural transmission without context, and the horror of the faithful copy  
- The Foundation's mission inverted: not concealing anomalies from humanity, but concealing humanity from an anomaly  
- Genocide committed as an act of devotion  
- Aspiration as a containment tool — the lie the Foundation tells is the world we say we want

**What makes it work:**

- The extended comedy of the first two-thirds is a deliberate anesthetic; the ambassador's silly name and the war machine's bluster are what make the Heshin material land  
- The atrocities are presented as a Xandromian achievement list — proud, itemized, in their voice — rather than as Foundation condemnation  
- The reveal is structural, not narrative: the containment procedure amendment at the top only becomes horrifying after Addendum 2670-D, so the article rereads itself  
- Protocol Paramount Regal's contents are a list of things that are not true about Earth, which makes the protocol double as an indictment

**The horror:**

- Someone recreating your worst history because they mistook it for your identity  
- The knowledge that the model was accurate — they got it right, that's the problem  
- A billion dead, and the only available lever is flattery  
- The Foundation choosing to preserve the delusion rather than end it, because the truth would kill more

#### SCP-2740 — It Wasn't There

**Object class:** Euclid

**Summary:** SCP-2740 may or may not exist. The Description opens by conceding it: "The nature, appearance, and potential existence of SCP-2740 are uncertain." It is thought to occupy the northwest corner of an attic in a house in Indiana, roughly six meters from the pull-down ladder. Containment is a refusal — direct containment is deemed unfeasible, access to the address is forbidden and enforced by Foundation security, cover story Delta-4B attributes the cordon to a gas leak, and amnestics are kept on hand for assigned personnel. The anomaly's only reliable property is negative: individuals can approach the ladder and in certain cases even begin to climb it, but on subsequent inspection it will always be discovered that they did not in actuality do so. They remember climbing. It did not happen. Proximity to the attic produces intense, escalating dread in anyone who knows about it.

The interviews carry the article. In Interview 2740-A, Franklin Lee describes years of low-grade wrongness — dread when he glances at the ceiling, the certainty that something is up there — and repeated attempts to climb the ladder, each of which ends with him discovering himself in bed or sitting in front of the television with no transition. He tried to have the house demolished; the contracting company turned out not to exist anymore. In Interview 2740-B, Yvette Lee describes twenty years in the house and initially denies ever going up. Then she admits that one night she heard something and climbed the ladder, and what she found up there "wasn't Olivia." She also speaks about the family's estrangement from their eldest daughter in terms that don't sound like grief: "I could still feel the wedge, like it never went away."

Interview 2740-C is with that daughter, Olivia Lee, located living under the name Rebecca Feldman in a redacted town, working as a landscape contractor. She left home at seventeen. She describes a childhood of ambient violence and sleeping with a knife under her pillow, and one night when her parents came into her room; she heard something above her and ran, and never went back. Her account of the feeling is the article's thesis: "like you're trying to run from something but you can't see it." Incident Log 2740-A is the Foundation's side of the same failure, and it is an escalating list of methods that all produce the same result — the ladder, explosives, drones, cutting through the roof, demolishing the house entirely — with personnel reporting success and the records showing, afterward, that none of it occurred.

The article closes on two lines that reframe everything. Olivia's: "There are always secrets, doctor. There's only so much that hate can build up in a place before it starts hating you back… I don't know what's in the attic, or if there's anything up there at all, and I don't think I want to." And then the file note: "Currently, it is believed that Olivia Lee does not exist, nor has ever existed. The information in Interview Log 2740-C is undergoing further examination." The most coherent, most sympathetic witness in the document — the one who explains the anomaly, the one who got out — is retroactively deleted, which leaves the reader holding an interview with nobody, conducted about a house whose parents remember a daughter, concerning an attic whose contents may be nothing at all. (Retrieval also surfaced a parenthetical contradiction in the file: a line stating the Lee family was moved to Site-81 for holding and examination, immediately undercut by a note that the Lee family has not been moved to Site-81 — the document disagreeing with itself the same way memory does here.)

**Themes:**

- A house as the accumulated residue of family hatred, given the power to reciprocate  
- Erasure rather than manifestation — the anomaly's signature is absence  
- Memory as unreliable evidence, and the Foundation's records as no better  
- Domestic abuse rendered as an architectural feature, never stated outright  
- Estrangement literalized: the daughter who left so completely she never was

**What makes it work:**

- The article is built almost entirely out of negative space — nothing is ever seen, and the anomaly's only confirmed behavior is making things not have happened  
- Three interviews with three unreliable people, each contradicting the others on the one thing all of them should know  
- The incident log escalates through absurdity (they demolished the house and it didn't happen) without ever raising its voice  
- The final note is one sentence and detonates the entire preceding document, including the reader's sympathies  
- Everything about the abuse is inferential: a knife under a pillow, "my parents came into my room," "the wedge"

**The horror:**

- Doing something, remembering it clearly, and being told with evidence that you did not  
- A dread with no object — the fear is confirmed but the thing never is  
- "It wasn't Olivia," a sentence whose horror depends on a daughter who apparently never existed to not be  
- The suspicion that the attic contains nothing, and that nothing is sufficient  
- A place that has absorbed enough hatred to start returning it
