# FOG BELT — REFERENCE REGISTER · ANNEX E — NEON GENESIS EVANGELION

*Companion to Fog Belt — Reference Register v2 (the master document). Spoilers throughout, by design.*

*Status note (compiled 26 August 2026). Unmarked claims come from the 1995–96 television series and* The End of Evangelion *(1997), which this annex treats as the primary text. Material that exists only in the Rebuild films is marked **[R]**. Material that exists only in the Sadamoto manga is marked **[M]**. Readings the works support but do not state outright are marked **[inferred]** — this annex does not present fan interpretation as text, because the register's whole value is that its claims can be checked.*

*Source tag in the Distillate: `[E]`.*

### 0. WHAT THE PROPERTY IS

**Neon Genesis Evangelion** (*Shin Seiki Evangerion*), twenty-six television episodes, Gainax with Tatsunoko Production, broadcast on TV Tokyo from October 1995 to 27 March 1996. Director: **Hideaki Anno**.

The television ending is famously not the ending. Episodes 25 and 26 abandon the exterior plot and stage the psychological resolution as an interior monologue in near-abstraction. **Death & Rebirth** (15 March 1997) is a recap plus the first movement of what came next; **The End of Evangelion** (19 July 1997) is a full theatrical rendering of the same two episodes' events from outside. The two endings are not a draft and a correction. They are the same event filmed from inside the subject and from inside the apparatus, and the register should treat them as a matched pair — this is the single most transferable structural device in the property.

**The Sadamoto manga**, illustrated and written by character designer **Yoshiyuki Sadamoto**, ran from December 1994 to June 2013 — nineteen years for a fourteen-volume retelling, begun before the series aired and finished seventeen years after it ended. It diverges in motive and characterisation, most consequentially in Gendo's.

**The Rebuild tetralogy [R]** — *Evangelion: 1.0 You Are (Not) Alone* (1 September 2007), *2.0 You Can (Not) Advance* (27 June 2009), *3.0 You Can (Not) Redo* (17 November 2012), *3.0+1.0 Thrice Upon a Time* (8 March 2021). Anno served as general director across the project. The first film is close to a remake; each subsequent film diverges further, and the fourth resolves the story in a register the television series explicitly refused. Treat Rebuild as a separate continuity that is *arguing with* the original rather than continuing it.

**On canon.** The property has no unreliability doctrine of the kind setting-c publishes about itself, but it has something more useful: a body of official material that contradicts itself, an author who has repeatedly declined to adjudicate, and decades of supplementary text (production materials, the *Classified Information* screens, interviews) of varying authority. Be precise about tier. A great deal of what "everyone knows" about Evangelion — the identity of the soul in Rei, the exact mechanics of Instrumentality, the meaning of the final scene — is inference from partial statements. That is not a defect for our purposes. It is **M17. Withhold the distinction** operating at the scale of an entire property.

### 1. THE PREMISE AND TIMELINE

**Second Impact**, 13 September 2000, Antarctica. Officially a meteorite strike. Actually the result of a research contact experiment with a being designated **Adam**, conducted by a human institution that knew broadly what it was doing. The southern ice cap vaporises, the axis shifts, sea levels rise, roughly half the human population dies in the event and the wars and famines that follow.

The story proper is set in **2015**, fifteen years later, in **Tokyo-3** — a fortress city built over the Geofront, itself built around the buried body of a second being, **Lilith**. Beginning in 2015, entities designated **Angels** (*shito*, more exactly "apostles") arrive one at a time and attempt to reach Lilith. If one succeeds, the result is **Third Impact** and the end of the species.

The only weapon that works against them is the **Evangelion**: a human-built giant constructed from the recovered material of Adam (Unit-00, Unit-02, and the production series) or Lilith (Unit-01) **[inferred in part]**, piloted by a fourteen-year-old child.

The reason the apparatus needs children is never given a satisfying operational justification in the television series, and the absence of that justification is load-bearing. What *is* stated is that the pilots must have been born after Second Impact. The register should notice what this does: it converts a cohort defined by a historical catastrophe into a procurement category.

**The clean structural fact:** the war against the Angels, which occupies almost the whole series, is not the plot. It is the cover story for the plot, and both the audience and most of the cast are inside the cover story for twenty-four episodes.

### 2. THE COSMOLOGY

Stated plainly, because the register's interest is in what is *materially true* in a setting rather than what is believed:

**Adam and Lilith** are two beings of the same order, delivered to Earth by an unexplained prior agency in vessels described as "Seeds of Life." By the rules the setting sets out, a planet is meant to receive only one. Earth received two. Adam's descendants are the Angels; Lilith's descendants are humans — **Lilin**, in the Angels' own usage. The Angels are therefore not invaders. They are the branch of the family that did not inherit, arriving to correct a clerical error in the distribution of the planet.

**The AT Field** — *Absolute Terror Field*. The barrier that makes an Angel effectively invulnerable, penetrable only by another AT Field. And, stated in the text and not merely implied: **every human being has one.** It is the light of the soul; it is what makes a person a discrete person; it is the reason you cannot be another person and the reason no one can reach you. The physics of the setting and its psychology are the same mechanism at two scales.

This is the property's central move, and it is worth stating in the register's own terms: **the self is real, it has a measurable field strength, it is the thing keeping the species alive, and it is also the precise cause of every character's suffering.**

**The Human Instrumentality Project** is the dissolution of every AT Field at once. All individual consciousness returns to a single undifferentiated body — the primordial soup of **LCL** — ending separation and therefore ending loneliness, misunderstanding, and the impossibility of being known. It is presented, seriously and without irony, as **a solution to a real problem.** The suffering it proposes to end is genuine suffering, established over twenty-six episodes in exhaustive psychological detail. This is not a scheme by liars. It is a mercy, correctly identified, with an unacceptable price, offered by people who have thought about it longer than anyone else alive.

**The Dead Sea Scrolls** are the governing document: a prophetic text held by SEELE which describes the sequence of Angel arrivals and the conditions of Instrumentality. The committee treats them as a project plan. The word used repeatedly across the series is **"scenario"** — as in *according to the scenario*, *a deviation from the scenario*, *the scenario has been altered*. An eschatology managed as a programme with a schedule and a change-control process.

### 3. THE INSTITUTIONS

#### NERV

A special agency operating under direct UN authority, headquartered in the Geofront beneath Tokyo-3, charged with the destruction of the Angels and the completion of "Project E." Its predecessor organisation was **Gehirn**, dissolved and reconstituted; the same people simply moved across.

Commander **Gendo Ikari**. Sub-commander **Kozo Fuyutsuki**, formerly a professor of metaphysical biology, brought in knowing more than is comfortable. Head of Project E and of Technical Division One: **Ritsuko Akagi**, whose mother **Naoko Akagi** built the MAGI and died in the building. Head of Operations: **Misato Katsuragi**, who was at Antarctica as a child and is the only survivor of the expedition that caused Second Impact.

**Why it is horrifying.** NERV is a competent organisation. It is funded, staffed, audited, and answerable, and it wins nearly every engagement it fights. It also exists to accomplish something other than what its charter says, and the number of people inside it who know that is roughly four. Everyone else — the bridge crew, the maintenance technicians, the section chiefs, the medical staff who take the children's blood pressure — is doing excellent work in good faith on the wrong project. The register's standing requirement that **nobody in the file is a sadist** is met here by an entire organisation.

#### SEELE and the Human Instrumentality Committee

Above NERV sits **SEELE** (German: *soul*), an ancient body that funds and directs it and holds the Scrolls. In the series it appears almost exclusively as a set of black monoliths in a void, labelled with numbers and *Sound Only* — a committee attending by teleconference, rendered as the least dramatic possible image.

That rendering is the best institutional-horror device in the property and costs nothing to steal. SEELE never appears as robed conspirators. It appears as **an agenda item**. The men deciding to end the species are attending remotely, one of them is chairing, and the minutes are being kept.

**Why it is horrifying.** SEELE's plan is not concealed from the audience for its shock value; it is disclosed steadily from the middle of the series. The horror is that disclosure changes nothing, because the committee has already secured the funding, written the specification, and commissioned the hardware. You are watching the back half of a procurement cycle.

#### The Marduk Institute

The body that selects the pilots. Presented as an independent screening organisation with a legitimate remit, later revealed to be **a shell — one of 108 dummy corporations**, all of them NERV, the selection having been made internally all along.

This is the single most transferable object in the property. An institution that requires a specific class of person, and that has built a plausible independent body to certify that the person it already chose is the right one. The paperwork is real. The committee meets. The finding is genuine. The finding was also never in doubt.

#### The MAGI

Three supercomputers — **Melchior, Balthasar, Casper** — that govern NERV's systems and vote on decisions. They were built by Naoko Akagi by transplanting her own personality, split three ways: **as a scientist, as a mother, and as a woman.** Deadlocks between them are resolved by majority. Ties are reported as such.

**Why it is horrifying.** The institution's decision procedure is one dead woman's unresolved internal conflict, running in perpetuity, and it is regarded as a technical achievement. When the MAGI deadlock, what the operators are watching is a person who has been dead for years failing to make up her mind. Nobody in the room finds this strange, because it is simply how the system works. Compare the register's existing line on **inherited containment** — this is its purest available form.

### 4. THE INSTRUMENT AND ITS OPERATORS

#### The Evangelions

Not vehicles. Not robots. Cloned bodies of the same order as the Angels, grown by humans, restrained. The "armour" is a restraint system, and the series is explicit about this: it is there to hold the thing in, not to protect it. Each unit runs from an external power cable; disconnected, it has an internal battery of approximately five minutes, and the countdown is displayed.

Under sufficient stress a unit will go **berserk** — act on its own, without power, without pilot input, and considerably more effectively than when it is being operated. The technicians' word for a unit exceeding its design performance is a word for a loss of control.

**Unit-01** contains the soul of **Yui Ikari**, Shinji's mother, absorbed during a contact experiment ten years earlier that her husband ran and her son watched. **[inferred, though heavily supported]** The boy is therefore placed inside his mother's body and told to fight. Nobody who knows this tells him. His father knows. Ritsuko knows. Fuyutsuki knows.

#### The entry plug, LCL, and synchronisation

The pilot sits in a cylindrical plug inserted into the spine of the unit, flooded with **LCL** — a liquid the pilot breathes, oxygenated directly. Control is not mechanical. It is **synchronisation**: a measured percentage, tracked over time, posted, discussed in meetings, and improved or degraded by the pilot's psychological state.

The register should sit with what that means administratively. A fourteen-year-old's emotional condition is a **performance metric with a numeric value**, reviewed by their supervisor, and a bad month at school shows up on the chart. When a pilot's sync ratio falls, the organisation's response is a management response. When it rises after a personal catastrophe, that is noted approvingly.

Damage to the unit is felt by the pilot. Not as feedback — as injury. There is a scene in which a pilot's arm is broken by the breaking of the machine's arm, and the operations staff watch the readouts.

#### The Children

Designated by number and referred to collectively as **the Children**: **Rei Ayanami** (First), **Asuka Langley Soryu** (Second), **Shinji Ikari** (Third), **Toji Suzuhara** (Fourth), **Kaworu Nagisa** (Fifth).

**Rei** is not a person the organisation recruited. She is a person the organisation *made*, from Lilith's material and Yui's genetic contribution **[inferred]**, in multiple copies, held in a tank in the basement. She has died and been replaced at least once, and the replacement continues the assignment. She is aware of this in outline and treats it as her circumstance rather than her grievance.

**Asuka** is a university graduate at fourteen, selected as a small child, whose entire identity is her competence in the role, and who is destroyed by the discovery that the role does not require her specifically.

**Shinji** is not a reluctant hero. He is a child who is asked, refuses, is told that a girl on a stretcher will be sent instead, and gets in. The apparatus does not need to coerce him. It needs only to show him the alternative and let him make the correct decision. He makes it repeatedly, over the entire series, and it destroys him each time.

**Why it is horrifying.** Every safeguarding institution a child would normally have — school, guardianship, medical care, the state — is present, funded, and staffed. Shinji has a teacher, an apartment, a legal guardian, a doctor. All of it is administered by his employer.

#### The Dummy Plug

A system permitting a unit to operate without a pilot, using a manufactured personality. The personality used is **Rei's**, mass-produced.

Its most consequential use: the pilot in the plug is a boy Shinji knows, and Shinji is locked out of his own controls while the dummy system tears that unit apart. The organisation had the capability, deployed it correctly, and achieved the objective.

The dummy plug is the answer to the question the whole apparatus is built to avoid asking — *do we need the person, or only the shape of the person* — and the answer is available in the basement, in quantity.

### 5. THE AESTHETIC AND TONE

- **Institutional flatness rendered as beauty.** Establishing shots hold on power pylons, cicadas, escalators, empty corridors, a vending machine, a ceiling. The camera lingers on infrastructure the way other shows linger on faces.
- **Bureaucratic typography as a visual language.** Warning placards, mission clocks, telemetry, classification banners, countdowns, the *Sound Only* monoliths. Text on screen is a primary channel, and it is almost always administrative text.
- **Silence and duration used as pressure.** Shots held past comfort. The famous elevator scene runs on nothing but two people not speaking.
- **Extreme tonal range, played straight.** Genuine comedy — a penguin, a school festival, a domestic sitcom register — installed early and load-bearing, exactly as the register's **M27** describes. It is not relief. It is anaesthetic.
- **Interiority rendered as documentary.** Episodes 16, 20, 25 and 26 drop into text cards, monochrome stills, therapy-transcript dialogue, and unattributed voices. The show's method for a psychological interior is *a file*.
- **Religious iconography used without theological commitment.** Crosses, Sephirot diagrams, angelic nomenclature, the Lance of Longinus. Staff have said in interviews the imagery was chosen substantially for effect. **Be honest about this rather than defending it** — and note what it means for us: the register's own tests would fail this material on the setting-b axis, since the theology is not materially true so much as materially *present*. Steal the apparatus, not the vestments.

### 6. THE INSTITUTIONAL LOGIC

#### The budget is the plot

Money is discussed constantly and seriously. Unit-02's transport, the cost of the N2 mines, the expense of a failed operation, the UN's willingness to keep paying, the political consequences of destroying a city block to kill an Angel. A whole episode turns on a rival agency's competing project and the funding fight around it. When Tokyo-3's defences fire, someone later signs for it.

#### The scenario as a governing document

Both SEELE and Gendo run against written plans, and the tension in the back half is a **specification dispute**. Two parties with the same document, differing on interpretation, each quietly building the capability to execute their own reading. The end of the world arrives as a contract disagreement between the client and the prime contractor.

#### Fourteen-year-olds as a procurement category

The requirement is stated in terms of birth date. The selection is laundered through an institute. The candidates are schooled, housed, insured, and reviewed. The paperwork for putting a child in the machine is complete and correct at every step, and the completeness is the horror — there is no missing form to point at, no oversight failure to reform. **Remove the awful thing and the system stops.**

#### What the apparatus does with a death

When a pilot is maimed, the organisation processes it: medical, notification, replacement, adjustment of the roster, a conversation about sync ratios. The narrative does not pause to grieve; the institution does not pause either, and those two facts are the same fact rendered twice.

### 7. ANALYSIS

#### Dominant themes

- **The self as a barrier with a measurable strength** — the only thing keeping you alive and the only thing keeping you alone, and these are one property, not two.
- **Salvation offered sincerely, as the removal of personhood.** The mercy is real, the diagnosis is correct, the price is everything.
- **The instrument is a person, and the institution knows.**
- **Parental use of a child as an instrument**, conducted through correct channels, with the child's consent obtained honestly.
- **Competence in a role as a substitute for a self**, and the collapse when the role proves indifferent to who fills it.
- **The interchangeable person** — the replacement who continues the assignment, and the manufactured personality that performs it adequately.
- **The cover story that occupies the entire visible plot.**
- **Intimacy as unbearable rather than redemptive.** The hedgehog's dilemma is named in the text; being known is presented as a genuine danger, not a reward withheld.

#### What makes it unique — specifically against the register's existing sources

- **Watts says the self is unnecessary overhead. Evangelion says the self is real, is the source of the pain, and can be switched off deliberately as an act of compassion.** Same target, opposite mechanism. Watts threatens the self's *necessity*; Evangelion threatens its *desirability*, which is harder to argue against and much harder to write around.
- **setting-c's institutions have forgotten what they maintain. Evangelion's institution knows exactly what it maintains and has a schedule for it.** setting-c supplies decay; Evangelion supplies a live project plan with a delivery date.
- **setting-b's Church is correct about a real God. Evangelion's committee is correct about a real cosmology and is using it for a purpose no scripture assigned.** Verified metaphysics turned to a private end.
- **Chiang follows a premise to its second-order effects. Evangelion is the rare case where the second-order effects are the show** — the school, the rent, the promotion, the funding fight — and the premise itself stays largely off-screen.
- **djkaktus makes the artifact do a job. Evangelion makes the *organisation chart* do the job**, and it is the register's best example of anatomy-as-dread at feature length.

#### What makes it horrifying — precisely

- **The apparatus never has to coerce.** It presents the true alternative and lets the correct decision be made. Consent is real, informed, freely given, and worse for it.
- **Every protective institution is present and is administered by the interested party.** There is no gap in the safeguarding to campaign about.
- **The thing that would fix it is available and would end the person.** Instrumentality works. That is the problem.
- **The replacement is adequate.** The dummy plug performs. Rei II continues. Asuka's collapse is caused by information, not injury.
- **The decision procedure is a dead woman's ambivalence,** and the staff find it unremarkable.
- **The reveal is reinterpretation, not information.** Almost everything is visible from early on — the tank, the monoliths, the Marduk letterhead, the budget. Only the meaning inverts. **M26**, executed across twenty-six hours.
- **The ending is delivered twice, and neither is retracted.** The audience adjudicates. **M29**, at the scale of an entire property.

#### WHAT A WRITER CAN STEAL — twelve concrete moves

1. **Give the self a field strength.** Make the boundary between persons a measurable quantity with a unit, a readout, and an engineering literature. Then have someone propose reducing it for good reasons, in a meeting, with slides.
2. **Build the shell that certifies the choice you already made.** An independent selection body, properly constituted, whose finding was never in doubt — and let the audience meet the body before they learn it is a subsidiary. The paperwork must be genuine. That is what makes it work.
3. **Make the operator's emotional state a performance metric.** A number, tracked over time, discussed by their manager, improved by catastrophe. Nothing dramatises institutional capture faster than a good month on the chart.
4. **Put the safeguards in and make them wholly owned.** Not an absence of protection — a complete apparatus of protection, correctly staffed, administered by the party it should be protecting against. Remove the reformable failure and the horror has nowhere to discharge.
5. **Render the committee as a teleconference.** Numbered participants, *Sound Only*, an agenda. Never robes, never a chamber. The most consequential decision in the setting should be minuted.
6. **Offer the mercy sincerely.** Have the terrible solution actually solve the suffering you have spent the whole piece establishing. It must be a real answer to a real problem, argued by its best advocate — **M28** — or the refusal of it is cheap.
7. **Make the alternative the coercion.** Never force the protagonist. Show them who goes instead, and let them volunteer. Repeat until it is load-bearing.
8. **Manufacture the replacement and let it be adequate.** The system that runs without the specific person, performing acceptably. The question "do we need *you*, or the shape of you" is answered in the affirmative for the shape, in the basement, in quantity.
9. **Let the cover story occupy the visible plot.** Spend the bulk of the runtime on an urgent, real, competently-executed emergency that is not what the institution is for. Both the audience and most of the cast should be inside it.
10. **Run the decision procedure on a dead person's unresolved conflict**, and have the staff treat this as a technical achievement rather than a haunting.
11. **Stage the apocalypse as a specification dispute.** Two parties, one document, differing readings, each building capability against their own interpretation. End the world on a contract disagreement.
12. **Deliver the ending twice and retract neither.** The same events from inside the subject and from inside the apparatus. Do not adjudicate. The audience assembling the two is the final move.
