# TEST CORPUS — EXTERNAL FICTION

*Well-reputed horror and science-fiction/horror short fiction available free and legal online, for testing a playbook against. Compiled 2026-09-01 across four research passes, roughly 300 fetches. Every URL below was fetched and confirmed by a researcher unless explicitly flagged otherwise.*

*Reputation here is **externally attested**, not asserted — the filter is awards (Shirley Jackson, Bram Stoker, World Fantasy, Nebula, Hugo, Ignyte, Locus, British Fantasy) or settled critical canon. Nothing is included because it seemed good.*

---

## 0. THE HEADLINE FINDINGS

**1. Availability splits sharply by award, and the split runs against the register this project cares about.**

| Award | Short fiction free online | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hugo / Nebula / Ignyte / Locus | **80%+** | These electorates read free online magazines; strong norm of free availability for voting |
| World Fantasy, Short Fiction | ~50–60% | The 10,000-word cap pushes it toward magazines |
| Shirley Jackson, Short Fiction | ~40% overall — **but 60–70% from 2019 onward** | Pre-2019 ballots are anthology- and literary-quarterly-dominated |
| Shirley Jackson, Novelette | ~20–25% | Chapbook publishers, and since 2020 **Amazon Original Stories**, a closed format |
| **Bram Stoker, Short Fiction** | **~10% of winners** | Of ten winners 2017–2026, exactly one is free online. HWA's electorate reads anthologies and collections. |

**Weight any corpus post-2019 for breadth.**

**2. The SF-horror hybrids are the *easy* category, not the hard one.** Horror produced by a technical or biological premise clusters in *Clarkesworld*, *Uncanny*, *Lightspeed*, *Strange Horizons* and *Reactor* — all free, all indexed. The award ecosystem that honours this work is the same one that publishes free. This was the opposite of the expected result.

**3. What you lose is concentrated in one place: the quiet, literary, Aickman-descended end of the Shirley Jackson ballot.** Ballingrud, Kiernan, Llewellyn, DeMeester, Wehunt, Langan, most of Barron's and Bulkin's collected work. That register is almost entirely print-only, and it is precisely the bleak, formally controlled, ambiguous register worth testing against. **There is no free workaround.** Any index claiming otherwise is linking to dead ChiZine and old Apex URLs. Supplementing it means buying *Looming Low*, *Aickman's Heirs*, *Fearful Symmetries*, *North American Lake Monsters*.

**4. The two deepest, best-matched horror archives have explicitly said no to automated collection.** *Nightmare* and *Lightspeed* carry formal anti-scraping policies naming bots, crawlers, scripts and bulk downloading, with stated enforcement. *Clarkesworld*'s robots.txt disallows fetching and its editor campaigns publicly against AI scraping. **These are free to read and link. They are not free to copy.** Respect that: read them in a browser, cite by URL, and keep only your own notes locally.

### 1.1 Peter Watts — the single highest-value source

**Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 2.5**, confirmed at the source. Non-commercial; he additionally *requests* no editing of the prose (not a licence term — BY-NC-SA formally permits derivatives; immaterial for a read-only corpus).

### 1.2 SCP Foundation

**Licence: CC BY-SA 3.0**, mandatory and unchangeable by contributors, commercial use permitted, share-alike required. Attribution must carry work name, author handle and a link back. **Exception: images are a mixed-rights minefield** — the SCP-173 sculpture photograph is not CC. **Take text only.**

**Bulk plain text, clean and confirmed: https://scp-data.tedivm.com/** (repo: https://github.com/scp-data/scp-api). Daily-crawled JSON covering items, tales, GOI and hubs, with `raw_content` (HTML) and `raw_source` (wikitext), **plus ratings and tags — so you can filter to critically-regarded material programmatically.**

Already a `[S]` source in this repo, but worth noting what it is *structurally*: the largest institutional-horror corpus in existence, and found-document form by default. For a literary corpus, skip the famous-but-thin monsters (173, 096, 682) and take the pieces with actual critical discussion:

**SCP-3999** (metafictional authorial collapse — the wiki's most formally ambitious piece) · **SCP-5000** ("Why?", Foundation-as-antagonist reversal, narrative not report-form) · **SCP-2521** (told **entirely in pictograms** because words summon it) · **SCP-3001** ("Red Reality", researcher log degrading over subjective years) · **SCP-1730** ("What Happened to Site-13?") · **SCP-231** · **SCP-2000** · **SCP-3000** · **S.D. Locke's Proposal** · **Dr Clef's Proposal** · **SCP-093** · **SCP-1981**

**https://smallbeerpress.com/creative-commons/** — genuinely under-known, and **plain text and RTF are offered directly**.

Contains **"The Specialist's Hat"** (World Fantasy Award, Best Short Fiction 1999 — dead-child narrators, chant-repetition, unreliable close) and "Travels with the Snow Queen" (second person). ### 1.4 Public domain — see §4 for the story-level set

**Standard Ebooks (CC0 on their production work)** — https://standardebooks.org/subjects/horror — best text quality available; OPDS feed and per-book Git repos at https://github.com/standardebooks/. **Project Gutenberg** — deeper but messier.

### 1.5 Two negative findings worth recording

⚠️ **Escape Artists / PseudoPod is NOT copyable.** This is a common and costly misconception. The legal page confirms the **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 covers the audio episodes only**; the story texts are not CC-licensed, and ND forbids derivatives regardless. PseudoPod *does* carry full story text on episode pages — so it is excellent to read and link — but it is not a CC text source.

⚠️ **Greg Egan has never CC-licensed anything.** gregegan.net is notes, applets and extras — no free-fiction index. Individual stories are free at their original venues under ordinary terms. Link-only.

⚠️ **Ted Chiang: almost nothing is legitimately free.** PDFs of "Hell Is the Absence of God" on squarespace CDNs and an archive.org item called `short_story_roulette` are **unauthorised scans — do not use them.** The one clean exception is **"The Great Silence" (2015)** at its authorised original venue, e-flux journal #65: **https://www.e-flux.com/journal/65/336684/the-great-silence** — ~2,000 words, written as the text component of an Allora & Calzadilla installation. A first-person address from a Puerto Rican parrot beside the Arecibo telescope: **SF-horror by demotion**, SETI listening to the stars while an intelligent species dies next to it. Everything else is collection-only.

**Efficiency note:** PG 11870 yields #5, #6 and #10 in one file (plus "The Stolen Bacillus" and "The Flowering of the Strange Orchid"). Standard Ebooks' *Can Such Things Be?* yields #4 and #8.

**Why each matters, compressed:** O'Brien is the first invisible-monster story *and* the first to treat the monster as a specimen — the empirical procedure is the horror. Bulwer-Lytton is the pivot from ghost story to materialist explanation. Maupassant is the most-cited text in the scientific-horror lineage. Bierce's "Damned Thing" explains an invisible killer as a colour outside the visible spectrum, delivered through a coroner's inquest — the direct ancestor of "The Colour Out of Space," and the cleanest demonstration of the category. "The Sea Raiders" is where the deadpan documentary mode of weird fiction starts. "Moxon's Master" is machine consciousness in 1899. Hodgson runs deep time as a horror device decades before it was standard.

## 5. REPOSITORIES, TEXT QUALITY, COPYRIGHT

### 5.1 Where to get text, and what needs cleaning

**Safe to ingest as-is:** **Standard Ebooks** (best quality available — Gutenberg transcriptions re-proofed against page scans, semantic markup, CC0, OPDS feed + per-book Git repos) · **hplovecraft.com** (only site chrome to strip) · **Faded Page** (~9,100 titles, Canadian public domain — useful for authors past the Canadian cutoff whose later work is still US-restricted; M.R. James *Collected Ghost Stories*: https://www.fadedpage.com/showbook.php?pid=20130801) · **Project Gutenberg files numbered ~50000+** (Distributed Proofreaders output).

**Needs cleanup:** **Gutenberg pre-2005 files** — human-proofread but with lost italics, ASCII quotes, dropped lines. **Strip the licence header and footer from every file** or they become repeated boilerplate. Search by author ID (`/ebooks/author/NNN`) rather than the search box. Bulk via `gutenberg.org/robot/harvest?filetypes[]=txt`. Individual stories usually only reachable inside collection files — **you will be splitting volumes yourself.** · **Wikisource** — holds *story-level* URLs, which Gutenberg largely does not; quality tracks proofreading stage, so **check page status before trusting** · **Gaslight (Mount Royal)** — ⚠️ **moved**: now a static S3 mirror at http://gaslight-lit.s3-website.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/gaslight/index.htm, frozen and unmaintained. Mirror anything you need. Distinctive holdings: Victorian/Edwardian periodical material that never reached Gutenberg · **Gutenberg Australia** — life+50, so it holds what the US site lacks; stopped accepting new titles January 2025.

**Do not ingest:** **Internet Archive `_djvu.txt`** — unproofed OCR; on pulp stock the error rate will poison a corpus. Use for page images and for locating material only. · **HathiTrust page text** — OCR plus access restrictions. Its genuinely useful public artifact is **metadata**, especially the **Renewal ID File** — the fastest way to check whether a 1923–1963 work was renewed. · **The Luminist Archives** — operates on a fair-use rationale with takedown, not a strict PD rule. Verify status yourself.

**Rule of thumb: build from Standard Ebooks where coverage allows, fall back to Gutenberg, treat everything scan-derived as page images to be transcribed rather than text to be ingested.**

### 5.2 Copyright — two tiers, and the distinction matters

**Tier 1 — the clean line.** As of 1 January 2026, works published **1930 and earlier** are US public domain (95 years from publication). **All 28 core-set items sit on the safe side.** *Weird Tales* began March 1923, so **issues 1923–1930 are public domain by term** — covering Lovecraft's early and middle career, Smith's arrival, most of Howard's weird fiction to that point. Scanned issues on Internet Archive; best index at https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/serial?id=weirdtales

**Tier 2 — the non-renewal basis, which is weaker.** That same index records that **the first actively renewed *Weird Tales* issue is February–March 1931.** US works 1923–1963 required renewal after 28 years; most pulp publishers never bothered. So later pulp material is public domain **by non-renewal — a factual claim about renewal records rather than an arithmetic one about dates.**

**Specifically resting on the weaker basis:** Lovecraft's "The Whisperer in Darkness" (1931), "The Dreams in the Witch House" (1933), *At the Mountains of Madness*, "The Shadow over Innsmouth" and "The Shadow Out of Time" (all 1936), plus Smith's and Howard's 1931+ output. The reasoning is sound and widely relied upon, but it has never been settled in court for the Lovecraft estate specifically, and Arkham House historically asserted rights.

**Decision rule:** if the corpus must be defensible without qualification, stop at 1930 — that still gives you 7 of the 12 Lovecraft stories. Otherwise take all 12 and spot-check the Stanford Copyright Renewal Database.

### 5.3 The translation trap

**A public-domain work does not mean a public-domain translation.** PG 10775 — the obvious hit for "Horla" — is **the French original**, which would silently corrupt an English corpus. PG 3090 (*Complete Original Short Stories*) is English but its landing page does not list contents. Resolve via the all-stories index at https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/28076. **This is the one core-set item without a verified clean-text English URL.**

## 6. INDEXES, FOR RE-RUNNING THIS LOOKUP

- **sfadb.com** — best single tool. 100+ awards. Its intro page says "last updated May 2019" but that is misleading — individual award pages are current. **Gives venue, which is the field that predicts free availability.** Does not link full texts.
- **File 770's annual "Where to Read Complete Works or Samples for Free"** — the most useful *free-text* index, published after each Hugo ballot. https://file770.com/2025-hugo-finalists-where-to-read-complete-works-or-samples-for-free/ — swap the year. Hugo/Nebula only.
- **Venue accolades pages — the highest hit-rate approach, and underrated.** Because the venue is free by definition, everything listed is a confirmed free award-attested story. https://www.thedarkmagazine.com/accolades/ · https://clarkesworldmagazine.com/awards_and_recognition/ · equivalents at Nightmare, Uncanny, Lightspeed, Strange Horizons.
- **Locus Online** — reliable news-of-record for shortlists. The legacy Locus Index to SF Awards is frozen; sfadb is its successor.
- **freesfonline.net** — structurally intact, organised by author not award, blocks robots (browser only), significant link rot.
- **ISFDB** — best for bibliographic resolution, not free text.
- **Official award sites are the weakest option** — shirleyjacksonawards.org links to Bookshop.org and nothing else.

**Link rot observed 2026-09-01:** **apex-magazine.com/* now 302-redirects to a Shopify storefront — every old Apex story link is dead**; content survives at `apexbookcompany.com/blogs/apex-magazine/<slug>`. This breaks a very large number of published citations. · tor.com → reactormag.com · Lightspeed slug drift (`/fiction/the-west-topeka-triangle/` 404s, `/fiction/west-topeka-triangle/` works — try both article-ful and article-less forms) · **ChiZine is gone** · Kim Fu's "Pre-Simulation Consultation XF007867" (SJA winner 2022, transcript form) is now **Substack paid-subscribers-only**.

## 8. WHAT YOU HAVE TO GET YOURSELF

*Everything above is what a research pass could reach. This section is the remainder — material that exists, matters, and needs a person with a wallet, a library card, a browser, or an email account. Ordered by value per unit of effort.*

### 8.1 Purchases — the missing register

**This is the important one.** §0 finding 3 stands: the quiet, formally controlled, ambiguous end of the field — the register this project is actually written in — is almost entirely print-only. No amount of further searching fixes it. If that register is to be represented in a test set, it has to be bought.

**Highest density per unit spent, in order:**

1. **Ellen Datlow, *The Best Horror of the Year*, any recent volumes.** The single most efficient purchase available. Datlow reads the entire field including the anthologies and chapbooks that never reach the web, and each volume's summation is also a *year-in-review index* naming what was published where — which doubles as a research tool for finding more. Buy two or three non-adjacent years rather than a run.
2. **Paula Guran, *The Year's Best Dark Fantasy & Horror*.** Different taste, overlapping remit; useful precisely because the two editors disagree.
3. **The anthologies the Shirley Jackson ballot keeps drawing from:** ***Aickman's Heirs*** (Undertow — an SJA winner, and the most on-register title in existence for this project), ***Looming Low*** (Dim Shores), ***Fearful Symmetries*** (ed. Datlow), ***The Weird*** (ed. VanderMeer — the big historical anthology, and the fastest way to acquire the pre-1980 weird canon in one volume).

**Single-author collections whose reputations rest entirely on them:**

- **Robert Aickman** — *Cold Hand in Mine*, *Dark Entries*, *The Wine-Dark Sea*. **Not free anywhere, and central to the register.** `research/craft.md` §6 leans on his introductions; the fiction itself is the thing the annex cannot show you.
- **Nathan Ballingrud** — *North American Lake Monsters* (Small Beer, SJA winner) and *Wounds*. The clearest case of a first-rank reputation with zero free representative work. Note: search results surface OceanofPDF for these. **That is a piracy site. Don't.**
- **Laird Barron** — *The Imago Sequence*, *Occultation*. The free stories at §3 are a thin sample of him.
- **Livia Llewellyn** — *Engines of Desire*, *Furnace* (Word Horde, both SJA finalists).
- **Caitlín R. Kiernan** — *The Ammonite Violin & Others*, *Two Worlds and In Between*.
- **Nadia Bulkin** — *She Said Destroy* (Word Horde, SJA finalist).
- **Michael Wehunt** — *Greener Pastures* (SJA finalist).
- **Kristi DeMeester** — *Everything That's Underneath* (Apex).
- **John Langan** — *Sefira and Other Betrayals*.
- **Ted Chiang** — *Stories of Your Life and Others* and *Exhalation*. `sources/texts/books/ (Chiang, full text)` exists; the fiction is collection-only except "The Great Silence."
- **Kim Fu** — *Lesser Known Monsters of the 21st Century*, which contains "Pre-Simulation Consultation XF007867" (SJA winner, transcript form) — the formally-unusual story I most wanted for §3 and could not reach.

**Two closed formats worth knowing about:** **Amazon Original Stories** has taken a large share of the SJA novelette ballot since 2020 (Nghi Vo's *What the Dead Know*, Kurt Fawver's *The Thirteen Ways We Turned Darryl Datson into a Monster*, both winners) and exists nowhere else. **Black Static** (TTA Press) was DeMeester's main venue and has no free archive — back issues are the only route.

### 8.2 Behind a login you can have and I can't

**A public library card is the highest-leverage thing on this page.** Many library systems include JSTOR, Gale, ProQuest or Project MUSE access at no cost, and several of the gaps below dissolve immediately with one.

**For this corpus:** the literary quarterlies that appear on pre-2019 SJA ballots — *Granta*, *Tin House*, *The Southern Review*, *The Paris Review*, *The New Yorker* — are paywalled to me and often free to a cardholder. Mariana Enriquez's "Spiderweb" (*New Yorker*, 2016) is one example among many.

**For `research/craft.md`, which is the same errand:** its Part VII lists what could not be sourced, and a good share is a single database away —

- **Joanna Russ, "The Wearing Out of Genre Materials,"** *College English* 33.1 (1971), pp. 46–54 — JSTOR. **The highest-value unretrieved item in the ideation material**; its three-phase schema (Innocence / Plausibility / Decadence) is the missing theory of why premises exhaust.
- **Bruce Robbins, "Cruelty Is Bad: Banality and Proximity in *Never Let Me Go*,"** *Novel* 40:3 (2007) — Project MUSE. The highest-value unretrieved item in the theme material.
- **Carol Clover, "Her Body, Himself,"** *Representations* 20 (1987) — UC Press/JSTOR. The 1987 original differs from the 1992 book chapter quoted in the annex.
- **Barbara Creed,** *Screen* 27:1 (1986) — Oxford Academic.
- **HathiTrust full-volume download** is institutional-only; a university affiliation unlocks it.

**Subscriptions:** Kiernan's **Sirenia Digest** is subscription-only and is where a lot of her short work lives.

### 8.3 Sites that refuse a script and welcome a reader

These blocked automated access during the research but are perfectly usable in a browser. **Nothing here is a paywall — it is an automation policy, and reading them yourself is exactly what they want.**

- **Clarkesworld** — robots.txt disallow. Free to read, and it holds "The Things," "Spar," "Things With Beards," "Sweetbaby," "Window Boy," the Omelas story. **Read it in a tab.**
- **Nightmare** and **Lightspeed** — formal anti-scraping policies. Same situation: the archives are free and open to a human.
- **freesfonline.net** — blocks robots, browser-only. Organised by author; the best way to sweep one writer's free output at a time.
- **sfadb.com** and **Locus Online** — both now block automated fetching. sfadb is still the best award index in existence and gives *venue*, which is the field that predicts availability.
- **Wikisource** — was unreachable from my fetch layer entirely (see §7). It holds **story-level URLs**, which Gutenberg largely does not. If you want single stories rather than whole volumes, this is where to look, and only you can look.
- **Wikipedia's award tables** — did not render through automated fetch; fine in a browser. Fastest way to walk a full ballot year by year.

### 8.4 Verification errands

Small, cheap for a person, and each closes a flagged uncertainty:

- **The Horla, in English.** The one core-set item with no verified clean English text (§5.3). PG 10775 is the French. Resolve via the story index at https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/28076 and confirm which volume of PG 3090 carries it.
- **The Wikisource URLs** in §4, which rest on search metadata rather than inspection.
- **Two Clarkesworld URLs** in §3 unverified by fetch but almost certainly live.
- **The `research/craft.md` print-check list** — King's *Danse Macabre* page numbers and the "phobic pressure points" wording, Gardner's page numbers, Clute's SIGHTING entry, Edmund Wilson on Lovecraft. Each is one physical copy and five minutes.

### 8.5 The one that needs a person, not a tool

**Ask.** I cannot send email; you can.

The two archives best matched to this project — **Nightmare** and **Clarkesworld** — are also the two that have most explicitly refused automated collection. But their policies are aimed at scraping and AI training, and **a named individual asking to use a defined set of stories for a private craft experiment is a different request entirely.** Editors answer that kind of mail. John Joseph Adams (Nightmare/Lightspeed) and Neil Clarke (Clarkesworld) are both reachable and both publicly engaged on exactly this issue.

Worth doing for **Escape Artists / PseudoPod** too — §7 records that two research passes disagreed about whether their Creative Commons licence covers story text or only the audio, and the fetched legal page says audio. **One email settles it**, and if the text is usable, PseudoPod's 1,000+ episodes with full transcripts become the largest clean horror corpus available.

The worst outcome of asking is that you are where you already are.
