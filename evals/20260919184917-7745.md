## Shape

The channel opens upside down in the wreck under three moons, names its
narrator and his engineer's need for a cause, and then stacks wrongnesses:
light from the west, a compass turning, water uphill, a flare answered,
footprints that circle and stop, the boy's notebook that draws what is
coming, a reflection that moves first, a second wreck with the same flight
number and the narrator's own bag in it, doubles of two people, a fold in
the sky, a fight, a swim to the boats, a coda at home where the watch loses
seventeen minutes once. First person past, reported speech only, an
ensemble of six named by role, every dread named as it is felt.

Our draft is a day-log. A loadmaster who was a nuclear machinist's mate
builds a still, a furnace, an antenna and wakes the weather radar from a
whole 767 on flat ground under a too-wide orange sun, in a field of black
towers. The horror arrives as measurements: the radar's ground moves as one
piece; the goats' kidneys fail; the seep water has Earth's deuterium ratio;
a boot-wide seam six hundred metres out closes into a circle of 2,100
metres. The reading, "we were freight, we're standing in the hole", comes
at 68 % and is said by the one man who assumed a floor. It ends on a radar
sweep that shows nothing, "and meant not yet".

Different bets again. The channel escalates a presence and pays it off with
doubles and an escape; the draft withholds a presence entirely and pays off
with a boundary. Ours has the ensemble this time, and dialogue, and the
captain's four versions of the crash as the running human thread. It has
no ending in the channel's sense: nobody leaves.
