the form to the brief: a first beat a listener cannot stop inside, a beat

## What the template did

- **The requirements travel; the skeleton does not have to.** Every one of
  the four landed, in a brief with no crew, no mission and no return, and
  the ledger stayed clean: one open flag against nine under `signal`.
- **The form the brief chose is literary, not spoken.** A written account
  addressed to nobody gives 17.8 words a sentence, a fifth of them over
  thirty, almost no quoted speech and half the pool's body words. That is
  the price of leaving the container to the brief, and it is the whole of
  the gap the judge saw.
- **theme-stated fired on 13 of 14 beats**, the highest yet. A
  retrospective account says what things meant; the screen reads every such
  line as a theme. The flag is measuring the register.
- **The arrival screen was looking at the aftermath.** See Deviations 3.
- **No register rewrites ran**, because the container is not told. Under
  `signal` the same brief spent about 90 calls on rewrites; this draft
  spent 166 calls in total against 139.

## Verdict

For interesting generations, `listen` is the better default: it kept the
premise stage's tail and the brief's own shape, and still produced the
arrival and the cost. For a story that competes as a listen, `signal` and
`told` are still ahead, because they fix a container that is spoken. The
next move is to keep the listen requirements and constrain the container
alone, leaving person, tense and chronology to the brief.
