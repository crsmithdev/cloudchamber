# Evaluation run — the Ark a fourth time, with every beat placed in time

Run 2026-09-20 (afternoon), the first draft under 6ec3d89: the schedule
gives each beat a `<when>`, one phrase placing it in the chronology the
`<form>` declares; the scene ask carries it and, when it differs from the
beat before, asks the opening to place the listener before the events
begin; `time-unplaced` is a `STRUCTURE_RULES` row with a new `moved`
selector, so the question costs a call only on the beats that move.

Motive: run 9 lost one axis, clarity at 8 of 15, and every judge sentence
named the timeline — "jumps between nine days, eleven years, and 393 with
no signposts". Reading the schedule showed the chronology it declared was
already simple, linear with one recursion, and that the fault was
elsewhere: a beat tag carried job, known, withheld, stakes, set_piece,
absorbs and pays, and **no time**, so neither the scene ask nor any screen
knew when a beat happened.

Same seed and source as runs 5, 7, 8 and 9 (`1a-yLGuSlM0`, 14,473 words,
88 minutes).

## Clarity: the axis the change was aimed at

Run 9 lost clarity 8 of 15. Run 10 takes it 14 of 15, on a chronology
with two strands and a fifty-six-year span.

**The screen never fired.** `time-unplaced` was asked on 28 beat-screenings
and raised zero flags. Every beat that moved in time opened by naming the
move, so the constraint in the scene ask did the work and the screen found
nothing to repair. This is the shape run 8 produced on presence: the
upstream ask earns the axis and the screen records that it did.

The single loss is Kimi, which still felt the decade leaps: "One leaps
across decades — from the sump to Josué and Aurelia's later conversation".
That is the ground strand's widening intervals, not an unsignposted jump.

## What the source still wins

**People, 12 of 15** — GPT-5.1 twice, GLM once, and the same sentence
every time: the source has a wide cast with separable cadences, ours has
two. "Story One's voices — Ela, Enkemlu, Aubry, Thorne, Dario, Okoro —
have distinct cadences and conversational habits, while Story Two's
characters tend to speak more sparsely and similarly." This was run 8's
only loss too, and `one-voice` still cannot see it: it asks whether two
speakers sound alike, and answers absent when only one or two speak.

**Cost, 12 of 15** — all three GPT-5.1. Not a craft judgement but a scale
one: "6,000 marked people, dead officers, and Isa losing Dario" against
"losses more contained to one man". The `cost-paid` and `cost-in-scene`
questions both pass; they ask whether a named person loses something
irreversible on the page, and they cannot ask how many.

## Defect found

A `<when>` on beat 7 of the second schedule closed with `</known>`, and
`tag()` took the malformed text. Harmless here — the beat moved from the
one before it either way — but `<when>` needs the same tag tolerance
`<answer>` and `<examined>` were given in 04f5327 and 8cf8a82.

## What this says to do next

1. **A one-speaker question.** People is now the joint-weakest axis and
   has lost on the same sentence for two runs. A deterministic count of
   quoted lines per speaker per beat, with a floor, would see what
   `one-voice` cannot.
2. **Leave clarity alone.** Two strands and fifty-six years scored 14 of
   15; the signposts are enough.
3. **Cost is a scale judgement, not a craft one.** Answering it means
   asking the premise for a loss that reaches past one person, which is a
   change to the premise ask, not to a screen.
