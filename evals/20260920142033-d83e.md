## The paying beat

Beat 10, the furrows on Kerrigan's Reach. On its first screen pass the
structure screen answered `presence-arrives` and `cost-paid` present, and
`presence-in-room` and `cost-in-scene` **absent**, so the beat was rewritten
once under both lines together.

| question | before the rewrite | after |
|---|---|---|
| cost-in-scene | absent | **present**: *"I tested them for nine days," she said. "I tested them in Elias."* |
| presence-in-room | absent: *"It did not bleed. It did not weep. It sat there pale and fibrous, exactly the way a cut stem sits."* | **absent**: *"The tips stood up out of the frost in their order."* |

The cost line worked on the first try. The presence line did not, and by
the rule the beat now waits for a person at gate 2. The screen's refusal is
correct under the bar ccf952b set: the withheld thing here is a blight. It
has no body, it cannot touch, move, break or take, and no rewrite of the
beat can make it. **The bar is unsatisfiable for a premise whose withheld
thing is not an agent**, and the premise stage did not know that when it
chose this one. The judges agree with the screen: GPT-5.1 gave the source
presence on all three clean passes, "the threat stays mostly conceptual
until the eerie rows on the Reach", against a source in which the enemy
"shows up embodied in Thomas Rayburn's inhumanly precise violence".

## Verdict

The `listen` template is at parity with its source on every run, and on the
clean text this seed goes 8/9 → 9/9 with the numeral budget. Cost, ending,
feeling, clarity and momentum are clean sweeps. **What the source still
wins is presence, people and the hook**, and every one of those is decided
upstream of the draft: the premise chose a thing with no body; the schedule
gave the cast one register; beat 1 opens on a soil audit where the source
opens on the end of the world.

   ask should require a thing that can act on a person, and the arrival
   should recur: on the page in three beats, the first by the one-third

   a one-line voice each, every scene prompt carries them, and a screen
   counts quoted speech per speaker.
