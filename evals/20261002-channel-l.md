# Channel L: a baseline against a second first-person channel

2 October. Channel L is a first-person horror narration channel next to
channel F in YouTube's results for sci-fi creepypasta narration. Six of its
transcripts are in `corpus/narration/`; three space or first-contact stories
are the ones built against here. One judge, GPT-5.1, eight passes a pair,
both orders, concurrency 2. Gemini is dropped (todo #25). Rows in
`bank/judgements.jsonl` on main.

## Why channel L

The neighbours of channel F in YouTube's results for "sci-fi creepypasta",
"scifi creepypasta narration", "space horror story narration" and "sci-fi
horror stories narrated", with the channels already in the corpus (F, S, G)
taken out:

| Channel | Format | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| L | One narrator, first-person title ("I work at…"), 37 to 57 minutes, weekly, 150 to 450 thousand views. Horror with a space and government subset (NASA, Area 51, the moon). | — |
| M | Sci-fi horror creepypasta, one narrator | Recent uploads are 13 to 15 minutes; the long ones are serial episodes. |
| D | Sci-fi creepypasta and public-domain audiobooks | Mostly audiobooks and compilations now; the single stories are two years old. |
| the broad narrators | Forum-story readings, compilations | Not one long story a video; not sci-fi as a rule. |

L is the only one with channel F's shape: a single narrator, a first-person
hook title, one story of forty to sixty minutes, a steady cadence. Its stories
are shorter on the page than F's (4,700 to 6,800 words against 6,800 to
7,600) and four of the six tracks are manual captions, so the judge reads
cleaner text than it does for F.

## Method

The recipe is the channel F one of 30 September to 2 October (`bf3e`, `17e7`,
`69c0` and the six drafts of 2 October):

```
cloudchamber draw --genre horror --sampling tail --shape listen --auto --seed "<title>"
cloudchamber draft <draw> --profile listen
cloudchamber lab compare --arm <video id> --arm <draw> --passes 8 --concurrency 2 --judges openai/gpt-5.1
```

The seed is the video's title alone. No transcript enters any prompt; the
transcript is the second arm of `lab compare` only. No setting, no new
profile: the channel F recipe runs on channel L titles as it stands.

## What the losses say

**Clarity is the one axis channel L still wins, and for the same reason as
channel F.** The judge's sentence is the same in every lost pass: the source
is "a straightforward chronological arc… easy to track on a single listen";
ours has "time jumps", "layered debriefs", "dense reflective narration",
"procedural detail". `a529` opens on day four, says "go back three nights",
and ends "months later"; its schedule carries `chronology=linear`. That is
todo #24's finding (the fixed-axis check reads the form line, not the beats'
order), seen on a third title. The clause list from the 30 September plan
(H2, the rule budget) did not reach it either: the counting is in the brief.

**Hook is not lost on channel L, except where the title's promise is not
kept.** `a529` and `379f` win hook 8 of 8 (+1.88): the judge takes a specific
first line ("the knocking started inside the tank"; "The horse comes down the
stairs") over the channel's "we don't talk about…" frame. `ba9d` ties hook at
0.00, losing 5 of 8: the title promises a signal from the moon in 1968 and
the draft opens on a woman in a cellar listening to her sewing machine, in
the third person and the present tense. The judge calls the source's first
two sentences "an immediate, high-concept jolt" every time. The draft does
not keep the title's promise in its first minute. Channel F's hook loss
(−0.17, 46%) looks like the same thing at a smaller scale: F's titles are
twist-clause titles too.

**The other six axes saturate.** Presence, people, feeling, cost and ending
sit at +1.6 to +2.2 with no pass lost; the judge scores the channel L
stories 3 and ours 5. The instrument has nothing to say about them here.
Channel L's texts are shorter (4,700 to 5,700 words against our 9,000 to
10,300), hold no dialogue (0 to 4 quotation marks per 1,000 words against our
7 to 13), and are told as one voice's report. GPT-5.1 reads that as the
weaker text on every axis but clarity, and the two F transcripts, at 6,700
and 7,400 words, drew a mean of +0.65 from the same judge against the same
recipe. Channel L is a weaker opponent than F on the page.

**One draft left the channel's form.** `ba9d` is third person, present tense:
2 first-person words per 1,000 against the channel's 35 to 69 and the other
two drafts' 44 to 46. The listen profile sets `person=auto`. The channel is
first person without exception. Todo #33.

## What this is not

- One judge, eight passes a pair, three pairs. At eight passes the halves of
  one pair differ by up to 0.5 on the 24 September pairs; the per-pair axis
  rows above carry that. The pooled mean over 24 passes is firmer, and the
  clarity loss is the same sign on all three pairs.
- Text only. Channel L is a produced listen with a voice and music; the judge
  reads a caption track with no intro, so it reads L at its cleanest and
  still scores it 3.
- Not a test of a change. Nothing in the pipeline changed; the baseline is
  the recipe as it stood on 2 October.
